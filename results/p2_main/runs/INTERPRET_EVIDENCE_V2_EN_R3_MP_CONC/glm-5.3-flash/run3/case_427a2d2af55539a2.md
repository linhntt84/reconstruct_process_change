### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The screening qualified 48 control-flow items where about 0.5 would be expected without any change, and each item's difference is larger than in about 995 of 1,000 random re-splits, so this volume cannot be explained by sampling variation. The evidence supports the hypothesis of a structural replacement of the segment between A39 and A24: in the earlier window each case ran A39, then exactly one of A14, A15 or A16 (chosen per case in roughly equal shares), then A24; in the later window each case runs A39, then all three of A21, A22 and A23 once each in no fixed order, then A24. This is a replacement of activities — three removed, three added, and a one-of-three choice turned into an all-three block — not a shift in how often existing paths are taken, since every involved activity is wholly present in one window and wholly absent in the other. [OCC001, OCC003, OCC004, DIR007, DIR004, REL019, LAG001]

The hypothesis is direct and internally consistent: participation flips between 0 and 200 of 200 (with 59–71 earlier traces behind each old activity's share), A39's successor profile is fully redistributed, pairwise relations flip from mutual exclusivity to universal co-occurrence, and the widened A39→A24 gap matches A24's later positions. An important competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that the two windows capture two different pre-existing process variants whose case mix switched, which would look identical to a modification of one process; the indicators also cannot show whether A21–A23 are genuinely new steps or re-labelled replacements of A14–A16. [OCC001, OCC004, DIR007, REL019, LAG001, POS007]

Other differences: none; A24's later position and the wider A39→A24 gap are consequences of the same replacement rather than separate changes. [POS007, LAG001]
