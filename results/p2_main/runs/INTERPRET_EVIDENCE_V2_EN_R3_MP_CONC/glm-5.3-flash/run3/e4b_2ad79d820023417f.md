### Observed differences

- Resource allocation shifted on five activities (A01, A03, A13, A14, A17). In the earlier window, assignments on each of these went to a stable group of resource codes: User_3 handled about 8–9%, roughly ten further codes 4–8% each, and about 44–49% was scattered over a long tail. In the later window, User_3 and most formerly prominent codes dropped to about zero, while a largely new group took over, led by User_56 at about 10–12% and several codes at 4–7%; a few codes kept similar shares and the tail stayed at about 42–46%. The allocation profile moved by about 0.32–0.35 (cosine dissimilarity) on each activity, and each difference exceeds all but about 1 of 2000 random re-splits of the pooled data; for example, the A01 profile rests on 200 assignments per window and A13/A14 on 262 each. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN006]
- A16 changed most drastically: in the earlier window its 143 assignments were spread over 19 distinct resources with the largest taking about 18%; in the later window a single code, User_64, handled all 143 assignments (concentration index about 0.09 to 1.0, top-resource share about 18% to 100%). [ASSIGN005, CONC001]
- In the later window, the elapsed time between consecutive completions of A04 and A01 lengthened: the median gap grew from about 5.6 to about 17.6 hours (roughly tripled), the mean from about 17.5 to about 32.8 hours, and the upper quartile from about 18 to about 45 hours, while the lower quartile stayed near zero — the widening sits in the slower half of cases. Based on 200 gaps per window, the 17.4-hour difference exceeds all but about 1 of 2000 random re-splits. [TWAIT001]
- No control-flow indicator qualified (0 items where about 0.15 would qualify by chance), so no structural difference is evident at these thresholds. [screening.control_flow]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the allocation of work to resources changed: on A01, A03, A13, A14, A16 and A17, assignments moved from a broad pool of resource codes in the earlier window to a largely different group in the later window, with A16 going from about 19 sharing resources to a single code (User_64) handling every assignment. Supporting signs: seven resource items qualified where about 0.005 (roughly 1 in 200) would qualify by chance — a count chance cannot plausibly produce even if all items describe one underlying reallocation; the change is direct (similar assignment volumes per window but different allocation profiles) and consistent in direction across items; and no control-flow difference qualified, so participation and structure look stable. Limitations: about 42–49% of assignments fall in an aggregated "other" bucket, so the tail of the shift is only partly visible; the A13 and A14 profiles are identical and likely reflect one behaviour rather than two; and the evidence cannot distinguish a change in assignment behaviour from a change in which resources were active in each window. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, CONC001, screening.resources]

Other differences: the gap between consecutive completions of A04 and A01 roughly tripled at the median (about 5.6 to about 17.6 hours). This is a timing difference in its own right, but with a single qualifying time item (about 1 where roughly 0.01 are expected by chance) the evidence cannot show whether it is an independent timing change or a by-product of the reallocation; that competing explanation cannot be ruled out.
