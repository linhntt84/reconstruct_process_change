### Observed differences

- Ordering of A02 and A04: in the earlier window the two activities occurred together in every trace and A02 always came first; in the later window they still occur together in every trace, but A02 comes first in only about 54 in 100 traces, while A04 comes first in about 46 in 100. [REL001]
- Routing at the three upstream activities changed: in the later window A01 sends about 43 in 100 of its outgoing transitions directly to A04 (about 6 in 100 before, when about 87 in 100 went to A02); A02 sends about 44 in 100 directly to A03 (not observed before, when about 94 in 100 went to A04); and A04 sends about 40 in 100 back to A02 (not observed before, when about 96 in 100 went to A03). [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003]
- Event distances shifted consistently: A02 to A03 was typically two events apart (about 90 in 100) and is now immediate in about 44 in 100; A04 to A03 was immediate in about 96 in 100 and is now immediate in only about 49 in 100, with about 43 in 100 two events apart; A01 to A04 was typically two events apart (about 94 in 100) and is now immediate in about 49 in 100. [LAG002, LAG003, LAG004]
- Positions within the trace spread in the same direction: A02 now sometimes appears later (mean relative position about 0.17 to 0.27, upper quartile 0.2 to 0.4) and A04 sometimes earlier (mean about 0.39 to 0.28, median 0.4 to 0.2). [POS001, POS002]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the fixed execution order between A02 and A04 loosened into a mixed order: earlier, traces ran A01, then A02, then A04, then A03; in the later window about half of the traces run A04 before A02, enabled by direct transitions A01→A04, A04→A02 and A02→A03, while A03 still follows the pair and both activities still occur in every trace, so participation itself did not change. The order change is shown directly by the relational evidence, and the successor redistributions, distance shifts and position spreads all match this single rerouting; ten items qualified against about 0.1 expected for an unchanged process, every difference exceeds about 995 of 1000 random re-splits, and each item rests on roughly 170–200 traces per window. The main competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that the reversed ordering existed before only at a rate too low to appear in 200 traces, so the later window might show a rare pre-existing route becoming frequent rather than a new ordering constraint; moreover, the DIR, LAG and POS items largely re-describe the same trace behaviour as REL001 rather than confirming it independently. [REL001, DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, POS001, POS002]

Other differences: A01's rare exits to A05 and A06 (together about 3 in 100 of its outgoing transitions before) are nearly unobserved in the later window, and A04's exit back to A01 rises from about 4 to about 11 in 100; both rest on small counts. [DIR001, DIR003]
