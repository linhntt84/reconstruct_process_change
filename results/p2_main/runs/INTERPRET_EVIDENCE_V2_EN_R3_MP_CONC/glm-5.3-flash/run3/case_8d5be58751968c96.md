### Observed differences

- Participation dropped for six activities: before, A33, A35, A36, A37, A38 and A39 each appeared in every trace (200 of 200); in the later window they each appear in only about 47 in 100 traces (A33, A35, A36) and about 56 in 100 (A37, A38, A39). A30 and A31 still appear in all traces in both windows. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, POS001, POS002]

[REL013, REL014, REL018, REL025, REL026, REL027]
- The block stays anchored between A30 and A31: A30 still precedes each of the six, and each of the six still precedes A31, in essentially every trace where they co-occur, in both windows (reversed order at most about 1 in 100). - Position gaps shrank where the bypass appears: the A30→A37/A38/A39 and A33/A35/A36→A31 event-position gaps were 4–5 steps before and are 1–3 steps in about 95 in 100 of later cases. [LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG007, LAG010]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the fixed middle section between A30 and A31 became a choice between two activity groups. Before, every trace ran A30, then A33, A35 and A36 (in no fixed order among themselves), then A37, A38 and A39 (likewise unordered), then A31. In the later window, roughly 43–45 in 100 traces run only the A33/A35/A36 group and continue directly to A31, about 53 in 100 run only the A37/A38/A39 group directly after A30, and about 3 in 100 still run both groups as before, with the A30→group→A31 order preserved throughout. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, REL007, REL008, REL009, REL010, REL011, REL012, REL013, REL015, REL018, REL022, REL025]

This is a structural change, not a shift in how often existing paths are taken: the six middle activities fell from universal participation to about half, the two groups went from always executing together to being near-exclusive alternatives, and new direct transitions appeared that skip one group (A30→A37/A38/A39; A33/A35/A36→A31), with the corresponding position gaps shrinking from 4–5 steps to 1–3. The screening comparison makes chance an unlikely explanation for the pattern as a whole: 57 control-flow items qualified where about 0.79 would be expected without a change. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG007, LAG010, REL015, REL016, REL017, REL019, REL020, REL021, REL022, REL023, REL024]

Limitations: the old both-groups pattern persists in about 3 in 100 later traces, and the evidence cannot show whether the choice between the two groups is random, case-dependent, or a changing mix of trace populations rather than a uniform rule. Many items also describe the same traces (the same six activities across OCC, REL, DIR, LAG), so they are not independent confirmations. [REL015, REL019, REL022]

Other differences: a handful of very rare new outgoing transitions appear in the later window (A30 to A02/A03/A06, A33 to A26/A10, A35 to A07/A10/A28, A36 to A11/A21/A27, each around 0.5–1 in 100 of outgoing transitions), and the LAG items for cross-group pairs rest on only 4–5 after-window observations, so they carry little weight. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, LAG005, LAG006, LAG008, LAG009, LAG011, LAG012]
