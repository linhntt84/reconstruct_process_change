### Observed differences

- The relative order of two blocks of activities was reversed. In the earlier window, A07 and A09 came before the A05/A06 pair: A07 preceded A05 in about 48 in 100 traces and A09 preceded A06 in about 48 in 100, with the reverse directions essentially absent. In the later window, A05 preceded A07 in about 55 in 100 traces and A06 preceded A09 in about 50 in 100, while the earlier directions dropped to about 14 and 5 in 100. [REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005]
- Typical event positions shifted accordingly: A05 moved from a mean position near the end of the trace (about 0.89) to the early middle (about 0.32), A06 from about 0.89 to about 0.65, A07 from about 0.36 to about 0.74, and A09 from about 0.59 to about 0.98, i.e. to the final position; A04 stayed early (about 0.21–0.22). [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005]
- Adjacencies changed with the order: in the later window, A04 was followed by A05 within 1–2 steps in all observed pairs (before: 3–4 steps in about 85 in 100), and A01 reached A05 within 1–2 steps (before: 4–5 steps in about 91 in 100); at the same time A01-to-A07 stretched from 1–2 steps to 3–4 steps in about 82 in 100, and A04-to-A09 from about 2 steps to 3–5 steps in about 93 in 100. [LAG002, LAG006, LAG004, LAG009]
- Outgoing transitions were redistributed: from A01, the share going to A04 fell from about 76% to 45% while about 45% went to A05; from A04, the share to A07 fell from about 80% to about 9% while about 51% went to A05; from A06, about 76% went to A07 and 16% to A09 in the later window, whereas before about 96% had gone to A05. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR004]
- The internal order of the A05/A06 pair became fixed: before, the two co-occurred in about 48 in 100 traces with both orders nearly equally common (about 25 vs 23 in 100); in the later window, A05 came first in every co-occurrence (about 55 in 100 traces). [REL001, LAG010]
- The earlier ordering was not completely gone: in the later window, about 16% of A07's observed outgoing transitions still went to A06, about 17% of A05's went to A07, and A09 was followed by A06 in about 10 pairs. [DIR003, DIR005, LAG011, LAG012]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the execution order of two blocks was exchanged: the pair A05→A06, which in the earlier window ran after A07 and A09 near the end of each case (in either internal order), ran before them in the later window, so the typical sequence changed from about A01→A04→A07→A09→(A05/A06) to about A01→A04→A05→A06→A07→A09. The reversal is shown directly for all four cross-activity relations, the position and distance shifts agree with it, and the successor profiles of A01, A04 and A06 were redistributed consistently; 28 items qualified against roughly 0.26 expected without a change, and each difference exceeded about 99.5% of random re-splits, so chance cannot explain the ensemble. [REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, LAG002, LAG004, LAG006, LAG009, DIR001, DIR002, DIR004]

Limitations: the items largely describe one underlying reorder rather than independent confirmations; a few later-window relations rest on few observations (about 12 outgoing transitions of A09, 27 pairs A07→A06, 10 pairs A09→A06); and a small share of later traces still shows the earlier direction, so the evidence cannot establish whether the old ordering was removed or persists occasionally. A competing explanation that cannot be ruled out is that both orderings coexisted in both windows and only their relative frequencies shifted — the samples would look the same. [DIR003, DIR005, LAG011, LAG012]

Other differences: the six activities appeared together in about 55 in 100 later-window traces versus about 48 in 100 earlier, a modest rise in how often this block participates at all. [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005]
