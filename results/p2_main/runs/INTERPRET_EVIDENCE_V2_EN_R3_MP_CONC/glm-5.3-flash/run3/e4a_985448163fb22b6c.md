### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The volume of qualifying evidence far exceeds what an unchanged process produces: the control-flow family yielded 27 items where about 0.44 would be expected by chance, and the time family 2 items where about 0.01 would be expected, so random sampling between the windows cannot account for the pattern as a whole. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, DIR001, POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, REL007, REL008, REL009, REL010, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006, TWAIT001, TWAIT002]

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the segment formed by A25, A26, A31, A32 and A33 — previously executed exactly once per case in a fixed pass (A25 first, then A26, A31 and A32 in no fixed order among themselves, then A33, continuing to A20) — is in the later window repeated about three times per case: A33 now leads back to A25 in about two-thirds of its observed outgoing transitions instead of always continuing to A20, and in about two-thirds of cases the five activities interleave or repeat rather than forming a single pass. The indicators agree in size and direction: recurrence in about 67 in 100 traces, interleaved-or-repeated shares of about 67 in 100 for all ten activity pairs, an A33→A25 share of about 67 in 100, a mean of about 3 occurrences per trace (consistent with a loop that returns to A25 with probability about two-thirds and otherwise exits to A20), and pair distances stretching beyond five positions. Limitations: only the outgoing transitions of A33 are profiled, so the loop-back edge is observed directly but the path of each repeated round after A25 is not, and the evidence cannot distinguish a genuine repeat of the segment through A25 from any other mechanism that repeatedly re-executes these five activities; the order among A26, A31 and A32 remains unfixed as before.
