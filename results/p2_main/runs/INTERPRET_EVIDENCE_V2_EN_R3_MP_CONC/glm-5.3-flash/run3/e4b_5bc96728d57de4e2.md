### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that work on the activities A01, A03, A13, A14 and A22 was reallocated from one group of resource codes to another: in the earlier window these activities were handled mainly by codes such as User_78, which later disappear from the leading shares, while previously minor codes such as User_42 and User_45 took over roughly 5–10 in 100 assignments each. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005]

Supporting signs: all five activity-level allocation profiles moved in the same direction, with the same codes gaining and losing share; the resource family produced 5 qualifying items where about 0.005 would be expected by chance, so chance cannot account for them as a whole; support is substantial (200 of 200 traces for A01, about 250–260 for A13 and A14, about 100 for A22); and the control-flow family produced no items, so the activities still occur at similar rates — what changed is who performs them, not whether they are performed. [screening, ASSIGN001, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN005]

Limitations: the evidence cannot show whether a single reassignment underlies all five activities, and it cannot rule out that a shift in the mix of cases routed to different resources produced the same pattern; the reason for the handover is not observable in this evidence. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005]

Other differences: elapsed time between consecutive completions decreased for A04→A01 (median about 68 hours to about 21 hours, with a wider spread) and for A09→A23 (median about 13 hours to about 1 hour, on 35 versus 25 observations). These are timing changes in their own right; whether they are connected to the reallocation cannot be determined from this evidence. [TWAIT002, TWAIT004]
