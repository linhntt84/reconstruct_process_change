### Observed differences

- The elapsed time between a completion of A28 and the next completion of A33 roughly halved. In the earlier window it averaged about 0.91 hours (median about 0.91, middle half about 0.82–0.99 hours) over 58 observed intervals; in the later window it averaged about 0.46 hours (median about 0.46, middle half about 0.42–0.50 hours) over 45 intervals — a reduction of about 0.45 hours. A difference of this size exceeds that seen in about 999.5 of 1,000 random re-splits of the pooled data. [TWAIT001]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a timing change between A28 and A33: in the later window, the elapsed time from a completion of A28 to the next completion of A33 is about half of what it was in the earlier window (median about 0.46 hours versus about 0.91 hours), and the interval became more consistent as well. The measurement is direct for this activity pair, the shift is consistent across every distribution component, it rests on 58 and 45 observations in the two windows, and it is unlikely to be a sampling artefact: the time family produced one qualifying item where about 0.01 would be expected without change, while no control-flow difference accompanies it. [TWAIT001]

Limitations: this indicator measures elapsed time between consecutive completion events, so it does not separate waiting from processing, and it cannot show whether A28's completions moved later, A33's moved earlier, or both. A change in the mix of cases reaching this pair in the later window is a competing explanation that a single item cannot fully rule out. [TWAIT001]

Other differences: the number of observed A28→A33 intervals fell from 58 to 45 (200 traces in each window); this modest reduction is consistent with sampling variation and is not part of the timing hypothesis. [TWAIT001]
