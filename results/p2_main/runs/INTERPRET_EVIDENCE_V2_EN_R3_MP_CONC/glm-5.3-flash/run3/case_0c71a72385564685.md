### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that an additional step now runs between A41 and A26: in the earlier window A41 led directly to A26 (one step apart in about 82 in 100 traces), while in the later window one of three mutually exclusive alternatives — A19 (about 38 in 100 traces), A25 (about 35 in 100) or A20 (about 30 in 100) — typically executes between them, with A41 preceding it and it preceding A26. This is shown directly by the successor change at A41, the new activities' outgoing edges to A26, the ordering relations, and the A41→A26 distance moving from one to mostly two steps; the pattern is consistent across all 21 qualifying items (about 0.5 would be expected by chance, and every item's difference exceeds about 995 in 1000 random re-splits), so sampling noise cannot explain the pattern as a whole, and a mere shift between existing paths does not apply because the three activities were not observed in the earlier window. Limitations: the evidence shows event order and participation, not clock time, and a direct A41→A26 routing may persist in a small share of later traces (about 1–2 in 100); the earlier absence of A19/A20/A25 strictly means only that they were not observed before. A competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that these activities already existed in the process before the first window but were too rare to appear in the sample.
