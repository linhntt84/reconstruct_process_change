### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

A hypothesis supported by the evidence is that the one-of-three alternative block A14/A15/A20 changed its placement: in the earlier window it was inserted into the late segment, either directly after A22 (before A23) or directly after A23 (before A27), whereas in the later window it runs in the early segment between A40 and A21, so that A22 → A23 → A27 now execute back-to-back and A40 dispatches to the block before A21. This is shown directly by the successor changes at A40, the block activities, A22 and A23 [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006], by the complete reversal of the order between the block and A21/A22 [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006], by the block's position shift from about 0.60 to about 0.32 of the trace [POS001, POS002, POS003], and by the surrounding gap changes [LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004]. The block's participation is stable at about one execution per case in both windows, which fits a relocation rather than an activity being added or removed, and 24 qualifying items against roughly 0.39 expected for an unchanged process cannot be explained by sampling noise as a whole. Limitations: the evidence is control-flow only and uses event positions rather than clock time, so nothing can be said about timing, and the block's internal choice split (A14 vs A15 vs A20) rests on about 61–76 outgoing transitions per window.

Other differences: A10 and A11, present in every trace in both windows, now repeat less often (mean about 1.9 → about 1.6 occurrences per trace; recurrence in about 47 → about 38 of 100 traces); no routing or ordering items cover them, so the evidence cannot show whether this reduction belongs to the same restructuring or is an independent second change.
