### Observed differences

- The relative execution order of A30 and A31 reversed. In the earlier window, the segment following A26 typically ran A26 → A31 → A30 → A27: among the roughly 103 traces (of 200) containing these pairs, A31 followed A26 at the very next event in about 60 in 100 cases while A30 followed two positions later in about 60 in 100, and A31's observed outgoing transitions led to A30, and A30's to A27, in about 60 in 100 of cases. In the later window the chain typically ran A26 → A30 → A31 → A27, with each of these steps holding in about 62 in 100 of about 87 traces. Both orders occur in both windows (roughly 60/40 earlier, 38/62 later), so the majority order flipped rather than one order replacing the other. [DIR002, DIR003, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004]

- A26's outgoing transitions were redistributed. Transitions from A26 to A31 fell from about 31 to about 17 in 100, while those to A30 rose from about 21 to 27 in 100 — a reversal of their relative ranking that matches the reorder above. Transitions to A32 rose from about 22 to 30 in 100, and those to A33 stayed at about 26 in 100. In line with this, the A26-to-A30/A31 segment was reached in about 87 of 200 traces in the later window versus about 103 of 200 earlier (about 44 versus 52 in 100). [DIR001, LAG001, LAG002]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the preferred execution order of A30 and A31 reversed in the later window: earlier, after A26, A31 was typically completed before A30, which then typically led directly to A27 (about 60 in 100 versus 40 in 100 for the opposite order); later, A30 typically came first and A31 led to A27 (about 62 in 100 versus 38 in 100). Both activities occurred in a substantial share of traces in both windows and both orders remain present, so this is a shift in the dominant ordering between them, not an exchange of fixed positions or the replacement of one path by another. The hypothesis rests on direct relational evidence: the distance distributions of the pairs (A26, A30), (A26, A31), (A30, A27) and (A31, A27) each flipped between the next event and two positions later, the dominant successors of A30 and A31 each switched, and A26's direct transitions to A31 and A30 reversed their ranking, with identical support (about 103 earlier and 87 later traces) across all six items. [DIR002, DIR003, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004]

Chance is an unlikely explanation for the pattern as a whole: seven control-flow items qualified where about 0.33 would be expected if nothing had changed — roughly twenty times that expectation — and every item's difference exceeded about 995 of 1,000 random re-splits of the pooled traces. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004]

Limitations: the six relational items almost certainly describe one underlying trace behaviour, so they are not independent confirmations, and the evidence cannot distinguish a reordering within individual traces from a shift in the mix of two variants that each fix the order of A30 and A31 — the two look identical at this level. Positions are event positions, not clock times, so no statement about calendar timing is implied. [DIR002, DIR003, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004]

Other differences: the share of A26's outgoing transitions going to A32 rose from about 22 to 30 in 100 while the A30/A31 block was reached in about 87 versus 103 of 200 traces; this volume shift away from the A30/A31 sequence is consistent with the reorder, but its frequency component is not directly tested by the qualifying indicators and could reflect sampling variation between the two windows. [DIR001, LAG001, LAG002]
