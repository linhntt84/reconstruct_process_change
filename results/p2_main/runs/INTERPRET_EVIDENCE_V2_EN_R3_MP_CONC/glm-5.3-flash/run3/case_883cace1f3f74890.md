### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that, in the later window, the process executes an additional mid-sequence step that did not run in the earlier window: in nearly every trace, exactly one of the mutually exclusive activities A19, A20 or A25 runs once — A20 in about 41 in 100 traces, A19 and A25 in about 30 in 100 each — executed between A27 and A29, immediately before A28 in about 55 in 100 traces and immediately after A28 in about 44 in 100. The signs are direct and mutually consistent: the three activities go from no participation to about 30–41 in 100 traces; A27 and A28, which previously flowed almost exclusively to A28 and A29, now redirect roughly half of their outgoing transitions to them; the new activities lead back mainly to A28 or A29; the A27→A28, A28→A29 and A27→A29 step distances each grow by exactly one; and the relational pattern (always after A27, always before A29, split around A28) matches those distance shifts. Screening also qualified 29 control-flow items where about 0.54 would be expected by chance in an unchanged process, so the pattern as a whole is far beyond what random sampling produces. [OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL007, REL008, REL009, REL010, REL011, REL012]

The main limitation is that zero participation in the earlier window means only that A19, A20 and A25 were not observed then; the evidence cannot distinguish a genuinely added step from a pre-existing path that simply was not executed before, nor can it show what governs the choice among the three activities or between the two positions relative to A28. [OCC002, OCC003, OCC004]

Other differences: A10's trace participation rises from about 5 in 100 traces in the earlier window to about 13.5 in 100 in the later window; the evidence does not tie this to the behaviour above. [OCC001]
