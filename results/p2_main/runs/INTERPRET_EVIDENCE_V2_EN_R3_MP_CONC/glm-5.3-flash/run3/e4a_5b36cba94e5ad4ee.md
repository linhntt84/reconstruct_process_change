### Observed differences

- **Ordering between two groups of activities flipped from mixed to fixed.** In the earlier window, the block A21–A25 frequently ran before the activities A15, A16, A17, A19 and A20 — for example, A21 preceded A20 in about 97 in 100 traces, A24 preceded A20 in about 86 in 100, and A21 preceded A15 in about 77 in 100. In the later window, every one of A15, A16, A17, A19 and A20 precedes every one of A21–A25 in 100% of traces, while both groups still co-occur in every trace in both windows — a change in ordering constraints, not in participation. [REL001, REL009, REL018, REL021, REL001–REL022]

- **Branching consolidated into a single chain.** In the earlier window, A15's observed outgoing transitions were spread over eight successors (largest share about 21% to A20); in the later window only three remain — about 36% to A16, 32% to A17 and 32% to A20 — and transitions from A15 to A21–A25 are no longer observed. A20 now always leads to A21 (earlier about 51% of its outgoing transitions went to A31 and only about 3% to A21), A22 now always leads to A31 (earlier about 50%), A30 now always leads to A19 (earlier split roughly 54/46 with A21), and A21 now leads only to A24, A23 and A25. [DIR001, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006, DIR010]

- **Steps became tightly adjacent.** Pairs within the early group, previously spread over lags of 1–5 events, now sit only at lags 1–2 (A16→A17 at lag 1: about 34% earlier vs 66% later). Distances from A15–A17 to A23–A25 stretched to lags 3–5 (A15→A25 at lag 5: about 13% earlier vs 51% later, with lags 1–2 disappearing). A19→A21 and A20→A22 now occur only at lag 5, and A22→A31 and A30→A19 only at lag 1. [LAG009, LAG016, LAG007, LAG021, LAG026, LAG027, LAG035, LAG051]

- **Positions shifted accordingly.** A15–A20 now occur earlier in the trace (A20's mean position about 0.71 → 0.61 of the trace) and A21–A25 later (A21 about 0.53 → 0.63); each of these ten activities appears in all 200 traces in both windows, so participation itself did not change. [POS001, POS005, POS006]

- **Timing lengthened on two transitions.** The elapsed time between consecutive completions rose from a median of about 0.91 to about 1.81 hours on A31→A38 (61 vs 41 intervals) and from about 0.15 to about 0.34 hours on A37→A38 (51 vs 64). [TWAIT001, TWAIT002]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the ordering constraints between two groups of activities tightened into a fixed sequence: in the later window, A15, A16, A17, A19 and A20 always execute before A21, A22, A23, A24 and A25, the flow through them became a tight chain (early-group pairs at lags 1–2, A22→A31 and A30→A19 at lag 1), and several alternative transitions are no longer observed (A20 only to A21, A22 only to A31, A30 only to A19), whereas in the earlier window the late block often ran first and transitions were dispersed across many alternatives. The relational evidence shows the ordering change directly — mixed orders became 100% fixed while co-occurrence stayed at 100% — and the lag, direction and position items all shift consistently in the same direction; the control-flow family produced 93 qualifying items where about 0.7 would be expected if nothing had changed, so chance cannot account for the set as a whole. [REL001, REL018, DIR001, DIR004, LAG007, POS005, screening.by_family]

The evidence cannot tell whether the earlier mixed order reflects one flexible (unordered or parallel) block or a mixture of two strict variants that coexisted; nor can it show whether the transitions no longer observed were removed from the process or merely not taken, since direction profiles cover observed outgoing transitions only. [DIR001, REL001]

Other differences: The elapsed time between consecutive completions roughly doubled on A31→A38 and A37→A38; this is a timing change in its own right that this evidence does not link to the structural hypothesis above. [TWAIT001, TWAIT002]
