- The clearest difference is a reversal in how often two mutually exclusive groups of activities run. In the earlier window, each of A16, A17 and A18 appeared in about 75 in 100 traces, while each of A19, A20 and A21 appeared in about 25 in 100; in the later window these shares reversed, with A16–A18 at about 25 in 100 and A19–A21 at about 75 in 100. ### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

A specific possibility supported by the evidence is that the choice between two alternative branches after A15 flipped its frequencies. Earlier, roughly 3 in 4 cases ran the branch consisting of A16, A17 and A18 — the three executed together after A15 in mixed order, alongside A25 — and roughly 1 in 4 ran the branch with A19, A20 and A21; in the later window the shares reversed to about 1 in 4 and 3 in 4. The flip is shown directly and consistently: participation of all six activities moves by about 50 in 100 traces [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006], A15's outgoing transitions redistribute from roughly 75/25 to roughly 25/75 between the groups [DIR001], and the relational profiles switch in step — the groups stay mutually exclusive, the executing group co-occurs with A15 and A25, and within-group co-execution with mixed order appears in both windows [REL001, REL004, REL007, REL009, REL012, REL022, REL024].

The screening comparison supports treating this as more than sampling noise: the control-flow family produced 35 qualifying items against roughly 0.53 expected without change, and the time family 1 item against roughly 0.005 — both many times the chance expectation. All activities involved are well observed in both windows (about 25 to 75 in 100 traces), no activity is skipped entirely, bypassed or newly repeated, and the same six outgoing targets of A15 appear throughout, which fits a shift in how often existing alternative paths are taken rather than activities being added, removed or replaced. The many relational items largely re-express the same underlying flip, so they indicate consistency rather than independent confirmations. [OCC001, DIR001, REL009, REL022]

An important competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is a difference in case mix between the two windows — for example, case types that were already routed differently upstream arriving in different proportions — which would produce the same flip without any change at this routing point; the finite samples of 200 traces per window also leave room for sampling error, though the size and uniformity of the shift make chance alone implausible. [OCC004, REL022]

Other differences:
- The elapsed time between consecutive completions of A10 and A11 roughly doubled, from about 1.0 hour to about 2.0 hours per interval with similar interval counts (344 versus 365). This is a timing change in its own right, is not part of the branch hypothesis, and has no structural counterpart in the evidence. [TWAIT001]
- Within the now more frequent branch, A19 follows A15 at a slightly longer event distance than before (one step in about 33 in 100 such traces versus about 54 in 100 earlier); this is a positional detail measured in event steps, not clock time. [LAG001]
