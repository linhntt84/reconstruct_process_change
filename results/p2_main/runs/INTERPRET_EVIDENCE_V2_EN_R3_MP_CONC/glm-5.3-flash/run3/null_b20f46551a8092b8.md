### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the routing between **A35, A36 and A32** changed: in the earlier window A35's majority successor was A32 (about 59 in 100) and A36's was A35 (about 58 in 100), while in the later window A35's majority successor is A36 (about 62 in 100) and A36's is A32 (about 59 in 100). The direct A35→A32 step, previously the majority route, is now typically reached via A36, i.e. the majority path through this pair reversed direction. [DIR001, DIR002]

Supporting signs: two successor profiles (out of A35 and out of A36) show the same reversal from complementary sides; both differences exceed about 995 of 1,000 random re-splits; both rest on substantial support (roughly 87–103 outgoing transitions per window); and the screening flagged 3 control-flow items where only about 0.44 would be expected without a change, several times the chance level. All activities involved hold substantial shares on both sides of the shift in both windows, so this is a redistribution between existing alternatives, not a path appearing or disappearing. [DIR001, DIR002]

Limitations: the shares are over outgoing transitions, not cases, and this evidence alone cannot distinguish a true swap in the order of A35 and A36 from a change in the exit probabilities of a repeating A35–A36 loop; no positional or relational evidence is available to settle that interpretation. The minor new successors of A36 in the later window (each about 1–2 in 100) may simply be rare variants. [DIR001, DIR002]

Other differences: A39, which earlier always led to A40, shows three other successors in the later window (A43, A14, A19, each about once among 34 outgoing transitions); the change is negligible in size and based on few observations, so it is most plausibly sampling noise rather than a behavioural change.
