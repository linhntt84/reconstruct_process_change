### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

A change in timing is supported: in the later window, the elapsed time between consecutive completion events leading into A28 is consistently about half of what it was in the earlier window, both from A21 (about 0.92 to 0.45 hours) and from A29 (about 0.18 to 0.09 hours). Supporting signs: both time items move in the same direction with a similar relative size, each is backed by roughly 100 traces per window, both differences exceed all but about 1 in 2,000 random re-splits, and the time family produced 2 qualifying items where about 0.009 would be expected without a change — a gap chance cannot plausibly explain. The absence of control-flow items (0 against about 0.27 expected) is consistent with a pure timing change, without activities being added, removed or reordered. Limitations: both measurements end at A28, so they are not independent and the change cannot be localised to A28 itself versus A21 or A29; and the indicator measures elapsed time between consecutive completions, so it cannot separate waiting from processing. An important competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that the reduction originates at A28's completion (it now follows its predecessors sooner) rather than at A21 or A29 — both fit the data equally well.
