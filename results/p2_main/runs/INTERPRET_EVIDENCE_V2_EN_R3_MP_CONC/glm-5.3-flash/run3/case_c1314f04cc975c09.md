### Observed differences

- The main difference is repetition. In the earlier window, A25, A26 and A31 each occurred exactly once per trace (mean 1.0 occurrence, never repeated); in the later window each occurs about 3.5 times per trace, and in about 68 in 100 traces these activities recur. All three still appear in every trace in both windows, so this is not a presence change. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003]
- Routing out of A31 changed: earlier, every observed outgoing transition of A31 went to A20; in the later window about 71 in 100 go to A25 and about 29 in 100 still go to A20 (697 outgoing transitions observed). [DIR001]
- The relationship between A25 and A31 changed from a fixed sequence to a mixed one: earlier A25 always preceded A31; in the later window that fixed order holds in only about 32 in 100 traces, while about 68 in 100 show the two interleaved or repeated. They still occur together in every trace in both windows. [REL001]
- The consistent two-step gap between A25 and A31 largely dissolved: earlier the pair sat two positions apart in essentially all cases; in the later window only about 12 in 100 pairs are two steps apart, with a small share about five steps apart. [LAG001]
- A25 and A31 occur at similar average positions in both windows, but their positions are roughly twice as spread out in the later window (standard deviation about 0.06 before vs about 0.12 after). [POS001, POS002]

These items plausibly describe one underlying behaviour (repetition) rather than several independent differences. [OCC001, DIR001, REL001]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that a repetition loop was introduced around A25, A26 and A31. In the earlier window the three activities each ran exactly once in a fixed order, with A25 preceding A31 and A31 always followed by A20; in the later window they recur about 3.5 times per trace, A31 flows back to A25 in about 71 in 100 of its outgoing transitions, and the A25–A31 order is interleaved or repeated in about 68 in 100 traces, while the exit from A31 to A20 persists in about 29 in 100 of its outgoing transitions. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, DIR001, REL001, LAG001]

Supporting signs: eight control-flow items qualified against an expectation of 0.3 without change (roughly 25 times the chance level), the key fractions agree closely (recurrence about 68 in 100, interleaving about 68 in 100, back-edge about 71 in 100), and full participation in both windows rules out a rare variant or a presence change as an explanation. The items likely reflect the same underlying trace behaviour, so they corroborate one another rather than independently confirm separate changes. [DIR001, REL001, OCC001]

Limitations: A25's outgoing transitions and A26's relations were not observed, so whether A26 sits inside the same cycle and how the repetition terminates are inferred from occurrence counts alone; the positional changes are small, position-based and do not show that any activity was moved. [OCC002, POS001, POS002]

Other differences: none beyond the increased positional spread already noted, which is consistent with the same repetition behaviour. [POS001, POS002]
