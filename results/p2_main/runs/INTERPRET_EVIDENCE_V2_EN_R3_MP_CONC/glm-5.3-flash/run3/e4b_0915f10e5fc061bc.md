### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a change in resource allocation in the later window: on A01, A03, A13, A14, A17 and A22, assignments shifted away from a group of resources that previously handled about half of each activity's work towards a largely different group of resources and a broader pooled remainder, while the activities themselves continued to be performed at similar rates (for example, A01 at 200 observations in both windows, A22 from 127 to 118). [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, ASSIGN007]

Supporting signs: the resource family produced 7 qualifying items where about 0.005 would be expected without a change — over a thousand times the chance expectation, so the family-wide difference cannot be explained by sampling; the six activities all move in the same direction (the same resources losing share, a largely different set gaining); every item's difference exceeded about 1,999 of 2,000 random re-splits; and no control-flow or timing items qualified, so the difference is confined to allocation rather than structure or timing. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, ASSIGN007]

Limitations: the resource identifiers are anonymous codes, so the evidence cannot distinguish an active rerouting of work from a change in which resources were available in each window — with these data the two look alike, and no roles or teams can be inferred. A13 and A14 duplicate each other, leaving five independent activity-level profiles in the shared pattern, and the windows are finite samples of 200 traces each. [ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005]

Other differences: A07 shows its own reallocation between two almost disjoint sets of resources, and its observation count fell from about 163 to about 141, which could indicate reduced participation in that activity; the allocation data alone cannot separate this from a pure change in shares. [ASSIGN003]
