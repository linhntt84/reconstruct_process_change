- Activity A17 became concentrated on a single resource. In the earlier window its assignments were spread across about 45 distinct resources, with no single resource holding more than about 14 in 100 of the assignments; in the later window one resource, User_84, handled all of them (top share 100 in 100, concentration index 1.0 versus about 0.05 before). Earlier, User_104 held about 16 in 100 with several other resources at roughly 3–6 in 100; later, User_104 fell to about 8 in 100, several former contributors dropped out of the main shares, and new resources took over, led by User_61 at about 8 in 100 and a group of newcomers at about 4–5 in 100 each. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, CONC001]
- The qualifying items come only from the resource family (3 items against about 0.006 expected by chance); no control-flow or timing items qualified. [screening]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a change in resource allocation. In the later window, A17 is handled exclusively by User_84, whereas earlier it was shared across a broad pool of about 45 resources with no dominant one; at the same time, A01's work moved away from its former main resource User_104 and other established contributors toward a new set of resources that includes User_84 and User_61. Supporting signs: the resource family produced 3 qualifying items where about 0.006 would be expected if nothing had changed, far beyond what chance can explain; the A17 shift is measured directly and consistently by the assignment profile and the concentration index, which describe the same underlying change rather than independent confirmations; and both profiles rest on large support in both windows. Limitations: the resource codes are anonymous, so no roles, people or teams can be inferred, and the evidence cannot show whether A17's concentration and A01's redistribution stem from a single reorganisation or are separate shifts. An important competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is a change in how assignments are recorded — for example a default assignee being logged — which could produce a single-resource profile without work actually being reallocated.

Other differences: none observed; with no control-flow or timing items qualifying, the detected differences are confined to resource allocation. [screening]
