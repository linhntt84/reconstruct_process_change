### Observed differences

- The elapsed time between the consecutive completions of A28 and A32 roughly halved. In the earlier window it averaged about 1.0 hour per case (median about 1.0 hour; middle 50% about 0.90–1.14 hours); in the later window it averaged about 0.5 hour (median about 0.49 hour; middle 50% about 0.44–0.54 hours) — a decrease of about half an hour. [TWAIT001]
- The interval also became more regular in the later window: its spread roughly halved (standard deviation about 0.18 hours down to about 0.09 hours), and every distribution component measured (mean, median, both quartiles, spread) fell together rather than only part of the distribution shifting. [TWAIT001]
- This was not a rare path: the A28–A32 completion interval was observed about 200 times in each window — essentially in every one of the 200 traces per window — and the difference is larger than in all but about 1 of 2,000 random re-splits of the pooled data. [TWAIT001]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a change in timing as the main hypothesis: in the later window, the elapsed time from the completion of A28 to the completion of A32 was about half of what it was in the earlier window (about 0.5 hour versus about 1.0 hour per case), and the interval became steadier. The hypothesis is direct — the indicator measures exactly this elapsed interval — and consistent: all distribution components decreased together, the interval occurred in essentially every trace in both windows (200 of 200), so it cannot be a rare-variant artefact, and the difference exceeds all but about 1 of 2,000 random re-splits. The screening comparison points the same way: the time family produced 1 qualifying item where about 0.006 would be expected without a change — far above what chance typically produces — while the 54 control-flow units produced no items (about 0.27 expected), so the timing shift appears without any detectable change in participation, routing or ordering, which a timing hypothesis does not require. [TWAIT001]

Limitations: the evidence rests on a single indicator for a single activity pair, so it shows that the interval shortened but cannot show whether the shortening lies in the activities themselves or in the handover between them; and an unchanged process would still occasionally produce one qualifying item (roughly 1 in 170 cases at these thresholds), so chance cannot be excluded with certainty, even though it is unlikely here.
