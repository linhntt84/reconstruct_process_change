### Observed differences

- The five activities A30, A31, A36, A37 and A38 appear in every trace in both windows, but their repetition collapsed: in the earlier window each occurred about 3.5–3.6 times per trace and repeated within about 70 in 100 traces, while in the later window each occurs about once per trace (means about 1.0), repeats in only about 1–2 in 100 traces, and sits at a much more stable event position (position spread roughly halved). [OCC006, OCC007, OCC008, OCC009, OCC010, POS006, POS007, POS008, POS009, POS010, LAG015]

- Ordering around this group became fixed. Earlier, A14 preceded A30 in only about 30 in 100 traces, with about 70 in 100 interleaved or repeated; in the later window A14 precedes each of A30, A31, A36, A37 and A38 in about 97–99 in 100 traces. Within the group, A30 now precedes A31/A36/A37/A38 in about 98–99 in 100 traces and A31/A36/A37 precede A38 in about 98 in 100, while A31, A36 and A37 remain mutually unordered (each order in about 50 in 100 traces). Reverse-order pairs that were common earlier (for example A36 followed by A30, or A38 followed by A37, each seen in about 140 traces) now appear in only 2–3 traces. A41, also present in every trace, now precedes the group in about 97–99 in 100 traces, previously about 30. [REL013, REL033, REL040, REL042, REL047, REL051, REL037, LAG013, LAG022]

- A10 changed from rare to regular: it appeared in about 21 in 100 traces in the earlier window and about 72 in 100 in the later window, at a far more stable event position (median about 0.29 → 0.32 of the trace, spread much narrower). [OCC005, POS001]

- A10's placement is new: it now co-occurs with A14, A15 and each group activity in about 72 in 100 traces (previously about 21); A41 precedes it in about 64 in 100 traces (previously about 11), and from A41 about 70 in 100 outgoing transitions now go to A10 (previously about 3), essentially always within one step; A10 precedes A30/A31/A36/A37/A38 in about 62–66 in 100 traces (previously about 3); and A10's direct flow now goes almost entirely to A19 and A20 (about 44 and 45 in 100 of its outgoing transitions, versus largest earlier exits of about 18 each to A47 and A16). [REL001, REL005, REL010, DIR007, DIR004, LAG023, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003]

- A02, A04, A06 and A08 became rare: each appeared in about 23–31 in 100 traces earlier and in only about 5–11 in 100 in the later window; the later shares rest on only 9–21 traces each. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004]

- Smaller shifts: A19 and A20 (each in roughly half of traces in both windows) now precede A30 in about 50 in 100 of their co-occurrences, previously about 10–19 in 100 with mostly interleaved execution, and sit slightly later in the trace; A46 → A47 within one step rose from about 84 to about 99 in 100 observed pairs; A14 → A15 as a direct successor rose from about 76 to about 97 in 100 transitions, and A44 → A45 similarly tightened. ### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the mid-process segment containing A30, A31, A36, A37 and A38 was restructured: in the later window these five activities run once per case in a fixed order (A14 and A41 before the segment, A30 first, A38 last, with A31, A36 and A37 in no fixed mutual order), whereas earlier they repeated about 3.5 times per case and were heavily interleaved with A14, A15 and one another; in the same segment, A10 — previously present in about 21 in 100 cases — now appears in about 72 in 100 and runs as a regular step between A41 and A19/A20 on one side and the segment on the other. [OCC005, OCC006, OCC007, OCC008, OCC009, OCC010, REL005, REL010, REL013, REL033, REL040, DIR004, DIR007, LAG015, LAG023]

The reading rests on direct signs: the repetition drop is measured on all 200 traces per window; the order changes are relational and consistent across about 40 activity-pair comparisons in the segment; lag distributions concentrate accordingly (A41 → A10 within one step in essentially all later observations, A10 → A30 within two steps in about 96 in 100); and screening flagged 116 control-flow items where about 1 would be expected without change, each difference larger than in about 199 of 200 random re-splits, so the ensemble is far beyond what an unchanged process typically produces. [OCC006, OCC010, REL013, REL057, LAG003, LAG023]

Other differences: the participation decline of A02, A04, A06 and A08 described above; the reordering of A19 and A20 relative to the segment; and the tightened successions A46 → A47, A44 → A45 and A14 → A15. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, REL023, REL028, LAG028, DIR008, DIR005]

A competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that the two windows sample different mixes of pre-existing behaviour — for instance, cases in which the segment repeats losing share to cases in which it runs once — rather than a modification within one process; the later-window shares for A02, A04, A06 and A08 also rest on few traces. All position and lag evidence uses event positions, not clock time, so no conclusion about timing is implied. [OCC006, OCC007, OCC008, OCC009, OCC010, OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004]
