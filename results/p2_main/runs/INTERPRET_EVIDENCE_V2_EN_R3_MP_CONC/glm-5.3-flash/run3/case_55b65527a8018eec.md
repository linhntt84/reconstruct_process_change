### Observed differences

- Earlier, each of the ten activities A33, A34, A35, A37, A38, A39, A40, A41, A42 and A43 ran in every single case (participation 100 in 100, about one occurrence per trace, essentially never repeated). In the later window, each of them runs in only about 46–55 in 100 cases, still about once per case where it appears. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, OCC007, OCC008, OCC009, OCC010]
- A30 and A31 still occur in every case in both windows. Earlier, A30 preceded each of the ten activities in about 98–100 in 100 cases, and each of them preceded A31 similarly; in the later window A30 co-occurs with each of them in only about 46–55 in 100 cases and appears without them in the remaining cases (symmetrically for A31). [REL001, REL006, REL007, REL010, REL015, REL016]
- In the later window the ten activities behave as two mutually exclusive groups: {A33, A34, A35, A37, A38} and {A39, A40, A41, A43} (A42 behaves like the second group by participation). Within each group, members still co-occur whenever their group runs (about 46 in 100 cases for the first group, about 55 for the second) and keep their earlier mixed, unfixed order. Across groups, co-occurrence collapsed from 100 in 100 cases earlier (first group always first, e.g., A33 before A39 in about 99 in 100) to at most about 2 in 100: in the later window roughly 46 in 100 cases show only A33 and roughly 54 in 100 only A39. [REL019, REL023, REL024, REL030, REL036, REL041, REL045, REL049]
- Routing at the block's edges changed. Earlier, A30's outgoing transitions went almost exclusively to the first group (A35 about 24, A34 about 20, A33/A37/A38 about 17–18 in 100 each); in the later window they go to both groups (e.g., A35 about 13, A41 about 12, A40 about 11, A39 about 10 in 100). First-group activities, which earlier often led into A39–A43, now lead directly to A31 (about 26 in 100 of A33's outgoing transitions, 25 for A34, 23 for A38, 14 for A37), and their transitions into A39–A43 are no longer observed. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005]
- Proximity confirms this: a first-group activity followed by A31 within five steps rises from about 1 of 200 cases earlier to about 90 of 200 later, while first-group-then-second-group pairs within five steps fall from about 113–129 of 200 cases to about 1. [LAG004, LAG006, LAG011, LAG001, LAG002, LAG005, LAG007, LAG008, LAG012, LAG013]
- Event positions moved as expected if the block between A30 and A31 shrank from about ten to about five activities per case: A30's mean relative position moved later (about 0.55 to 0.64 of the trace), A31 slightly earlier (about 0.84 to 0.82), first-group activities later (about 0.62–0.63 to 0.69–0.73) and second-group activities slightly earlier (about 0.76 to 0.71–0.73). [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006, POS007, POS008, POS009, POS010]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the fixed sequence between A30 and A31 became a choice between two alternative activity groups. Earlier, every case ran A33/A34/A35/A37/A38 (in varying order) and then A39/A40/A41/A42/A43 (in varying order) between A30 and A31; in the later window, almost every case runs exactly one of the two groups — about 46 in 100 cases the first group, about 54 in 100 the second — and the group that runs is followed directly by A31, so each group's activities have effectively become skippable as a block. [OCC001, OCC006, REL023, REL030, REL041, REL049, DIR002, DIR005, LAG004]

The pattern is direct and consistent across indicator families: participation of all ten activities fell from every case to roughly half with no repetition change [OCC001, OCC006, OCC010]; cross-group pairs flipped from always co-occurring in a fixed order to being near-perfectly mutually exclusive, while within-group pairs kept co-occurring with their mixed order [REL019, REL023, REL030, REL049]; new direct transitions from the first group to A31 and from A30 into the second group appeared while first-group-to-second-group transitions disappeared [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005]; and the lag and position items match the same restructuring [LAG004, LAG006, LAG011, POS001, POS003, POS008]. Chance is an unlikely overall explanation: 93 control-flow items qualified where about 1 would be expected without a change, and every item's difference exceeds those seen in about 199 of 200 random re-splits of the pooled data.

Limitations: the items describe one underlying restructuring rather than independent confirmations, and A42's placement in the second group rests only on its participation and A30's outgoing profile, as no pair-level item covers it. An important competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that the later window mixes two case populations (for example, case types routed apart before A30) rather than a single new routing decision inside the process — the split itself is observed, its mechanism is not. [OCC009, DIR001]

Other differences: a handful of rare new transitions (about 1 in 200 of the source's outgoing transitions) from A30 and first-group activities to activities outside the block — A04, A07, A18, A26 and A51 from A30, A47/A10/A28 from A33, A06/A19/A36 from A37, A08 from A38 — appear only in the later window. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR004, DIR005]
