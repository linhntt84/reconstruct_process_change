### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the use frequency of two existing alternative branches after A20 flipped: in the earlier window the branch executing A21, A22 and A23 together (in no fixed order) was taken in about 75 in 100 traces and the branch executing A24, A25 and A26 together in about 25 in 100; in the later window the shares are about 25 and about 76 in 100, and A30 now runs alongside the A24–A26 branch (about 76 in 100 co-occurrence) where it previously ran alongside A21–A23 (about 75 in 100). The shift is shown directly in A20's successor distribution and consistently across participation, co-occurrence and relation items with matching magnitudes; both branches remain well observed in both windows (at least about 25 in 100 traces), so this reads as existing paths being taken with different frequencies rather than a path appearing or disappearing, and 62 qualified items against roughly 1 expected without change far exceeds what an unchanged process typically produces. The evidence shows only that the shares between the branches moved; it cannot establish why, and it cannot rule out that the two windows sampled a different mix of cases that uses the branches differently rather than a modified routing.
