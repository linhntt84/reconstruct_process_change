### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that, in the later window, the activities A31, A22, A20, A23, A24, A26, A25 and A32 form a fixed execution sequence (A31→A22→A20→A23→A24→A26→A25→A32, each member immediately followed by the next), whereas in the earlier window the same activities ran in variable order — for example, A24 preceded A23 in about 87 in 100 traces before, and A20 and A26 appeared in both orders about equally. The order change is shown directly by relational evidence with full support in both windows (200 of 200 traces per pair) [REL014, REL016, REL017, REL019, REL021], successor choices became near-deterministic [DIR007, DIR009, DIR010, DIR011, DIR012, DIR013], inter-activity distances collapsed to single fixed gaps [LAG006, LAG012, LAG016, LAG034, LAG036], and positions moved in the matching directions [POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006]. All block members keep full participation in both windows, so this is a change in ordering constraints rather than a replacement of one path by another, and with 82 qualifying items against about 1 expected by chance for the 201 tested units, sampling variation cannot account for the differences as a whole. Note that these indicator families describe one underlying reordering rather than independent confirmations.

Limitations: the picture is built from pairwise relations and distances of at most five steps, so the chain is reconstructed from relational signals rather than observed as a whole, and the roughly 1–6 in 100 traces that still show the earlier orders leave open the competing explanation that a small fraction of cases continues to follow the old variable order [REL014, REL019].

Other differences: A08 is executed in far fewer cases (about 72 in 100 traces before, 29 in 100 in the later window) and, when present, occurs later and no longer leads almost exclusively to A16; this is a participation change in its own right, with the occurrence, relation, position and direction items all mirroring the same underlying behaviour rather than independent changes [OCC001, REL001, REL004, POS001, LAG001, DIR003]. A10 appears more often (about 7.5→22 in 100 traces) with redistributed successors, but with only 15 earlier-window traces behind it, this could still be a sampling artefact [OCC002, DIR004].
