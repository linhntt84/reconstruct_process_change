### Observed differences

- The activities A36, A37 and A42 changed from optional to essentially mandatory: in the earlier window each ran in about 46 in 100 traces, in the later window in about 995 in 1000, once per trace. A27 rose similarly, from about 54 in 100 traces to all traces. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004]
- Earlier, A27 and the group A36/A37/A42 were alternatives: they co-occurred in at most about 1 in 200 traces. In the later window they co-occur in about 995 in 1000 traces, with the group executed before A27. [REL011, REL012, REL013]
- The group's position in the order flipped relative to A26 and A28: earlier A26 and A28 each preceded the group in the roughly 46 in 100 traces where both sides occurred; later the group precedes A26 and A28 in about 99 in 100 traces. [REL006, REL007, REL008, REL014, REL015, REL016]

- The group's exit changed: earlier A36, A37 and A42 each led mostly to A31 (about 29–36% of outgoing transitions) and essentially never to A28; later each leads to A28 in about 31–36% and no longer to A31. A26 now leads to A27 in about 99 in 100 transitions, versus about 54 in 100 earlier. [DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005]
- Event positions shifted accordingly: the group's mean position moved from about 0.59 to about 0.40 of the trace, while A26 (0.54 → 0.60), A27 (0.59 → 0.63) and A28 (0.39 → 0.46) moved later. [POS002, POS003, POS004, POS006, POS007, POS008]
- Step distances confirm the insertion: A28 followed A25 within one step in about 99 in 100 traces earlier, but about four steps later in about 97 in 100 traces in the later window; A31 followed A26 within two steps in about 96 in 100 later traces, versus an earlier split between two and four steps. - The internal order within the group stays unordered in both windows: A36/A37 (and the other pairs) occur in both orders in similar shares, so their mutual ordering did not change — only their co-occurrence did. [REL020, REL021, REL022]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the segment A36–A37–A42 was made mandatory and placed much earlier in the case: in the earlier window it formed an optional branch, taken in about 46 in 100 cases after A26 as an alternative to A27 and ending at A31; in the later window all three activities run in virtually every case, directly after A25 and before A28, A26 and A27, in varying internal order, after which A28, A26, A27 and A31 follow in sequence. A27 changed from the alternative that the branch replaced into a mandatory step. The order flips against A25, A26 and A28 are shown directly by relational evidence, the successor changes at A25 and the group members by directional evidence, the relocation by position evidence, and the A25→A28 distance growing from one to about four steps matches the group being inserted between them [REL002, REL006, REL011, REL014, DIR001, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, POS006, POS007, POS008, LAG001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004]. With 45 qualifying items against roughly 0.7 expected without a change, chance sampling cannot account for the ensemble as a whole [OCC001, DIR001, REL011, LAG001].

Limitations: many of the qualifying items describe one coherent restructuring rather than independent confirmations, and some step-distance estimates rest on about one observation in one window (A25–A36 and A36–A28 earlier; A37–A31 and A42–A31 later), so the exact local wiring around A31 is uncertain [LAG002, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006]. A competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is a shift in the case mix between the two windows (different proportions of case types) rather than a modification of one process; the coordinated flips across many indicators make pure sampling noise implausible, but the finite samples cannot exclude it.

Other differences: A25's mean position shifts slightly earlier (about 0.36 to 0.35) and A31's slightly later (about 0.63 to 0.66); a handful of outgoing transitions appear at about 0.5 in 100 in the later window (for example from A25 to A02/A08 and from A37 to A10/A21/A39/A46), too rare to characterise further.
