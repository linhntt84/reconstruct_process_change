### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the six-activity block between A25 and A26 — A28, A30, A31, A32, A33, A34 — became optional and split into two mutually exclusive sub-blocks: in the later window a case typically executes either A28 with A30 and A31 (about 45 in 100 cases) or A32 with A33 and A34 (about 54 in 100), and only rarely both (about 2 in 100), whereas previously every case executed all six in a fixed group order. This is shown directly by the collapse of co-occurrence between the two trios from 100% to about 2% with one-trio-only cases appearing, by the drop in participation from 100% to roughly half, and consistently by the new bridging transitions (A25 to the second trio, first trio to A26) and the shortened event distances. With 58 qualifying items against roughly 0.6 expected if nothing had changed, chance alone cannot explain the pattern as a whole, and the occurrence, direction, relation and lag indicators all point to the same structure. A competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that the later window mixes two case types that each always used one trio, rather than a single process with a new exclusive choice; also, only control-flow indicators were screened, so timing or resource aspects of this difference cannot be assessed.
