### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a change in timing as a possibility: in the later window, the elapsed time between the completion of each of the activities A32, A33, A34 or A35 and the completion of A29 is roughly halved, from about one hour to about half an hour, with a visibly narrower spread. The signs are direct and consistent — all four pair-level timing items move in the same direction with similar magnitude (about 0.46–0.56 hours lower), each exceeds virtually all reference comparisons, and the number of qualifying time items (4) is far above that family's chance expectation (about 0.009), while control-flow screening found nothing, consistent with a timing change that needs no structural counterpart. [TWAIT001, TWAIT002, TWAIT003, TWAIT004]

Limitations: T_WAIT measures elapsed time between consecutive completion events, so the evidence cannot show whether A29 now completes sooner after its predecessors or the predecessors' completions shifted relative to A29, nor whether the gaps changed because a different set of completions counts as consecutive. An important competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is therefore a shift in which events fall between these completions rather than a change in the timing around A29 itself. Because all four items share A29, they may reflect one underlying timing difference rather than four independent confirmations. [TWAIT001, TWAIT002, TWAIT003, TWAIT004]

Other differences: the pair frequencies shifted modestly and in different directions (A34–A29 from about 20 to about 31 in 100 traces; A33–A29 from about 29 to about 22; A35–A29 from about 29 to about 26); these bidirectional shifts are within what finite samples can produce and no control-flow difference qualified.
