### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, five activities behaved as a one-of-five choice: A14, A17, A18, A19 and A20 each appeared in about 17%, 25%, 27%, 14% and 19% of cases (shares summing to 100%, so every case contained exactly one of them); no two of them ever occurred in the same case, and each was followed only by A28. [OCC001, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, OCC007, REL003, REL029, REL037, DIR001, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006, DIR007]
- In the later window, none of these five is observed (0 of 200 cases), while five other activities — A15, A16, A25, A26 and A27, all absent earlier — each occur exactly once in every one of the 200 cases. [OCC001, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, OCC007, OCC002, OCC003, OCC008, OCC009, OCC010]
- The five new activities run as a jointly executed group without a fixed internal order: every pair of them co-occurs in 100% of later cases, with each order taken in roughly 45–60% of cases (e.g., A15 before A16 in about 52%, A16 before A15 in about 48%); each of the five always follows A43 and always precedes A28. [REL010, REL015, REL051, REL055, REL018, REL019, REL027, REL028, REL053, REL054, REL056, REL057, REL058, REL059]
- A43's successor profile changed accordingly: earlier it was followed by one of the old five (about 14–27% each); later it is followed by one of the new five (about 17–25% each). [DIR011]
- Within cases, A28 now occurs later (mean event position about 0.39 to 0.47) and A43 earlier (about 0.32 to 0.29), consistent with a larger block of activity lying between them. [POS011, POS012]
- The elapsed time between consecutive completion events of A44 and A45 fell from a mean of about 1.0 hours to about 0.5 hours (median about 1.02 to 0.50 hours; 157 vs 148 intervals). [TWAIT001]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a structural change in the segment between A43 and A28: in the earlier window each case ran A43, then exactly one of the five alternatives A14, A17, A18, A19 or A20, then A28; in the later window that alternative segment is no longer observed, and instead every case runs A43, then all five of A15, A16, A25, A26 and A27 exactly once each in varying order, then A28. Since the old activities are absent later and the new ones absent earlier, this is a change in which activities occupy the segment and in whether it is optional or mandatory, not a shift in how often existing paths are taken. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, OCC007, OCC008, OCC009, OCC010, DIR011, REL010, REL018, REL019, REL029, POS011, POS012]

This goes well beyond chance: the control-flow family produced 92 qualifying items where roughly 0.7 would be expected if nothing had changed, and the occurrence, successor, relational and positional indicators all describe the same difference, with the key relations supported by all 200 traces in each window. [OCC002, DIR002, DIR011, REL010, REL018, REL019, POS011, POS012]

An important competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out: the five new codes could be the same steps as the old five recorded under different labels, since the traces show only that the old codes stopped appearing and the new ones appear in all cases; zero participation means only that an activity was not observed in that window. Also, the new block's internal order varies from case to case, so no single fixed sequence can be inferred. [OCC001, OCC002, REL010, REL015]

Other differences: the elapsed time between consecutive completions of A44 and A45 roughly halved (mean about 1.0 to 0.5 hours). The time family produced one item where about 0.01 would be expected without change, and a single item at that level is what an unchanged process typically produces, so this does not by itself establish a timing change. [TWAIT001]
