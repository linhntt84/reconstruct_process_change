### Observed differences

- Resource allocation changed on six activities (A01, A03, A13, A14, A17, A22). In the earlier window, one group of resources handled most of the named work — for example User_19 took about 6–8 in 100 assignments of each of these activities — while in the later window that group no longer appears among the main assignees, and a different group took over: User_37 and User_61, not among the main assignees earlier, now take roughly 6–13 in 100 assignments of each activity, and the catch-all share of remaining resources fell by about 3–9 in 100. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, ASSIGN007]
- A10 became much less concentrated. Earlier, about 90 in 100 A10 assignments went to a small group of eight resources (the largest taking about 19%, 15% and 14%); in the later window that group kept only about a third of the assignments with no single one above about 7%, while assignments spread over a broad tail of other resources rose from about 9 in 100 to about 57 in 100. [ASSIGN003]
- The spacing between consecutive A17 occurrences shifted: pairs five event steps apart fell from about 43 in 100 to about 20 in 100, while pairs one step apart rose from about 23 in 100 to about 39 in 100 (observed in 46 vs 32 traces). [LAG001]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that work was reallocated between groups of resources. For A01, A03, A13, A14, A17 and A22, assignments moved from one set of resources to another — User_19's share of roughly 6–8 in 100 assignments disappeared, while User_37 and User_61 now take about 6–13 in 100 each — and A10's work dispersed from a small concentrated group to a wide set of resources. Supporting signs: seven resource items qualified where about 0.005 would be expected by chance in an unchanged process, a gap far too large to explain as sampling; the same gaining and losing groups recur across the six activities; each activity is observed in a substantial share of traces in both windows (for example A01 in 200 of 200 traces in both, A22 in 126 and 114); and every item's difference exceeds about 999 in 1000 random re-splits of the pooled data. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, ASSIGN007]

Limitations: the resource codes are anonymous, so the evidence shows that allocation changed but cannot distinguish a change in the available resource pool from a change in assignment routing; A13 and A14 have identical profiles and may reflect one underlying behaviour rather than two independent changes. [ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005]

Other differences: the A17 self-spacing shift (one-step pairs up from about 23% to 39%, five-step pairs down from about 43% to 20%) is the single control-flow item, where about 0.2 items would be expected by chance in an unchanged process, so it may be a sampling artefact; it is not part of the allocation hypothesis. [LAG001]
