### Observed differences

- The order between A26 and each of A35, A36 and A41 reversed. In the earlier window A26 came before A35, A36 and A41 in about 98 in 100 traces; in the later window each of the three came before A26 in about 97–98 in 100 traces. All three pairs still co-occur in 100 in 100 traces in both windows. [REL001, REL002, REL003]
- The same reversal occurred against A27: earlier, A27 preceded A35, A36 and A41 in about 98–99 in 100 traces; later, each of the three preceded A27 in about 97 in 100 traces, again with co-occurrence unchanged at 100 in 100. [REL004, REL005, REL006]
- Successors shifted accordingly. Earlier, A25 was followed by A27 in about 97.5 in 100 of its outgoing transitions, A26 was followed by A35, A41 or A36 (roughly 28–38 in 100 each), and each of A35, A36, A41 was followed by A30 in about 28–34 in 100 of its outgoing transitions. Later, A25 is followed by A35, A36 or A41 in roughly equal thirds (about 31–33 in 100 each), each of the three is followed by A27 in about 27–35 in 100, and A26 is followed by A30 in about 97.5 in 100. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005]
- Step distances moved with them: A25 to A27 went from 1 step apart in 98.5 in 100 cases to 4–5 steps apart in about 99.5 in 100 cases, while A26 to A30 went from 4–5 steps apart in 100 in 100 cases to 1 step apart in 98.5 in 100 cases. [LAG001, LAG003]
- Average event positions agree in direction: A35, A36 and A41 each moved from a mean position of about 0.58 to about 0.42 of the trace (earlier), while A27 moved from about 0.38 to 0.47 and A26 from about 0.53 to 0.62 (later). These are event positions, not clock times. [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005]
- Several other LAG items qualify but rest on only 1–2 observations in one window and carry little weight. [LAG002, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006, LAG007, LAG008, LAG009, LAG010]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the block of three activities A35, A36 and A41 changed its place in the flow: in the earlier window it executed after A26 and before A30, while in the later window it executes directly after A25 and before A27 — the order between this block and the segment running from A27 to A26 reversed. Since all six activities still occur in essentially every trace in both windows (co-occurrence 100 in 100), this reads as a reordering of existing activities rather than activities being added, removed or skipped. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, LAG001, LAG003, POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005]

The supporting signs are direct and consistent: the six relational items show the order reversal itself at 97–99+ in 100 shares, the successor, distance and position items all point the same way, and the number of qualifying items (26 in the control-flow family, against about 0.63 expected without any change) is far beyond what chance sampling in an unchanged process produces. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, DIR001, DIR002, LAG001, LAG003, screening]

Limitations: the evidence establishes the reversal of order but cannot distinguish whether the three-activity block moved earlier or the A27-to-A26 segment moved later — both descriptions fit the same relational facts. POS and LAG use event positions rather than clock time, so nothing can be said about timing, and the low-support LAG items were not relied upon. [LAG002, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006, LAG007, LAG008, LAG009, LAG010, POS001]
