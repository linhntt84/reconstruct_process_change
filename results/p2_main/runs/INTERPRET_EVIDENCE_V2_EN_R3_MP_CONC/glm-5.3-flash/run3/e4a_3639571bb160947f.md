### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a timing change as the main hypothesis: in the later window, the elapsed time between the completion of each of the activities A03, A04, A05, A06, A07 and A08 and the completion of A09 is roughly halved, from about one hour to about half an hour, and the intervals are also steadier, with their spread roughly halved. The change is measured directly by the elapsed-time indicator, all six pairs move in the same direction with nearly the same magnitude (about 0.45–0.50 hours), each rests on 27–42 observations, and six qualifying time items against an expectation of about 0.02 for an unchanged process cannot be explained by chance as a whole. Limitations: all six intervals share the endpoint A09, so they may reflect one underlying difference in the segment ending at A09 rather than six independent changes, and elapsed time between consecutive completions cannot separate reduced waiting from faster processing. A competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that the saved time is located anywhere along the shared path up to the completion of A09 — including A09 itself — since the intervals do not localise where the reduction occurs.
