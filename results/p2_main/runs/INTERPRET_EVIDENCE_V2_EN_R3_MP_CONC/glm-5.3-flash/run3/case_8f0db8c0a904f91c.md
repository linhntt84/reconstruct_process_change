### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, A19, A20 and A25 each ran in every trace (200 of 200); in the later window, each appears in only about 53 in 100 traces (106–107 of 200), while A26, A41, A46 and A47 still appear in essentially every trace in both windows. [OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, POS002, POS003, POS004]
- Pairs between the block and its surroundings changed from always co-occurring (100 in 100 traces) to co-occurring in about 53 in 100 later-window traces, with the surrounding activity appearing without the block in about 47 in 100. Pairs inside the block are either both present (about 53 in 100) or both absent (about 47 in 100), so the block disappears as a unit rather than being partially dropped or reordered. [REL008, REL010, REL014, REL015, REL019]
- Routing out of A41 changed: before, about 96 in 100 of A41's outgoing transitions led to A19; in the later window, about 51 in 100 lead to A19 and about 44 in 100 lead directly to A26, a successor not observed there before. [DIR005]
- The A41→A26 distance shrank accordingly: before, A26 followed A41 about four positions later in 93 in 100 cases; in the later window it follows one position later in 44 in 100 cases, while the four-position gap remains in about 49 in 100 (the traces where the block still runs). [LAG002]
- When the block does run, its internal order is unchanged: A25 still leads to A20 in about 98 in 100 of its outgoing transitions and A20 still leads to A26 in about 96 in 100, in both windows. [DIR003, DIR004]
- A02 became much rarer: about 29 in 100 traces before (58 of 200) versus about 8 in 100 in the later window (17 of 200); its outgoing transitions, previously mostly to A47 (about 85 in 100), now spread over many targets, and it now co-occurs with A26/A41/A46/A47 in only about 8 in 100 traces (before 29 in 100). These figures rest on only 17 later-window traces. [OCC001, DIR001, REL004, REL006, POS001]
- Smaller shifts: A04 and A06 each appear in about 12 in 100 later-window traces versus about 4 in 100 before (9→24 and 8→25 traces); A46 now leads to A47 in about 96 in 100 of its outgoing transitions (before about 75 in 100, with about 25 in 100 to A02), and the A46→A47 gap is almost always one position (about 96 in 100 versus 75 in 100). [OCC002, OCC003, DIR006, LAG003]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the fixed sequence A19 → A25 → A20, which in the earlier window always ran between A41 and the downstream activities (A26, A46, A47), became optional in the later window: it is skipped in roughly 47 in 100 traces, and A41 then leads directly on to A26. Supporting signs: the participation of all three block activities fell in step from 100 to about 53 in 100 traces while their partners stayed universal; the block is absent as a unit rather than reordered; A41 gained a direct edge to A26 carrying about 44 in 100 of its outgoing transitions; and the A41→A26 gap moved from mostly four positions to often one. Chance is an unlikely explanation overall: 41 control-flow items qualified where fewer than one (0.72) would be expected without a change, and the largest participation shifts involve about 200 versus 106 traces. [OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, REL008, REL010, DIR005, LAG002]

Limitations: the occurrence, relation, direction and lag items here largely describe the same underlying traces, so they corroborate one change rather than providing independent confirmations. The evidence also cannot show what distinguishes traces that skip the block from those that run it, and cannot rule out that the later window combines two case populations (one always running the block, one never) rather than a per-case skip choice. [OCC004, REL008, LAG002]

Other differences: A02's decline (participation about 29 → 8 in 100 traces, average position moving earlier, successors spreading across many targets) and the increased appearance of A04 and A06 stand outside the main hypothesis, as does A46's outgoing traffic concentrating on A47; the evidence does not connect these to the block change. [OCC001, POS001, DIR001, OCC002, OCC003, DIR006]
