### Observed differences

- In the later window, A01 appears for the first time: it was observed in none of the 200 earlier traces but in about 44 of 200 (about 22 in 100) later traces, exactly once per trace and always at the same event position (median position 1.0) [OCC001, POS001].
- A01 always occurs together with A02: co-occurrence rose from 0 to about 22 in 100 traces; within those, A02 strictly precedes A01 in about 11 in 100 traces and the pair is interleaved or repeated in the other 11, so their order is not fixed. A01 is also now a direct successor of A02, receiving about 21% of A02's roughly 210 observed outgoing transitions, an edge not observed before [REL001, DIR001].
- A02's remaining routing shifted: A15 is no longer observed as a direct successor (about 12% of A02's outgoing transitions before, none after), and the shares going to A07, A08 and A04 fell from roughly 32%, 14% and 10% to about 25%, 9% and 6%, while A06 and A12 now also receive direct transitions from A02 (about 15% and 8%) [DIR001].
- A06 changed role: before, it appeared in about 27 of 200 traces with no outgoing transition observed and at a constant late position (median 1.0); in the later window it appears in about 32 of 200 traces, earlier (median about 0.5), and all 32 of its observed outgoing transitions lead to A15. Consistently, A15 now follows A02 two steps later (lag 2 in about 67 of 100 pairs) instead of immediately (lag 1 in about 52 of 100 pairs before) [DIR002, POS002, LAG002].
- The direct A08→A12 transition disappeared: about 84% of A08's observed outgoing transitions went to A12 before, none after; A08 now leads mainly to A09 (about 46%), A02 (about 23%) and A04 (about 27%) [DIR003]. A12 now follows A02 immediately (lag 1 in about 29 of 100 pairs, versus lag 2–3 before), participates in fewer traces (about 19 of 200 after versus 35 of 200 before) and appears earlier [LAG001, OCC002, POS003].

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that routing after A02 was restructured and that A01, unobserved in the earlier window, now executes as a direct successor of A02 in about one in five traces, absorbing part of A02's former routing (the formerly direct edge to A15 and reduced shares to A07, A08 and A04) [OCC001, REL001, DIR001]. The screening comparison makes chance an unlikely account of the pattern as a whole: 14 items qualified across 46 tested control-flow units, where about 0.23 would be expected without change, and each item's difference exceeds about 995 of 1000 random re-splits of the pooled traces [OCC001, OCC002, DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, POS001, POS002, POS003, REL001, REL002, REL003, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003].

Limitations: the occurrence, position, relation and direction items about A01 largely reflect the same traces, so they are consistent but not independent confirmations [OCC001, POS001, REL001, DIR001]. The evidence also cannot rule out that A01 already occurred at a very low rate before — 0 of 200 traces is still compatible with a rate of roughly 1 in 100 — so "newly added" and "rare then much more frequent" cannot be fully separated, and no outgoing profile for A01 shows what follows it [OCC001].

Other differences:
- A06 and A15 now execute together where their connection was previously unobserved: A06, formerly seen without outgoing transitions, is always followed by A15 in the later window and sits earlier in the trace, while A15 reaches from A02 at lag 2 rather than lag 1 — a pattern consistent with A06 now lying between A02 and A15. Traces containing A01 never contain A06 or A15, so this concerns a separate part of the process [DIR002, POS002, LAG002, REL002, REL003].
- A08's link to A12 disappeared (about 84% of A08's outgoing transitions before, none after), and A12 now follows A02 directly while participating in fewer traces; however, the after-side distance between A08 and A12 rests on only about 5 observations, so that timing detail is weakly supported [DIR003, LAG001, OCC002, LAG003].
