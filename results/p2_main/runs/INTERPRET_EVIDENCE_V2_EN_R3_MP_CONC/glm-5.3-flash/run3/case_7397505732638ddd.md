### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, the traced activities ran as one fixed chain, A26 → A17 → A15 → A18 → A19 → A21 → A20 → A27: each of these activities had exactly one observed successor taking 100% of its outgoing transitions (A15 always led to A18, A18 always to A19, A26 always to A17, and so on), and every observed pair appeared in the same order in all 200 earlier traces [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006, DIR007, REL001].
- In the later window, the same activities still occur in essentially every trace (200 of 200 for each activity, and every observed pair still occurs together in all traces), but the fixed order is gone: pairs now occur in both directions — A15 precedes A19 in only about 26 in 100 later traces (about 74 in 100 have A19 first), A18 precedes A19 in about 15 in 100, and A15 versus A21 is close to 50/50, whereas earlier the first-named activity always came first [REL001, REL003, REL006, REL008, DIR001].
- Direct successions were redistributed rather than removed: A15 still leads to A18 in about 68 in 100 of its outgoing transitions but now also goes to A19, A20 and A21 (about 11% each); A18's most frequent successor is now A27 (about 52 in 100) instead of A19; A20 now returns to A15, A18 or A17 in about half of its transitions; and A26 splits roughly evenly between A17 (53%) and A19 (47%) [DIR001, DIR003, DIR005, DIR007].
- Step distances spread out: A15 and A18 were always adjacent and A15 and A27 always 5 steps apart in the earlier window; in the later window A15–A18 are 1 step apart in about 68 in 100 cases and up to 4 steps apart otherwise, and A15–A27 ranges over 2 to 5 steps [LAG001, LAG005, LAG013].
- Average positions within traces shifted mildly: A15, A17 and A18 now occur somewhat later (mean relative position up by about 0.05, 0.03 and 0.06), while A19, A20 and A21 occur somewhat earlier (down by about 0.06, 0.03 and 0.04) [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006].

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that, in the later window, the earlier fixed sequence among A26, A17, A15, A18, A19, A21, A20 and A27 gave way to a flexible ordering of the same eight activities: they are still executed in essentially every case, but the order between them now varies from case to case — several pairs occur in both directions (A15/A21 and A18/A20 near 50/50), others are reversed relative to before (A19 precedes A18 in about 85 in 100 later traces), and direct successions changed accordingly, for example A18 now passes straight to A27 in about half of its outgoing transitions where it previously always went to A19 [REL003, REL006, REL007, DIR003].

The change is shown directly by relational evidence: co-occurrence stayed at 100% in both windows while order shares moved from 100/0 to mixed for all eight observed pairs, which is a change in ordering constraints rather than an activity swap, an added or removed activity, or a replacement of one path by another; the successor, distance and position indicators all point the same way, and participation is unchanged [REL001, REL002, REL005, REL006, REL008, DIR001, LAG001, POS004]. The volume of qualified evidence also rules out chance: 45 control-flow items qualified where about 0.47 would be expected if nothing had changed, and each item's difference exceeds those seen in about 995 of 1,000 random re-splits of the pooled traces [screening, DIR001, REL001].

Limitations: the evidence cannot distinguish a fully flexible ordering from a mixture of a few new fixed sequences — the pair-level proportions differ (from near 50/50 for A15/A21 to about 76 in 100 still favouring the old direction for A17 before A21), so some constraints may be weakened rather than removed, and about 26 in 100 later traces still place A15 before A19 [REL001, REL003, REL005]. Some distance estimates also rest on fewer observations (the A15–A19 pair within five steps appears in only about 52 of 200 later traces), and the position shifts concern event order within traces, not clock time [LAG002, POS001].
