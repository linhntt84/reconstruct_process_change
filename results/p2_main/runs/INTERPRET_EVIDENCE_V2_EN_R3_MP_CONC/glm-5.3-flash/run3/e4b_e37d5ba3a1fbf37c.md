### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a broad reallocation of work between resources: for A01, A03, A13, A14, A17 and A22, execution shifted from a group of resources that included User_5 as its largest member to a largely new group in which User_49 is prominent, and A24, previously shared by about 13 resources, became handled solely by User_30. Supporting signs: the resource family produced 8 qualifying items against about 0.004 expected without change, a count chance cannot explain as a whole; the same resources gain and lose across all six activities, pointing to one consistent reallocation rather than isolated fluctuations; every difference exceeds all but about 1 of 2000 random re-splits; and each profile rests on at least about 100 observations per window. Control-flow and timing indicators produced no qualifying items, so the difference is confined to allocation rather than to structure or timing. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, ASSIGN007, CONC001]

Limitations: the resource codes are anonymous, and the evidence cannot rule out that the pool of active resources itself changed between the windows (previous contributors ceasing and new ones appearing), which would look identical to a deliberate reallocation of work; A13 and A14 show identical profiles and so likely reflect one underlying behaviour rather than two independent changes.
