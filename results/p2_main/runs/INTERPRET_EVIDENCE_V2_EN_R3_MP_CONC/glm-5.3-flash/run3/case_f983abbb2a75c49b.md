### Observed differences

- The ten activities form two groups whose relative order changed fundamentally. In the earlier window, the first group (A15, A16, A17, A19, A20) and the second group (A21, A22, A23, A24, A25) were interleaved: their order varied from case to case, with A21 preceding A15 in about 77 in 100 traces, A15 preceding A23 in about 58 in 100, and A20 preceding A21 in only about 2 in 100. In the later window, every first-group activity precedes every second-group activity in 100 in 100 traces. [REL001–REL022]

- Observed routing narrowed accordingly. Earlier, A15 sent about 49 in 100 of its observed outgoing transitions to A21–A25 (A16 and A17 similar, roughly 38–42 in 100), and A21, A23, A24 and A25 sent roughly a third to two-fifths of theirs back to the first group. In the later window, the observed outgoing transitions of A15, A16 and A17 go only to A16, A17 and A20 in roughly equal thirds, those of A21, A23, A24 and A25 only within A22–A25, and A22 leads to A31 in every observed transition (earlier about half, with the rest returning to the first group). [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR005, DIR006, DIR007, DIR008, DIR009]

- A20 became the bridge between the blocks: earlier, about half of its observed outgoing transitions went to A31 and most of the rest to A22–A25; in the later window it leads to A21 in every observed case. [DIR004]

- The entry activity A30 earlier led to A21 in about 54 in 100 of its observed outgoing transitions and to A19 in about 46 in 100; in the later window it leads to A19 every time and immediately (lag 1 in 100 in 100 cases, against about 49 in 100 earlier). [DIR010, LAG051]

- Positions and lags moved consistently with a block structure. First-group activities now sit earlier in the trace (A20 mean relative position about 0.71 to 0.61; A15 about 0.61 to 0.55) and second-group activities later (A21 about 0.53 to 0.63; A25 about 0.62 to 0.69) — event positions, not clock times. Within-group pairs became consecutive: A15 to A16 now occurs at lag 1–2 in 100 in 100 cases, against a spread over lags 1–5 earlier. Former cross-group adjacencies lengthened or vanished: A15 to A23, earlier spread over lags 1–5, now sits at lags 3–5 (about 56 in 100 at lag 5), A19 to A21 and A20 to A22 now occur only at lag 5, and A22 reaches A31 at lag 1 in every case. [POS001, POS005, POS006, POS010, LAG001, LAG005, LAG026, LAG027, LAG035]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a specific restructuring of ordering constraints: the two activity groups moved from mixed, interleaved execution to two strictly sequential blocks. In the earlier window, A15, A16, A17, A19 and A20 interleaved with A21, A22, A23, A24 and A25 in case-dependent order, and the entry activity A30 could hand over to either group (A21 in about half of its outgoing transitions). In the later window, A30 always starts with A19, the first group runs as a tight block ending in A20, A20 always hands over to A21, the second group runs as a block (A21, then A23/A24/A25 in quick succession, then A22), and A31 — earlier also reached directly from A20 — is now reached only at the end, from A22. [REL001, REL018, DIR004, DIR006, DIR010, LAG026, LAG035]

The support is direct and consistent: all 22 cross-group pairs flip from mixed orders (some near even, e.g. A16/A25 at about 51 against 49; some strongly reversed, e.g. A21 before A20 in about 97 in 100 traces) to one fixed order in 100 in 100 traces, with both activities present in every trace in both windows, so participation differences and rare variants do not drive the result; the direction, position and lag indicators all point the same way; and 93 qualified items against fewer than 1 expected for an unchanged process cannot be explained by chance as a whole. [REL001–REL022, DIR001, POS005, LAG001]

Limitations: the indicator families largely mirror one underlying resequencing rather than independent confirmations; the indicators show ordering and observed transitions, not clock-time behaviour, and a transition not observed in the later window is not proof that the edge is gone from the underlying process. A competing explanation the evidence cannot fully rule out is a change in case mix — windows drawing different case types that always followed different orderings — though the widely varying order shares across pairs in the earlier window (from about 2 to about 80 in 100 favouring one order) do not fit a simple two-variant mix. [REL001–REL022, DIR001]
