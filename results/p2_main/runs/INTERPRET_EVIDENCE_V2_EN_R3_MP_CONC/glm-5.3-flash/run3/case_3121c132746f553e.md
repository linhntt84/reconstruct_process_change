### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, every trace ran A38 → A14 → A15, then exactly one of two blocks — A20 followed by A21, or A22 — and A23 later; A38's outgoing transitions led to A14 in 100% of observations, A14 co-occurred with A15 and preceded A23 in all 200 traces, and A20 and A22 never occurred in the same trace. ### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a structural change in which a bypass route from A38 directly to A23 is taken in about 48 in 100 later-window traces, skipping A14, A15 and the A20/A21/A22 segment that every trace executed in the earlier window. The signs are direct and mutually consistent: A38's successor profile changes from exclusively A14 to a roughly 52/48 split with A23 [DIR001]; the A38→A23 gap becomes one step in ~47.5% of traces where it was 4–5 steps throughout before [LAG001]; A23 and A38 now occur without A14 in about 47.5% of traces [REL005, REL006, REL014]; and participation in A14, A15, A20, A21 and A22 falls by roughly half in exactly those traces [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, REL013]. The screening context reinforces this: 29 control-flow items qualified where about 0.45 would be expected without a change, so the ensemble cannot be explained by random re-splitting, and each item's difference exceeds about 995 of 1000 random reference splits. Limitations: the items largely describe one underlying trace behaviour and are not independent confirmations; the evidence cannot show whether the direct A38→A23 link is newly added or was merely unobserved in the earlier sample (implausible at ~48% frequency, but not testable here); and a change in the mix of case types between the windows cannot be ruled out from this evidence alone.

Other differences: none beyond the above; the small position shifts of A23 and A38 [POS001, POS002] and the stable conditional A20/A22 split [REL013] are consequences and context of the main change rather than separate differences.
