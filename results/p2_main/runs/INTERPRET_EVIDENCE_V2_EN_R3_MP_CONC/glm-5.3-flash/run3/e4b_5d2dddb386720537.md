### Observed differences

- Repetition of A13 and A14 decreased while their presence stayed unchanged. In the earlier window, both activities appeared in every trace (100 in 100), about 33 in 100 traces contained more than one execution of them, and the mean was about 1.4 occurrences per trace. In the later window they still appeared in every trace, but only about 19 in 100 traces contained a repeat, and the mean fell to about 1.2 occurrences per trace. Both activities show identical values in both windows, so the two items likely describe one shared behaviour rather than two independent changes. [OCC001, OCC002]
- The spacing between A17 and a following A10 shifted. In the earlier window (about 145 of 200 traces contained the pair within five steps), the most common gap was two positions — about 29 in 100 observed pairs, with about 9 in 100 immediately adjacent. In the later window (about 143 traces), a gap of three positions became the most common at about 44 in 100, while immediate succession dropped to about 3 in 100. [LAG001]
- Both differences are unusual for random variation: each exceeds about 199 of 200 reference comparisons that re-split the pooled traces at random. Overall, 3 control-flow items qualified against roughly 0.2 expected by chance if nothing had changed (86 units screened). [OCC001, OCC002, LAG001]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that, in the later window, A13 and A14 are repeated in fewer traces: both activities still occur in every trace, but traces with more than one execution fell from about one in three to about one in five, and the mean number of executions per trace fell from about 1.4 to about 1.2. This is a change in repetition, not in participation — neither activity was observed less often per trace. The sign is direct (the occurrence indicator measures exactly this), consistent across both activities and both affected components, rests on all 200 traces in each window, and the number of qualifying control-flow items (3) is many times the roughly 0.2 expected by chance. Limitations: the two occurrence items carry identical values and so amount to one behavioural signal rather than independent confirmations, and the evidence cannot show which repeated structure of the process these executions belong to. An important competing explanation that cannot be fully excluded is window sampling — the later window could contain fewer repeat-heavy traces by chance — although the difference being larger than in about 199 of 200 random re-splits makes this unlikely. [OCC001, OCC002]

Other differences: The A17→A10 gap redistribution described above, which the evidence cannot tie to the reduced repetition of A13 and A14: A10 now most often follows A17 three positions later (about 44 in 100 observed pairs, up from about 25) rather than two positions later (about 26 in 100, down from about 29), and immediate succession became rare (about 3 in 100, down from about 9). No other control-flow difference passed the screening thresholds, so the evidence does not show what now tends to separate the two activities, and a gap shift alone does not show that an activity was moved. [LAG001]
