### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, each of the five activities A25, A26, A31, A32 and A33 ran exactly once in every case (mean 1.0 occurrence per trace, no within-case repetition, present in 100% of traces). In the later window, each runs about 3 times per case on average and occurs more than once per case in about 66 in 100 traces, while still appearing in every trace. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005]
- The step out of A33 changed: before, A33 was always followed by A20; in the later window about 67 in 100 of A33's outgoing steps go to A25 (a step not observed before) and about 33 in 100 still go to A20. [DIR001]
- The fixed order inside the group dissolved. Before, A23 preceded A25, A26, A31, A32 and A33 in every case, and A25 preceded A26, A31, A32 and A33 in every case (A26 versus A31/A32 had no fixed order, splitting roughly 50/50). In the later window, about 66 in 100 cases show each of these pairs interleaved or repeated, with the previous order remaining in roughly a third. [REL006, REL007, REL008, REL009, REL010, REL011, REL012, REL013, REL014, REL015, REL016, REL017, REL018, REL019, REL020]
- The group members also spread apart within cases. Before, A26, A31 and A32 followed one another within 1–2 event positions in essentially all cases (about two-thirds at exactly 1 step); in the later window only about 1 in 10 ordered co-occurrences sit 1 step apart, and gaps of 3–5 positions, previously absent, now appear. [LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006]
- Consistently, the relative positions of the five activities became much more variable within cases (spread of about 0.05 before vs about 0.14 after) and A23 sits slightly later (median relative position about 0.71 vs 0.77); these are event positions, not clock times. [POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006, POS007]
- A17 appears in fewer cases (about 56 in 100 before vs 46 in 100 after) and sits later in the case; among cases where A17 and a group activity both occur, "group strictly after A17" fell from all such cases to about 4 in 10, with about 6 in 10 interleaved or repeated. [POS001, REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that, in the later window, the block of activities A25, A26, A31, A32 and A33 is repeated within cases: each previously ran exactly once per case in a fixed sequence (A23 ahead of the group, A33 closing into A20), whereas now each recurs in about two thirds of cases at roughly three executions per case, the group's fixed internal order has given way to interleaving, and A33 routes back to A25 in about two thirds of its executions — a pattern consistent with a repetition loop over this block, with A23, still present in every case, now interleaved with the repeating group in about two thirds of cases. [OCC001, DIR001, REL006, REL011, LAG001]

The signs are direct and mutually consistent: the repetition is measured directly (within-case recurrence rising from never to about 66 in 100 cases), the new A33→A25 routing is observed directly, and 15 activity-pair relations and 6 gap distributions all move in the same direction; overall, 39 items qualified against fewer than 1 expected by chance in an unchanged process, so sampling variation cannot account for the set as a whole. [OCC001, DIR001, REL015, LAG001]

Limitation: the evidence shows that the five activities repeat and that A33 feeds back into A25, but not the complete loop body; whether the block repeats as a unit or the activities re-execute in a partly interleaved (for example concurrent) fashion cannot be distinguished, and the loop's exit share can only be read from the A33 split (about one third of its steps still go to A20). [DIR001, REL011]

Other differences: A17 participates less often (about 56 in 100 cases before vs 46 in 100 after) and its fixed precedence over the repeating group dissolved; this participation change is reported separately and is not part of the repetition hypothesis above. [POS001, REL001]
