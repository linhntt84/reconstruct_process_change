### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, the activities A15, A17, A18, A19, A20 and A21 followed one another in varying orders: each had several successors (from A15, A18 followed in about 62 in 100 cases while A19, A20 and A21 each followed in about 12–14 in 100; from A18, A27 followed directly in about 53 in 100; from A26, A17 and A19 each followed in about 50 in 100). ### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a reorganisation of the execution order of the block A26, A17, A15, A18, A19, A21, A20, A27. In the earlier window these activities followed one another in varying orders — A19 typically came before A15 and A18, A27 often followed A18 directly, and several pairs had no fixed order — whereas in the later window they execute as one fixed sequence A26 → A17 → A15 → A18 → A19 → A21 → A20 → A27. This is a change in ordering constraints rather than a shift in how often existing paths are taken: every measured pair co-occurs in 100 in 100 traces in both windows, so participation did not change while the order relations did. The screening comparison supports this reading: 50 control-flow items qualified against roughly 0.5 expected by chance among 95 tested units, far too many to attribute to random re-splits, and all of them describe the same chain. The support is direct (order relations, step distances, successor distributions and positions), mutually consistent, and the near-disappearance of previously common reverse orderings rules out the alternative that only the shares between stable paths shifted. The evidence cannot, however, distinguish a modification of the process definition from the after window sampling a different mix of process variants that already existed before. [DIR001, DIR003, DIR004, DIR007, REL001, REL003, REL006, LAG005, LAG014, LAG015]

Other differences: the elapsed time between consecutive completion events before A29 roughly doubled after both A22 (about 0.9 → 1.9 hours) and A30 (about 0.20 → 0.40 hours); the two time items qualify far beyond chance (2 items against roughly 0.006 expected among 13 tested units) and involve activities for which the evidence contains no structural items, so this stands as a separate timing hypothesis in its own right; these are elapsed times between consecutive completions, not pure waiting or service times. [TWAIT001, TWAIT002]
