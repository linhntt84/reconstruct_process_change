### Observed differences

- The elapsed time between the completion of A11 and the completion of A12 roughly doubled. In the earlier window these intervals averaged about 0.87 hours (median about 0.89, middle half between about 0.75 and 0.95 hours); in the later window they averaged about 1.80 hours (median about 1.88, middle half between about 1.60 and 1.99 hours), an increase of about 0.93 hours with somewhat more spread. The pair occurred equally often in both windows (45 intervals each), so this is a shift in timing, not in how often the pair happens; the difference was larger than in all but about 1 in 2,000 random re-splits of the pooled data. [TWAIT001]

- The dominant successors of A31 and A32 were redistributed. In the earlier window, about 64 in 100 of A31's observed outgoing transitions led to A28 and about 36 to A32, while about 64 in 100 of A32's transitions led to A31 and about 36 to A28; in the later window this flipped, with about 38 in 100 of A31's transitions leading to A28 and about 62 to A32, and about 38 of A32's leading to A31 and about 62 to A28. The dominant route through these activities thus ran A32 → A31 → A28 before and A31 → A32 → A28 after (about 90 outgoing transitions per activity in each window). The two items have identical size and strength and likely reflect this one behaviour; the difference exceeded about 199 of 200 random re-splits. [DIR001, DIR002]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a change in timing as a possibility: the elapsed time between the completion of A11 and the completion of A12 roughly doubled, from an average of about 0.87 hours to about 1.80 hours, with the whole distribution shifting upward (middle half from about 0.75–0.95 to about 1.60–1.99 hours) while the pair occurred equally often in both windows (45 intervals each). This is direct (the indicator measures exactly this interval), consistent across all distribution components, and not explainable by chance: the time family produced one item where about 0.006 would be expected if nothing had changed, and the difference was larger than in all but about 1 in 2,000 random re-splits. Limitations: only one activity pair (45 intervals per window) is involved, and elapsed time between completions cannot be decomposed into waiting, service or rework; an important competing explanation is that a step was inserted or lengthened between A11 and A12, which would produce the same longer interval and cannot be ruled out from these times alone. [TWAIT001]

Other differences: The successor shares around A31 and A32 flipped as described above, a pattern consistent with the two activities exchanging their order before A28; however, the control-flow family produced two items where about 0.27 would be expected without any change, which is within the range an unchanged process typically produces, so this difference is not established as a change and is kept out of the hypothesis.
