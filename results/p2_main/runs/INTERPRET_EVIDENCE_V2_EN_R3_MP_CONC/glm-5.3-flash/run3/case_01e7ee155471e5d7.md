### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the three-activity group A30, A31 and A36, which in the earlier window ran after A21 (segment A20 → A22 → A21 → group → A25), is executed between A20 and A22 in the later window (segment A20 → group → A22 → A21 → A25), so that A22 and A21 now run after the group and A25 immediately follows A21. The reversal is observed directly on the activity pairs, the successor changes at A20, A21 and the group's exit match it exactly, and the step-distance and position shifts agree; since every activity still occurs in every trace, this is a reordering of existing activities, not activities being added, removed or made optional. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, LAG001, LAG002, POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006]

The volume of evidence far exceeds what an unchanged process produces: 24 items qualified against roughly 0.41 expected by chance in the control-flow family, and every item's difference exceeds about 99.5 in 100 random re-splits, with the decisive items backed by all 200 traces per window. [DIR001, DIR002, LAG001, LAG002, REL001, REL004]

Limitations: the evidence covers only these seven activities and their pairwise relations, so it cannot show whether anything else between them changed, and it cannot distinguish "the group moved earlier" from "A22 and the following segment moved later" — both describe the same observed reordering. About one in 200 later-window traces still shows the old order, which is why the remaining gap items (pairs such as A21–A30 and A30–A25) each rest on a single after-window observation and add little weight on their own.
