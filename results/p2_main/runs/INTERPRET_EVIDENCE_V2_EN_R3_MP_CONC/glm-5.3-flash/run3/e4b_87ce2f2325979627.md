### Observed differences

- **Work on A01, A03, A17 and A22 is allocated to a different, more concentrated group of resources in the later window.** In the earlier window, assignments on each of these activities were spread thinly: the ten most used resources held only about 3–9 in 100 assignment events each, and roughly half of all events (47–55 in 100) went to a long tail of others. In the later window, User_3 — previously not among the ten most used resources (share below about 4 in 100) — is the most used resource at about 11–13 in 100, with User_39 and User_53 next at roughly 5–9 in 100, while the long-tail share falls to about 35–45 in 100. The same resources gain and the same previously frequent resources lose share on all four activities; the shift is largest for A22 (profile dissimilarity about 0.53, versus about 0.29–0.35 for the other three). Each profile rests on roughly 125–273 traces per window. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004]

- **A13 and A14 repeat less often within a case in the later window.** Both activities still appear in every trace in both windows (participation 100 in 100), but mean occurrences per trace fall from about 1.5 to about 1.25, and recurrence support falls from about 34 to about 20 in 100; the two activities show identical values throughout. Each difference is larger than about 99.5 in 100 random re-splits of the pooled data. [OCC001, OCC002]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the allocation of work for A01, A03, A17 and A22 changed between the windows: in the earlier window these activities were handled by a diffuse set of resources with about half of all assignments in a long tail, while in the later window they are handled by a smaller, partly different group led by User_3, with User_39 and User_53 also taking larger shares and the previously most used resources falling to about 3–4 in 100 or dropping out of the most-used group. The resource family produced 4 qualifying items where about 0.006 are expected without change — many times the chance expectation — and the four items agree in direction and in which resources gain and lose, each with an empirical strength of about 0.9995. An important competing explanation remains: the evidence cannot show whether the redistribution originates in assignment behaviour itself or in a different mix of cases or a different set of active resources in the later window. Other differences: A13 and A14 occur fewer times per trace in the later window (about 1.5 → 1.25; recurrence support about 34 → 20 in 100). However, the control-flow family yielded 2 items where about 0.21 are expected without change — within the range an unchanged process typically produces — so this repetition decrease does not by itself support a structural hypothesis.
