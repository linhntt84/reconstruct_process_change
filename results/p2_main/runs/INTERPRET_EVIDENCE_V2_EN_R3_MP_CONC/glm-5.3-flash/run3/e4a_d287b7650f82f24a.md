### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a change in timing around A41 as a possibility: in the later window, the elapsed time between consecutive completion events from A38 to A41 and from A40 to A41 is roughly half of what it was in the earlier window (about 1.0 hours down to about 0.5 hours on average), and the gaps became more consistent. This rests on direct measurements of exactly these gaps, with roughly 100 observations per window for each pair; the two items agree in direction and magnitude; and the time family produced 2 qualifying items where only about 0.01 were expected if nothing had changed, so chance sampling cannot plausibly account for the findings as a whole. [TWAIT001, TWAIT002]

Limitations: TWAIT measures elapsed time between consecutive completions, so the evidence does not show whether the reduction lies in waiting, processing or handover time. The two items share A41 and may reflect one underlying difference rather than two independent ones. An important competing explanation the evidence cannot fully rule out is a shift in the mix of trace variants that changes the composition of these gaps without any per-case timing change; control flow showed no qualifying items (0 of 52 tested units against about 0.26 expected by chance), but smaller sub-threshold shifts remain possible. [TWAIT001, TWAIT002]

Other differences: the number of gap observations moved in opposite directions (about 97 to 113 for A38–A41, about 103 to 87 for A40–A41); with no control-flow item qualifying, this modest shift in how often these consecutive pairs occur is not established as a structural change.
