### Observed differences

- Participation did not change: A21, A22 and the five block activities A30, A31, A36, A37 and A38 co-occur in 100% of traces in both windows (200 of 200 before and after). Every difference below is about order, not about activities appearing or disappearing. [REL001–REL010]
- Order reversal against A21: in the earlier window, A21 came before each of A30, A31, A36, A37 and A38 in all 200 traces; in the later window, each of these five comes before A21 in about 199 of 200 traces. [REL001–REL005]
- The same reversal against A22: before, A22 preceded each of the five in every trace; after, each of the five precedes A22 in about 199 of 200 traces. [REL006–REL010]
- Entry point rewired: before, A20's outgoing transitions went to A22 in 100% of cases; in the later window A20 leads to one of the five block activities (roughly 17–23% each) and to A22 in only about 1 in 200 transitions. [DIR001]
- A21 rewired: before, A21 led into the block, splitting its outgoing transitions roughly evenly over the five block activities (about 16–23% each); after, A21 leads directly to A25 in about 995 of 1,000 transitions. [DIR002]
- Block exit rewired: before, each block activity sent about 18–25% of its outgoing transitions to A25; after, that exit role has moved to A22 (about 17–22% each) and A25 is nearly gone as a successor (at most about 0.5%). Transitions among the five block activities themselves remain spread at roughly 20% over the other four members in both windows, so the block's internal behaviour looks unchanged. [DIR003–DIR007]
- Relative positions agree: the block activities moved earlier in the trace (mean relative position about 0.60 before vs about 0.44 after), while A22 (about 0.39 → 0.52) and A21 (about 0.52 → 0.65) moved later. [POS001–POS007]
- Ordered pairs within five steps corroborate this: pairs of the form (A21, block member) and (block member, A25) occurred within five steps in about 200 traces before but in only 1 trace after. The after-side lag profiles rest on a single observation each and are weak on their own. [LAG001–LAG010]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a reordering hypothesis: the five-activity block A30, A31, A36, A37, A38, which in the earlier window ran after A21 (with A20 feeding A22, A21 feeding into the block, and the block exiting to A25), now runs directly after A20 and before A22 and A21, exiting to A22, while A21 now leads directly to A25. In short, the block and the A22/A21 segment exchanged their relative order. [REL001–REL010, DIR001–DIR007, POS001–POS007]

The support is direct and consistent: ten activity pairs each flip from a perfect order to the near-perfect opposite order, the successor changes at all three boundaries (A20's entry, the block's exit, A21's exit) point the same way, the position shifts agree, and participation is unchanged, which rules out the activities being added, removed or skipped. The volume of qualifying items (34 against roughly 0.55 expected without a change) cannot be explained by random sampling as a whole. [REL001–REL010, DIR001–DIR007, POS001–POS007, LAG001–LAG010]

Limitations: the mutual order and adjacency of A22 and A21 in the later window is not directly observed, so whether A22 still directly precedes A21 is inferred from their relative positions only, and the after-side lag distances rest on one observation each. The evidence also cannot distinguish whether the block moved earlier or the A22/A21 segment moved later — both descriptions fit the same rewiring. [POS001, POS002, LAG001–LAG010]
