### Observed differences

- Resource allocation for A12 changed completely: in the earlier window its assignments were spread over about 44 different resources, with the most used one (User_1) handling about 40 in 100; in the later window a single resource (User_36) handled every observed A12 assignment (top-resource share from about 40 to 100 in 100, distinct resources from 44 to 1). [ASSIGN003, CONC001]
- On A13, A14 and A17 the work moved to a largely different set of resources: User_71 went from about 5–6 in 100 assignments to about 24–25 in 100, while the previously most used resource on these activities (about 11–13 in 100) no longer appears and several smaller contributors were replaced by new ones. [ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006]
- A01, A03 and A22 show the same turnover in a milder form: resources that each handled roughly 4–13 in 100 of the assignments in the earlier window no longer appear, new resources take shares of similar size, and the long-tail "other" share stays around 38–48 in 100 in both windows. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN007]
- The elapsed time between consecutive completions of A03 and A13 is much shorter in the later window: the median fell from about 168 hours to about 66 hours (mean from about 167 to about 72 hours), with 80 such intervals observed versus 58 before. [TWAIT001]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the allocation of work to resources changed between the windows. In the earlier window, A01, A03, A12, A13, A14, A17 and A22 were each handled by a broad pool of resources; in the later window, A12 was executed entirely by one resource (User_36) after previously being led by User_1 with about 40 in 100 of the assignments, and on A13, A14 and A17 User_71 roughly quadrupled its share while several earlier resources, including the previously largest, stopped appearing; A01, A03 and A22 show the same replacement of resources with smaller share shifts. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, ASSIGN007, CONC001]

This goes well beyond what an unchanged process produces: the resource family yielded 8 qualifying items against roughly 0.005 expected by chance, each difference exceeds all but about 1 in 2000 random re-splits, and the changes are measured directly on assignment shares and concentration with substantial samples in both windows (for example 199 versus 189 traces for A12, about 343 occurrences per window for A13 and A14). No control-flow items qualified (0 against about 0.18 expected), so the difference is confined to allocation rather than to activity structure. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, ASSIGN007, CONC001]

Limitations: the resource codes are anonymous, so the evidence cannot distinguish a genuine reallocation of work from a re-identification of resources between the windows, and it cannot show what drove the shift; the A13 and A14 profiles are identical, so those two items likely describe one underlying behaviour rather than two independent confirmations. [ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005]

Other differences: The elapsed time between consecutive completions of A03 and A13 fell by roughly 100 hours at the median (about 168 hours before, about 66 hours after). As the only qualifying time item (1 against about 0.02 expected without change), it does not by itself rule out a sampling artefact, even though its size is unusual under random re-splits; it is best read as a possible timing change that this evidence alone cannot confirm. [TWAIT001]
