### Observed differences

- The three activities A30, A31 and A36 stopped repeating. In the earlier window each appeared in every trace, about 3.5 times per trace on average, with more than one occurrence in about 75 in 100 traces; in the later window they still appear in every trace but only about once per trace, with repeats in only about 1–2 in 100 traces. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003]
- A36's exit routing flipped. Earlier, about 70 in 100 of A36's outgoing transitions led to A30 and about 28 in 100 to A25; in the later window about 92 in 100 lead to A25 and only about 4 in 100 to A30 (based on roughly 690 vs 210 outgoing transitions). [DIR002]
- A30's own routing is essentially unchanged: about 99 in 100 of its outgoing transitions went to A31 earlier, and about 97 in 100 in the later window. [DIR001]
- The order between A30 and A36 changed from mixed/repeating to fixed. Earlier the two were interleaved or repeated in about 76 in 100 traces (A30 before A36 in about 25 in 100); in the later window A30 precedes A36 in about 98 in 100 traces, while they still occur together in every trace in both windows. [REL001]
- A36 now follows A30 closely: in the later window it comes two positions after A30 in about 89 in 100 traces, against about 12 in 100 earlier. [LAG001]
- The return paths of the earlier repetition disappeared: A36 followed by A30 within five steps occurs in about 150 of 200 traces earlier but only 1 of 200 later, and A36 repeated within five steps in about 150 of 200 traces earlier but 2 of 200 later. [LAG002, LAG003]
- The relative positions of A30 and A36 barely shift (means about 0.42–0.49 in both windows), but their position spread narrows (standard deviation about 0.12 down to about 0.07). [POS001, POS002]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the repeating cycle through A36, A30 and A31 is no longer exercised: earlier, about three quarters of traces cycled through these activities roughly three to four times, with A36 returning to A30 (about 70 in 100 of its outgoing transitions) and A30 passing on to A31 (about 99 in 100); in the later window each of the three executes about once per trace in a fixed order, A30 before A36 (98 in 100, typically two positions apart, consistent with the preserved A30→A31 step), after which A36 continues to A25 (about 92 in 100) instead of returning to A30. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, DIR001, DIR002, REL001, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003]

The signs are direct and mutually consistent: the repetition drop is measured on all 200 traces in both windows, the routing flip at A36 is direct successor evidence, and the relational and lag items show the same restructuring from the ordering side. The 11 qualifying items far exceed the roughly 0.47 expected by chance among the 94 tested units, so the set as a whole is not what an unchanged process typically produces; the items nevertheless describe one underlying difference and are not independent confirmations. [OCC001, DIR002, REL001, LAG002]

A competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that the cycle still exists but is now taken in only a small minority of traces: about 4 in 100 of A36's outgoing transitions still go to A30, and isolated A36→A30 and A36→A36 repetitions remain in the later window. In addition, A31's outgoing behaviour was not tested, so the path from A31 back to A36 in the earlier cycle is inferred rather than directly observed. [DIR002, LAG002, LAG003]

Other differences: A30 shows a handful of very small new destination shares in the later window (about 0.5 in 100 each, e.g. to A02, A06, A33, A42); given the much smaller number of outgoing transitions later (about 210 vs 690), these are likely sampling noise. The position distributions of A30 and A36 narrowed without a meaningful shift in mean position, which is consistent with single execution rather than a relocation of these activities. [DIR001, POS001, POS002]
