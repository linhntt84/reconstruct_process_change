### Observed differences

- Three activities — A14, A15 and A20 — were observed in the earlier window but not at all in the later one: A14 and A20 each appeared in about 35 in 100 traces (70 of 200) and A15 in about 30 in 100 (60 of 200), each at most once per trace; in the later window, none of the 200 traces contains any of them. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003]
- Before, A36 passed to exactly one of these three activities (roughly 35 in 100 onward transitions to A20, 30 to A15 and 35 to A14); in the later window, A36 leads directly to A21 in all of its observed outgoing transitions. [DIR005]
- Before, A14, A15 and A20 each led exclusively to A21; in the later window these transitions are no longer observed, since the activities themselves no longer occur. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR004]
- The distance from A36 to A21 shortened: before, A21 followed A36 two event positions later in every trace; in the later window it immediately follows A36 (one position) in every trace. [LAG001]
- Pairwise relations match this pattern: before, A21 co-occurred with each of A14, A15 and A20 in roughly 30–35 in 100 traces and always came after them, while A14, A15 and A20 were mutually exclusive alternatives (never in the same trace); in the later window A21 occurs without any of them in all traces, and no trace contains more than one of the three. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, REL007, REL008, REL009]
- A21 and A36 still occur in every trace in both windows; A21's mean relative position moved slightly earlier (about 0.39 to 0.37) and A36's slightly later (about 0.32 to 0.33), consistent with one intermediate step disappearing between them. [POS004, POS005]
- Separately, A19's outgoing transitions shifted toward A26 (from about 41 in 100 to about 60 in 100) and away from A16 (about 31 to 16) and A17 (about 28 to 23), with A19 observed in about 97 outgoing transitions before and 111 after. [DIR003]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the segment between A36 and A21 was bypassed: in the earlier window each trace went from A36 to one of three alternative activities — A14, A15 or A20, taken in roughly equal shares — and then on to A21, whereas in the later window A36 leads directly to A21 and A14, A15 and A20 are no longer executed. This rests on direct relational evidence — A36's successor set changed from A14/A15/A20 to A21 alone, and the A36-to-A21 lag fell from two positions to one — together with the complete disappearance of the three activities (0 of 200 traces each, down from 60–70 of 200) and nine relation items that all show the same pattern. The screening context makes chance an unlikely explanation for the ensemble: 23 control-flow items qualified against roughly 0.37 expected if nothing had changed. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, DIR001, DIR002, DIR004, DIR005, LAG001, REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, REL007, REL008, REL009]

A limitation: zero participation only means the activities were not observed, so the evidence cannot distinguish complete removal of A14, A15 and A20 from them becoming very rare, nor rule out that a rare branch retaining them persists outside these samples. The main competing explanation — that the three activities were relocated elsewhere in the process rather than dropped — is weakened by the fact that no later-window trace shows them in any position, while their former exclusive successor A21 now occurs in every trace without them. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, REL003, REL006, REL008]

Other differences: A19's routing among its existing successors A26, A16 and A17 shifted toward A26; A19 and all three successors were observed in both windows (A19 in about half of the traces in each), so this looks like a change in how often existing paths are taken rather than a structural replacement, but it is not part of the bypass hypothesis above.
