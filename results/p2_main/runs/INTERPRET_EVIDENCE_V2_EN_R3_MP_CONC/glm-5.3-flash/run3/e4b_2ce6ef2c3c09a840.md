### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a change in resource allocation. In the earlier window, activities A01, A03, A13, A14, A17 and A22 were each executed by a broad spread of resources; in the later window, a largely different set of resources performs them (User_97 now among the most used where User_16 was before), with a somewhat larger share of occurrences falling to a long tail of resources. A11 shows the sharpest shift: from being shared across about 20 resources to being handled exclusively by User_115. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, ASSIGN007, CONC001]

Supporting signs: the assignment profiles measure allocation directly on each affected activity, all affected activities move in the same direction, the A11 concentration is corroborated by a second indicator, and eight resource items against about 0.004 expected by chance is far beyond what random re-splits of these windows produce. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, ASSIGN007, CONC001]

Limitations: the evidence cannot rule out that the two windows simply capture periods with different active resource pools rather than a changed allocation rule, and the anonymous codes do not allow distinguishing genuinely new resources from re-labelled ones; the identical A13/A14 profiles count as one underlying difference, not two. [ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005]

Other differences: none are evidenced — no control-flow items qualified, so the flagged change is confined to resource allocation. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, ASSIGN007, CONC001]
