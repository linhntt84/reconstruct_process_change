### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that resource allocation changed: in the later window, six activities (A01, A03, A13, A14, A17, A22) are executed by a largely different set of resource codes than before — the codes that previously took the largest shares (roughly 4–9 in 100 assignments each) dropped out of the leading positions, a largely new set led by User_84 and User_96 (about 7–11 in 100) took over, and work became somewhat less concentrated on the leading codes. The signs are direct and consistent: the resources family produced 6 qualifying items against about 0.005 expected without change, all six show the same redistribution with differences larger than in all but about 1 of 2,000 random re-splits, and each rests on substantial counts. No structural counterpart was detected (control flow: 0 items against about 0.17 expected), so the hypothesis concerns allocation only. Limitations: A13 and A14 show identical distributions in both windows and likely reflect one underlying pattern rather than two independent confirmations, and because resource identifiers are anonymous codes, a re-coding of resources between the windows cannot be fully ruled out — although a few codes appear in both windows with similar shares, which argues for a stable coding. Other differences: the elapsed time between consecutive completions of A05 and A09 rose consistently (median about 0.8 to about 1.1 seconds). As a single time item (about 0.02 expected by chance), it suggests a possible timing change but does not establish one, and the evidence cannot link it to the reallocation. [TWAIT001]
