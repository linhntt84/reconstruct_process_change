### Observed differences

- Five activities — A19, A20, A25, A26 and A27 — were not observed in any of the 200 earlier traces; in the later window each occurs in about 16–23 in 100 traces (33–46 of 200), and tested pairs of them share a trace in at most about 1 in 100 cases, so a later-window trace typically contains one of the five rather than several. [OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, REL010, REL011, REL012, REL016, REL017, REL021]
- These activities sit inside the segment between A29, A30 and A31. Earlier, about 98 in 100 of A29's outgoing transitions went to A30 and about 98 in 100 of A30's went to A31; in the later window A29 sends about half of its outgoing transitions to the five new activities (about 51 in 100 combined) and A30 sends about 44 in 100 to them, while the new activities themselves lead mainly to A30 (about 43–61 in 100) and A31 (about 36–52 in 100). - Step distances agree: the A29→A31 distance was 2 steps in about 97 in 100 earlier traces and is 3 steps in about 95 in 100 later traces; A29→A30 and A30→A31 each changed from almost always 1 step to a roughly even split between 1 and 2 steps. [LAG001, LAG002, LAG003]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that, in the later window, exactly one of five alternative activities — A19, A20, A25, A26 or A27 — is executed within the segment between A29/A30 and A31, placed either between A29 and A30 or between A30 and A31, whereas in the earlier window this segment ran directly from A29 via A30 to A31 and none of the five was observed. The support is direct and consistent across indicator families: the five rise from zero to about 16–23 in 100 traces and are almost mutually exclusive; A29 and A30 now route about half of their outgoing transitions into them; the new activities lead back mainly to A30 and A31; they co-occur with A29/A30/A31 in about 22 in 100 traces in the order A29 first and A31 last; and the A29→A31 step distance grew from two to three steps. Screening produced 49 qualifying items against roughly 0.68 expected for 137 tested units if nothing had changed, so the pattern as a whole cannot be explained by chance sampling. A limitation: the five activities' earlier behaviour was not observed at all, so the hypothesis rests on their later profiles plus the directly observed routing and gap changes at A29, A30 and A31; the evidence cannot rule out that these activities existed earlier but were too rare to appear in the earlier sample. Other differences: A06 more than tripled its participation (about 8 to 28 in 100 traces), moved to the late part of the trace, and is now placed between A46 and A47 (A46→A06 about 32 in 100 of A46's outgoing transitions; A06→A47 about 85 in 100 of A06's), whereas earlier it was rare, sat around mid-trace and led to many different activities. Since it almost never co-occurs with the five new activities, this appears to be a second change distinct from the main hypothesis; its routing and order evidence is direct, but only 15 earlier traces contain A06, so its earlier profile is thinly supported.
