### Observed differences

- Work at four activities — A03, A13, A14 and A22 — was reallocated to a different set of resources. In the earlier window, assignments at each of these activities were spread thinly: the pooled remainder of many resources held about 45–56 in 100 assignments, and no single resource more than about 6–8 in 100. In the later window, that remainder shrank to about 28–48 in 100, and a small group of resources took the largest shares: User_67, User_79 and User_104 held roughly 5–13 in 100 depending on the activity, while a largely disjoint set of resources that previously each handled about 4–8 in 100 fell to zero or near-zero share. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004]
- The shift is largest at A22: its assignment-profile difference (cosine dissimilarity about 0.50) exceeded all but about 1 in 2000 random re-splits of the two windows; A03, A13 and A14 each show about 0.28–0.29, also above essentially all re-splits. At A22, User_79 rose from about 5 to about 13 in 100 and User_67 and User_104, previously not among the visible shares, reached about 11 and 10 in 100; at A13 and A14, User_67 rose from no visible share to about 9 in 100; at A03, User_67 and User_79 reached about 7 and 6–7 in 100. The profiles rest on about 119 vs 100 assignment records for A22, about 277 vs 270 for A13 and A14, and about 200 vs 198 for A03. All four items come from the resource family, which produced 4 qualifying items where about 0.005 would be expected if nothing had changed. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004]
- A13 and A14 show identical allocation profiles in both windows, so they most likely reflect one shared assignment behaviour rather than two independent changes. [ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003]
- Timing: for the elapsed time between consecutive completion events involving A22 and A03 (intervals on the order of a hundredth of a second), the median interval was unchanged between windows, but the spread roughly tripled and the upper-quartile interval grew by about 18%; about 20 fewer intervals were observed (119 vs 99, about 17% fewer). ### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that resource allocation changed for part of the process: in the later window, the activities A03, A13, A14 and A22 were handled by a different and more concentrated set of resources than before. Earlier, work at these activities was dispersed across a long tail of resources with none above about 6–8 in 100 assignments; later, User_67, User_79 and User_104 held the top shares (about 5–13 in 100) at all four activities, while a largely disjoint group of previously used resources dropped to zero or near-zero share. Supporting signs: four direct allocation measurements that agree in direction, each larger than all but about 1 in 2000 random re-splits; a resource-family count (4) many times its chance expectation (about 0.005), so the pattern as a whole is not explainable by random sampling; and substantial support bases behind each profile. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004]

Limitations: the identical profiles of A13 and A14 mean the four items are not fully independent, so the pattern effectively covers about three distinct allocation behaviours. A shift in the case mix between the windows could in principle redistribute assignment shares without any reallocation; no control-flow differences qualified (0 items vs about 0.17 expected), which argues against this but does not rule out subtler mix effects. [ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003]

Other differences: the elapsed time between consecutive completions involving A22 and A03 became more variable in the later window (spread about three times wider, upper quartile about 18% longer, median unchanged) with about 17% fewer observed intervals. This is a single qualifying item in a family where about 0.02 items would be expected by chance, so a timing change accompanying the reallocation remains possible but is not established on its own; it involves the same activities as the allocation shift and is not an independent confirmation.
