### Observed differences

- Two groups of three activities swapped how often they run. In the earlier window, A16, A17 and A18 each appeared in about 19 in 100 traces; in the later window, about 79 in 100 (37 → 158 traces). A19, A20 and A21 went the opposite way, from about 82 in 100 traces earlier to about 21 in 100 later (163 → 42 traces). In both windows each of these activities occurs at most once per trace. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006]
- The routing out of A15 flipped in step. A15 runs in every trace in both windows. Earlier, its outgoing transitions went mainly to A19 (about 29 in 100), A21 (about 28) and A20 (about 24), rarely to A16 (about 7), A17 (about 9) and A18 (about 3); in the later window most went to A18 (about 29), A16 (about 28) and A17 (about 22), with few to A19, A20 and A21 (about 6–8 each). [DIR001]
- The two groups are mutually exclusive alternatives in both windows, and only their shares moved. Activities from different groups never share a trace in either window; within-group co-occurrence for A19/A20/A21 fell from about 82 in 100 traces to about 21 in 100, while for A16/A17/A18 it rose from about 19 in 100 to about 79 in 100. [REL009, REL010, REL011, REL014, REL015, REL016, REL018, REL019, REL020, REL007, REL008, REL013, REL022, REL023, REL025]
- Within each group the three activities run together without a fixed order, in both windows. Earlier, A19 preceded A20 in about 48 in 100 traces and A20 preceded A19 in about 34 in 100; later, A16 preceded A17 in about 42 in 100 and A17 preceded A16 in about 38 in 100, with similar mixing in the other within-group pairs. [REL022, REL023, REL025, REL007, REL008, REL013]
- A25 runs in every trace in both windows and follows whichever group executed. Earlier, A19/A20/A21 preceded A25 in about 46 in 100 traces and overlapped with it in about 36 in 100; later, A16/A17/A18 preceded A25 in about 45 in 100 and overlapped in about 34 in 100. [REL012, REL017, REL021, REL024, REL026, REL027]
- Scale of the signal: 34 control-flow items qualified out of 104 tested units, against roughly 0.52 expected if nothing had changed, and each item's difference exceeded about 199 of 200 random re-splits of the pooled data. [OCC001, DIR001, REL001]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that two existing alternative paths between A15 and A25 exchanged frequencies. In the earlier window, about 82 in 100 cases ran A15, then the group A19–A20–A21 (all three, in no fixed order), then A25, with the A16–A17–A18 group as the minority alternative (about 19 in 100); in the later window the shares flipped to about 79 in 100 for the A16/A17/A18 path and about 21 in 100 for the A19/A20/A21 path. This qualifies as a shift between existing alternatives rather than activities being added, removed, bypassed or repeated: all six activities were observed in both windows with non-trivial support (at least 37 traces), the groups never co-occurred in either window, the frame A15 → group → A25 is intact in both windows, and no activity recurs within a trace. [OCC001, OCC004, DIR001, REL009, REL022, REL012]

The three views of the behaviour — participation of the six activities, the mirrored successor shares at A15, and the within-group and cross-group relations — all describe the same flip, and the volume of qualifying items (34 against roughly 0.52 expected for an unchanged process) cannot be explained by chance sampling as a whole. A competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that the two windows draw on different case populations with different variant mixes rather than a single change in routing; the evidence shows that the shares flipped, not what governs the choice between the groups. [OCC001, DIR001, REL001, REL012]
