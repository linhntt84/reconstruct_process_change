### Observed differences

- Two blocks of activities exchanged where they sit in the case. The block A14, A18, A21, A22, A23 — each present in roughly 16–23 in 100 traces in both windows — sat at about one-third of the trace in the earlier window (mean relative position 0.32–0.33) and at about seven-tenths in the later window (0.68–0.71). The block A15, A16, A17, A19, A20 — present in every trace in both windows — moved the opposite way, from about 0.62–0.63 to about 0.38–0.39. [POS001, POS005, POS008, POS009, POS010, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS006, POS007]

- The handover points swapped with them. In the earlier window, every observed outgoing transition of A14, A18, A21, A22 and A23 (32–46 traces each) led to A24; in the later window, every one leads to A29. In the earlier window, A15, A16, A17, A19 and A20 sent about 14–27 in 100 of their outgoing transitions to A29; in the later window none do, and A24 instead receives about 17–25 in 100. [DIR001, DIR005, DIR008, DIR009, DIR010, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR006, DIR007]

- The feeding activities swapped as well: earlier, all outgoing transitions of A39 led to the early block (A14, A18, A21–A23) and those of A28 to the late block (A15–A17, A19, A20); later, A39 leads only to the latter group and A28 only to the former, with no overlap between the windows. [DIR011, DIR012]

- Order relations reversed completely where the blocks meet the junction points: A24 preceded A15, A16, A17, A19 and A20 in 200 out of 200 traces earlier and followed them in 200 out of 200 later; A28 shows the same complete reversal against all five. [REL012, REL013, REL018, REL019, REL024, REL025, REL033, REL034, REL038, REL039]

- Pairs across the two blocks flipped consistently wherever they co-occur — for example A14 preceded A15 in about 21 in 100 joint traces earlier, while later A15 precedes A14 in about 17 in 100, with co-occurrence itself nearly unchanged. [REL001, REL008, REL026, REL030, REL040]

- A24 itself moved later (mean position 0.36 to 0.48) and A28 moved later (0.54 to 0.66), consistent with the blocks around them exchanging places. [POS011, POS012]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that two blocks of activities swapped their order within the case: the block A14, A18, A21, A22 and A23, which previously ran early (around one-third of the trace) and flowed into A24, now runs late (around seven-tenths) and flows into A29, while the block A15, A16, A17, A19 and A20, which previously ran late and flowed into A29, now runs early and flows into A24; accordingly, A39 now precedes the A15-group and A28 the A14-group, the reverse of before. [DIR001, DIR011, DIR012, REL012, REL013, POS001, POS002]

This is direct and consistent rather than a chance artefact: 69 control-flow items qualified, more than a hundred times the roughly 0.65 expected if nothing had changed; the reversals against A24 and A28 are complete in both windows; all twelve direction, twelve position and forty-five relation items point in the same coordinated direction; and the position shifts (0.23–0.38 on the position scale) mirror one another. [REL012, DIR002, POS002]

Limitations: the many items largely restate one underlying rearrangement, so they are not independent confirmations. No relations within either block were measured, so the evidence cannot show that each block's internal sequence was preserved — a coordinated repositioning of individual activities, or a change confined to how A39 and A28 route into the blocks, would look the same here. Positions are measured in event order, not clock time. [REL001, REL008, REL012, REL040]

Other differences: the share of traces containing the rare-block activities wavered by a few points between the windows (for example A23 appears in 16 in 100 traces earlier and 21 in 100 later, A14 in 21 earlier and 17 later), which is within what samples of about 40 traces typically show. [POS010, POS001]
