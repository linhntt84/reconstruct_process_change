### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that, in the later window, the activities A03, A05, A15 and A17 are executed more often and repeated more often within cases: each appears in a larger share of traces (A15 roughly doubling from about 22 to 38 in 100), occurs more times per trace (A03 and A17 gaining about 0.8 occurrences per trace), and recurs more frequently within a trace [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004]. This is consistent with part of the process involving these activities being performed, and repeated, more often than before. The hypothesis rests on direct evidence — occurrence indicators measure exactly this behaviour — and it is consistent in direction across all items, while the item count (4) is far above the roughly 0.14 expected without a change, making sampling variability an unlikely explanation for the pattern as a whole [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004]. Limitations: the evidence is purely occurrence-based; with no relational or positional items, it cannot show where in the flow the extra executions take place, and the identical profiles of A03 and A17 suggest the four items may reflect one underlying change rather than four independent ones [OCC001, OCC004]. A competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that a single shared, repeating segment containing these activities drives all four increases, as opposed to the activities increasing independently.

Other differences: none — every qualifying item concerns the same occurrence and repetition increases [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004].
