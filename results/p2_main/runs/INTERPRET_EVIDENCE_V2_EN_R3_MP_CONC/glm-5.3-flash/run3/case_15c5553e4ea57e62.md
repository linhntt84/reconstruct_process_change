### Observed differences

- Participation itself is unchanged: every checked activity pair still co-occurs in 100 in 100 traces in both windows, and the direction and position profiles rest on all 200 traces per window. The differences below are about order and routing, not about activities appearing or disappearing. [REL001–REL021, POS001–POS010, DIR001–DIR011]

- In the earlier window the process ran as two strictly ordered groups: in every trace, A15, A16, A17, A19 and A20 all preceded A21, A22, A23, A24 and A25 (100 in 100 traces for all 21 checked pairs); all of A20's observed outgoing transitions went to A21, all of A22's went to A31, and A30 entered at A19. [REL001–REL021, DIR005, DIR007, DIR011]

- In the later window this fixed order largely dissolved: A21 now precedes A15, A16 and A17 in about 73 in 100 traces (before: never) and precedes A20 in about 94 in 100 (before: never); A23–A25 now precede A20 in about 76–84 in 100; the pairs between {A15, A16, A17} and {A23, A24, A25} split about 52–57 in 100 early-first, i.e. no fixed order; A19 still usually precedes A23–A25 (about 77–79 in 100) but no longer always. Within single traces each pair keeps one order, so individual cases follow different orders rather than interleaving. [REL001, REL005, REL009, REL017, REL019, REL020, REL021, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL006, REL007, REL008, REL010, REL011, REL012, REL013, REL014]

- Routing changed accordingly: A20 → A21 fell from all outgoing transitions to about 7 in 100, while about 48 in 100 now go directly to A31; A22 → A31 fell to about 53 in 100, with about 18 in 100 returning to A20; A30 now sends about 46 in 100 of its transitions to A21 (before: none); and the groups exchange substantial transition shares in both directions (about 28–48 in 100 of each early-group source's outgoing transitions go to A21–A25; about 31–46 in 100 of A21's and A23–A25's go back to A15–A20). [DIR005, DIR007, DIR011, DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR006, DIR008, DIR009, DIR010]

- Relative event positions moved consistently: A15–A17 and A20 moved later (A20's mean relative position from about 0.61 to 0.71), A21 and A23–A25 moved earlier (A21 from about 0.64 to 0.55), and the spread of positions widened for all ten activities (e.g., A23's standard deviation from about 0.05 to 0.08). [POS001–POS010]

- Step distances changed in the same direction: A15 → A21 was 2–4 steps apart before and is now 1 step in about 46 in 100 observed cases; A20 → A22 was always 5 steps before and is now 1–2 steps in about 69 in 100; distances inside the early group grew (A15 → A17 from lag 1 in about 70 in 100 to a spread over 1–5); the exits A22 → A31 and A30 → A19 are no longer uniformly at distance 1 (about 56 and 57 in 100 later cases at lag 1). Several later-window distance profiles rest on few observations (about 33–58 traces). [LAG005, LAG028, LAG003, LAG036, LAG051, LAG029]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that, in the later window, the strict sequencing between the two activity groups was relaxed into a choice of orders: the group {A21, A22, A23, A24, A25}, which previously always executed after {A15, A16, A17, A19, A20}, now frequently executes before it or in mixed order with it, and the fixed bridges lost their exclusivity — A20 often bypasses A21–A25 and exits directly to A31, A22 often returns to A15–A20, and A30 can enter the late group at A21. [REL001, REL005, REL017, REL019, DIR005, DIR007, DIR011]

The order changes are shown directly by the relation indicators with full co-occurrence and 200 traces behind each window; the successor redistributions across all eleven direction units, the position shifts and the distance changes all point the same way; and 93 items qualified against fewer than 1 expected if nothing had changed, so the pattern as a whole is not what random re-splits produce. [REL001–REL021, DIR001–DIR011, POS001–POS010, LAG005, LAG028]

An important competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out: a partial swap of the two groups versus added interleaving or repetition between them (for example cases looping back from the late group into the early group) — the near-even order splits and the wider position and distance spreads fit both readings, and several later-window distance profiles rest on only about 33–58 traces. [REL002, REL006, POS001, LAG029]

Other differences: the A03 → A05 distance became more concentrated, with lag 1 rising from about 60 to about 84 in 100 observed cases (45 and 58 traces behind the two windows), a small change outside the two-group restructuring above. [LAG001]
