### Observed differences

- The successor profile of A35 flipped: in the earlier window, among A35's observed outgoing transitions (104 observed), about 58 in 100 went to A32 and about 38 in 100 to A36; in the later window (89 observed), about 61 in 100 go to A36 and about 37 in 100 to A32. [DIR001]
- The successor profile of A36 flipped in the mirror image: earlier, about 60 in 100 of A36's outgoing transitions went to A35 and about 39 in 100 to A32; in the later window, about 60 in 100 go to A32 and about 36 in 100 to A35. [DIR002]
- The spacing after A31 changed in opposite directions for the two activities: earlier, A35 typically followed A31 two positions later (about 60 in 100) while A36 typically followed immediately (about 60 in 100); in the later window, A35 typically follows A31 immediately (about 61 in 100) while A36 follows two positions later (about 61 in 100). [LAG001, LAG002]
- A32 now typically follows A35 two positions later (about 62 in 100) rather than immediately (about 58 in 100 earlier). [LAG003]
- Direct A36→A35 sequencing became less common: about 62 in 200 traces earlier versus about 34 in 200 in the later window, and where it still occurs it is almost always at a distance of one position. [LAG004]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that A35 and A36 exchanged their order in the sequence between A31 and A32. In the earlier window the dominant flow ran A31 → A36 → A35 → A32 (A36 immediately after A31, A35 next, A32 immediately after A35); in the later window it runs A31 → A35 → A36 → A32, with A35 immediately after A31, A36 next, and A32 two positions after A35. [DIR001, DIR002, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003]

Supporting signs: six control-flow items qualified where about 0.5 would be expected by chance among the 100 tested units, so this set of differences is unlikely for an unchanged process, and each item's difference is larger than in about 99 out of 100 random re-splits of the pooled traces. The two successor changes are exact mirror images of each other, and the three distance shifts move precisely as this reordering predicts, giving direct relational and positional support rather than a single isolated signal. [DIR001, DIR002, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004]

An important competing explanation the evidence cannot fully rule out: the reversal appears partial rather than complete, since direct A36→A35 sequences still occur in about 34 of 200 later-window traces. The evidence therefore shows a change in which of the two orders dominates, and cannot establish whether every case reversed or only a subset in which the new order dominates; distances are also measured in event positions, not clock time, and each window is a finite 200-trace sample. [LAG004]

Other differences: a few rare outgoing transitions from A35 (to A25 and A34) and from A36 (to A21, A30, A25 and A07) appear at about 1 in 100 each only in the later window; each rests on very few observations and may be sampling noise. [DIR001, DIR002]
