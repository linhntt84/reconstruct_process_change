### Observed differences

- Assignments of A01 were spread thinly in the earlier window: no resource held more than about 6 in 100 executions and roughly 47 in 100 went to a long tail of occasional handlers. In the later window a new group holds the largest shares — User_60 about 10 in 100, User_48 about 7, and several further resources about 4–7 each — while about five of the earlier handlers no longer appear at all [ASSIGN001].
- A07 shows the same kind of shift with a different cast: earlier, one resource held about 15 in 100 assignments and two others about 10 each; in the later window the largest shares belong to a largely new set of resources (about 13 and 12 in 100), the former leaders fall to about 6 in 100 or below, and three of them are no longer observed. A07 also occurs in more traces (about 144 of 200 earlier vs about 170 later) [ASSIGN002].
- A22 moves in step with A01: earlier the top share was about 9 in 100; in the later window User_60 leads with about 12 in 100 and the same resources that gained on A01 take most of the rest, while about seven earlier handlers are no longer observed. A22 appears in fewer traces (about 114 of 200 earlier vs about 91 later) [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN003].
- In the later window the elapsed time between consecutive completions of A04 and A01 is much longer: the median rises from about 20 to about 66 hours, the mean from about 26 to about 73 hours, and the middle half of the gaps moves from roughly 0–24 hours to roughly 17–93 hours [TWAIT001].
- Routing out of A12 shifted: of its observed outgoing transitions, the share leading back to A12 itself rises from about 49 to about 75 in 100, transitions to A13 fall from about 24 to about 5 in 100, and transitions to A17, A20 and A22 (about 2–7 in 100 each earlier) are no longer observed. This rests on only about 40 outgoing transitions per window [DIR001].

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that, in the later window, the execution of activities A01, A07 and A22 was reallocated to a largely different set of resources (with some overlap): for A01 and A22 a common group of resources, including User_60 and User_48, took over the largest shares from the previously most-used handlers, and A07 likewise moved from a few former leaders to a largely new set. The support is direct — the three indicators measure exactly how these activities are distributed over resources — and consistent, since all three show the earlier dominant resources losing share while new ones gain, with the same gaining group appearing for both A01 and A22. Chance is an unlikely explanation for this family as a whole: three resource items qualified where about 0.005 (roughly 1 in 200) would be expected if nothing had changed, and each difference exceeds about 999 of 1,000 random re-splits; for A01 the activity occurs in all 200 traces in both windows, so its shift is not an artifact of the activity appearing or disappearing [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003]. Limitations: the resource codes are anonymous, so nothing can be said about roles or teams, and for A07 and A22 the share of traces containing them also changed, so a shift in the case mix remains a competing explanation the evidence cannot fully rule out [ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003].

Other differences:
- The lengthening of the elapsed time between consecutive completions of A04 and A01 (median about 20 to about 66 hours) is consistent with a timing change, but it rests on a single time item (one qualified where about 0.015, roughly 3 in 200, would be expected without change), so a rare fluctuation cannot be ruled out [TWAIT001].
- The routing shift around A12 (more transitions back to A12 itself, fewer to A13) is based on about 40 outgoing transitions per window, and the control-flow family produced one item where about 0.17 (roughly 1 in 6) would be expected without change — a count an unchanged process typically produces — so it does not establish a structural change on its own [DIR001].
