- **A13 moved from widely shared execution to a single resource.** In the earlier window, A13's 269 recorded assignments were spread across 46 different resources: the busiest (User_49) handled about 7 in 100, and more than half of the work sat with a long tail of occasional performers (concentration index about 0.03). The number of assignments per window was similar, so the activity itself did not become rarer or more frequent. [ASSIGN001, CONC001]

Earlier, the largest single contributor held about 10 in 100 of A22's assignments and about half of the work was in a long tail; in the later window the work is still spread thinly (largest share about 8 in 100), but several resources seen earlier no longer appear and new ones take shares of roughly 5–7 in 100 each. ### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that resource allocation changed: in the later window, execution of A13, previously shared thinly across many resources with none above about 7 in 100 assignments, was performed entirely by a single resource (User_134), and A22's assignments moved to a partly different set of resources while remaining dispersed. The assignment profiles and concentration measures show this directly, and activity volumes were stable (269 vs 277 assignments for A13; 123 vs 118 for A22), so the shift is in who performs the work, not in how often the activities occur. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, CONC001]

Chance alone does not plausibly account for the pattern: the resource family produced three qualifying items against about 0.006 expected if nothing had changed, the time family one against about 0.014, and control flow none against about 0.19; each observed difference also exceeds all but about 1 in 2000 random re-splits of the pooled data. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, CONC001, TWAIT001]

Limitations: the two A13 items describe the same underlying behaviour, so they amount to one observation of that change rather than two independent confirmations. The evidence cannot rule out that the mix of cases containing A13 changed in a way the tested indicators do not capture (for example, A13 now arising only within one kind of case handled by a single resource), and the resource codes are anonymous, so no roles, people or teams can be inferred. [ASSIGN001, CONC001]

Other differences: the elapsed time between consecutive completion events for the pair A04–A01 increased — median from about 25 to about 42 hours and upper quartile from about 38 to about 66 hours, over 200 intervals per window; this rests on a single time item, is a timing hypothesis in its own right, and measures elapsed time between completions rather than pure waiting or processing time. [TWAIT001]
