### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, A14, A15, A20, A21 and A22 acted as a block of alternatives: each appeared in about 18–23 of 100 traces, together running exactly once per trace (their trace counts sum to 200 in the 200-trace window). A42 always handed over to one of them (about 18–23% each), and each of the five always continued to A23, so every trace ran A42, then one of the five, then A23, always two steps apart. - In the later window, A42 leads directly to A23 in all traces (one step apart), the five no longer continue to A23 in any observed transition, and their outgoing transitions instead split between A25 (about 37–68%) and A29 (about 32–63%). [DIR008, LAG004, DIR001–DIR005]

- The order between the block and A23/A24 reversed: earlier, in the roughly 18–23 of 100 traces where one of the profiled block activities (A14, A20, A21, A22) co-occurred with A23 or A24, it always ran before them; in the later window the co-occurrence rate is essentially unchanged (about 20–23 of 100) but A23/A24 always run first, never the reverse. [TWAIT001–TWAIT006]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the alternative block A14, A15, A20, A21, A22 — which executes exactly once per trace in both windows — was relocated: earlier it ran between A42 and A23, and in the later window it runs after A23 and A24, inserted between A24 and A25 or between A25 and A29 in roughly equal shares. This is a change of placement, not of participation, and it is shown directly by agreeing relational evidence: reversed order against A23/A24, flipped successor profiles of A42, the five sources, A24 and A25, a position shift of the five from about 0.31 to about 0.60, and the matching step-gap changes. The screening comparison makes sampling an implausible explanation for the family as a whole: 28 control-flow items qualified where about 0.43 would be expected without a change, and each item's difference exceeds about 995 of 1000 random re-splits. Limitations: positions and lags are event orders rather than clock times, and because lags are only observed within five steps, the reading that exactly one block activity is inserted per trace, and only in those two gaps, is inferred rather than directly observed; the evidence also cannot rule out changes to activities that were not profiled. [DIR001–DIR008, POS001–POS008, REL001–REL008, LAG001–LAG004]

Other differences: a second, separately supported difference is timing — the transitions from each of A03–A08 into A09 took about half as long between consecutive completions in the later window (about 1.0 hour down to about 0.5 hours on average, with a narrower spread), consistent across all six pairs, with 6 time items against roughly 0.008 expected by chance. This elapsed-time measure covers the whole interval between consecutive completions, not pure waiting or processing time; it rests on only 22–51 observations per pair per window, and these activities are disjoint from the relocated block, so the evidence does not tie this change to the relocation. [TWAIT001–TWAIT006]
