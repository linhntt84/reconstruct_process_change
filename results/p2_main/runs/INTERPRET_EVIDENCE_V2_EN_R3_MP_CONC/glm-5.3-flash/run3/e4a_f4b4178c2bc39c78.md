### Observed differences

- The three activities A25, A26 and A31 were each executed exactly once in every case in the earlier window (mean 1.0 occurrence per trace, no repeats); in the later window each runs about 3.5 times per case on average, with repeated executions in about 68 in 100 cases, while still appearing in every case. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003]
- The routing out of A31 changed: before, all of its observed outgoing transitions led to A20; in the later window about 71 in 100 lead back to A25 and only about 29 in 100 to A20. The number of observed outgoing transitions of A31 rose from 200 to about 697, matching the roughly 3.5-fold increase in its executions. [DIR001, OCC003]
- A25 and A31 still occur together in every case in both windows, but their fixed single pass (A25 before A31) held in all cases before and in only about 32 in 100 cases afterwards; in about 68 in 100 cases the pair is now interleaved or repeated. [REL001]
- The tight spacing between the two loosened: before, A31 followed A25 exactly two positions later in essentially every pair; afterwards that two-position gap covers only about 12 in 100 pairs, with some pairs five positions apart, and the relative positions of A25 and A31 within the trace became about twice as spread out (standard deviation roughly 0.06 to 0.12 for both) with only small shifts in their median positions. [LAG001, POS001, POS002]
- The elapsed time between the completions of A34 and A14 roughly doubled: median about 1.0 hour before versus about 2.0 hours after (means about 1.0 and 2.0 hours), with all quartiles shifted up by roughly 0.9–1.2 hours and a similar number of observations in both windows (93 vs 95). [TWAIT001]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that in the later window a block consisting of A25, A26 and A31 is repeated within cases. Previously each of the three activities ran exactly once per case in a fixed order (A25 before A31, two positions apart, with A31 always moving on to A20); afterwards they run about 3.5 times per case, repeats occur in about 68 in 100 cases, and A31 passes back to A25 in about 71 in 100 of its outgoing transitions — consistent with a loop over these activities that now usually iterates more than once. The three activities show identical occurrence profiles, suggesting they repeat together as a block. The signs are direct and mutually consistent: the occurrence and recurrence counts, the new dominant A31→A25 back edge, the shift from fixed order to interleaved/repeated execution, and the wider positional spread. The control-flow family produced 8 qualifying items against roughly 0.3 expected for an unchanged process (and the time family 1 against 0.01), so this volume of signals is far beyond what chance sampling produces, and each difference exceeds about 995 in 1,000 random re-splits of the pooled data. Limitations: most indicators describe the same traces and are not independent confirmations, and the evidence shows that repetition increased, not what brought it about. A competing explanation the evidence cannot fully exclude is that part of the "interleaved or repeated" behaviour reflects a different repeat structure (for example interleaved rather than strictly sequential repeats), although the dominant A31→A25 back edge points to sequential repetition. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, DIR001, REL001, LAG001, POS001, POS002]

Other differences: The elapsed time between the completions of A34 and A14 consistently doubled across the whole distribution, which is a timing hypothesis in its own right; it rests on a single indicator, and this evidence cannot show whether it is connected to the repetition change or has a separate origin. [TWAIT001]
