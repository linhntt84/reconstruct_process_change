### Observed differences

- A15 acts as a branching point whose successor choice flipped: in the earlier window about 82 in 100 of its outgoing transitions led to A24 and about 19 in 100 to A19; in the later window about 24 in 100 led to A24 and about 77 in 100 to A19. [DIR001]
- The two downstream groups moved in opposite directions: each of A16–A20 appeared in about 19 in 100 traces earlier and about 77 in 100 later, while each of A21–A25 appeared in about 82 in 100 earlier and about 24 in 100 later; no activity repeats within a trace in either window. [OCC001, OCC006]
- The two groups behave as alternatives rather than stages: they never occur in the same trace in either window. Earlier, A21–A25 typically ran without any of A16–A20 (about 82 in 100 traces); later, A16–A20 typically ran without any of A21–A25 (about 77 in 100). [REL015, REL019]
- A15's company changed accordingly: earlier it co-occurred with each of A21–A25 in about 82 in 100 traces, always before them; later it co-occurs with each of A16–A20 in about 77 in 100 traces, always before them. [REL001, REL006]
- A29 occurs in essentially every trace in both windows but switched companions: earlier it ran with A21–A25 (about 82 in 100, usually after A21 and partly interleaved); later it runs with A16–A20 (about 77 in 100, in mixed orders). [REL020, REL055]
- Within the now-dominant branch, ordering is partly fixed and partly free: A19 precedes A16, A17 and A18, and A16–A18 precede A20, in about 77 in 100 of their joint traces, while A16/A17 and A17/A18 appear in both orders in similar proportions (no fixed order between them). [REL011, REL013, REL014]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the routing shares at A15 between two existing alternative branches flipped: earlier, roughly 82 in 100 cases continued from A15 into the branch containing A21–A25 (entered via A24, together with A29), while later roughly 77 in 100 continued into the branch containing A16–A20 (entered via A19, together with A29). [DIR001, OCC001, OCC006, REL015, REL020]

Supporting signs: the successor redistribution at A15 is observed directly; the participation and relation items agree with it across all involved activities; both branches and every involved activity are observed in substantial shares in both windows (A16–A20 in about 37 of 200 earlier traces, A21–A25 in about 47 of 200 later traces), so this is a shift between existing alternatives rather than activities appearing or disappearing; and the screening expected roughly 0.84 items by chance while 80 control-flow items qualified, so chance cannot account for the collection as a whole. [DIR001, OCC001, OCC006, REL001, REL006, REL015]

Limitations: the evidence cannot distinguish a change in the branching decision at A15 from a shift in the mix of cases arriving with one of the two paths already determined, since both produce the same flip in shares; and because the two branches consist of different activities, the evidence shows the choice between them changed, not that identical work was rerouted. [DIR001, REL015]

Other differences: small redistributions in event-position gaps between some pairs (for example, A15→A17 gaps moved from mostly 3–4 steps to a flatter spread including 2, and A17→A29 gaps from mostly 2–3 steps towards 4–5); these are minor and consistent with the branch change rather than an independent change in placement. [LAG001, LAG003]
