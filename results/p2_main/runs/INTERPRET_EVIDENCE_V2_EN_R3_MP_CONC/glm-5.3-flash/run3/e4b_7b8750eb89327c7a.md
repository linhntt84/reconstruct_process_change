### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a change in how work was allocated to resources. In the earlier window, a stable group of resources — including User_13 — carried most executions of A01, A03, A13, A14, A17 and A22; in the later window that group's shares collapsed and a largely disjoint group took over, with User_85 roughly doubling its share on A13, A14 and A17. In addition, A08 moved from being shared by about 16 resources to being executed exclusively by User_115. Supporting signs: the resource family produced 8 items against about 0.004 expected without change — a count many times larger than chance can explain — the same rising and falling groups of resources recur across activities, A08's concentration is shown directly by two indicators, and every item rests on roughly 100–330 traces per window. Limitations: the evidence cannot rule out that the shift stems from a change in the case mix reaching these activities rather than from the assignment step itself, and the resource codes are anonymous, so no roles or groups beyond the observed shares can be inferred.
