### Observed differences

- Two mutually exclusive activity blocks swapped how often they run. In the earlier window the block A21/A22/A23 appeared in about 75 in 100 traces and the block A24/A25/A26 in about 25 in 100; in the later window the shares are reversed, with A21/A22/A23 at about 25 in 100 and A24/A25/A26 at about 76 in 100. Within each block the activities still always occur together, the two blocks still essentially never share a trace, and almost every trace contains exactly one of the two blocks in both windows. [OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, OCC007, OCC008, OCC009, REL025, REL027]

- The swap preserved each block's position. Earlier, A19 and A20 preceded A21–A23 in about 74 in 100 traces, and A21–A23 preceded A34 and A35 in about 72–74 in 100; later, A19 and A20 precede A24–A26 in about 75 in 100 traces, and A24–A26 precede A34 and A35 in about 73–76 in 100. A20's observed outgoing transitions shifted accordingly: A21/A22/A23 together fell from about 75% to about 24% of these transitions, while A24/A25/A26 rose from about 25% to about 75%. [REL013, REL016, REL019, REL022, REL032, REL033, REL047, REL048, REL053, REL054, DIR002]

- A06 changed separately: it appeared in about 17 in 100 traces earlier and about 53 in 100 later, and its position moved from a median of roughly half the trace to the final tenth (median relative position about 0.96). About 90% of A06's observed outgoing transitions now lead to A35 (about 12% earlier), A34 now leads to A06 in about 50% of its observed outgoing transitions (about 2% earlier), and the direct A34→A35 step within one position fell from 90% to 49% while a two-step gap rose to 51% — consistent with A06 running between A34 and A35 in about half of the later traces. [OCC002, POS001, DIR001, DIR005, LAG001, LAG013, REL012]

- Two low-frequency activities receded: A02 fell from about 17 to about 8 in 100 traces and A08 from about 14 to about 5 in 100; both rest on few cases (34→15 and 27→10 traces). [OCC001, OCC003]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that two alternative paths were taken with very different frequencies in the later window: the path through A21, A22 and A23, executed in about 75 in 100 traces earlier, was executed in about 25 in 100 later, while the alternative path through A24, A25 and A26 rose from about 25 to about 76 in 100. This reads as a change in how often existing paths are taken rather than activities being added, removed or relocated: all six activities occur in substantial shares in both windows, each block stays internally fixed (its members always co-occur), the blocks stay mutually exclusive, and both keep the same upstream company (A19, A20 and A29) and downstream activities (A34, A35). The mirrored participation changes of roughly 0.5 for all six activities, the flipped co-occurrence and ordering relations, and the redistribution of A20's outgoing transitions all point the same way. The main competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that two separate changes (one block becoming rarer, the other more common) produced this pattern rather than a single flipped routing share, and the evidence cannot show where the choice between the blocks is made. Chance sampling is implausible as an overall explanation, since 92 items qualified against roughly 1 expected for an unchanged process, of which the items cited here are the core. [OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, OCC007, OCC008, OCC009, REL013, REL016, REL025, REL027, REL032, REL053, REL054, DIR002]

Other differences: A06 became much more frequent (about 17 to about 53 in 100 traces) and now runs late in the trace, apparently between A34 and A35 in about half of the later traces; the relational and positional evidence supports this change in A06's frequency and placement, but it is reported separately because it is behaviour distinct from the path-frequency swap. A02 and A08 also receded slightly, on few observations. [OCC002, POS001, DIR001, DIR005, LAG001, LAG013, REL012, OCC001, OCC003]
