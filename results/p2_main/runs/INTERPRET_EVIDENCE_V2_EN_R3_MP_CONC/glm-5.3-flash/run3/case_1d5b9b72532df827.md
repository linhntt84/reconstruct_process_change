### Observed differences

- The same activities occur at similar shares in both windows, but two groups swapped their place in the case. In the earlier window, A20, A21, A22, A24 and A25 each sat on average around position 0.39–0.40 of the case, while A19, A23, A26, A27 and A28 sat around 0.69–0.70; in the later window, the first group sits around 0.64 and the second around 0.31–0.36 [POS002, POS003, POS004, POS006, POS007, POS001, POS005, POS008, POS009, POS010].

- The two activities between the groups kept their relative order but moved with the swap: A29 shifted from a mean position of about 0.49 to about 0.37, and A33 from about 0.67 to about 0.55, remaining between the two groups in both windows [POS011, POS012].

A23, A27 and A28, which previously followed A20–A25 (for example, A20 before A23 in 20 of 100 traces), now precede them (A23 before A20 in 19 of 100), and A27 and A28 now precede A29 and A33 instead of following them [REL004, REL005, REL009, REL014, REL021, REL025, REL001, REL002, REL003, REL011, REL012, REL013, REL016, REL019, REL020, REL023, REL024, REL027, REL028, REL029, REL030].

- Successors flipped at the boundaries of the swapped groups: A44 previously flowed into A20/A21/A22/A24/A25 (about 15–22% of its outgoing transitions each) and now into A19/A23/A26/A27/A28 (about 15–24% each), while A33 shows the mirror image, previously into A19/A23/A26–A28 (about 17–23% each) and now into A20–A22/A24/A25 (about 16–24% each) [DIR012, DIR011].

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that two blocks of activities swapped their execution order. In the earlier window a case typically ran A44, then the group A20/A21/A22/A24/A25, then A29 and A33, then one of A19/A23/A26–A28, ending in A34; in the later window the two middle blocks have exchanged places, so A44 leads into one of A19/A23/A26–A28, then come A29 and A33, then the A20–A25 group, and the case still ends in A34, with A29 and A33 keeping their relative order between the blocks [DIR012, DIR011, DIR001, DIR002, POS011, POS012, REL004, REL027, LAG010, LAG012].

The support is direct and consistent: screening tested 176 control-flow units with about 0.88 items expected by chance, yet 66 qualified, so chance re-splits cannot explain the findings as a whole; every cross-block activity pair reversed its order in the direction the swap predicts; all twelve position profiles moved as predicted; the successor profiles of A44 and A33 flipped between the groups; and stable participation shares rule out activities being added, removed or made optional as an explanation [DIR001–DIR012, POS001–POS012, REL001–REL030, LAG001–LAG012].

Competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out: the windows could sample two pre-existing process variants with different fixed orders, with the change being a shift in their mix rather than a reordering within one process — each window shows a single dominant order in about 98–100 of 100 traces, so the two explanations look alike here [REL004, REL014].

Other differences: a few low-frequency successor edges (about 1–3 in 100 outgoing transitions) appear or disappear around the swapped groups — for example, in the later window A19 also shows small flows to A38, A35 and A14 — and A19's position becomes more spread out (standard deviation about 0.06 to 0.11); both look like minor side-effects of the same reordering [DIR001, POS001].
