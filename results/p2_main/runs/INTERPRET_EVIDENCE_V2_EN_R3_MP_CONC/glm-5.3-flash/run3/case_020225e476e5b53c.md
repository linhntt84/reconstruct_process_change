### Observed differences

- The five activities A30, A31, A36, A37 and A38 each occurred exactly once in every before-window trace; in the later window each occurs about 3.1 times per trace on average, and about 66–68 in 100 traces contain repeats of each of them (before: essentially none). [OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006]
- Routing out of A38 changed: in the earlier window about 99 in 100 of its observed outgoing transitions went to A25; in the later window about 67 in 100 go back to A30 and only about 33 in 100 to A25 (613 outgoing transitions observed after versus 200 before, matching A38's higher frequency). [DIR001]
- The order among the five changed from a single fixed pass to interleaving: before, A30 preceded A31/A36/A37/A38 in essentially all traces, A31, A36 and A37 were mutually unordered (roughly 50/50 both orders), and A38 came last; after, all ten pairs show "interleaved or repeated" in about 66–68 in 100 traces, while the two activities in each pair still co-occur in every trace. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, REL007, REL008, REL009, REL010]
- Gaps between occurrences of A31, A36 and A37 stretched: before, consecutive occurrences were 1–2 event positions apart (about 65% and 35%); after, gaps of 1–2 positions are rare (about 11% and 6%) and gaps of 3–5 positions appear. Repeats of A37 itself, seen in only 1 before-trace, occur in 109 after-traces, typically 4–5 positions apart. [LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006, LAG007]
- The positions of the five activities within the trace spread out (e.g., A30's positional standard deviation rose from about 0.05 to about 0.14 of trace length), consistent with multiple occurrences rather than a uniform shift; these are event positions, not clock times. [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005]
- A04 became more common: it appears in about 9 of 200 before-traces (about 4.5%) versus about 29 of 200 after-traces (about 14.5%), and it shows up as a rare successor of A38 in the later window. [OCC001, DIR001]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that, in the later window, the sequence A30 → (A31, A36, A37 in no fixed order among themselves) → A38 is executed about three times per case instead of once: A38 now routes back to A30 in about two thirds of its outgoing transitions (previously it led on to A25 in about 99 in 100), each of the five activities repeats in about two of three traces, the pairs among them are interleaved or repeated rather than executed in a single pass, and consecutive occurrences are separated by 3–5 event positions. This rests on direct evidence — occurrence counts with full support (200 of 200 traces in both windows) and an observed successor change at A38 — and the screening context fits: 29 control-flow items qualified where roughly 0.6 would be expected for an unchanged process, about fifty times that expectation. [OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, DIR001, REL001, REL005, LAG001, LAG007]

Limitations: all items come from the control-flow family, and the REL, LAG and POS items largely restate the same underlying repetition, so they are not independent confirmations; the exact distribution of repeat counts per trace beyond the ~3.1 average is not established. A competing explanation the evidence cannot fully rule out is that the later window mixes two case populations — about one in three after-traces still run the block exactly once as before — rather than a single routing change applying to all cases. [OCC002, DIR001, REL001]

Other differences: A04's participation rose from about 4.5% to about 14.5% of traces and it appears as a rare successor of A38; with only 9 versus 29 supporting traces this is a weak, secondary observation and is not part of the main hypothesis. [OCC001, DIR001]
