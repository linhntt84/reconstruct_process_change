### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

Twelve control-flow items qualified, against roughly 0.17 expected by chance alone in an unchanged process, so the volume of findings is far beyond what random re-splits of these windows produce. The evidence supports the hypothesis that, in the later window, a new activity A01 is inserted between A12 and the A04/A13 branch within a segment that repeats inside traces: earlier, A12 led directly to A04 (about 52 in 100 of its outgoing transitions) or A13 (about 48 in 100), whereas in the later window A12 leads to A01 in essentially every observed case, and A01 in turn leads to A04 (about 53 in 100) or A13 (about 47 in 100). A01 appears in about 30 in 100 traces around 40 in 100 of the way through the trace and co-occurs with A12, A04 and A13 in every trace in which it appears, with interleaved or repeated orderings. The one-step lengthening of the A12-to-A04/A13 distances and of the self-repetition lags of A12, A04 and A13 fits a repeating block that is now one activity longer, and A04/A13 participation stayed near 50 in 100 traces in both windows, so the branch activities were neither added nor removed — this is an inserted step, not a replaced path. [OCC001, DIR001, DIR002, POS001, REL001, REL002, REL003, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG005]

An important competing explanation remains: because the activity codes are anonymous, the evidence cannot show whether A01 is genuinely new work or a re-labelled or newly distinguished portion of existing work; moreover, the trace-level orderings between A01 and A04/A13 are interleaved or repeated, so the exact internal sequence of the repeated block (for example, whether A01 precedes each branch activity in every cycle) is not established. [REL001, REL003]

Other differences: A12 itself runs somewhat less often in the later window — about 175 observed outgoing transitions before versus about 131 after, and present in about 33 versus 30 in 100 traces — a decline that the insertion alone does not explain. [DIR002, REL002]
