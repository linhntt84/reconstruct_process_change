### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that resource allocation changed for a set of activities: in the earlier window, work on A01, A03, A13, A14, A17 and A22 was spread broadly and evenly over a large pool of resources, whereas in the later window it concentrated on User_46 (its share roughly tripled to about 15–19 in 100 assignments per activity), User_39 dropped out of the most frequent assignees, and User_61 together with other previously uninvolved resources took on shares of roughly 3–9 in 100. This reads as work on all six activities shifting at once from one group of resources to another. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006]

Supporting signs: the resource family produced 6 qualifying items where about 0.006 would be expected without any change, far beyond what chance produces; the redistribution has the same direction at every affected activity; each difference exceeds about 999.5 of 1000 random re-splits; and the profiles rest on roughly 130–270 observations per window. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006]

Limitations: A13 and A14 show identical profiles, so they most likely mirror one underlying behaviour rather than confirming it independently; the evidence cannot show whether the shift reflects a reallocation of the work itself or a change in the mix of cases that happen to be handled by different resources, since no relational or control-flow evidence ties the resource shift to case mix; the resource codes are anonymous, so nothing beyond the observed shares can be said about roles or groups. [ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004]

Other differences: A21 was executed in about 35 of 200 traces before and about 15 of 200 after — participation roughly halved — but as the only control-flow item, against a chance expectation of about 0.17 in that family, it does not by itself establish a structural change and is not part of the main hypothesis; no timing indicators qualified.
