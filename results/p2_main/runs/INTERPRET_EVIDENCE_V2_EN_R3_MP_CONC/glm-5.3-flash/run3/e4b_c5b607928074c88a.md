### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a change in resource allocation: the activities A01, A03, A13, A14, A17 and A22, which in the earlier window were performed mainly by User_65 (about 8–13% each) together with a long tail of resources covering about half of the volume, are in the later window performed by a largely different set of resources led by User_69 (about 10–15% each), with User_78 among the few continuing performers. The hypothesis is direct (the assignment indicator measures allocation itself), consistent (the same resources leave and the same new ones appear across all six activities, so this is a coordinated shift between groups of resources rather than isolated noise), and the resource family produced 6 qualifying items where about 0.005 would be expected if nothing had changed, so chance cannot account for the pattern as a whole. Limitations: the resource codes are anonymous, so the evidence shows a shift between groups of codes without revealing what they represent, and a changed mix of cases between the windows cannot be ruled out as a contributing explanation. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006]

Other differences: a change in timing. The time family produced 2 items against about 0.016 expected without change, so together they exceed what an unchanged process typically produces: elapsed time between consecutive completion events shortened for A04 → A01 (median about 28 hours to about 19 hours, 200 intervals per window) and for A09 → A23 (median about 11 hours to about 1.3 hours, but only 17 vs 27 intervals, so weak on its own). Whether the shorter intervals are connected to the reallocation — A01 appears in both — cannot be determined from this evidence. No control-flow items qualified (0 against about 0.18 expected), so no structural change was detected alongside these. [TWAIT001, TWAIT002]
