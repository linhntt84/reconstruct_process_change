### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the earlier choice between two alternative segments — A33, A34, A35, A37 and A38 on one side, A39–A43 on the other — was replaced in the later window by both segments running in sequence: before, one of the two segments ran in essentially every case and they shared a case in only about 1–2 in 100, whereas now both run together in about 97–98 in 100 cases with the first group before the second in about 95 in 100 of them, the block bracketed by the always-present A14/A15/A30 before it and A31 and A51/A52 after it. The relational evidence shows this directly (cross-group co-occurrence up from about 1–2 in 100 to about 97–98 in 100 with a stable first-before-second order), the routing evidence matches it (A30 feeds only the first group; first-group-to-A31 edges replaced by first-group-to-second-group edges), participation, position and relation indicators all move the same way, and screening flagged 219 control-flow items against about 2 expected for an unchanged process, so the ensemble is not what chance produces. [OCC005, OCC010, REL063, REL079, REL105, DIR007, DIR008, DIR012, POS004, POS005]

Limitations: the order within each group remains unfixed, so the hypothesis is that two alternative groups became one always-executed, two-part block rather than a single fixed sequence, and the evidence cannot rule out that the later window simply contains a different mix of cases that always run both segments rather than a modified process. [REL059, REL114, REL122]
