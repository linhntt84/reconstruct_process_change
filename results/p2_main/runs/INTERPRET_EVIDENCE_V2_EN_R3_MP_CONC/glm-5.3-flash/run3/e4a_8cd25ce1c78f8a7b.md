### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a change in timing: in the later window, the elapsed time between the completion of each of the activities A03, A04, A05, A06, A07 and A08 and the subsequent completion of A09 roughly doubled, from about 1 hour per occurrence to about 2 hours. The sign is direct (the indicator measures exactly these elapsed times), the pattern is consistent across all six transitions into A09 (increases of about 0.9–1.1 hours with quartiles shifted upward in each), and it is unlikely to be a sampling artefact: the time family yielded 6 qualifying items against about 0.008 expected without change, and each difference exceeded about 1999 of 2000 reference re-splits. Control-flow screening found no qualifying items (0 of 46 units, about 0.23 expected by chance), so the timing change appears without a detectable structural counterpart at these thresholds. [TWAIT001, TWAIT002, TWAIT003, TWAIT004, TWAIT005, TWAIT006]

Limitations: TWAIT measures elapsed time between consecutive completion events, so the evidence cannot show whether the added hour is waiting, processing or handover time. All six items share A09 as the later activity, so they may reflect one underlying difference rather than six independent confirmations, and the evidence cannot localise the added time beyond the transitions ending at A09. A competing explanation that cannot be fully ruled out is a subtle change below the screening thresholds — for example, events occasionally intervening between these predecessors and A09, which would also fit the modest drops in consecutive-pair counts such as A04→A09 from 40 to 30. [TWAIT001, TWAIT002, TWAIT003, TWAIT004, TWAIT005, TWAIT006]

Other differences: apart from the timing shift, only the small, direction-inconsistent changes in how often the consecutive pairs occur (e.g., A03→A09 from 36 to 31, A05→A09 from 35 to 43), which remain within what sampling variation between the two windows produces; no control-flow difference passed the screening thresholds.
