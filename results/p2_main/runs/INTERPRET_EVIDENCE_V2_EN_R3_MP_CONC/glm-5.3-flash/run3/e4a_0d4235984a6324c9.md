### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a change in timing: in the later window, the elapsed time between the completion of A01 and the completion of A03 is about half of what it was in the earlier window (about 0.42 hours versus about 0.84 hours on average), while the two activities continue to follow one another at essentially the same rate. [TWAIT001]

Supporting signs: the indicator measures exactly this elapsed time; the shift is consistent across the distribution (quartiles, median and spread all decreased together, with no sign of an outlier-driven change); only about 1 in 2,000 random re-splits of the pooled data produced a difference this large; and the time family yielded 1 qualifying item where an unchanged process would yield about 0.009 — more than 100 times its chance expectation — so sampling variation alone is a poor explanation. Limitations and competing explanations: TWAIT is the elapsed time between consecutive completion events, so the evidence cannot show whether the half hour was saved in A01 finishing sooner, in A03 being taken up sooner after A01, or in waiting between the two completions; the profile also rests on a modest sample (34 and 35 observations). A change in the mix of trace contexts in which the pair occurs cannot be fully excluded, although control-flow screening found nothing (0 of 62 units, about 0.31 expected by chance). [TWAIT001]
