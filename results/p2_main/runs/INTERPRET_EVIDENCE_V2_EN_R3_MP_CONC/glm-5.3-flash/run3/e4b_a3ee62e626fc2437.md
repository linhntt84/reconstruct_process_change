### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that resource allocation for activities A13, A14, A17 and A22 changed: in the later window these activities are performed by a largely different and broader set of resources, with the earlier main handlers (including User_132, whose share drops on every affected activity) losing ground or disappearing from the top handlers, new handlers (for example User_18 on A22) appearing, and the unnamed remainder growing from about 45 to about 53–54 in 100 assignments on A13/A14/A17 and from about 30 to about 37 on A22. Supporting signs: the change is measured directly on the assignment indicator for all four activities, all four point in the same direction, each difference exceeds about 1,999 of 2,000 random re-splits, and 4 resource items against a chance expectation of about 0.004 cannot be explained by sampling variation as a whole. Limitations: A13 and A14 have identical profiles, so they likely reflect one shared allocation pattern rather than two independent confirmations; the resource codes are anonymous, so the evidence cannot show whether work was reassigned within the same population of resources or the set of active resources itself changed. A competing explanation the evidence cannot fully rule out is a shift in the case mix between the windows that happens to carry different resources; the similar activity volumes make this less likely but do not exclude it.

Other differences: none — no control-flow or timing items passed screening (0 of 39 and 0 of 31 units tested), so no structural or timing differences are indicated at these thresholds. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004]
