### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, A19, A20 and A21 were near-exclusive optional steps: A19 ran in about 35 in 100 traces, A20 in about 30 and A21 in about 36, while pairs of them almost never ran together (co-occurrence in roughly 1 in 200 traces), so almost every trace contained exactly one of the three. In the later window, each of them appears in only 1–2 of 200 traces. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, REL001, REL002, REL008]
- A26, A27 and A28 show the opposite flip: not observed at all in the earlier window, each runs in essentially every later-window trace (200 of 200), about once per trace. [OCC004, OCC005, OCC006]
- The handover from A44 changed: earlier, A44's outgoing transitions went mainly to A19 (about 31 in 100), A21 (about 30) and A20 (about 25); in the later window they go to A26 (about 31), A27 (about 27) and A28 (about 26). [DIR007]
- Ordering around this segment changed as well. Earlier, A44 preceded A19, A20 or A21 (in about 30–36 in 100 traces each), and each of the three preceded A29 in virtually all of its traces. In the later window, A44 precedes A26, A27 and A28 in about 98–99 in 100 traces, and each of the three precedes A29 in about 98–99 in 100. [REL007, REL013, REL018, REL006, REL012, REL017, REL022, REL025, REL027, REL021, REL024, REL026]
- The three new activities run together without a fixed internal order: A26, A27 and A28 co-occur in all later-window traces, with both orders in near-equal shares (for example, A26 before A27 in about 51 in 100 and A27 before A26 in about 49). [REL019, REL020, REL023]
- The span from A44 to A29 grew accordingly: earlier, A29 followed A44 within 2 steps in about 84 in 100 traces (3 steps in about 16); in the later window it follows within 4 steps in about 82 in 100 (5 steps in about 18). A29 still runs in every trace in both windows, but its mean position moved later, from about 0.40 to about 0.44 of the trace. [LAG001, POS007]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that, in the later window, the segment after A44 changed from running exactly one of the three near-exclusive activities A19, A20 or A21 — each of which was in turn followed by A29 — to running all three of A26, A27 and A28 together, in varying order, between A44 and A29. The signs are direct and consistent: participation flips from about 30–36 in 100 traces to almost none for A19/A20/A21, and from none to all traces for A26/A27/A28; A44's successors switch accordingly; the relational items place A44 before the new trio and the trio before A29, with mixed order inside the trio; and the A44→A29 distance grows from 2–3 to 4–5 steps, matching three activities now sitting between them. With 48 qualifying items against roughly 0.65 expected without a change, chance sampling cannot account for the pattern as a whole. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, DIR007, REL019, REL021, REL022, LAG001]

Limitations: the many agreeing items largely describe one underlying restructuring rather than independent confirmations, and the later-window profiles of A19, A20 and A21 rest on only 1–2 traces each, so residual old behaviour is barely documented. An important competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is a shift in the mix of case variants between the windows (for example, two coexisting variants exchanging dominance) rather than a modification of a single process, since only aggregate window behaviour is observed. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006]

Other differences: in the few later traces that still contain A19, A20 or A21, these activities lead to different successors than before (A19 to A10/A09, A20 to A45, A21 to A34), but each of these profiles is based on 1–2 observations and is weak. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003]
