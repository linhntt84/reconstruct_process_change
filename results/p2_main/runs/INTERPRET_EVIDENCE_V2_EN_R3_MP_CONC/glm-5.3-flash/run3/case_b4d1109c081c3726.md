### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the previously strict sequencing between two activity groups was replaced by a mixed, interleaving order. Before, every trace ran the early group — A20, A21, A22, A24 and A25 — before A26 and before A27–A30; in the later window A26 typically runs before A20, A21, A22 and A25 (about 73–93 of 100 traces depending on the pair), A27–A30 occur before and after the early group in roughly equal shares, and the block boundaries were rerouted: A25 now sends most of its transitions to A36 and A27–A30 instead of to A26, and A27–A30 gained transitions back into the early group. [REL001, REL005, REL010, REL018, REL002, REL004, REL013, DIR005, DIR006, DIR007]

The support is direct and consistent: all 22 relational items move in the same direction while co-occurrence stays at 100% in both windows, so every one of these activities still occurs in every trace and only their order and routing changed; the positional and distance items corroborate this, the transition profiles show the matching new edges, and 98 items qualified against fewer than 1 expected by chance in an unchanged process, far beyond what random re-splits produce. [REL002, REL018, POS006, LAG004, LAG028, DIR001, DIR005]

Limitations: the evidence shows that the fixed order gave way to mixed orders but cannot distinguish whether each case now interleaves the two groups freely or whether the later window combines two different fixed orders across cases; positions and distances are measured in event positions, not clock time, so no timing statement is implied; and a few distance items rest on very few observations (for example A24→A36, with 2 traces before and 9 after). [LAG027]
