### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a change in timing: in the later window, the elapsed time from the completion of the preceding activity to the completion of A32 was roughly halved — from about 1.0 hours to about 0.5 hours — both when the preceding completion was A11 and when it was A31, and the intervals became more uniform, with their spread roughly halving. Supporting signs: the change is measured directly by the completion-to-completion time indicator on substantial samples (105/111 and 40/36 intervals); both items show the same direction and nearly the same magnitude (about 0.48 and 0.50 hours), and each difference exceeded about 1,999 of 2,000 random re-splits. The time family produced 2 items where about 0.01 would be expected without change, so chance sampling cannot explain the pattern as a whole, and the absence of control-flow items (0 of 52 units) means no structural counterpart is indicated. A consistent change in these times is a timing hypothesis in its own right and does not require structural evidence. [TWAIT001, TWAIT002]

Limitations: TWAIT measures elapsed time between consecutive completion events, so the evidence cannot distinguish a genuinely shorter elapsed time from a changed composition of what happens between those completions; both items also share the endpoint A32, so they may describe one underlying difference rather than two independent confirmations.
