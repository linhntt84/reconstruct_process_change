### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility of a structural change in the segment between A44 and A29. In the earlier window, about one third of cases ran exactly one of the mutually exclusive alternatives A19, A20 or A21 there — A44 preceded each of them in about 30–36 in 100 shared traces, and each led to A29 in about 97–99 in 100 of its outgoing transitions. In the later window, these three activities were observed in almost no traces, while A26, A27 and A28 all run in every trace in that same segment: A44 precedes them in about 99 in 100 traces, they execute in no fixed order among themselves, and A29 follows them in about 99 in 100. The stretching of the A44→A29 distance from 2–3 to 4–5 steps matches the larger number of activities now running between them, and A45 and A46 now accompany the new trio instead of the old one. Supporting signs: the screening produced 74 qualifying items against fewer than 1 (0.82) expected by chance in the control-flow family, so the ensemble is not what an unchanged process typically yields; the participation flips are near-total (0–1 vs 200 of 200 traces), which a mere shift in the shares of existing paths could not produce, and the occurrence, direction, relation and lag indicators all describe the same reorganisation. Limitations: the later profiles for A19, A20 and A21 rest on a single observation each; positions and lags use event order rather than clock time, so whether A26, A27 and A28 run sequentially or concurrently cannot be determined; and window-level aggregates cannot rule out that the two windows sample different case mixes (for example two coexisting variants whose shares shifted) rather than a change within the same cases.
