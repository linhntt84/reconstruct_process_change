### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility of a reallocation of work across resources affecting five activities. In the earlier window, A01, A03, A13, A17 and A22 were each executed by a broad pool of resources, with the largest single code holding at most about 14 in 100 of an activity's events; in the later window, A13 was executed entirely by User_3, and for A01, A03, A17 and A22 User_91 took the largest share (about 11–19 in 100 events, up from about 5–7 in 100) while User_3 and several other earlier contributors lost share and new codes entered the top shares. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, CONC001]

The support rests on the resource family producing 6 flagged items where an unchanged process would produce about 0.005, a gap chance cannot explain; on every item exceeding essentially all 2000 reference re-splits; and on the assignment profile and the concentration measure showing the A13 shift directly (these two items describe the same underlying behaviour and are not independent confirmations). [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, CONC001]

Limitations: the resource codes are anonymous, so the evidence establishes reallocation between codes but not what drove it; it also cannot show whether the extreme concentration of A13 on User_3 and the milder shifts toward User_91 in the other four activities are one coordinated reallocation or separate per-activity changes, and the exact set of minor codes entering and leaving the top shares could partly reflect sampling noise in the two windows.
