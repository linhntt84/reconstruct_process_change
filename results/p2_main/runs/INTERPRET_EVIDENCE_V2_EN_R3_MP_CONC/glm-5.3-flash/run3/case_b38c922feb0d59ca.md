### Observed differences

- The fixed order between two groups of activities was lost. Before, A20, A21, A22, A24 and A25 ran before A26, A27, A28, A29 and A30 in almost every trace (e.g., A20 before A26 in about 98 in 100 traces, A24 before A28 in about 99 in 100), and A25 was immediately followed by A26 in essentially all 200 traces. In the later window, A26 precedes A20/A21/A22 in about 72–74 in 100 traces and precedes A25 in about 93 in 100; A28–A30 versus A20–A22 now appear in both orders in similar proportions (about 43–53 in 100 for the reversed order); A24 still usually precedes A28–A30 (about 76–79 in 100) but with about 21–22 in 100 reversed. Both groups still co-occur in every trace in both windows, so participation did not change — only the order did. [REL001-REL021, LAG027]
- Event positions shifted accordingly: in the later window A26–A30 occur earlier in the trace (mean relative position, e.g., A26 from about 0.63 to about 0.55, A28–A30 from about 0.69 to about 0.62–0.63), while A20–A25 occur later (A25 from about 0.61 to about 0.70, A20–A22 from about 0.56 to about 0.62). [POS001-POS010]
- Outgoing transitions redistributed across the two groups. Before, A20–A24 led almost only within their own group, and A25 led to A26 in about 99.5 in 100 observed transitions. In the later window, roughly 26–45 in 100 of A20–A24's outgoing transitions go to A26–A30, A25's most frequent target is now A36 (about 39 in 100, with A26 down to about 7 in 100), and A26–A30 send about 30–48 in 100 of their transitions back to A20–A25 (A26 to A24 about 12 in 100). [DIR001-DIR011]
- Adjacencies changed: the A25→A26 succession within five steps fell from 200 to 14 traces and A24→A25 from 200 to 50; successions observed in only about 1 of 200 traces before are now common — A26→A24 (92 traces), A27→A25 (95), A28→A25 (110), A29→A25 (89), A30→A25 (106), A25→A36 (187); A26→A36 within five steps fell from 181 to 12 traces, and A27→A36 in one step from about 90 to about 47 in 100. Distances within each group also spread from 1–2 steps to 1–5 steps (e.g., A20→A21). [LAG001, LAG025, LAG027, LAG032-LAG035, LAG040-LAG043, LAG048, LAG052]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the strict ordering constraint between two groups of activities was dropped and partly reversed: the block A26, A27, A28, A29, A30, which previously always executed after the block A20, A21, A22, A24, A25 (with A25 handing over directly to A26), now typically executes earlier in the trace and often before or interleaved with it. A25 and A26 have exchanged their relative order (A26 before A25 in about 93 in 100 later-window traces versus about 98 in 100 the other way before), A26 now typically precedes A20–A22, while most pairs between A28–A30 and A20–A22/A24 changed from a fixed order to a mixed order rather than a clean reversal. [REL001-REL021, POS001-POS010, LAG027]

The order change is shown directly by the relational indicator on all 21 cross-group pairs, each backed by 200 co-occurrence traces per window with co-occurrence unchanged at 100 in 100, and all items point in the same direction; the positional shifts and the new and lost successions corroborate the same reordering. [REL001-REL021, POS001-POS010, DIR001-DIR011, LAG027, LAG032-LAG035, LAG040-LAG043]

The volume of qualifying evidence also far exceeds what a stable process produces: 102 items passed the control-flow screening against roughly 1 expected without a change, so the pattern as a whole is not a sampling artefact — though the items largely describe one underlying reordering rather than independent confirmations. [screening]

Limitation: the evidence establishes that the fixed order was lost and partly reversed, but not which concrete new structure replaced it — the later window could reflect one new sequence, a mixture of traces still following the old order and traces following a new one, or interleaved execution with repetitions; POS and LAG are based on event positions, so no claim about clock-time timing is supported. [REL001-REL021, POS001-POS010]

Other differences: A35 now splits its outgoing transitions between A24 (about 53 in 100) and A26 (about 46 in 100) where it previously went to A24 exclusively, and its distances to A20, A21, A22 and A24 shifted toward larger gaps [DIR011, LAG057-LAG060]. A36 is now reached mainly from A25 (about 39 in 100 of A25's outgoing transitions, versus under 1 in 100 before) while immediate A27→A36 handovers fell from about 90 to about 47 in 100 and A26→A36 within five steps from 181 to 12 traces [DIR005, DIR007, LAG032, LAG040, LAG042].
