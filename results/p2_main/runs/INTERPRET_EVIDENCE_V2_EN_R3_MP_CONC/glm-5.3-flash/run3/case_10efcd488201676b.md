### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

One possibility supported by the evidence is a structural restructuring of the control flow. In the earlier window, A01 fed A09 in about two thirds of traces, A17 closed every trace next to A10, A06/A16 (together with A14/A15) formed an optional repeating cluster in about 40 in 100 traces, and A02 ran about twice per trace. In the later window, A01 and A17 are no longer observed, A07 appears between A03 and A04, A02 repeats about nine times per trace cycling with A11, A12, A14 and A15 (fed by A13), A14 and A15 repeat as a pair, and traces close with A04 leading to A10 and then to A06 and A16, each occurring exactly once, adjacent and in either order. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, OCC007, OCC008, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006, DIR007, DIR009, DIR010, DIR011, POS004, POS011, POS012, REL031, REL045]

The support is direct and consistent: successor profiles, co-occurrence/order shares and positions change exactly where participation and repetition change, and the screening produced 171 qualifying control-flow items against roughly 1 expected by chance for 188 tested units, so the differences as a whole cannot be explained by sampling noise. The main competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that the two 200-trace windows sample different mixes of case variants of an unchanged process rather than one redesigned behaviour; moreover, many items re-express the same trace-level changes and are not independent confirmations, and POS/LAG reflect event positions rather than clock time. [DIR005, DIR007, OCC003, OCC007, POS004]

Other differences: on top of this restructuring, A10 and A04 now sit somewhat earlier in the trace (mean relative position about 0.95 to 0.81 and 0.80 to 0.72) while A09, A11 and A12 sit somewhat later (for example A12 from about 0.50 to 0.56), and the gaps between A02's own repetitions flattened from about 2 and 4 steps to a mix of about 2 to 5 steps; these shifts are consistent with the structural changes above rather than separate moves.
