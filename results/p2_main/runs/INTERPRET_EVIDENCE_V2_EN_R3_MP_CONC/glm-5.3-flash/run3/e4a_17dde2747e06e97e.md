### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, the three activities A14, A15 and A20 were executed in every trace (participation 100%, once per trace, never repeated). In the later window, each appears in only about 53 in 100 traces. They move as a block: in about 47 in 100 later traces none of the three occurs, and traces containing only one or two of them are not observed. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, REL001, REL002, REL005]
- A36 and A21 still occur in every trace in both windows. In the earlier window, all of A36's observed outgoing transitions led to A14; in the later window, about 53 in 100 still lead to A14, while about 47 in 100 lead directly to A21. [DIR001, REL003, REL004, REL006, REL007, REL008, REL009]
- The distance from A36 to A21 was always four steps in the earlier window; in the later window it is one step in about 47 in 100 cases and four steps in the remaining about 53 in 100. [LAG001]
- When the block A14, A15, A20 does run in the later window, the order is unchanged: A36 before A14 before A20 before A15 before A21, with no reversed or interleaved cases in either window. [REL001, REL002, REL004, REL005, REL008, REL009, LAG001]

- Minor positional shifts: A21 sits slightly earlier in the trace (mean relative position about 0.43 vs 0.40) and A36 slightly later (about 0.30 vs 0.32). [POS001, POS002]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that, in the later window, the segment A14 → A20 → A15 between A36 and A21 is skipped in about 47 in 100 cases, whereas it was executed in every case in the earlier window; when skipped, A36 leads directly to A21 (one step apart instead of four), and when executed, the sequence A36 → A14 → A20 → A15 → A21 is unchanged. The support is direct and consistent: the three block activities drop from full participation to about 53 in 100 traces and always appear or disappear together, A36's routing splits exactly along the executed/skipped split, and A21 and A36 remain in all traces throughout. The control-flow family produced 16 qualifying items against roughly 0.35 expected if nothing had changed (and the time family 2 against 0.01), with each item's difference exceeding about 99.5 in 100 random re-splits, so the pattern as a whole is not what a stable process typically produces. A competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that the skip path already existed as a rare variant in the earlier window (it was not observed in 200 traces) and only its share rose; the evidence also cannot indicate why cases skip the block.

Other differences: Two timing changes stand on their own and are not explained by the structural pattern: the elapsed time between consecutive completions of A23 and A24 roughly halved (about 1.0 h to about 0.5 h), and the elapsed time between completions of A36 and A14 roughly doubled (about 1.0 h to about 2.0 h) in the 106 later traces containing that pair; whether these shifts are connected to the block being skipped cannot be determined from the evidence.
