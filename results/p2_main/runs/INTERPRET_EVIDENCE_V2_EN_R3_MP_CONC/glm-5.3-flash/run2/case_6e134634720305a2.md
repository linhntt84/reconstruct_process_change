### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the cycle through A30, A31 and A36, which previously ran about three to four times per trace, is now executed only once per trace: A30, A31 and A36 each occur exactly once, in the fixed order A30 then A36 two positions apart, and A36 now exits to A25 instead of returning to A30, which is what previously closed the loop. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, DIR002, REL001, LAG001]

Supporting signs: eleven control-flow items qualified against roughly 0.47 expected for an unchanged process, so the set as a whole is not a chance artefact of the two samples; the occurrence, direction, relation and lag indicators all describe one and the same underlying difference — repetition stopped and the exit from A36 was rerouted — and the unchanged participation of all three activities in 100 in 100 of traces rules out their removal from the process. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, DIR002, REL001, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003]

Limitations: the evidence does not show which activities lie between A31 and A36, so the exact scope of the former cycle is inferred rather than observed, and it cannot distinguish a cycle that still exists but now runs at most once from a restructuring that placed the three activities once in the main flow. The near-zero after counts in LAG002 and LAG003 rest on 1–2 observations and serve only as corroboration of what the occurrence indicator shows directly. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, LAG002, LAG003]

Other differences: only a minor reshuffling among A30's rare outgoing edges, a few of which appear or disappear at about the 0.5-in-100 level, with no practical change to its dominant edge to A31. [DIR001]
