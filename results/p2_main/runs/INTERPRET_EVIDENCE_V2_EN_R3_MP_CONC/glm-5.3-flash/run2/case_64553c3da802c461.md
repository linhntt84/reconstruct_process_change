### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, A22 appeared in about 49 of 100 traces; in the later window it appears in about 99 of 100 (mean occurrences per trace 0.49 → 0.99). [OCC001]
- Five activities — A27, A28, A33, A34 and A35 — each appeared in about 51 of 100 traces before, but in only about 2 of 200 traces in the later window (participation ~0.51 → ~0.01). [OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006]
- In the earlier window, A22 and each of these five were mutually exclusive alternatives: they never occurred in the same trace (A22-only ~49%, other-only ~51%). In the later window, A22 is present and the others absent in about 99 of 100 traces. [REL008, REL009, REL010, REL011, REL012]
- In the earlier window, the five typically occurred together (about 51 of 100 traces) in a mixed order among themselves, after A21 (about 51 of 100 traces) and before A23 (about 51 of 100 traces). In the later window they co-occur with A21, with A23 or with each other in only about 1 of 100 traces. [REL002–REL006, REL013–REL017, REL018–REL027]
- From A21, the share of observed outgoing transitions going to A22 rose from about 49% to about 99%, while transitions into the five activities fell from roughly 8–12% each to about 0.5% or became unobserved. [DIR001]
- Relative positions shifted only slightly: A21's mean position moved from 0.46 to 0.49 and A23's from 0.59 to 0.56, moving the two slightly toward each other. [POS001, POS002]
- The after-window profiles for the five activities (their outgoing transitions, positions and lags) rest on only 1–2 traces, so their internal ordering in the later window is not reliably observed. [DIR002–DIR006, LAG001, LAG018]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the middle segment between A21 and A23 running through A27, A28, A33, A34 and A35 is (nearly) no longer executed in the later window, while the alternative segment via A22 — previously taken in about half of the traces — is now taken in almost every trace. Earlier, traces typically ran A21 → (either A22 alone, or the five-activity block in mixed internal order) → A23, with the two segments mutually exclusive; in the later window, about 99 in 100 traces show A21 → A22 → A23, and the five activities appear in only about 2 of 200 traces. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC006, REL001, REL007, REL008, REL018]

Supporting signs: the participation changes are large on 200-trace windows (98 → 198 and 102 → 2 traces), the relational items show the before-state directly (mutual exclusivity of A22 and the block; the block sitting between A21 and A23) and its after-state, the successor profile of A21 shifted accordingly, and the screening produced 62 control-flow items against roughly 0.62 expected without a change — far beyond what a stable process typically yields. [OCC001, OCC002, REL008, REL013, REL018, DIR001]

Limitations: the evidence cannot distinguish whether the block was removed from the process or merely not chosen (zero participation only means it was not observed), and a shift in the mix of cases reaching the process — the two windows capturing different variant populations — is a competing explanation it cannot rule out; the after-window ordering details for the block rest on 1–2 traces. [OCC006, DIR002, LAG001]

Other differences: the slight relative-position shifts of A21 (0.46 → 0.49) and A23 (0.59 → 0.56) are consistent with the shortened traces rather than an independent relocation, and the LAG changes mirror the block's disappearance while resting on 1–2 after-window observations. [POS001, POS002, LAG001, LAG005]
