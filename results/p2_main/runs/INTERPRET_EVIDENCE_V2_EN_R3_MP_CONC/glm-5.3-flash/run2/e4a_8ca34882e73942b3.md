### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a change in timing: in the later window, the elapsed time between consecutive completions leading into A28 is roughly half of what it was, for both observed predecessors — after A21 (about 0.9 down to about 0.45 hours) and after A29 (about 0.18 down to about 0.09 hours) — and both gaps became less variable. Supporting signs: both items measure this elapsed time directly, agree in direction and rough magnitude, rest on about 100 observations per window each, and the number of time items (2) is far above the roughly 0.01 expected by chance, while no control-flow differences were flagged. Limitations: TWAIT is the elapsed time between consecutive completion events, so the evidence cannot show whether the reduction lies in waiting, service or handover time; and because both measured gaps end at A28, the evidence cannot localise the change to A28's completion timing as opposed to that of the preceding activities. A competing explanation the evidence cannot fully rule out is an undetected change in which events are adjacent, so that shorter gaps would reflect different pairings rather than faster execution; control-flow screening found nothing, but a subtle reordering cannot be excluded.
