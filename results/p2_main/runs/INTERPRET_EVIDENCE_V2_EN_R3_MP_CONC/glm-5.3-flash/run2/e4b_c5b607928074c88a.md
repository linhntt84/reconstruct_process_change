### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The volume of qualifying evidence is far beyond what an unchanged process would produce: the resource family yielded 6 qualifying items against about 0.005 expected by chance, and the time family 2 against about 0.016, while the control-flow family yielded none (0 against about 0.18 expected). [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, TWAIT001, TWAIT002]

The evidence supports the possibility that the work on A01, A03, A13, A14, A17 and A22 was reassigned to a largely different set of resources: before, User_65 was the single most frequent assignee for all six (about 8–13%), whereas in the later window User_69 holds the largest share of each (about 10–15%) and the remaining work is distributed over mostly new resources at about 4–7% each, with User_78 continuing at about 5%. The change is direct (it appears in the assignment mix itself), consistent (all six activities shift in the same direction, with the same resources leaving and entering), and the family-level item count rules out chance as a whole. An important competing explanation remains: a changed mix of cases in the later window, rather than a changed allocation rule, could produce the new assignment profiles, and the anonymous codes prevent any check of roles or continuity beyond the shares shown. Other differences: a timing change stands alongside the reallocation and involves different activity pairs — gaps between consecutive completions of A04 and A01 fell by roughly a third at the median (about 28 to about 19.5 hours) with much lower spread, and gaps between A09 and A23 fell from about 11 to about 1.3 hours on a small base (17 versus 27 gaps).
