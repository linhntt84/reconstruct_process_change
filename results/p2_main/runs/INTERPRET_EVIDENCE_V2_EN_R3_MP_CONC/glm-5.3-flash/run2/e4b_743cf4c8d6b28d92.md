### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the allocation of work to resources changed, with control flow and timing showing no qualifying differences at the configured thresholds. In A01, A03, A17 and A22, work shifted away from a group of previously active resources (including User_37) toward User_91 and several resources that previously handled little or none of this work; in A13, allocation collapsed from about 45 resources to User_3 alone, who handled all occurrences in the later window. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, CONC001]

Supporting signs: the resources family produced six qualifying items where about 0.005 would be expected by chance, a count chance cannot plausibly explain; the indicators measure allocation directly; the direction of change is consistent across all five activities; and A13's concentration is directly confirmed by the concentration index (45 distinct resources down to 1, top share from about 8 in 100 to 100%) on roughly 270 and 260 observations per window. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, CONC001]

Limitations: ASSIGN003 and CONC001 describe the same A13 behaviour and do not independently confirm each other; A22 rests on the smallest support (about 110–120 observations per window, though still substantial). For the four moderate redistributions, the evidence cannot distinguish an activity-specific reassignment from a general shift in how active particular resources are across the whole process, since only per-activity shares are observed; A13's collapse to a single resource is not explained by such a pool-wide shift. Resource codes are anonymous, so no roles, people or reasons can be inferred. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, CONC001]
