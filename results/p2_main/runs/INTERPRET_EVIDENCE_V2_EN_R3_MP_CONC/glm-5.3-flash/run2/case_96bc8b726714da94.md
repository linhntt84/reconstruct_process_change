### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, A26 and A28 ran in every trace, and between them each trace took exactly one of two paths: the single activity A27 (about 50 in 100 traces) or a group of five activities — A32, A33, A38, A39 and A40 — that ran together in about 51 in 100 traces, with no fixed order among them (each pair of the five appears in both orders in similar proportions). A27 and this group were mutually exclusive, co-occurring in at most about 1 in 100 traces. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC006, REL007, REL008, REL018, REL022, REL027]
- In the later window, A27 runs in about 99 in 100 traces (up from about 50 in 100), once per trace, placed between A26 and A28: A26 precedes A27 in about 99 in 100 traces, and A27 precedes A28 in about 99 in 100. [OCC001, REL001, REL007]
- In the later window, the five activities have nearly disappeared: each appears in only about 1–3 in 100 traces (3–5 of 200), down from about 51 in 100; each now occurs almost only without A26 and without A28, and pairs of them co-occur in about 1 in 100 traces, down from about 51 in 100. [OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, REL002, REL013, REL018]
- Routing out of A26 changed accordingly: earlier, about 48 in 100 of A26's observed outgoing transitions went to A27 and about half went to the five activities (roughly 7–12 in 100 each); in the later window, about 96 in 100 go to A27. [DIR001]
- A smaller positional shift: A26's mean relative position moved slightly later (about 0.46 to 0.49 of the trace) and A28's slightly earlier (about 0.58 to 0.56), consistent with a shorter middle section; these are event-position, not clock-time, measures. [POS001, POS002]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the branch running the five activities A32, A33, A38, A39 and A40 between A26 and A28 — previously taken in about half of the traces — is now almost never executed, while the alternative single-activity path through A27 is taken in nearly every trace; A26 and A28 continue to bracket this section in both windows. This is a change in participation and routing (which existing path runs), not a repositioning of A26 or A28.

Supporting signs: the participation flip is large and two-sided (A27 about 50 to 99 in 100 traces; each of the five about 51 to 1–3 in 100) [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006]; the relational items consistently flip from mutual exclusivity between A27 and the five to near-universal co-occurrence of A26–A27–A28 [REL001, REL007, REL008, REL013, REL018]; and A26's outgoing flow shifts from roughly half going to A27 to about 96 in 100 [DIR001]. The volume of qualifying items (63 against roughly 0.8 expected without change, all in the control-flow family) makes a chance explanation of the pattern as a whole implausible [screening], and every item's difference exceeds about 995 in 1000 random re-splits of the pooled data [OCC001, DIR001, REL001].

Limitations: the later window still contains 3–5 traces with the five activities, so the evidence shows the branch became rare rather than proving it was removed entirely; what happens inside those rare traces cannot be characterised, because the later outgoing profiles for A32–A40 rest on only 3–5 transitions [DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006]. Several indicators re-express the same trace-level switch, so their agreement is not independent confirmation. The evidence also cannot show whether the skipped branch's work was taken over by other activities.

Other differences: the slight positional shifts of A26 and A28 noted above [POS001, POS002]; the spacing between A26/A28 and the five activities, and among the five themselves, also changed in the later window, but those profiles rest on only 1–3 observations and are weak [LAG001, LAG005, LAG010, LAG017].
