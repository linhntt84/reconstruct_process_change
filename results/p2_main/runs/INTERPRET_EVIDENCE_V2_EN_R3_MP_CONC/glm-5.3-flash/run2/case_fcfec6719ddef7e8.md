### Observed differences

- **The order between two groups of activities flipped.** In the earlier window, A26 typically ran *before* A20, A21, A22 and A25 (for example, A26 before A20 in about 74 in 100 traces and after it in about 23; A26 before A25 in about 95 in 100), and pairs such as A20–A28 or A25–A27 appeared in both orders in similar shares. In the later window, every tested cross-group pair runs one way: A20, A21, A22, A24 and A25 precede A26, A27, A28, A29 and A30 in about 96–100 in 100 traces (A20 before A26: about 99 in 100; A25 before A26: about 98 in 100). Both activities of every pair co-occur in all traces in both windows, so presence did not change — only the order. [REL001–REL022]

- **Sequencing became tight and layered.** In the later window, A21 follows A20 within 1–2 events in essentially all observed cases, whereas before, about 39 in 100 of these pairs were 3–5 events apart [LAG001]; A25 follows A24 exactly four events later in all later traces, where before the gap was four or five events (about 50 observed traces) [LAG025]; A26 immediately follows A25 in every later trace, an ordered pair seen only about 7 times in 200 earlier traces [LAG027]. Distances from the first group to A28, A29 and A30 stretched: A20 reaches A30 four to five events later in about 87 in 100 later cases versus a flat spread of 1–5 before [LAG005–LAG007], and the same holds from A21 and A22 to A28–A30 [LAG012–LAG014, LAG019–LAG021].

- **Successor sets no longer cross between the groups.** Earlier, roughly 38–48 in 100 of the outgoing transitions of each of A20, A21 and A22 went to A26–A30; in the later window A20 leads only to A21, A22 and A25 (about 36/32/32 in 100), and A21 and A22 similarly lead only within their own group [DIR001–DIR003]. A25 now leads almost exclusively to A26 (98 in 100) versus a largest earlier share of about 40 in 100 to A36 [DIR004]. A26 leads only to A28, A29 and A30 [DIR005]; A28, A29 and A30 lead only within their own group [DIR007–DIR009]; A27 leads mostly to A36 (about 77 in 100, up from about 42) [DIR006].

- **Positions shifted accordingly.** A20–A25 now occur earlier in the trace (A25's mean relative position from about 0.70 to 0.60; A20 from 0.61 to 0.55), while A26–A30 occur later (A26 from 0.53 to 0.63; A30 from 0.61 to 0.68); each of these activities still appears in essentially all traces in both windows [POS001–POS010].

- **Reverse-order pairs almost disappeared.** Ordered pairs running from the second group back to the first (A26→A25, A27→A22, A28→A22, A28→A25, A29→A25, A30→A25) were observed in about 45–110 of 200 earlier traces but in only 1–3 later traces [LAG033, LAG039, LAG041, LAG042, LAG046, LAG050].

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the relative execution order of two groups of activities changed: the group A20, A21, A22, A24 and A25, which earlier ran after A26 or interleaved with A26–A30 without a fixed order, now consistently precedes the group A26, A27, A28, A29 and A30, with A25→A26 as the connecting step (A24 reaches A25 after four events, and A26 immediately follows A25); within each group the activities now follow one another closely (A20/A21/A22 within 1–2 events; A26→A28/A29/A30→A27 within about 1–4 events) [REL001–REL022, LAG001–LAG058].

Supporting signs: the relational evidence is direct and unanimous — all 22 cross-group pairs keep 100 in 100 co-occurrence in both windows while their order shares flip from mixed or reversed to about 96–100 in 100 forward, which is a change in ordering constraints rather than in participation [REL001–REL022]; the lag, successor and position indicators agree with this picture from three further angles [LAG001–LAG058, DIR001–DIR010, POS001–POS010]. The volume of qualifying items also far exceeds what an unchanged process produces: about 100 control-flow items against roughly 1 expected by chance [screening].

Limitations: the positional and lag indicators measure event positions, not clock time, so this is a reordering of sequence, not evidence about timing; and the evidence cannot rule out that the earlier mixed orders came from a mixture of case variants whose proportions shifted between the windows, rather than from a single process whose ordering constraint was tightened. A few pair-level lag items rest on only 1–3 later observations and are weak detail [LAG032, LAG033, LAG039, LAG041, LAG042, LAG046, LAG050].

Other differences: A35's outgoing traffic moved from an even split between A26 and A24 to almost exclusively A24 (about 100 in 100), and A27→A36 became A27's dominant successor (about 77 in 100 versus about 42); both are consistent with the same reordering but are not independently established as separate changes [DIR010, LAG058, DIR006, LAG040].
