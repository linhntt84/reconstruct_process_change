### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that resource allocation changed: for activities A01, A13, A14, A17 and A22, a group of resources that executed roughly a quarter to two-fifths of the assignments in the earlier window (including User_56) withdrew from this work in the later window, and a largely disjoint group (including User_3) took over a comparable share, while a few resources such as User_60 continued largely unchanged. This rests on five assignment profiles that all shift in the same direction, each stronger than all but about 1 in 2,000 random re-splits, and on a resource-family count (5 items) many times the chance expectation (about 0.005), which an unchanged process would rarely produce; stable activity volumes indicate the change is in who performed the work rather than in how often it was performed. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005]

The evidence cannot show whether the handover applied to all cases or only to a changed subset of cases (for example a different case mix being routed to different resources), because the profiles aggregate whole windows and no control-flow differences were flagged; resource codes are anonymous, so no roles or teams can be inferred. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005]

Other differences: The elapsed time between consecutive completions of A04 and A01 fell markedly (median about 67 hours to about 28 hours). As the only time item — against roughly 0.013 expected by chance in a stable process — it does not on its own establish a timing change, so it is kept outside the main hypothesis. [TWAIT001]
