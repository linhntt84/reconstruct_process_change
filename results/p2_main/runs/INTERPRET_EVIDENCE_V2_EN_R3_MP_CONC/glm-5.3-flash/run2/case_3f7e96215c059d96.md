### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, every one of the 200 traces contained the same middle segment between A25 and A26: A28, A29, A30, A32 and A33 (group 1) followed by A34–A38 (group 2). Each of these ten activities appeared in 100 in 100 traces, group 1 always preceded group 2, and the order inside each group was unfixed (roughly 50/50 both ways). In the later window, group-1 activities appear in only about 46 in 100 traces and group-2 activities in about 55 in 100. [OCC001, OCC005, OCC006, OCC010, REL021, REL025, REL056]

- In the later window the two groups became near-exclusive alternatives: pairs spanning the two groups co-occur in only about 1 in 200 traces (against 200 in 200 earlier), while within each group the activities still always appear together and still in no fixed order. About 46 in 100 later traces contain A25 and A26 with only group-1 activities, and about 54 in 100 with only group-2 activities. [REL001, REL006, REL011, REL016, REL025, REL056]

- Routing changed accordingly in the later window: A28, A29 and A33 now lead directly to A26 in roughly 22–26 in 100 of their outgoing transitions, edges not observed earlier, and A25's outgoing transitions now spread over both groups (about 54 in 100 into group 2, where earlier they went only to group 1); the earlier small transition shares from group-1 activities into group-2 activities are no longer observed. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004]

- Event positions shifted consistently with a shorter middle segment: A25's mean relative position rose from about 0.55 to about 0.64, group-1 activities occur relatively later when they occur (mean about 0.63 to 0.72) and group-2 activities slightly earlier (about 0.76 to 0.73); these are event-position, not clock-time, observations. [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS008]

- The lag items show degenerate after-window distributions, but each rests on a single later observation, so they add little beyond the co-occurrence collapse already described. [LAG001, LAG009]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the ten-activity segment between A25 and A26 — A28, A29, A30, A32, A33 and A34–A38 — which in the earlier window always executed in full (group 1 before group 2, internal order unfixed), became two mutually exclusive alternatives in the later window: about 46 in 100 traces execute only A28–A33 and about 55 in 100 only A34–A38, so each block is skipped in roughly half of the executions, while A25 and A26 continue to appear in every trace. The signs are direct and mutually consistent: participation falls from 100 in 100 traces to the two complementary shares; cross-group co-occurrence collapses from 200 in 200 to 1 in 200 while within-group co-occurrence stays complete; and the new direct edges (A28/A29/A33 to A26, A25 into group 2) show each block being bypassed. Screening flagged 106 control-flow items against about 1 expected for an unchanged process, so this volume cannot be explained by random re-splits of stable behaviour. [OCC001, OCC006, REL021, REL025, REL056, DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, screening]

Limitations: the evidence cannot distinguish a single process in which one of the two blocks is conditionally skipped from a later window that mixes two case populations; the lag items rest on one observation each, and the position shifts are consequences of the shorter segment rather than independent confirmation that any activity was moved. [LAG001, LAG009, POS001]

Other differences: none beyond the above — the position and lag changes behave as consequences of the same restructuring rather than separate behavioural differences. [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS008, LAG001]
