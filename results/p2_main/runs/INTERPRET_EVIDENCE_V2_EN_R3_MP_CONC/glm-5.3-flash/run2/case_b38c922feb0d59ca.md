### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that in the later window the fixed sequencing between the two blocks — A20, A21, A22, A24 and A25 first, ending in the A25→A26 handoff, then A26 and A27, A28, A29, A30 — is no longer enforced: the later-block activities now frequently run before or interleaved with the earlier ones, the A25→A26 handoff has nearly disappeared, and transitions connect the blocks in both directions. The order change is shown directly by the relation items with co-occurrence unchanged, the direction, position and lag items agree with it, and with 102 qualifying items against roughly 1 expected if nothing had changed, the pattern as a whole cannot be a sampling artefact. Limitations: the items describe one underlying reordering from different angles rather than independent confirmations; positions and lags are measured in event positions, not clock time; direction shares are over observed outgoing transitions, not cases; and the precise new ordering rule is not identifiable, since the relation to A26 largely reversed while that to A28–A30 became unordered. [REL001, REL005, REL009, REL013, REL017, REL002, REL019, DIR005, DIR006, POS005, POS006, LAG027]

An important competing explanation that cannot be ruled out is that part of the mixed ordering arises from repeated executions of these activities rather than a relaxed sequence, although interleaved-or-repeated executions stay at or below about 2–3 in 100 traces in both windows. [REL001, REL007]
