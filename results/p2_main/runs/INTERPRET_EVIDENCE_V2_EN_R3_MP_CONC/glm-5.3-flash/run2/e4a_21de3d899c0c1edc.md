### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

A change in timing is supported: in the later window, the elapsed time between the completion of each of A27, A28, A29 and A30 and the subsequent completion of A24 is about one hour longer than in the earlier window, roughly doubling from about 1 hour to about 2 hours with a similarly increased spread. The shift is measured directly by the elapsed-time indicator on all four pairs, has the same direction and near-identical size in each, rests on roughly 45–60 intervals per window, and the time-family item count (4 against about 0.01 expected) cannot be explained by chance; the absence of control-flow findings and the stable pair frequencies argue against a structural counterpart. [TWAIT001, TWAIT002, TWAIT003, TWAIT004]

Limitations: all four items share the endpoint A24, so they likely reflect one underlying difference rather than four independent confirmations, and because the indicator measures elapsed time between consecutive completions, the evidence cannot show whether the extra hour accumulates before A24's completion, after the predecessors' completions, or through other work inserted in between. An important competing explanation the evidence cannot fully rule out is a positional or structural rearrangement around A24 that lengthens these gaps without changing how often the pairs occur; no ordering or control-flow item was observed to support it.
