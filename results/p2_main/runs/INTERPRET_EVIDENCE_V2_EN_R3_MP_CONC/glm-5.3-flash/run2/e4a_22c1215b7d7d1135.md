### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a timing change as a possibility: in the later window, the time elapsed between the consecutive completions of A23 and A24 is about twice as long as in the earlier window, with the entire distribution shifted upward and proportionally wider. The supporting signs are that the timing indicator measures exactly this interval, the change is consistent across every distribution component, and it is based on the full sample in both windows. The screening comparison also fits a real change rather than sampling noise: the timing family produced one qualifying item where about 0.007 would be expected without a change (roughly 140 times the chance expectation), while no control-flow item qualified (0 against about 0.27 expected), so a timing hypothesis needs no structural counterpart here. Two limitations remain: elapsed time between completion events does not show where the extra hour arises (it is not pure waiting or processing time), and a structural explanation — for example an additional step inserted between the two completions — would produce the same pattern; control-flow screening found nothing, but its thresholds cannot exclude subtle structural changes.
