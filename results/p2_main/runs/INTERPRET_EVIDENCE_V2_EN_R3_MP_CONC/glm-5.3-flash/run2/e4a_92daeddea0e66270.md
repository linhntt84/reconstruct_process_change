### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, the elapsed time between the completion of A38 and the completion of A41 was typically about 1.0 hour (median ~1.03 h, middle half of intervals roughly 0.93–1.13 h); in the later window it is typically about 0.5 hours (median ~0.49 h, middle half roughly 0.45–0.54 h) — a reduction of about half, seen in roughly 100 intervals per window in both cases [TWAIT001].
- The same pattern holds for the pair A40 → A41: about 1.0 hour (median ~1.01 h) in the earlier window versus about 0.5 hours (median ~0.52 h) in the later one, again with about 100 intervals per window [TWAIT002].
- The spread of these intervals also narrowed, with the standard deviation falling from about 0.15–0.16 hours to about 0.08 hours, so the shortening is consistent across the whole distribution rather than driven by a few outliers [TWAIT001, TWAIT002].
- Both differences are larger than those seen in effectively all random re-splits of the pooled data (about 999.5 in 1,000 reference comparisons) [TWAIT001, TWAIT002].
- Participation did not change: these intervals occur in about half of the traces in both windows (about 100 of 200 before and 104 of 200 after for A38 → A41; 100 of 200 before and 96 of 200 after for A40 → A41) [TWAIT001, TWAIT002].
- Screening context: the time family produced 2 qualifying items where only about 0.008 would be expected by chance, while the control-flow family produced 0 items against about 0.31 expected, so no structural differences were flagged [TWAIT001, TWAIT002].

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a change in timing centred on A41: in the later window, the elapsed time from the completion of A38 to the completion of A41, and from the completion of A40 to the completion of A41, is roughly halved, from about 1 hour to about half an hour, with a correspondingly narrower spread. This rests on direct timing evidence for both pairs, agreement in direction and magnitude between them, large and balanced observation counts, and a time-family item count (2) far above the chance expectation (about 0.008) [TWAIT001, TWAIT002].

Limitations: both items share the successor activity A41, so they may reflect a single underlying difference rather than two independent ones. TWAIT measures elapsed time between consecutive completion events, so the evidence cannot show whether the reduction comes from shorter waiting, faster processing, or a change in how events are recorded. A competing explanation the evidence cannot fully exclude is a shift in the mix of cases (for example, different path frequencies producing shorter intervals on average); control-flow screening found no flagged items, which argues against a large structural shift but does not rule out subtler mix changes.

Other differences: none — the control-flow family produced no qualifying items, and no participation, routing or ordering differences were flagged.
