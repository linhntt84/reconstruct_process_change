### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, the five activities A30, A31, A36, A37 and A38 each ran about 3.6 times per trace and were repeated in roughly 70 in 100 traces; in the later window each runs almost exactly once per trace (mean about 1.0) and repeats in only about 1–2 in 100 traces, while every trace still contains all five (participation 100% in both windows). [OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006]
- Relations among these five changed from mostly interleaved-or-repeated (about 70 in 100 traces) to strictly sequential (about 2 in 100): A30 now precedes A31, A36, A37 and A38 in about 98 in 100 traces (before, about 30 in 100), A38 now follows A31, A36 and A37 in about 98 in 100 (before, about 30 in 100), while A31, A36 and A37 follow one another in both orders in roughly equal shares (about 50 in 100 each), i.e. no fixed order among themselves. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL009, REL013, REL016, REL007, REL008, REL012]

- A31, A36 and A37 now follow one another within one to two steps in most observations (e.g. A31 before A36 within one step in about 63 of 100 later-window observations versus about 8 of 100 before), and five-step self-pairs such as A31–A31 or A37–A37 nearly disappear (about 2 observations versus about 125, though on very few cases). [LAG003, LAG004, LAG006, LAG007, LAG009, LAG010, LAG002, LAG011]
- The relative positions of the five activities (by event order, not clock time) tightened, with spread falling from about 0.15 to about 0.06 and means roughly unchanged, consistent with single rather than repeated execution; their relations with A44 and A45 also became more sequential (A-before-B in about 37–38 of 100 traces versus about 12–13; interleaving about 18 versus about 47 of 100). [POS001, DIR001, DIR003, OCC001, POS007, POS008]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the group A30, A31, A36, A37 and A38 stopped repeating: earlier these activities were executed about three to four times per trace in an interleaved fashion, with A38 frequently leading back to A30, whereas in the later window each of the five executes exactly once per trace, A30 first and A38 last, with A31, A36 and A37 following one another closely but without a fixed order among themselves, and A38 continuing to A25 instead of returning to A30. [OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, REL001, REL004, REL007, REL009, REL012, REL013, REL016, DIR002, LAG003, LAG006, LAG009]

The signs are direct and mutually consistent, though they largely reflect one underlying difference rather than independent confirmations: participation of all five activities is unchanged at 100% of traces while recurrence collapses (about 70 to about 1–2 of 100 traces), the interleaved-or-repeated share of their pairwise relations falls to about 2 of 100, A38's back-transition to A30 disappears from its outgoing profile, and the control-flow screening produced 51 qualifying items where fewer than 1 would be expected without a change — far beyond what chance re-splits produce. [OCC002, OCC006, REL001, REL013, DIR002]

Limitations: the evidence shows the behaviour, not the underlying model — it cannot distinguish a removed loop construct from an existing loop that is simply no longer iterated, nor rule out that the earlier interleaving reflected a mixture of variants with different repeat counts rather than a single loop; the lag items on self-repetition and A38-to-A30/A31/A36/A37 pairs rest on only 2–3 later-window observations and were not used as pillars. [LAG001, LAG002, LAG005, LAG008, LAG011, LAG012, LAG013, LAG014]
