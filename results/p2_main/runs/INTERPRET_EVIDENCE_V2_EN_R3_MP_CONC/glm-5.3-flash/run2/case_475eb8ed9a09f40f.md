### Observed differences

- The two alternative branches after A20 exchanged dominance. In the earlier window, A20 was followed by A26–A30 in about 82 in 100 traces (with A20 executed before each of them in about 81 in 100), while A21–A25 appeared in only about 19 in 100 traces; in the later window, A20 is followed by A21–A25 in about 77 in 100 traces (before each in about 76 in 100), while A26–A30 appear in only about 24 in 100 and no longer after A20. - Both blocks stayed internally intact, so this is a swap in which branch is taken, not a dissolution: pairwise co-occurrence within A21–A25 rose from about 19 to about 77 in 100 traces, while co-occurrence within A26–A30 fell from about 82 to about 24 in 100; A22's position within traces is nearly unchanged (median about 0.70 before, about 0.67 after). [LAG003, LAG002, LAG005]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the two alternative branches following A20 exchanged their frequencies: the branch in which A20 is followed by A21–A25 became the common path, rising from about 19 to about 77 in 100 traces, while the branch in which A20 is followed by A26–A30 became rare, falling from about 82 to about 24 in 100, with A34 and, in about half of traces, A36 and A37 now accompanying the A21–A25 branch instead of the A26–A30 branch. [OCC002, OCC007, DIR002, REL011, REL016, REL030, REL031]

This reads as a change in how often existing paths are taken rather than activities being added, removed or reordered: every activity involved is observed with a substantial share in both windows, A20 still precedes whichever block runs, the blocks keep their internal cohesion, and the A20→A24 adjacency is preserved. The screening comparison makes chance an unlikely explanation for the aggregate — about 125 qualifying control-flow items against roughly 1 expected without change — although most items describe this one underlying difference and are not independent confirmations. An important competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is a changed mix of pre-existing case variants between the windows (for example a different intake of case types), which these indicators cannot distinguish from a changed routing rule. [REL021, LAG003]

Other differences: A02's decline and rewiring — lower participation, loss of its dominant A02→A37 link, loss of the A36→A02 link, and an earlier position in the trace — is a separate difference not covered by the branch-swap hypothesis, and with only about 20 supporting traces in the later window its exact new routing remains uncertain. [OCC001, DIR001, DIR005, POS001]
