### Observed differences

- In the later window, the three activities A20, A22 and A23 — which occur in every case in both windows — sit much earlier in the case: their mean position is about 0.38 of the case length, versus about 0.63–0.64 in the earlier window. [POS002, POS004, POS005]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that, in the later window, two blocks of activities exchanged their placement around the A25–A29 stage. In the earlier window a case ran A40, then — in about a third of cases — one of the optional activities A19, A21 or A24, then A25, then A29, then the mandatory trio A20/A22/A23, and finally A30; in the later window the trio A20/A22/A23 runs directly after A40 and before A25, while A19/A21/A24 (when present) run between A29 and A30. A25 and A29 keep their order, each sitting slightly later because a three-activity block now precedes A25. [REL008, REL009, REL015, REL016, REL018, REL019, REL001, REL006, REL010, REL020, POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006, POS007, POS008]

Supporting signs: the order reversals are near-total in both directions across every cross-pair between the groups; the position shifts of all six activities match; the successor changes at A40, A29 and the trio members point the same way; and the step-distance changes (A40→A25 from 2 to 4 steps, A29→A30 from 4 to 2) quantitatively fit a three-activity block moving across the A25–A29 stage. Participation of the optional activities and the co-occurrence shares are stable, so the difference is placement, not how often activities occur. The screening flagged 46 control-flow items where about 0.69 would be expected if nothing had changed, so the ensemble is far beyond what random re-splits of unchanged behaviour produce. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR007, DIR008, LAG005, LAG009, REL001, REL007]

Limitations: the evidence shows pairwise relations and positions rather than full cases, and the internal order within each trio varies (successors of A40 and A29 are spread across the trio members), so the exact routing inside the blocks is not pinned down. The windows are finite samples, so a shift in the mix of pre-existing case variants with opposite block order cannot be fully excluded, although the near-total reversal in both windows makes a pure frequency shift unlikely. LAG items resting on only 1–2 observations in one window are individually weak, but the hypothesis does not rest on them. [LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG006, LAG007, LAG008, DIR007, DIR008]

Other differences: a few low-frequency successor edges (each about 1–2 in 100 transitions) appear or disappear — for example A20 gains A10 and A06, A24 gains A36, A32 and A04 while losing A14, and A29 gains A31 and A37 while losing A26; these are too small to characterise separately and are consistent with the rerouting described above.
