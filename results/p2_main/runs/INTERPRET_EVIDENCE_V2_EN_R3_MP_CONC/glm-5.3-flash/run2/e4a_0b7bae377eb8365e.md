### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, every trace contained the same fixed chain: A22 was followed directly by A23 in 100 in 100 of its outgoing transitions, A23 was followed directly by A24 in 100 in 100, and the step distances A22→A23 and A23→A24 were always 1. [DIR004, DIR005, LAG001, LAG003]
- In the later window, three activities that had no occurrences at all before now run in most traces, each at most once per trace: A14 in about 29 in 100 traces, A15 in about 41 in 100, and A20 in about 30 in 100; together they appear in 199 of the 200 later traces. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, REL001, REL002, REL006]
- The three never occur in the same trace as one another (zero co-occurrence across 140, 117 and 141 traces containing either member of each pair), so they behave as mutually exclusive alternatives. [REL001, REL002, REL006]
- The former direct successions are now often interrupted: A22 leads directly to A23 in only about 44 in 100 of its outgoing transitions (the rest going to A15 in about 24, A20 in about 17 and A14 in about 15), and A23 leads directly to A24 in about 57 in 100 (the rest to A15 in about 18, A14 in about 13 and A20 in about 13). [DIR004, DIR005]
- Each new activity leads back into the chain — it is followed by A23 in roughly 53–58 in 100 of its outgoing transitions and by A24 in the remainder — so the new step sits either between A22 and A23 or between A23 and A24. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003]
- Ordering evidence agrees: every new activity runs after A22 and before A24 in all cases, but relative to A23 the order is mixed (for example, A14 precedes A23 in about 15 in 100 traces and follows it in about 14 in 100). [REL003, REL004, REL005, REL007, REL008, REL009, REL010, REL011, REL012]
- Step distances shifted accordingly: the A22→A23 distance is now 2 steps in about 56 in 100 cases (previously always 1), A23→A24 is 2 steps in about 43 in 100 (previously always 1), and A22→A24 is 3 steps in all but about 1 of 200 traces (previously always 2). [LAG001, LAG002, LAG003]
- Positions moved only slightly: A22 now sits slightly earlier (mean position about 0.46 vs 0.48) and A24 slightly later (about 0.56 vs 0.55), with the new activities clustered around position 0.51 between them. [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005]
- Elapsed time between consecutive completions roughly doubled on two intervals: A26→A32 (median about 0.9 hours before vs about 1.8 hours after, on 42 vs 46 intervals) and A33→A32 (median about 0.15 hours vs about 0.28 hours, on 48 vs 46 intervals). [TWAIT001, TWAIT002]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that in the later window a choice step operates inside the former fixed sequence: in nearly every trace exactly one of the three previously unobserved activities A14, A15 or A20 executes once, placed between A22 and A24 on either side of A23, while A22, A23 and A24 still occur in every trace and their direct succession dropped from 100 in 100 to about 44 in 100 (A22→A23) and about 57 in 100 (A23→A24). [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, DIR004, DIR005, REL005, REL009, REL012, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003]

The hypothesis is shown directly by relational and directional evidence — new edges from A22 and A23 to the three activities and from them onward to A23 or A24, co-occurrence of each new activity with A22, A23 and A24 rising from zero to its full participation, and mixed order around A23 — and it is consistent across occurrence, direction, position and distance indicators, with 28 control-flow and 2 time items qualifying against roughly 0.4 and 0.01 expected by chance; participation levels of 29–41 in 100 traces also rule out a rare variant appearing in one window only. The main limitation is that zero participation in the earlier window only means the activities were not observed there, so the evidence cannot rule out that A14, A15 or A20 already existed as a rare path (below roughly 1–2 in 200 traces) and only became frequent, rather than being newly introduced; the agreeing items likewise largely describe one underlying change rather than independent confirmations. [DIR001, DIR004, REL004, LAG001, OCC002]

Other differences: elapsed times between consecutive completions roughly doubled for A26→A32 (median about 0.9 to about 1.8 hours) and for A33→A32 (about 0.15 to about 0.28 hours) — a consistent timing change in its own right — but it rests on only about 42–48 intervals per window, these elapsed times are not pure waiting or service time, and this evidence does not link A26, A32 or A33 to the structural change around A22, A23 and A24. [TWAIT001, TWAIT002]
