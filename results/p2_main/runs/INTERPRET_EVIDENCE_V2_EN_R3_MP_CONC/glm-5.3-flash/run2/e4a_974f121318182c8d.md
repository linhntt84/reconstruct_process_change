### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, the elapsed time between consecutive completion events of A10 and A11 centred on about 1.0 hour (mean ≈ 1.0 h, median ≈ 1.0 h, middle half between ≈ 0.9 and 1.1 h). In the later window it centred on about 2.0 hours (mean ≈ 2.0 h, median ≈ 2.0 h, middle half between ≈ 1.8 and 2.2 h) — an increase of about 0.98 hours, close to a doubling, based on 334 measured intervals before and 305 after. - The shift is uniform across the whole distribution: the 25th percentile, median and 75th percentile each rose by roughly 0.9–1.1 hours, and the spread grew in proportion (standard deviation 0.17 h → 0.35 h), so the distribution kept its shape and the entire segment moved up by about an hour rather than a few cases becoming extreme outliers. Only about 1 in 2000 random re-splits of the pooled data produced a larger difference. [TWAIT001]
- This was the only item to qualify among 72 tested units: the timing family produced 1 item against roughly 0.009 expected without a change (about a hundred times its chance expectation), while the control-flow family produced 0 items against about 0.26 expected, so no structural difference accompanies the timing shift. [TWAIT001]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility of a timing change in the segment from A10 to A11: in the later window, the elapsed time from the completion of A10 to the following completion of A11 was about twice as long as before (mean about 1.0 h → about 2.0 h, +0.98 h). The change is direct, since this indicator measures exactly that elapsed time; it is consistent, since all quartiles rose by a similar 0.9–1.1 hours with a proportional spread, which argues against a few outliers or a reshaped distribution; and it is unlikely to be a sampling artefact, as the timing family yielded about a hundred times its chance expectation (1 item vs ~0.009) and the difference exceeded about 999 of 1000 random re-splits. Control-flow screening found nothing beyond chance (0 items vs ~0.26 expected), which fits a change confined to timing; a consistent change in these elapsed times is a hypothesis in its own right and needs no structural counterpart. [TWAIT001]

Limitations: the evidence rests on a single indicator for a single activity pair, and elapsed time between consecutive completions cannot show whether the extra hour is waiting or processing; a subtle compositional change, such as a rare path with longer times that stays below the control-flow thresholds, cannot be ruled out. [TWAIT001]

Other differences: the number of measured consecutive A10–A11 completion intervals fell slightly, from 334 to 305 (about 9% fewer); with no relational evidence on ordering or participation, this small shift may be sampling variation. [TWAIT001]
