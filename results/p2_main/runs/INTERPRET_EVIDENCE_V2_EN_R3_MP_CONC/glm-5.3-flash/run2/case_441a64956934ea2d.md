### Observed differences

- Activities A01 and A13 traded their overall presence. In the earlier window A01 appeared in about 66 in 100 traces (roughly 2.2 times per trace) and A13 in about 23 in 100 (roughly 0.4 times per trace); in the later window A01 appears in about 28 in 100 traces (roughly 0.6 times per trace) and A13 in about 61 in 100 (roughly 2.1 times per trace) [OCC001, OCC002].
- Their typical positions also exchanged: A01 moved from the early part of traces (median relative position about 0.44) to the late part (about 0.82), while A13 moved from late (about 0.82) to early (about 0.32) [POS001, POS002].
- Outgoing flow swapped between the two: earlier, A01 split its outgoing transitions across A08 (about 48 in 100), A15 (about 26) and A07 (about 26), while A13 led only to A06; in the later window A13 splits across A08 (about 45), A15 (about 28) and A07 (about 28), while A01 leads only to A06 [DIR001, DIR006].
- Predecessors switched accordingly: A06 now leads to A01 (about 54 in 100 of its outgoing transitions) instead of A13, while its link to A14 stayed roughly stable (about 53 before, 46 after); A02, A04, A07 and A15 now lead to A13 where they previously led to A01, with similar shares [DIR004, DIR002, DIR003, DIR005, DIR007].
- Co-occurrence patterns flipped: A01 used to appear together with A08 in about 66 in 100 traces and now in about 18, while A08 with A13 rose from about 17 to about 61 in 100; A01's co-occurrence with A07 and A15 fell from about 43 to about 11 in 100 while A13's rose from about 11 to about 39 [REL005, REL012, REL004, REL007, REL011, REL013]. Earlier A01 typically occurred without A13 (about 49 in 100 traces); later A13 typically occurs without A01 (about 44 in 100) [REL006].
- Short-range succession transferred: A02, A04, A07 and A15 used to be followed by A01 within one to two steps; in the later window they are followed by A13 within one to two steps, and the corresponding A01 pairs appear only at distances of four to five steps and in few traces [LAG003, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006, LAG007, LAG008, LAG013, LAG014]. Even self-repetition distances exchanged (A01–A01 from about three steps to two; A13–A13 from two to three) [LAG001, LAG012].

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that A01 and A13 exchanged their places in the process. In the earlier window A01 was the frequent, early activity — reached from A02, A04, A06, A07 and A15 and followed by A08, A07 and A15 — while A13 was a rare, late activity reached from A06 and leading back to A06; in the later window these roles are reversed, with A13 occupying A01's former position and relations and A01 occupying A13's [DIR001, DIR006, DIR004, POS001, POS002, OCC001, OCC002].

The exchange is shown directly by the outgoing-transition profiles, which swapped almost exactly between A01 and A13, and it is consistent across predecessor changes, positions, co-occurrence and short-lag items; the number of qualifying items (38, against roughly 0.5 expected if nothing had changed) is far beyond what chance sampling produces, and each item's difference exceeds about 99 of 100 random re-splits. Many items, however, mirror the same underlying trace behaviour (for example the duplicated relation profiles and the paired lag items), so they should be read as one coherent picture rather than independent confirmations [DIR001, DIR006, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, DIR007, REL005, REL012, LAG003, LAG004, LAG013, LAG014].

An important competing explanation remains: a re-labelling of the two activity codes between the windows would leave the recorded traces looking exactly like this exchange, and the evidence cannot distinguish the two. With 200 traces per window, small differences in the exact shares also remain within sampling variation [OCC001, OCC002, DIR001, DIR006].
