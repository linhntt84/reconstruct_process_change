### Observed differences

- The elapsed time between the completion of A23 and the completion of A29 roughly doubled. In the earlier window these intervals averaged about 0.9 hours (median about 0.92 hours, the middle half between roughly 0.84 and 0.96 hours); in the later window they averaged about 1.8 hours (median about 1.75 hours, the middle half between roughly 1.6 and 2.0 hours), and became more variable (spread of about 0.13 vs 0.28 hours). This rests on 48 intervals before (in roughly 24 of 100 traces) and 42 after (roughly 21 of 100). [TWAIT001]
- The same pattern appears for the pair A30 → A29: intervals averaged about 0.16 hours before (median about 0.13 hours) and about 0.33 hours after (median about 0.30 hours), with the upper quartile rising from about 0.22 to about 0.52 hours. This rests on 51 intervals before and 48 after (roughly 26 and 24 of 100 traces). [TWAIT002]
- Both timing differences are larger than in all but about 1 in 2,000 random re-splits of the pooled data (empirical strength 0.9995 for both items). [TWAIT001, TWAIT002]
- No structural difference qualified: among 52 control-flow units tested, no item qualified where about 0.26 would be expected by chance alone, and the number of observed intervals per pair changed only slightly (48→42 and 51→48). [TWAIT001, TWAIT002]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a change in timing around A29: in the later window, the elapsed time between consecutive completion events ending with A29 is about twice as long as before, both when the preceding completion is A23 (about 0.9 → 1.8 hours on average) and when it is A30 (about 0.16 → 0.33 hours). Supporting signs: the two time items agree in direction, each difference exceeds all but about 1 in 2,000 random re-splits, the time family produced 2 qualifying items where roughly 0.009 would be expected if nothing had changed, and each item rests on 42–51 observed intervals per window. No control-flow item qualified, so the sequencing itself shows no detectable change; the timing shift stands as the hypothesis in its own right. [TWAIT001, TWAIT002]

Limitations: T_WAIT measures elapsed time between consecutive completions, so the evidence cannot show where within the interval the additional time arises. Because both items share A29, they may describe one underlying change rather than two independent ones. An important competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that the completion timing of the predecessor activities (A23, A30) shifted rather than the timing at A29 itself, since the intervals span both sides of the pair. [TWAIT001, TWAIT002]
