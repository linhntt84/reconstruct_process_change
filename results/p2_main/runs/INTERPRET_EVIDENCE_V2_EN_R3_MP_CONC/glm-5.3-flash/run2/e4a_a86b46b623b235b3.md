In the later window it averaged about 2.0 hours (median about 2.0 hours; half between roughly 1.7 and 2.2 hours) — roughly a doubling, about +1 hour, based on about 100 intervals per window; the difference was larger than in 1999 of 2000 random re-splits of the pooled data. ### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility of a change in timing: in the later window, the elapsed time between consecutive completion events around A20 roughly doubled, from about one hour to about two hours, both for the pair A14–A20 and for the pair A15–A20. This is measured directly by the timing indicator, both items agree in direction and magnitude (about +1 hour, with medians and quartiles all roughly doubling), each rests on about 100 observations per window, and the time family produced 2 qualifying items where roughly 0.008 would be expected if nothing had changed — far beyond what chance produces. A consistent change in these elapsed times is a hypothesis in its own right and needs no structural counterpart. [TWAIT001, TWAIT002]

Limitations: the two pairs share A20, so they may reflect one underlying delay rather than two independent changes, and the evidence cannot show whether the added hour accrues before or after A20. TWAIT measures elapsed time between consecutive completions, so the increase cannot be attributed to waiting or processing alone. [TWAIT001, TWAIT002]

Other differences: A32 occurred less often per trace and in a smaller share of traces in the later window. This is the only control-flow item, in a family where about 0.25 items would be expected by chance, so it is within the range an unchanged process typically produces; it may be sampling variation and does not independently corroborate the timing change.
