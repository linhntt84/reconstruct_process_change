### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a change in timing: in the later window, the elapsed time between consecutive completion events on the pairs leading to A31 is about twice as long as before — from A23 to A31 it rose from about 0.9 to about 1.8 hours, and from A30 to A31 from about 0.18 to about 0.38 hours. The screening found 2 time items where about 0.008 would be expected if nothing had changed, so the time family as a whole cannot be explained by chance sampling, while the control-flow family found nothing (0 vs about 0.29 expected), consistent with a pure timing change. The supporting signs are that both items measure the changed behaviour directly, agree in direction and magnitude, rest on roughly 100 observations per window, and show differences larger than about 1999 of 2000 random re-splits. Limitations: TWAIT is elapsed time between consecutive completions and cannot separate waiting, service or scheduling effects; both items share A31, so they may reflect one underlying timing difference around A31 rather than two independent changes. A competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that the predecessors A23 and A30 (or event recording between the pairs) changed their timing, producing the same elapsed-time pattern without any change at A31 itself [TWAIT001, TWAIT002].
