### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a change in timing centred on A23: in the later window, the elapsed time between the completion of A16, A17, A18 or A19 and the subsequent completion of A23 is about twice as long as before, moving from roughly 1 hour to roughly 2 hours. Supporting signs: four pair-level measurements all show the same roughly one-hour shift with similar spread; the time family produced 4 qualifying items where only about 0.01 would be expected if nothing had changed, so chance cannot account for them as a whole; each difference exceeds about 1,999 of 2,000 random re-splits; and the pairs remain consecutive at similar rates (41–58 traces before, 44–59 after), arguing against the shift being an artefact of the pairs breaking apart. Limitations: T_WAIT measures elapsed time between consecutive completion events, not pure waiting or service time, and the evidence cannot show whether the extra hour accrues before A23's completion or within it, nor whether A23 moved later relative to its predecessors or the predecessors' completions moved earlier relative to A23; the four items all share A23 and may reflect a single underlying shift rather than four independent confirmations. [TWAIT001, TWAIT002, TWAIT003, TWAIT004]

These control-flow items also exceed what chance would produce (3 items versus about 0.3 expected), but they involve activities disjoint from the timing hypothesis and describe two separate behaviours, so they are not treated as confirmation of it.
