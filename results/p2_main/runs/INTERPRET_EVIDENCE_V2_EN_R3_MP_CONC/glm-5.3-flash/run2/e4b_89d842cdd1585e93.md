### Observed differences

- Work on the six activities with allocation profiles (A01, A03, A13, A14, A17, A21) moved from one group of resources to another. In the earlier window the load was spread broadly, with the most-used resources each handling about 6–10 assignments in 100 (for example User_28 and User_3). In the later window, User_78 is the single largest resource for every one of these activities (about 8–12 assignments in 100), while User_28 and User_3 no longer appear among the notable resources; one mid-sized resource keeps a similar share (about 5–8 in 100) in both windows. All six profiles changed with empirical strength about 0.9995 — larger than in about 1999 of 2000 random re-splits — each based on roughly 130–300 assignment observations per window [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006].
- The pooled "other resources" share grew in the later window by about 3–7 assignments in 100 depending on the activity (for example from about 49 to about 55 in 100 for A01), so part of the reassigned work is not attributable to any single named resource [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006].
- The elapsed time between consecutive completions of A04 followed by A01 roughly doubled: the median rose from about 22 to about 45 hours, the mean from about 29 to about 56 hours, and the 75th percentile from about 33 to about 72 hours, while the fastest quarter of gaps stayed near zero (a few minutes) in both windows. The difference (about 27 hours on the mean) is larger than in about 1999 of 2000 random re-splits, based on 200 gap observations in each window [TWAIT001].
- Screening context: the resource family produced 6 qualifying items against about 0.005 expected if nothing had changed, the time family 1 item against about 0.017 expected, and the control-flow family 0 items against about 0.17 expected [screening].

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a change in resource allocation: for activities A01, A03, A13, A14, A17 and A21, assignments shifted from a group of resources that carried much of the work earlier (including User_28 and User_3) to a different group led by User_78, with one mid-sized resource continuing at a similar share. The support is direct and consistent: all six allocation profiles qualify (6 items against about 0.005 expected by chance in the resource family), every one shows the same direction — the same resources gaining and the same losing across activities — and each rests on roughly 130–300 assignment observations per window, so sampling variation and an activity-specific fluke are unlikely explanations [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006].

Limitations: the evidence shows a redistribution of shares, not its cause; A13 and A14 have identical profiles and support counts, so the six items most likely describe one underlying reallocation rather than six independent changes; the growing pooled "other" share means some reassigned work cannot be tied to named resources; and the data cannot distinguish a targeted reassignment of these activities' work from a broader change in which resources are active at all [ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN001].

Other differences: the elapsed time between consecutive completions of A04 → A01 increased (median about 22 → about 45 hours; mean about 29 → about 56 hours), which is a timing hypothesis in its own right; it rests on a single time item (1 against about 0.017 expected by chance), so chance cannot be fully excluded for it in isolation, and no control-flow, ordering or participation evidence qualified that would connect it to the reallocation [TWAIT001, screening]. No structural difference was detected: the control-flow family produced no items (0 against about 0.17 expected) [screening].

Competing explanation: the longer A04 → A01 gaps could also result from changes elsewhere in the process that feed the interval (for example different paths or volumes between the two completions) rather than from a change in the handover timing itself; the evidence contains no ordering or participation items that could rule this out [TWAIT001].
