### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

Screening qualified 62 control-flow items against fewer than 1 expected if nothing had changed (183 units tested), so the ensemble of differences cannot be explained by random re-splits of a stable process. [screening]

The evidence supports the possibility that the branch choice after A20 flipped between two alternative three-activity groups: before, about 75 in 100 traces continued from A20 into A21–A23 (run together, with no fixed internal order) and about 25 in 100 into A24–A26; in the later window the shares are reversed, with A24–A26 taken in about 75 in 100 traces and A21–A23 in about 25. The hypothesis rests on direct successor evidence at A20, corroborated by the matching participation and co-occurrence changes of both groups and by downstream activities A30, A32 and A33 shifting their co-occurrence to whichever group runs. Because all six activities and both groups remain well observed in both windows (about 25–76 in 100 traces) and each occurs essentially once per trace when present, this reads as existing paths being taken with different frequencies rather than activities being added, removed or repeated differently. Limitations: the agreeing items largely describe the same traces rather than independent confirmations, and the evidence cannot rule out that the two windows draw a different mix of case types rather than a changed routing rule.
