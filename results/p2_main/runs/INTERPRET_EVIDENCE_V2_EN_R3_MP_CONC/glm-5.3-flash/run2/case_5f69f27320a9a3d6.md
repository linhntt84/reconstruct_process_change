### Observed differences

- In the later window, A01 appears in every trace (200 of 200, once per trace) at early-to-middle event positions (median 0.4, middle half between 0.2 and 0.6); in the earlier window it was not observed at all (0 of 200 traces) [OCC001, POS001].
- A04 shows the opposite pattern: it was present in every earlier trace at a fixed late position (0.8) and is absent from all 200 later traces [OCC002, POS003].
- Successors were rewired around these two activities: A03, which previously always led to A04, now always leads to A07; A04's edge to A07 is no longer observed; and A02, which previously split only between A05 and A06 (about 50/50), now also leads to A01 in roughly 38 of 100 of its observed outgoing transitions [DIR002, DIR003, DIR004].
- In the later window, A01 always comes after A02 and before A03 and A07; relative to A05 and A06 both orders occur in similar proportions (A01 first in about 55 of 100 traces for A05, about 49 of 100 for A06) [REL001, REL002, REL004, REL005, REL006].
- A04's earlier relations all disappeared: it previously co-occurred with A02, A03, A05, A06 and A07 in fixed orders (e.g., A03 before A04 and A04 before A07 in every trace), whereas in the later window it never co-occurs with any of them [REL003, REL007, REL008, REL009, REL010, REL011].
- Step distances changed consistently with this: A02→A03 now spans 4 events in all traces (previously 3), A03→A07 spans 1 (previously 2, with A04 in between), and distances from A02, A05 and A06 to later activities are more varied than before; A05 and A06 also now lead to A01 (about 26 and 36 of 100 of their outgoing transitions) [LAG001, LAG004, LAG002, LAG005, LAG007, LAG008, LAG010, DIR005, DIR006].
- A03 now appears later in the trace (position 0.6 in every earlier trace vs 0.8 in every later trace), and A05/A06 positions spread slightly later (A05 median 0.2→0.4; both activities' 75th percentile 0.4→0.6) [POS002, POS004, POS005].

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a structural reorganisation of the control flow: A01 was inserted as a new step between A02 and A03 — always after A02 and before A03 and A07, but at a variable spot relative to A05 and A06, which it now sometimes precedes and sometimes follows — while A04, previously a fixed step between A03 and A07, is no longer executed. The participation flip is complete in both directions and rests on all 200 traces per window (0 of 200 vs 200 of 200 for each activity); the rewiring is shown directly by successor changes (A03 now leads to A07 instead of A04; A02 gained A01 as a successor); A04's co-occurrence with every other traced activity fell to zero; and the distance shifts (A02→A03 from 3 to 4 steps, A03→A07 from 2 to 1) match an insertion before A03 and the loss of the step after it. With 34 qualifying items against roughly 0.17 expected if nothing had changed, sampling differences cannot account for the pattern as a whole [OCC001, OCC002, DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, REL007, REL008, REL009, REL010, REL011, LAG001, LAG004, POS001, POS003]. A competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that A01 replaced A04 (a substitution) rather than one activity being added and the other removed, since one appears exactly as the other disappears; it also cannot distinguish whether A01's variable position relative to A05/A06 reflects unordered or parallel execution rather than a routing choice, and positions and distances are in event positions, not clock time.

Other differences: A03 appears later in the trace and A05/A06 positions spread slightly later, and A05/A06 still occur in both mutual orders in similar shares (about 103 vs 97 of 200 later traces); these shifts look like fallout from the insertion and removal rather than independent changes [POS002, POS004, POS005, LAG006, LAG009].
