### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that A01 and A04 exchanged their places in the repeating cycle around A06: in the earlier window the cycle ran A01 → A06 → A04 → back to A01 with an exit from A06 to A05, and in the later window it runs A04 → A06 → A01 → back to A04 with the same exit. The exchange is shown directly by the successor changes at all three activities [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003], by the reversed order relations between the pairs [REL001, REL002, REL003], and by the relocation of the distance-1 adjacencies to the new neighbours [LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006]; participation and median event positions shift in the mirror-image direction expected under such an exchange [OCC001, OCC002, POS001, POS002]. All 16 qualified items belong to the control-flow family, against roughly 0.1 expected without change, so chance sampling cannot account for the pattern as a whole, and each item's difference exceeds about 995 in 1000 random re-splits of the pooled data.

Limitations: the 16 items largely describe one underlying rewiring rather than independent confirmations; positions and lags use event order rather than clock time; and A01's participation fell slightly more than a pure mirror swap would predict (about 20 versus about 24 in 100 traces), so an accompanying change in how often the cycle is entered, or in which activity starts it, cannot be ruled out. The pair-based shares also rest on moderate counts (roughly 40–104 traces per window).

Other differences: the loop-back share at A06 is slightly lower in the later window (about 43 in 100 of its transitions, versus about 46 earlier, now pointing to A01 instead of A04), with the exit to A05 correspondingly a bit higher; this small shift could be sampling. [DIR003]
