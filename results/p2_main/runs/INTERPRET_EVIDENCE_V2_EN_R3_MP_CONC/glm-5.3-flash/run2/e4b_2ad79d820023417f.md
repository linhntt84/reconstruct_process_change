### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis of a resource-allocation change without a detected structural counterpart: in the later window, the activities A01, A03, A13, A14, A16 and A17 were executed by a largely different set of resources than before. A16 moved from being shared by about 19 resources (largest share about 18 in 100) to being handled exclusively by User_64, and on A01, A03, A13, A14 and A17 the work rotated away from former regulars such as User_3 towards a new set of performers led by User_56. The support is direct (assignment and concentration indicators measure allocation itself), consistent (all six activities shift in the same direction, with similar magnitudes of about 0.32–0.35 cosine dissimilarity for the five spread activities and the maximal 1.0 for A16), and the seven resource items far exceed the roughly 0.005 expected without change, so sampling variation cannot explain the pattern as a whole; with zero control-flow items against about 0.15 expected, the change is specific to allocation. Limitations: the resource codes are anonymous, so the evidence cannot show whether the new codes are genuinely different resources or the same capacity under different codes, nor what drove the reallocation; A13 and A14 rest on identical profiles and are not independent confirmations.
