### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a change in **resource allocation**: the execution of activities A01, A03, A13, A14, A17 and A22 appears to have been handed over from the group of resources that handled them in the earlier window to a largely different group, led by User_84 and User_96, with only two resources keeping small shares in both windows and assignments spreading slightly more across minor resources (the aggregated remainder reaching about half of all assignments in five of the six activities). [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006]

Supporting signs: the resources family produced 6 qualifying items where only 0.005 were expected without change — an excess of roughly a thousand-fold that random re-splits cannot explain as a whole; all six items point the same way ("redistributed", each larger than all but about 1 in 2,000 reference comparisons) and rest on 96–274 observations per window; no control-flow items qualified, so the difference is confined to allocation rather than structure. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN006, screening]

Limitations and competing explanation: the aggregated "OTHER" bucket holds roughly 40–54 assignments in 100, so the evidence cannot show whether the earlier resources stopped entirely or only fell out of the top ten — a reshuffle inside that large group could partly mimic a clean handover. The identical A13/A14 profiles mean the six items are not six independent changes, and with only two windows the evidence cannot distinguish a single reassignment from a turnover that began before the later window. [ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004]

Other differences: a single timing item shows the A05–A09 completion-to-completion gap lengthening (mean about 0.8 to about 1.0 seconds; 120 vs 132 gaps).
