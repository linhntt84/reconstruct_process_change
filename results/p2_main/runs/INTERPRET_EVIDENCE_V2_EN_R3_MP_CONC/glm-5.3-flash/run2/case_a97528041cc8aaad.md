### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The 87 qualifying control-flow items are far more than the roughly 1 expected if nothing had changed, so chance sampling cannot account for the pattern as a whole. The evidence supports a structural reordering in the middle of the process: the group A19, A21, A24, which previously ran after A25 and A29 and closed into A30, now runs before A25 and A29 and closes into A25, while the group A20, A22, A23, which previously ran before A25/A29, now runs after them and closes into A30; A29 now hands over to A20/A22/A23 instead of A19/A21/A24, and A40 now feeds A19/A21/A24 instead of A20/A22/A23. The reversal is shown directly on every cross-group pair (for example, A20 preceded A25 in about 98 of 100 traces before, whereas A25 precedes A20 in about 97 of 100 after, with 100% co-occurrence in both windows) and is consistent across the position shifts, the successor changes and the changed handovers. Limitations: the evidence establishes the reversed orders and retargeted edges but not the underlying flow model, so a broader rewiring producing the same order statistics cannot be excluded; about 2–3 in 100 later traces still follow the old order; and several lag-level details rest on very few observations in one window (often 1–10 traces), so they are indicative only. A competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that the two windows sample different mixtures of coexisting variants rather than a single modified flow.
