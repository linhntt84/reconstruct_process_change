### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the choice between two existing alternative paths after A15 shifted its frequencies: in the earlier window about 82 in 100 cases continued from A15 into the group A19–A21 (with A16–A18 running in only about 19 in 100 cases), while in the later window about 79 in 100 cases continue into A16–A18 and only about 21 in 100 into A19–A21, with A25 present throughout and accompanying the chosen group. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, DIR001, REL009, REL012, REL024]

Supporting signs: 34 control-flow items qualified against roughly 0.5 expected if nothing had changed, so chance cannot account for the set as a whole; participation, A15's outgoing shares and the pairwise relations all show the same flip with the same magnitude (about a 60-in-100 swing, each larger than in about 995 of 1000 random re-splits) and matching case support (37 rising to 158 traces, and 163 falling to 42). Both branches and all six activities are observed in substantial shares in both windows, A15 still leads to all six, and repetition per trace is unchanged, which fits a shift in how often existing paths are taken rather than paths being added, removed or bypassed. [OCC001, DIR001, REL001, REL009, REL022]

Limitations: the items largely re-describe one underlying difference and are not independent confirmations of each other, and the evidence cannot rule out that the two windows simply contain a different mix of cases that favours the other branch, rather than a changed routing decision at A15. [OCC001, DIR001]

Other differences: Interleaving or repetition with A25 tracks the branch choice — in the later window A16, A17 and A18 interleave or repeat with A25 in about 34 in 100 traces while A19–A21 do so in only about 10 in 100, mirroring the earlier window (about 36 in 100 for A19–A21) — which reads as a stable characteristic of the branches rather than a separate change.
