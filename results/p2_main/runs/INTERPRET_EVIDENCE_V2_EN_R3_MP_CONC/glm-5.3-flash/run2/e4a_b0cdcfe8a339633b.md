### Observed differences

- Participation: in the earlier window, A28, A30 and A31 each occurred in about 49 in 100 traces and A32, A33 and A34 in about 52 in 100, each at most once per trace; in the later window all six activities occurred in about 99–100 in 100 traces, still at most once per trace. - Alternatives became joint execution: in the earlier window, the triads A28/A30/A31 and A32/A33/A34 never occurred in the same trace (0 of 200 traces), each triad running in roughly half of the cases with its three members together in mixed order; in the later window, both triads ran in the same trace in about 98 in 100 cases, always with the A28/A30/A31 triad first (no reverse order observed), while the order within each triad remained mixed. ### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the two three-activity blocks A28/A30/A31 and A32/A33/A34, which in the earlier window were strict alternatives — each executed in about half of the cases and never together, bracketed by A25 before and A26 after — are in the later window executed together in almost every case, in the sequence A25, then A28/A30/A31 (no fixed internal order), then A32/A33/A34 (no fixed internal order), then A26. The change is shown directly by relational evidence (co-occurrence between the triads rose from 0 to about 98 in 100 traces, always with the first triad first), by the participation rise of all six activities, by the successor redistribution away from A26 toward the other triad, and by the widened step gaps; every item's difference exceeded 99.5% of random re-splits, and the control-flow family produced 51 qualifying items where about 0.6 would be expected without change, so the ensemble is not what a stable process typically produces. Limitations: the order within each triad shows no fixed order in either window, so the hypothesis concerns the sequence of the two triads, not their internal ordering; about 1–2 in 100 later traces still resemble the old either-or pattern; and the evidence cannot show why the either-or pattern disappeared. An important competing explanation the evidence cannot fully exclude is that a small share of cases still follows the old either-or routing, which 200 traces per window might be too few to detect reliably. [OCC001, OCC004, REL001, REL007, REL013, REL015, REL019, REL022, REL025, DIR001, DIR002, LAG001, LAG004]

Other differences: The elapsed time between consecutive completion events into A27 roughly doubled from both A20 (mean about 0.9 to about 1.8 hours) and A29 (about 0.2 to about 0.4 hours), while the number of such consecutive transitions stayed similar (94 vs 105, and 106 vs 95); the time family produced 2 qualifying items where fewer than 0.05 were expected without change. This is a timing hypothesis in its own right, but the evidence contains no structural items for A20, A27 or A29, so it cannot be linked to the structural change above, nor can a change in the pacing of these specific transitions be separated from other explanations.
