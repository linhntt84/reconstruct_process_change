### Observed differences

- Assignments on the main activities moved from one group of resource codes to a largely different group. In the earlier window, the busiest single code on A01, A03, A13, A14 and A17 held only about 6–8% of that activity's executions, and roughly half of all executions went to a long tail of others; in the later window, most previous leaders (for example User_77, with about 5–7% before) no longer hold leading shares, while a largely new set led by User_49 (about 8%, from no top share before) took over. The unnamed remainder stayed near half in both windows (about 49–55% before, about 47–51% after), so execution volume per activity was stable while the resources carrying it out changed. - A16 moved from widely shared to single-code execution. In the earlier window, 18 distinct codes executed it and the busiest (User_123) handled about 14 in 100 executions; in the later window, User_123 handled all of them and no other code appears, with the concentration index rising from about 0.10 to 1.0. [ASSIGN005, CONC001]
- The elapsed time between consecutive completions of A04 and A01 fell: the median gap went from about 30 hours to about 22 hours, the mean from about 48 to about 29 hours (roughly 40% lower), and the spread roughly halved (standard deviation about 74 to about 39 hours), based on 200 paired completions per window. ### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a change in how work was allocated to resources. In the earlier window, A01, A03, A13, A14 and A17 were executed by one set of resource codes — none above about 8%, with about half of executions spread over a long tail — while in the later window a largely different set took over: User_49 rose from no top share to about 8%, and previous leaders such as User_77 (about 5–7%) dropped out of the leading shares. In addition, A16 moved from being shared by 18 codes (top share about 14%) to being executed entirely by User_123. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, CONC001]

Supporting signs:
- The resource family produced 7 qualifying items where about 0.006 would be expected if nothing had changed — a count many times the chance expectation, so random re-splits cannot explain the family as a whole. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, CONC001]
- Every item's difference exceeded all 2,000 reference comparisons, and the direction is consistent: the same codes gain and the same codes lose across all five reassignment activities, each backed by roughly 200–260 observations per window. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN006]
- A16's concentration is reported by two indicators on 154 and 165 observations. [ASSIGN005, CONC001]

Limitations: the codes are anonymous, so the evidence shows a swap between two groups of codes without saying what distinguishes them, and it cannot rule out that a changed mix of cases between the windows, rather than a reallocation, produced the different profiles. A13 and A14 show identical profiles, so they likely reflect one behaviour rather than two independent confirmations. No ordering, participation or routing difference qualified (0 of 36 control-flow units, about 0.18 expected without change), so the supported change is confined to allocation, not structure. [ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004]

Other differences: the fall in elapsed time between consecutive completions of A04 and A01 rests on a single time item, where about 0.013 would be expected by chance — a count an unchanged process typically produces — so the evidence cannot separate this timing difference from sampling variation, and it stays outside the main hypothesis. [TWAIT001]
