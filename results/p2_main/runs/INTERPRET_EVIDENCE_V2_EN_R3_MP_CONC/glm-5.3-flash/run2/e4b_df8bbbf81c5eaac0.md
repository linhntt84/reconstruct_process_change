### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a change in how work on activities A01, A03 and A07 is allocated to resources: in the later window, assignments on all three activities are spread over more different resources and rely less on the previously dominant ones, and for A07 the group of main resources was almost entirely replaced by different codes. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003]

This rests on direct allocation profiles for the three activities that all move in the same direction, each larger than in all but 1 of 2000 random re-splits and built from 140 to 200 traces per window; the resource family produced 3 qualifying items where about 0.004 are expected if nothing had changed, a count chance cannot plausibly explain. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003]

The evidence cannot distinguish a reallocation within the same resource pool from a replacement of the available resources, because the identifiers are anonymous codes and no relational evidence about resources is included; the three profiles may also reflect one pool-wide substitution rather than activity-specific allocation rules.
