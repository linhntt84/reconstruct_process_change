### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a change in timing: in the later window, the elapsed time between consecutive completions of A41 and A18 is about half of what it was in the earlier window (about 1.0 hour versus about 2.0 hours on average). The change is measured directly on that gap with about 100 observations per window, it is consistent across the whole distribution (mean, median and both quartiles each dropped by roughly 0.9 to 1.1 hours, and the spread halved), it exceeded all but about 1 in 2000 random re-splits of the pooled data, and the time family produced 1 qualifying item where about 0.007 were expected without change — far more than an unchanged process would typically produce. [TWAIT001]

An important competing explanation remains open: this indicator measures elapsed time between consecutive completion events, so it bundles everything between the two completions; the evidence cannot show whether the reduction comes from shorter waiting, faster handling, or a change in what happens between A41 and A18, nor can it link this timing shift to the routing change at A21. [TWAIT001, DIR001]

Other differences: The redistribution of A21's outgoing transitions (away from A23 and A25 toward A24 and A19) is the only control-flow item, and its family produced 1 item where about 0.29 were expected without change — a level an unchanged process typically reaches — even though the difference itself was larger than about 199 of 200 random re-splits. This shift may therefore be a sampling difference, and the evidence cannot confirm it as a structural routing change. [DIR001]
