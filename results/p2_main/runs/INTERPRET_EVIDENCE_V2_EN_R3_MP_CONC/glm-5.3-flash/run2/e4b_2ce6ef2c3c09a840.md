### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a change in resource allocation. In the earlier window the affected activities were executed by broad, fragmented groups of resources; in the later window, work on A01, A03, A13, A14, A17 and A22 was handed over to a largely different, similarly fragmented group (top shares of only about 5–9 in 100, with the unnamed tail absorbing about 8–11 in 100 more), while A11 became fully concentrated on the single resource User_115, which had already been its largest performer at about 15 in 100 assignments. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, ASSIGN007, CONC001]

The screening comparison backs this: 8 resource items qualified where about 0.004 would be expected if nothing had changed (8 units tested), a count many times that expectation, while control flow and timing produced no items (against expectations of about 0.2 and 0.02), so the qualified differences are confined to allocation; all items point the same way, and the A11 concentration is shown directly by two indicators.
