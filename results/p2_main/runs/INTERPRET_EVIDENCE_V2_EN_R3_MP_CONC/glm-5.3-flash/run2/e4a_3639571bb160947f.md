### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a timing change as the main hypothesis: in the later window, the elapsed time between consecutive completion events on the segments from A03, A04, A05, A06, A07 and A08 into A09 was consistently about half of the earlier level (roughly 0.5 hours versus roughly 1 hour), with a correspondingly tighter distribution. The support is direct (the indicator measures exactly these elapsed times), consistent (all six pairs move in the same direction with similar magnitude, decreases of about 0.45–0.50 hours), and chance is an unlikely explanation at the family level: the time family produced 6 qualifying items where about 0.02 would be expected if nothing had changed — roughly 270 times the chance expectation — while the control-flow family produced none, consistent with a timing change that has no structural counterpart. [TWAIT001, TWAIT002, TWAIT003, TWAIT004, TWAIT005, TWAIT006, screening]

Limitations: T_WAIT is elapsed time between consecutive completion events, so the shortening cannot be pinned to a single step's duration; support per pair is moderate (about 27–42 observations per window); and all six items share A09, so they likely describe one underlying difference rather than six independent confirmations. An important competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is a shift in the mix of cases or in the work interleaved between these events, which would shorten the measured elapsed times without any activity itself being executed faster; the empty control-flow family makes this less plausible but does not exclude it. [TWAIT001, TWAIT002, TWAIT003, TWAIT004, TWAIT005, TWAIT006, screening]

Other differences: the only further signals are the modest count shifts for A05 into A09 (40 to 27) and A07 into A09 (33 to 42); with no control-flow items qualifying, these are consistent with sampling variation rather than a participation change. [TWAIT003, TWAIT005, screening]
