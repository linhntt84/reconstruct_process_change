- In the later window, work on four activities (A01, A03, A17, A22) is concentrated on a small group of resources instead of being spread thinly. In the earlier window no single resource held more than about 9 in 100 assignments for any of these activities, and a long tail of resources collectively handled about 47–55 in 100; in the later window User_3 alone takes about 11–13 in 100 on each activity, with User_39 and User_53 among the other main gainers, and the long tail falls to about 35–45 in 100. Each difference exceeds all but about 1 of 2000 random re-splits, and the profiles rest on roughly 125–270 observations per window. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that resource allocation changed for activities A01, A03, A17 and A22: in the later window, assignments concentrate on a small group of resources, with User_3, User_39 and User_53 taking the largest new shares, while the diffuse tail's share drops from roughly half of assignments to about 35–45 in 100. This rests on the resource family producing 4 qualifying items where about 0.006 would be expected if nothing had changed — a gap chance cannot explain — and on the four profiles agreeing in direction and in which resources gain, each backed by substantial observations. The evidence cannot rule out that the shift is indirect, for example a changed mix of cases reaching these activities, each kind with its own assigners, rather than a change in the assignment step itself; the anonymous resource codes also prevent any inference about roles or teams. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004]

Other differences: A13 and A14 still occur in every trace but repeat less often (mean occurrences per trace about 1.48 to 1.26; recurrence about 34 to 20 in 100 traces), which would fit a repetition change; however, the control-flow family produced 2 items where about 0.21 are expected without change, which is within the range an unchanged process typically produces, so the evidence does not establish this as a real change. [OCC001, OCC002]
