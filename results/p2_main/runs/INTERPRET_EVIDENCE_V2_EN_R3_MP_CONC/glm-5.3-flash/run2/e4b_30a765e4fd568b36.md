### Observed differences

- The clearest difference is in who executes the work. In the earlier window, activities A03, A13, A14 and A22 each spread their assignments over a wide pool: the ten most used codes took roughly 3–8 in 100 assignments each, and a long tail of other codes about 45–56 in 100. In the later window, a largely different set of codes carries the work: User_67, User_79 and User_104, previously minor or outside the top ranks, now take about 7–13 in 100 each, most formerly frequent codes fall to a few in 100 or drop out of the top ranks, and the tail share shrinks — from about 45 to 28 in 100 assignments for A22, and from about 54–56 to about 48 for the other three. The shift is strongest for A22 (dissimilarity about 0.50, versus about 0.28–0.29 for the others); each of the four differences is larger than in all but about 1 of 2,000 random re-splits. - The same codes rise and fall across all four activities, pointing to one shared reallocation rather than four unrelated shifts; A13 and A14 even have identical before and after profiles, so they do not count as independent confirmations. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN003]
- Participation stayed broadly stable, so the difference is about allocation rather than frequency: the profiles rest on 200 vs 198 observations for A03, 277 vs 270 for A13 and A14, and 119 vs 100 for A22 (200 traces per window); A22 was observed somewhat less often in the later window. - One timing item: the elapsed time between consecutive completions involving A22 and A03 kept the same median (about 10 milliseconds, converted from the hours used in the evidence) in both windows, but became more variable — the standard deviation roughly tripled (about 3 to 8 ms) and the upper quartile rose from about 11 to 13 ms — while the number of such intervals fell from 119 to 99. - No control-flow difference passed screening (0 items against about 0.17 expected by chance among 35 units), so no structural change was detected at these thresholds. [screening.by_family.control_flow]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a change in resource allocation: in the later window, the work of activities A03, A13, A14 and A22 was reassigned from the previously dominant, broad group of resources to a different and somewhat more concentrated group, in which User_67, User_79 and User_104 — previously minor — hold the largest shares while most previously frequent codes recede and the residual "other" mass shrinks (most markedly for A22, from about 45 to 28 in 100 assignments). The resource family produced 4 qualifying items where about 0.005 were expected without a change — a count chance cannot plausibly explain — all four profiles move in the same direction with the same rising and falling codes, and each difference exceeds all but about 1 of 2,000 random re-splits; because assignment shares measure allocation directly and no control-flow item qualified, the change reads as reallocation of existing work rather than a structural reorganisation. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, screening.by_family.resources]

Limitations: A13 and A14 contribute only one distinct profile, the evidence cannot show what drove the reallocation, and for A22 the fall in observations (119 to 100) leaves open that part of the share shift reflects a changed case mix — cases handled by the declining codes leaving the process — rather than reallocation within unchanged work; the codes are anonymous, so no roles or teams can be inferred. [ASSIGN004]

Other differences: the single time item suggests, but does not establish, a timing change — intervals between consecutive completions involving A22 and A03 kept the same median but became markedly more variable (standard deviation about three times larger, upper quartile up about 18%), with fewer intervals observed (119 vs 99), consistent with A22 occurring somewhat less often. The time family yielded 1 item where about 0.017 were expected by chance, and a lone item of this kind can appear in an unchanged process, so a timing hypothesis would need corroborating time evidence. [TWAIT001, screening.by_family.time]
