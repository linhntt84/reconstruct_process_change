### Observed differences

- Both A17 and A18 ran in fewer traces in the later window: A17 was present in about 60 in 100 traces before (119 of 200) and about 46 in 100 after (92 of 200); A18 was present in about 60 in 100 before (120 of 200) and about 47 in 100 after (93 of 200). In both windows each activity occurred at most once per trace, so the drop lies in how many traces contain them, not in repetition. [OCC001, OCC002]
- The flow leaving A27 was redistributed: in the earlier window its observed outgoing transitions led mainly to A30 (about 53 in 100), then A28 (about 29) and A26 (about 18); in the later window A26 led (about 58 in 100), A28 stayed near 29, and A30 fell to about 13. Fewer outgoing transitions were observed overall (49 before, 31 after), so the after-window shares rest on fewer observations. [DIR001]
- Consistently, A27 and A30 became less tightly coupled: occurrences immediately consecutive (one step apart) fell from about 53% to about 13% of observed pairs, while distances of two or three steps grew from about 47% to about 87%. [LAG001]
- Typical distances from A33 shifted slightly and in opposite directions: A33 to A22 moved from mostly four steps (about 69%) toward five (about 55%), while A33 to A23 moved from mostly five steps (about 66%) toward four (about 56%). [LAG002, LAG003]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

In the later window, A17 and A18 were each executed in fewer cases — from roughly 60 in 100 traces to about 46–47 in 100 — suggesting this part of the process was skipped more often, with no change in within-trace repetition. Supporting signs: two participation items of nearly identical size and direction, each based on roughly 90–120 traces per window, with differences larger than in about 995 of 1000 random re-splits, and six qualifying control-flow items overall against roughly 0.5 expected if nothing had changed, so chance alone is unlikely to explain the evidence as a whole. Limitations: no relational evidence shows whether A17 and A18 are skipped together or independently, or where they sit in the flow, and the evidence cannot establish whether their drop and the rerouting after A27 are one underlying difference, since no relation between these activities was observed. An important competing explanation that cannot be ruled out is sampling variation in the finite windows: about 0.5 qualifying items per screen are expected without any change, so individual items — especially the small A33 distance shifts — may still be fluctuations. [OCC001, OCC002]

Other differences:
- Routing after A27 shifted from A30 towards A26 (A30 about 53% to about 13% of outgoing transitions; A26 about 18% to about 58%; A28 stable near 29%), and remaining A27–A30 pairs became less often immediately consecutive (one step: about 53% to about 13%; two–three steps: about 47% to about 87%). This rests on 49 versus 31 observed transitions, and the after-window share for A30 on only about 4 transitions, so it is a clear but thinly supported difference. [DIR001, LAG001]
- The typical distances of A22 and A23 from A33 exchanged their usual values (four versus five steps). This would be consistent with a reordering of A22 and A23 relative to A33, but the distance data alone cannot distinguish that from an activity being inserted or removed between them, and the shifts are small. [LAG002, LAG003]
