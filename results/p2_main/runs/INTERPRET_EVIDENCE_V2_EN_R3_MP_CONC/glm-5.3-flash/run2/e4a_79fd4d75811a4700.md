### Observed differences

- Four control-flow indicators qualified out of 68 tested units, roughly twelve times the ~0.34 items expected if nothing had changed; all four concern successions around A22 and A16, and each difference is larger than in about 995 of 1,000 random re-splits of the pooled data. [DIR001, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003]
- Routing out of A22 shifted markedly. Earlier, of 49 observed outgoing transitions about 53 in 100 went to A25, 29 to A23 and 18 to A21; in the later window, with 31 outgoing transitions observed, about 58 in 100 go to A21, 29 still to A23 and only about 13 to A25. [DIR001]
- A25 did not disappear from behind A22: traces in which A25 follows A22 within five steps number 49 before and 31 after, matching A22's outgoing-transition counts, but immediate succession fell from about 53 in 100 to 13, while gaps of two or three steps grew from about 47 to 87 in 100. This item shares its immediate-succession shares and support counts with the routing result, so the two are not independent confirmations. [DIR001, LAG003]
- Around A16 the two successors swapped their typical distance: A17 followed A16 immediately in about 59 in 100 cases before versus 78 in 100 later (distance two fell from 41 to 22), while A18 followed A16 immediately in about 78 in 100 before versus 57 in 100 later (distance two rose from 22 to 43). [LAG001, LAG002]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that direct routing out of A22 changed: in the earlier window A22 led mainly to A25 (about 53 in 100 outgoing transitions, with A21 at about 18), whereas in the later window A21 is the dominant successor (about 58 in 100) and A25 is direct in only about 13 in 100, with A23's share stable near 29 in 100; A25 still follows A22 within a few steps but typically one to two positions later than before. This rests on direct successor evidence at A22, an agreeing distance shift for the A22→A25 pair, a qualified-item count many times the chance expectation, and per-item differences unusual for random re-splits; all routing targets were observed in both windows, so this is a redistribution between existing alternatives rather than a path appearing or vanishing. Limitations: the later-window shares rest on only 31 outgoing transitions, and the two supporting items describe the same underlying successions, so they are not independent confirmations. An important competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is a reordering behind A22 — for example A21 now placed between A22 and A25 — rather than A22 selecting a different branch; the evidence also cannot show whether A22 itself occurred less often, since its observed outgoing transitions fell from 49 to 31. [DIR001, LAG003]

Other differences: Around A16, the typical distance of its two successors swapped — A17 now usually follows A16 immediately (about 78 in 100, up from 59) while A18 more often follows at distance two (immediate succession down from about 78 to 57 in 100). This mirrored pattern is consistent with A17 and A18 exchanging their order after A16, but only position-gap distributions are available for these pairs and no direct order relation was measured, so a reorder cannot be established from this evidence. [LAG001, LAG002]
