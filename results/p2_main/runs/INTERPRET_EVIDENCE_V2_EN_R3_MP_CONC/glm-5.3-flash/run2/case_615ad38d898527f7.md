### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The screening flagged 152 control-flow items where roughly 1–2 would qualify by chance in an unchanged process, so the volume of differences cannot be explained by random re-splits of stable data. The evidence supports the hypothesis that the segment built around the activity group A36, A37, A42, A43 and A44, together with A27, was restructured: a group of activities that previously ran in every trace became optional and is now skipped as a whole in about 45 in 100 later traces; A25 now leads directly to A28, bypassing the group; A26 now runs before the group and feeds into it or into A27, which has become an alternative to the group; and the group now flows into A31 instead of A28. This rests on direct relational and directional evidence — order flips between A26 (and A28) and the group, successor changes at A25, A26 and the group members, and the collapse of co-occurrence within the group — consistent across the occurrence, direction, position and relation indicators. [OCC005, DIR005, DIR006, DIR007, REL042, REL049, REL057, REL067]

The evidence cannot distinguish a rewiring of one process from a changed mix of coexisting case populations or variants, and it says nothing about why the behaviour differs; several lag-based items pointing in the same direction rest on only one or two after-window observations and were not relied on. [LAG011, LAG013, LAG020]

A02's outgoing transitions also spread from a few targets to many, but that profile rests on only 17 before-window observations and is weak.
