### Observed differences

- Ten activities became near-universal: A28, A29, A30, A32 and A33 each appeared in about 48 of 100 traces in the earlier window and in 199 of 200 traces in the later window; A34–A38 rose from about 52 to 196 of 200. None of them repeats within a trace in either window. [OCC001, OCC005, OCC006, OCC010]
- In the earlier window these two sets were alternatives: pairs spanning the sets (for example A28 with A34) never shared a trace — one set alone in about 48 of 100 cases, the other alone in about 52. In the later window such pairs share a trace in about 97–98 of 100 cases, always with the A28-side activity first. [REL025, REL033, REL040, REL051]
- Within each set there is no fixed order in either window: for pairs such as A28–A29 or A34–A35, each order occurs in roughly half of the traces containing both, before and after. [REL021, REL030, REL056, REL060]
- A25 and A26 kept their roles around the blocks: A25 preceded the executing block activities in about 48–52 of 100 traces earlier and in about 98–100 of 100 later, and the block activities preceded A26 in the same pattern. [REL001, REL006, REL011, REL016]
- Successors shifted accordingly: earlier, A25's outgoing transitions led to first activities of either set (about 48 vs 52 in 100); in the later window they lead almost only to A28-side activities (a single trace went to A35). A28 and A30, which earlier handed over to A26 in about 28 and 17 of 100 outgoing transitions, now hand over to A34-side activities (about 20 in 100), with A26 nearly gone from their successors. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003]
- Relative event positions moved as expected for a longer middle section: A25's mean position fell from about 0.64 to 0.55 of the trace, A28-side activities from about 0.73 to 0.63, A34-side activities rose from about 0.72 to 0.76, and A26 from about 0.82 to 0.84 (event positions, not clock time). [POS001, POS003, POS008, POS002]
- Gaps from A25 to A34–A38, earlier spread across one to five steps, now exceed the five-step window in almost all traces (1 of 200 qualifies), consistent with the five A28-side activities now lying in between; each later profile rests on a single trace. [LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG005]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the exclusive choice between two alternative activity blocks was removed and both blocks now run one after the other in the same cases. In the earlier window each case executed either the block A28/A29/A30/A32/A33 (about 48 in 100 cases) or the block A34–A38 (about 52 in 100), never both; in the later window both blocks execute in nearly every case (about 98–100 in 100), first the A28-side block and then the A34-side block, each still without a fixed internal order, while A25 keeps preceding and A26 keeps following them. [REL025, REL033, REL051, OCC001, OCC006, DIR001, REL001, REL011]

Supporting signs: participation of all ten activities rose from about half of traces to nearly all; the cross-group relation flipped from mutual exclusivity (0 of 200 traces) to near-universal co-occurrence with a fixed block order; the first block's handover moved from A26 to the second block while A25 now enters only the first block; and the position shifts match a longer middle section. Screening qualified 95 items where about 1 would be expected if nothing had changed, so chance cannot account for the ensemble, and every item's difference exceeds about 995 of 1000 random re-splits — though most items describe one underlying restructuring rather than independent confirmations. [OCC001, OCC006, REL025, DIR002, DIR003, POS001, POS008]

Competing explanation and limitations: a rare both-blocks path in the earlier window (below roughly 1–2 in 100 cases) cannot be excluded, the relative-position shifts could partly reflect changes elsewhere in the trace outside the observed A25–A26 span, and the new block-to-block distances are documented by single traces only. [LAG001, POS001, POS002]

Other differences: none beyond those described above.
