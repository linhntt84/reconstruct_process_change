### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, each of the five activities A16–A20 appeared in about 19 in 100 traces (37 of 200); in the later window, in about 77 in 100 (153 of 200). None of them occurs more than once per trace in either window. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005]
- The five activities A21–A25 show the mirror image: about 82 in 100 traces earlier (163 of 200) versus about 24 in 100 later (47 of 200), likewise never repeating within a trace. [OCC006, OCC007, OCC008, OCC009, OCC010]
- A15, which appears in every trace, redirected its flow: earlier, about 82 in 100 of its observed outgoing transitions led to A24 and about 19 in 100 to A19; later, about 24 in 100 led to A24 and about 77 in 100 to A19. [DIR001]
- The two groups of five behave as alternatives: no cross-pair of one A16–A20 activity and one A21–A25 activity ever shares a trace in either window. Earlier, A15 typically ran together with A21–A25 (about 82 in 100 traces, with A15 first) and typically without A16–A20; later it typically ran with A16–A20 (about 77 in 100, again A15 first) and without A21–A25. [REL001, REL006, REL015, REL024, REL039]
- Within the A16–A20 group the order pattern is the same in both windows, only more frequent later: A19 precedes A16, A17 and A18; those three occur in both orders in similar shares (no fixed order among them); and A20 follows them. [REL011, REL013, REL014, REL022, REL023, REL030, REL031, REL038]
- Within the A21–A25 group the same holds with falling frequency: A24 precedes A21, A22 and A23, those three are mutually unordered, and A25 comes last. [REL051, REL053, REL054, REL056, REL058, REL060, REL063]
- A29 was present in essentially every trace in both windows; earlier it usually co-occurred with A21–A25 (about 82 in 100 traces, mostly after them), later with A16–A20 (about 77 in 100, split roughly evenly between directly after A16 and interleaved or repeated with it). [REL020, REL050, REL055, REL065]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the two alternative paths out of A15 exchanged frequencies while keeping their shape. Earlier, roughly 82 in 100 cases ran A15 → A24, then A21, A22 and A23 in no fixed order, then A25, with A29 afterwards, while roughly 19 in 100 ran A15 → A19, then A16, A17 and A18 in no fixed order, then A20, with A29 afterwards; in the later window the shares were about 24 in 100 and 77 in 100. The rerouting at A15 is observed directly, participation of all ten activities moved by about 58 percentage points, the two groups never co-occur, all activities involved are observed in at least about 19 in 100 traces in both windows, and no repetition counts change — so this reads as the same two existing paths being taken at different rates rather than one path replacing the other. The screening produced 80 qualifying control-flow items against fewer than 1 expected if nothing had changed (0.84 over 168 units), and each item's difference exceeds about 995 of 1,000 random re-splits, so sampling variation alone cannot account for the pattern. [DIR001, OCC001, OCC006, REL001, REL006, REL013, REL015, REL053]

The evidence cannot show why the shares moved: a shift in the mix of cases entering the process (case types that route differently through A15) would look identical to a modification of the branching itself. The qualifying items also largely describe this one underlying trace behaviour, so their count should not be read as independent confirmations. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005]

Other differences: small, position-based gap shifts occur inside the A16–A20 path — the gaps from A15 and from A19 to A17 moved slightly toward shorter values, while the gaps from A17 to A20 and to A29 moved toward longer values (changes of about 0.09–0.10 on the gap-distribution scale). The evidence does not show what produced them; they are minor next to the branch-frequency shift and, being position-based, do not by themselves show that an activity was repositioned. [LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004]
