### Observed differences

- The order between the five-activity group {A30, A31, A36, A37, A38} and the pair {A21, A22} reversed completely. In the earlier window, every group member preceded A21 and preceded A22 in all traces containing both (co-occurrence in 200 of 200 traces in both windows); in the later window, A21 and A22 precede each group member in about 995 in 1000 traces. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, REL007, REL008, REL009, REL010]
- Relative event positions shifted consistently with this flip: A21's mean position fell from about 0.65 to about 0.52 of the trace and A22's from about 0.52 to about 0.39, while each group member rose from about 0.44 to about 0.60. [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006, POS007]
- Successor profiles at the group's boundaries differ sharply. In the earlier window, A20's observed outgoing transitions were spread almost evenly over the five group members (about 19–23% each); in the later window, nearly all of them (about 995 in 1000) go to A22. A21's transitions switched from exclusively A25 to spread over the five group members (about 16–23% each, A25 down to about 0.5%). Each group member lost its transitions into A22 (about 18–22% before, near zero after) and gained transitions into A25 (about 18–23% after), while transitions among the group members stayed similar. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006, DIR007]
- Before, each group member followed A20 within five steps in essentially every trace, with the gap spread evenly over positions 1–5; in the later window this is observed in only about 1 in 200 traces. The same holds for pairs from a group member to A22. Each later-window gap profile rests on a single trace, so the specific gap values are not reliable. [LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006, LAG007, LAG008, LAG009, LAG010]
- Participation did not change: every listed activity occurs in essentially all traces in both windows (support 200 of 200; co-occurrence 1.0), so the difference lies in ordering and wiring, not in whether the activities run. [REL001, POS001, DIR001]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

Main hypothesis: the five-activity group {A30, A31, A36, A37, A38} now runs after A21 instead of before A22 and A21 — in the earlier window the flow ran from A20 into the group (members in varying order), the group exited into A22, and A21 followed before closing to A25; in the later window A20 leads to A22, then A21, which then opens into the group, and the group exits to A25. The group's internal wiring (members following one another in varying order) is essentially unchanged. [REL001, REL006, DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, POS001, POS002, POS003]

Supporting signs: the order flip is complete and identical across all ten activity pairs; the position shifts move the two groups in opposite directions by similar amounts (about 0.13–0.16); the successor changes sit exactly at the group's entry and exit points (A20 loses the group and gains A22, A21 gains the group, the group swaps A22 for A25 as exit); participation is unchanged, ruling out activities being added, removed or made optional; and 34 items qualified against roughly 0.55 expected without a change, so the volume cannot be explained by random re-splits, with every item's difference exceeding about 995 in 1000 reference comparisons. [REL001, REL010, POS003, POS007, DIR002, DIR003, LAG001]

Limitations and competing explanation: the REL, POS and DIR items describe one underlying trace behaviour, so they are not independent confirmations; the LAG profiles after the change rest on one trace each and cannot establish the group's spacing afterwards. The evidence also cannot separate a single move of the whole group from an equivalent rewiring in which A20 simply skips the group and A21 starts it — both produce the same observed orders — and the A22-before-A21 order itself is inferred only from positions, as no relation or direction item covers that pair. [LAG001, LAG006, POS001, POS002]
