### Observed differences

- Resource allocation shifted broadly across activities. In the earlier window, A01, A03, A13, A14, A17 and A22 were each spread over a wide set of resources, with User_79 the most frequent performer (about 7–9 in 100 occurrences of each activity) and roughly half of the work dispersed across many others. In the later window, User_79 and most previously frequent resources no longer hold top shares, while a largely different set of resources — including User_67, which held no top share before — took over about 4–8 in 100 occurrences each; the dispersed remainder stayed between about 40 and 55 in 100 in both windows. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, ASSIGN007]
- A07 changed most sharply: earlier it was spread across 29 distinct resources, with the top performer, User_100, doing about 18 in 100 occurrences (concentration index about 0.07); in the later window, every observed A07 occurrence was performed by User_100 alone (top-resource share 100 in 100, concentration index 1.0). [ASSIGN003, CONC001]
- Timing: the elapsed time between consecutive completion events for A09 and A23 lengthened considerably — the median interval rose from about 1.5 hours to about 17 hours (mean from about 4 to about 16 hours) — while the number of observed consecutive A09–A23 pairs fell from 26 to 14. [TWAIT001]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a change in resource allocation as the main hypothesis: in the later window, work on A01, A03, A13, A14, A17 and A22 was reallocated from the group of resources that included User_79 to a largely different group that included User_67, and A07, previously shared across 29 resources, was performed exclusively by User_100 in every observed case. The signs are direct and consistent: all seven allocation profiles move in the same direction with the same resources rising and falling; the concentration measure independently confirms A07's collapse onto a single resource; each difference is larger than those seen in all but about 1 of 2000 random re-splits; and the resource family produced eight qualifying items against roughly 0.006 expected without change, while no control-flow items qualified (0 against about 0.19), so the detected change lies in allocation rather than structure. Limitations: the resource codes are anonymous, so the evidence shows reallocation between groups of codes but not their roles; A13 and A14 show identical profiles and likely mirror one underlying behaviour rather than two independent confirmations; and a shift in the mix of cases — with different case types naturally handled by different resources — could produce a similar reallocation pattern and cannot be ruled out. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, ASSIGN007, CONC001]

Other differences: the A09–A23 timing shift is a candidate timing change in its own right, but it rests on a single qualifying time item (against about 0.013 expected for that family) and on only 26 versus 14 intervals, so it remains tentative, and the evidence does not show whether it is connected to the reallocation.
