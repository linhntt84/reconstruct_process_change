### Observed differences

- A30, A31 and A36 were mandatory in both windows (present in every trace). In the earlier window each occurred about 3.4 times per trace, with repeats in about 75 in 100 traces; in the later window each occurs only about 1.05 times per trace, and repeats remain in only about 1–2 in 100 traces [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003].
- The branch after A36 flipped: earlier, about 70 in 100 of A36's outgoing transitions returned to A30 and about 29 in 100 went to A25; in the later window, about 93 in 100 go to A25 and only about 4 in 100 return to A30 [DIR003].
- The forward chain stayed intact: A30 led to A31 in about 98 in 100 of its transitions earlier and about 97 in 100 later; A31 led to A36 in about 99 in 100 earlier and about 98 in 100 later [DIR001, DIR002].
- A30 and A36 still co-occur in every trace, but their relation changed: earlier they were interleaved or repeated in about 75 in 100 traces (fixed A30-before-A36 order in about 25 in 100); later the single fixed A30-before-A36 order holds in about 98 in 100 traces [REL001].
- The distance from A30 to A36 concentrated at 2 event positions (about 89 in 100 of the pair weight later, versus about 12 in 100 earlier), consistent with a single pass through A31; the return pair A36→A30, earlier seen within one step in about 13 in 100 of its weight, is nearly unobserved later (only 1 supporting observation) [LAG002, LAG003].
- Self-repetition gaps of A30 and of A36, earlier concentrated at a distance of about 3, are barely observable later (2 supporting observations each) [LAG001, LAG004].
- The positions of A30 and A36 within the trace kept roughly the same mean location (about 0.42–0.49 of the trace) but their spread roughly halved [POS001, POS002].

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the cycle A30 → A31 → A36 stopped repeating: in the earlier window traces typically ran this cycle about three to four times, with A36 returning to A30 in about 70 in 100 of its transitions, while in the later window the cycle runs about once per trace and A36 exits to A25 in about 93 in 100 of its transitions; A30, A31 and A36 remain in every trace throughout [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, DIR003, REL001].

The supporting signs are direct and consistent: occurrence counts and repeat shares dropped in unison for all three activities, the return edge A36→A30 gave way to the exit to A25, the A30–A36 relation changed from interleaved/repeated to a single fixed order, and the A30→A36 distance concentrated at 2 steps, matching one pass through A31. The forward edges A30→A31 and A31→A36 stayed dominant, so the activities were neither removed nor bypassed. Screening produced 13 qualifying items where about 0.46 would be expected without change, so the item set as a whole is unlikely under no change, although most items describe the same underlying behaviour and are not independent confirmations [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, REL001, LAG002].

Limitations: the gap-based items LAG001, LAG003 and LAG004 rest on only 1–2 later-window observations and add little weight; and about 1–2 in 100 later traces still show repeats, so the evidence cannot show whether the cycle can no longer repeat or only rarely does, nor what now determines the single pass [LAG001, LAG003, LAG004, OCC001, OCC002, OCC003].

Other differences: minor shifts of a few percent among rarely used outgoing edges of A30 and A31, and a narrowing of the position spread of A30 and A36, both consistent with the same repetition change rather than separate changes [DIR001, DIR002, POS001, POS002].
