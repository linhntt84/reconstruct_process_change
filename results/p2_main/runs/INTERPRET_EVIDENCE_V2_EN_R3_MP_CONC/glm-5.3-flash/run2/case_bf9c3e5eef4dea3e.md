### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that control flow was restructured at two points. First, A08 became a frequent early step between A15 and A16: in the later window A15 routes about 45 of 100 of its outgoing transitions to A08 and A08 passes to A16 in about 86 of 100 cases, so the earlier direct A15→A16 succession is often interrupted by A08, whose participation rose from about 21 to 71 of 100 traces and whose median position moved from about 0.60 to about 0.19 of the trace. Second, the group A19, A20, A25, A26 and A27 was relocated: earlier it was fed mainly by A47 and led into A28 about one third into the trace; in the later window A47 connects straight to A28, the group is reached after A29/A30, sits about 0.6 into the trace, and leads to A30/A34. [OCC003, POS001, DIR004, DIR006, LAG001, LAG009, DIR007, DIR011, DIR012, DIR013, DIR014, LAG033, REL016, REL020]

The rewiring is shown directly by directional, relational and lag evidence rather than inferred from frequencies alone, and the occurrence, position, direction, relation and lag indicators agree at both loci; 84 control-flow items qualified where about 1 would be expected if nothing had changed, so the ensemble is not what random re-splits of these windows produce. The evidence cannot rule out that the two patterns are independent local changes rather than one reorganisation, a few lag shifts rest on only 1–2 observations in one window, and positions and lags concern event order within traces, not clock time.
