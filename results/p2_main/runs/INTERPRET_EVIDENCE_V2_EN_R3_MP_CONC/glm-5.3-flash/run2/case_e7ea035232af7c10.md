### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

In the later window, A36, A37 and A42 form a mandatory block executed directly after A25 and before A28, and A27 is executed after A26 in every trace; before, the block occurred in about 46 in 100 traces as an alternative to A27 (about 54 in 100) at the same spot after A26, and A28 immediately followed A25. As consequences visible in the data, A28 now follows the block rather than A25, the block exits to A28 instead of directly to A31, and A31 is now always reached after A26 → A27. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, REL006, REL011, REL012, REL013, REL014, DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, LAG001]

The hypothesis is shown directly and consistently: the order flips (alternatives to always-together, and reversed precedence around A26 and A28), the successor changes at A25, A26 and the group members, the position shifts, and the lag growth from 1 to about 4 positions all describe the same reordering, and 45 qualifying control-flow items against fewer than 1 expected without change make a chance origin of the ensemble implausible. [REL014, REL015, REL016, DIR004, DIR005, POS006, POS007, POS008, LAG001, LAG003]

Limitations: the relational items establish order rather than adjacency, so the exact new edges from A28 and A26 onward are inferred; the later-window lag profiles for A37→A31 and A42→A31 rest on a single observation each; and no direction profiles exist for A27, A28 or A31. A competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that further unobserved connections inside this segment (for example between A28 and A26) also changed alongside the reordering. [LAG005, LAG006, REL005, REL009]

Other differences: rare outgoing edges of A25, A26, A37 and A42 (to activities such as A02, A04, A06, A08, A09, A10, A14, A20, A21, A33, A39, A46) appear or disappear at about 0.5–1 in 100 transitions, consistent with sampling noise, and A25's position shifted only slightly earlier (about 0.36 to 0.35).
