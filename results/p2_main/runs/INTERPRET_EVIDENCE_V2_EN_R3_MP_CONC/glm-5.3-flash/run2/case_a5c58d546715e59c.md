### Observed differences

- The dominant difference concerns two mutually exclusive activity blocks whose frequencies exchanged roles. In the earlier window, A21–A25 each ran in about 85 in 100 traces while A26–A30 each ran in about 16 in 100; in the later window, A26–A30 each run in about 82 in 100 while A21–A25 each run in about 18–19 in 100. The two blocks still almost never share a trace (co-occurrence about 0–2 in 100 in both windows). [OCC006, OCC007, OCC008, OCC009, OCC010, OCC011, OCC012, OCC013, OCC014, OCC015, REL029, REL040, REL067, REL075]
- A20, which still occurs in every trace in both windows (as do A34, A38 and A39), flipped its main successor: about 84 in 100 of its outgoing transitions went to A24 earlier and about 15 to A29, whereas in the later window about 82 in 100 go to A29 and about 18 to A24, with A29 following A20 within one step in essentially all later cases. ### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The 138 qualifying control-flow items are roughly one hundred times the 1.3 expected by chance without a change, so sampling noise cannot explain the flagged set as a whole. The evidence supports the possibility that the two existing alternative branches around A20 exchanged how often they run: earlier, the branch A20 → A21–A25 (A20 leading to A24, A25 last) carried about 85 in 100 cases and the branch A20 → A26–A30 about 16 in 100; in the later window, the branch A20 → A29 → A26–A28 (no fixed order) → A30 → A34 carries about 82 in 100 and the A21–A25 branch about 18 in 100. Because every activity involved is observed in a substantial share of traces in both windows, the blocks remain mutually exclusive, and their internal order is preserved, this is a change in how often existing paths are taken rather than a replacement of one path by another; participation, co-occurrence, direct-successor and adjacency evidence all agree. [OCC006, OCC011, DIR006, LAG002, REL015, REL020, REL029, REL077, REL078, REL093, DIR008]

Limitations: the later-window picture of the now-rare A21–A25 path rests on only about 36–39 traces, and the position and lag indicators use event positions rather than clock time, so nothing follows about real timing. An important competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that the two windows contain different mixes of the same two case types rather than a changed routing decision. [OCC006, OCC007, OCC008, OCC009, OCC010, POS002, LAG002]

Other differences:
- A04 was largely bypassed: beyond running in far fewer traces (about 12 in 100 versus 56), it no longer sits between A38 and A39 — A38 leads directly to A39 in about 89 in 100 of its transitions (before about 45) and to A04 in about 2 (before about 54) — and its few remaining occurrences sit mid-trace with varied successors instead of near the end leading to A39.
