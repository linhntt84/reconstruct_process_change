### Observed differences

- In the earlier window the activities formed one fixed chain: A31 ran before A22, then A20, A23, A24, A26 and A25 in that order, with A25 leading on to A32. Outgoing transitions were essentially single-valued: A23 always went to A24, A24 went to A26 in about 99 in 100 cases, A26 to A25 in about 99 in 100, and A25 to A32 in about 93 in 100. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006, DIR007]
- In the later window these transitions fan out to several successors: A20 still goes to A23 in about 67 in 100 of its outgoing transitions but also to A24, A26 and A25 (about 10–11 in 100 each); A23 now leads directly to A32 in about 49 in 100, a transition not observed before; A24 goes to A22 in about 23 in 100; and A31 splits roughly evenly between A22 (52 in 100) and A24 (47 in 100). [DIR001, DIR003, DIR004, DIR007]
- The order between the two groups reversed or became mixed: A20 preceded A24 in about 98 in 100 traces before, whereas now A24 precedes A20 in about 73 in 100; A23 before A24 fell from about 99 in 100 to 15 in 100; pairs such as A20–A26, A22–A24 and A23–A25 went from a fixed order to roughly even splits (about 49/51, 53/47 and 48/52). [REL001, REL003, REL004, REL006, REL007]
- Step distances spread from single fixed values to ranges: A20 to A23 was always one position apart and is now one apart in about 68 in 100 cases but up to four apart otherwise; A20 to A25 was always four apart and now ranges from one to four; A31 to A24 was always four apart and is now one apart in about 47 in 100. [LAG001, LAG003, LAG027]
- Relative positions moved in opposite directions for the two groups: A20, A22 and A23 now occur later in the trace (mean relative position about 0.59 to 0.63, 0.56 to 0.59 and 0.62 to 0.68), while A24, A25 and A26 occur earlier (about 0.65 to 0.59, 0.70 to 0.68 and 0.67 to 0.64). [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006]
- Participation did not change: every pair of these activities still co-occurs in 100 in 100 traces in both windows, and interleaving or repetition stays at about 0–2 in 100, so the difference lies in ordering, not in which activities run. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, REL007, REL008]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a reordering hypothesis: the strict sequence A31 → A22 → A20 → A23 → A24 → A26 → A25 (→ A32) gave way to a mixed order in the later window, in which A24, A25 and A26 frequently execute before A20, A22 and A23 — for example A24 precedes A20 within five steps in about 148 in 200 traces, versus about 1 in 200 before — and each activity's outgoing transitions fan out to several successors, including a new direct A23 → A32 transition in about half of A23's outgoing transitions. [REL001, REL004, REL006, LAG014, DIR001, DIR003, DIR007]

The change is shown directly by the relational items, is consistent across all eight ordered pairs, and is corroborated by the spreading step distances, the opposite position shifts of the two groups, and the successor fan-out; co-occurrence remaining at 100 in 100 rules out added, removed or optional activities, and 49 qualifying control-flow items against roughly 0.66 expected by chance in a stable process cannot be explained by sampling alone. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, REL007, REL008, LAG001, LAG013, POS001, POS004, DIR003]

Limitations: positions and lags are based on event order, not clock time, so nothing can be said about timing; and the evidence cannot establish the exact new routing structure — it cannot distinguish a fully loosened order from a small set of new fixed sequences, nor rule out that part of the mixing arises because the chain now starts at different points, since A31 also leads to A24 in about 47 in 100 of its outgoing transitions. [LAG027, DIR007]
