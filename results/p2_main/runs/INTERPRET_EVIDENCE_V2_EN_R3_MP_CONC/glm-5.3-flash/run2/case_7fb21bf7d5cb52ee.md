### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that A10 became a frequent, early step running between A14 and A15. In the earlier window, A14 passed directly to A15 in about 86 in 100 of its outgoing transitions and A10 appeared in only about 19 in 100 traces, late and with scattered successors; in the later window, A14 leads to A10 in about two thirds of its outgoing transitions, A10 leads to A15 in about 85 in 100 of its own, the two links are usually one event position apart, and A10 participates in about 70 in 100 traces at an early median position. The change is visible directly at both endpoints (the successor profiles of A14 and A10), is consistent across adjacency, co-occurrence and position measures, and A15's slight shift to a later position together with the A14→A15 gap moving from one to two positions fits the same picture. The screening context also makes a chance origin of the ensemble implausible: 77 items qualified where about 1 would be expected without change. [DIR002, DIR003, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, OCC003, POS001, POS002, REL001, REL002]

Limitations: positions measure event order, not clock time, so this is a change in sequence placement; in about 30 in 100 traces where A10 and A15 both run they are interleaved or repeated, so the between-placement is the typical pattern rather than a universal one; and the evidence cannot show where A10's additional occurrences originate — in particular, it cannot rule out that some enter from predecessors other than A14, since only A14's outgoing behaviour is observed directly. The many agreeing items describe one underlying behaviour and are not independent confirmations. [REL002, DIR002, DIR003]
