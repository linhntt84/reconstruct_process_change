### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that resource allocation changed: in the later window, work on A01, A03, A13, A14, A17 and A22 was handed from the earlier leading resources to a largely different group that includes User_3 and User_78, and A11, previously shared across about 23 resources, was handled exclusively by User_100. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, ASSIGN007, CONC001]

The resource family produced 8 qualifying items where about 0.006 would be expected if nothing had changed — an excess that random splitting cannot explain — while control flow produced 1 item against about 0.2 expected and the time family none. The reallocation reading is direct (the indicators measure assignment shares and concentration) and consistent (the same incoming group appears across six activities), and the A11 concentration is confirmed by two indicators with about 100 traces behind each window. Participation in these activities stayed stable (for example, A01 appears in all 200 traces in both windows), so this is a handover of existing work rather than a change in how often the activities run. Limitations: the resource codes are anonymous, so no roles or teams can be inferred; A13 and A14 show identical profiles, so those two items likely reflect one shared allocation rather than two independent changes; and the evidence cannot distinguish a reassignment of the work from a change in which resources were active in the later window.
