### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that two blocks of activities exchanged their places around the unchanged A24–A28 middle segment: the group A14, A18, A21, A22, A23 — of which each trace contains exactly one member — moved from the end of the case (after A28, leading to A29) to the beginning (directly after A39, before A24), while the mandatory group A15, A16, A17, A19, A20 moved from the beginning (after A39, before A24) to the end (after A28, now exiting to A29), with A39, A24, A28 and A29 keeping their entry, middle and closing roles. [REL004, REL005, REL018, REL023, REL028, DIR001, DIR005, DIR011, DIR012, POS011, POS012]

The support is direct and consistent: 55 items qualified against fewer than 1 expected by chance in a stable process; the relational, positional and successor evidence all describe the same exchange, with order reversals that are total (100 in 100 to 0 in 100) rather than a changed mix of coexisting orderings; and participation shares are essentially unchanged, so the difference cannot be read as activities being executed more or less often. The items largely restate one block-level swap from different angles, so they agree but are not independent confirmations. [REL004, REL009, REL014, REL022, REL026, POS002, POS011, DIR005, DIR007]

Limitations: the order inside each block remains variable in both windows, so exact sequences within a block cannot be fixed, and the relative order of A24 and A28 rests on position statistics because no direct relation item for that pair qualified. An important competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that both end-to-end orderings already existed as variants and the case mix flipped completely from one to the other between the windows; since each window is internally homogeneous, a structural reordering and a total routing flip look identical here.
