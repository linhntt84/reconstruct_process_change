### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the dominant execution order of A36 and A37 reversed, with A33 remaining downstream. In the earlier window the dominant chain ran A37 → A36 → A33 (A37's most common successor was A36, and A36's was A33); in the later window it runs A36 → A37 → A33 (A36's most common successor is A37, and A37's is A33). The two activities stay immediately adjacent in both windows, but the direction of the adjacency reversed, and the ordered pair A36-then-A37 is now seen in about 28 in 100 traces versus about 16 in 100 before. [DIR001, DIR002, LAG001]

Supporting signs: three related control-flow items qualified, against roughly 0.5 expected if nothing had changed; the successor profiles of both activities flipped in mutually consistent ways; and the lag item shows the change relationally rather than through marginal frequencies alone. [DIR001, DIR002, LAG001]

Limitations: both directions between A36 and A37 still occur in both windows (A36→A37 already made up about a third of A36's outgoing transitions before, and A37→A36 still accounts for about 38 in 100 after), so the evidence shows a reversal of the dominant order within an existing adjacency rather than a clean swap of positions. A change in how often a loop between A36 and A37 repeats could produce a similar pattern and cannot be ruled out; successor shares are also measured over outgoing transitions, not whole cases. [DIR001, DIR002, LAG001]

Other differences: the handful of new low-frequency successors and the disappearance of A07 and A14 from the profiles are single-transition changes of the kind rare variants produce; they are not part of the main hypothesis. [DIR001, DIR002]
