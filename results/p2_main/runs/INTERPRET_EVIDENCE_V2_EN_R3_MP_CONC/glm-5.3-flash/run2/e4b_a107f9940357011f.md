### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a change in resource allocation. In the earlier window the activities were served by broad pools of resources; in the later window the work was reallocated: A12 moved from 44 distinct resources (top share about 40 in 100) to being handled entirely by User_36; on A13, A14 and A17, User_71 took over roughly a quarter of the assignments while User_76 dropped out of the leading group; and on A01, A03 and A22 a common group of resources lost most of its share to a group of previously minor resources. Assignment volumes per activity stayed roughly stable throughout, so the activities ran at similar rates and only the allocation of work changed. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, ASSIGN007, CONC001]

Supporting signs: the resource family produced eight qualifying items where about 0.005 would be expected by chance — many times the chance level — and each difference is larger than in about 1999 of 2000 random re-splits. The indicators measure allocation directly, the direction is consistent (the same resources gain or lose share across activities), and no control-flow item qualified (0 against about 0.18 expected), so the redistribution is not accompanied by any detectable structural change. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, ASSIGN007, CONC001]

Limitations: the items are not independent — A13 and A14 have identical profiles and the same gain/loss groups recur across activities, so the evidence is equally consistent with one underlying reallocation rather than eight separate changes. Because the resource codes are anonymous, the evidence also cannot rule out that part of the shift reflects a change in how assignments are recorded rather than a genuine reallocation of work. [ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005]

Other differences: the shortening of completion-to-completion times around A03 and A13 (median about 168 hours to about 66 hours) would be a timing change in its own right, but it rests on a single time item, in a family where about 0.02 items would be expected by chance; a single item is what an unchanged process can produce occasionally, so the evidence does not establish an independent timing change. [TWAIT001]
