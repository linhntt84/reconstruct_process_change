- Participation swapped in a mirror-image pattern between two groups of five activities: A16–A20 each ran in about 85 in 100 traces in the earlier window but only about 18 in 100 in the later window, while A21–A25 each ran in about 16 in 100 traces earlier and about 82 in 100 later. ### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the choice between two existing alternative paths after A15 flipped in frequency. Earlier, about 85 in 100 cases ran A15 → A19 → (A16, A17, A18 in any order) → A20 and about 15 in 100 ran A15 → A24 → (A21, A22, A23 in any order) → A25; in the later window the shares were about 18 and about 82 in 100. The flip is shown directly in A15's outgoing transitions, all ten block activities moved in matching mirror-image fashion, the blocks never co-occur in either window, and each block's internal order is unchanged — so existing paths were taken with different frequencies, not activities added, removed or repositioned. Both paths remain in use throughout (the less-taken block still covers 31–36 of 200 traces in each window), and the screening comparison rules out chance as an explanation of the whole: 79 qualifying control-flow items against roughly 0.84 expected without a change (about 90 times the chance expectation), with each item's difference larger than in about 995 of 1,000 random re-splits. [OCC001, OCC006, DIR001, REL015, REL011, REL051]

A competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that the two windows sampled a different case mix (different kinds of cases) rather than that the process itself changed; the evidence also cannot show what determines the routing choice. [DIR001, OCC001, OCC006]

Other differences: Elapsed times into A13 roughly doubled or tripled (A11→A13 median about 0.9 → 1.8 hours; A12→A13 median about 0.12 → 0.29 hours), a timing difference that far exceeds the time family's chance expectation (2 items vs about 0.006) but cannot be linked to the routing flip from this evidence.
