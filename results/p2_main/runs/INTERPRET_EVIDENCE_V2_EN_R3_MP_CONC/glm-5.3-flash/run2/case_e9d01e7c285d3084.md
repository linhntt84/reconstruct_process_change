### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, A30, A31 and A36 each occurred exactly once in virtually every trace; in the later window each occurs about 3.5 times per trace on average and appears more than once in a case in about 68 in 100 traces, compared with at most about 2 in 100 before. All three activities still appear in every trace in both windows. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003]
- The fixed sequence among these activities dissolved. Before, A14 preceded A30 in about 99 in 100 traces, A14 preceded A36 in about 98–99 in 100, and A30 preceded A36 in about 98 in 100; in the later window each of these orders holds in only about 32 in 100 traces, with interleaved or repeated order in about 68 in 100. The activities still co-occur in all traces in both windows. [REL003, REL004, REL005]
- A36's routing changed: before, about 97 in 100 of its outgoing transitions led to A25; in the later window about 70 in 100 lead to A30 and about 28 in 100 to A25. The observed outgoing transitions of A36 rose from about 200 to about 700, matching its roughly 3.5-fold occurrence increase. [DIR001]
- The tight succession between A30 and A36 largely dissolved: before, A30 was followed by A36 about two steps later in about 98 in 100 observations; in the later window this holds for only about 12 in 100, with longer gaps now appearing (about 5 in 100 at five steps). [LAG002]
- A05, present in about half the traces in both windows, previously ran before A30 and A36 in about 53–54 in 100 of those traces; in the later window it precedes them in only about 16 in 100, while interleaved or repeated order rose to about 35 in 100. [REL001, REL002]
- Smaller shifts: A14 now sits slightly earlier in the trace (mean relative position about 0.14 to 0.12) and A30 and A36 slightly later, in all three cases with a clearly wider spread of positions; gaps between A05 and A14 shifted from mostly one to three steps to mostly two to three steps. [POS001, POS002, POS003, LAG001]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that, in the later window, A30, A31 and A36 are executed repeatedly within the same case — each about three to four times per trace instead of once — and that the previously strict sequence A14 → A30 → A36 became a repeating, interleaved pattern in which A36 most often leads back to A30 rather than forward to A25. The repetition is observed directly for all three activities (occurrence counts and within-case recurrence, each backed by all 200 traces per window), the same order dissolution appears simultaneously in all three pairs among A14, A30 and A36, and A36's successor profile redirected accordingly; fourteen qualifying control-flow items against fewer than 0.5 expected by chance makes a chance origin of the pattern as a whole implausible, and every item's difference exceeds all but about 1 in 200 random re-splits of the pooled data. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, REL003, REL004, REL005, DIR001, LAG002]

The evidence cannot show the exact shape of the repeated execution: there is no relational or successor evidence for A31, so whether it belongs to the same repeated block as A30 and A36 remains unverified, and an interleaved order could partly reflect concurrent execution of these activities rather than strictly one repetition after another. [OCC002, REL003, REL004, REL005]

Other differences: A05's relation to the repeated activities changed in the same direction (from mostly preceding A30 and A36 to interleaving with them in about a third of the traces containing it), and the positional shifts described above — A14 slightly earlier, A30 and A36 slightly later, all with wider spread, plus the A05–A14 gap shift — accompany the repetition without being part of its core statement. [REL001, REL002, POS001, POS002, POS003, LAG001]
