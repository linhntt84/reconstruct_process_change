### Observed differences

- **A05 largely disappeared.** In the earlier window, A05 ran in about 21 in 100 traces and was often repeated within a trace (about 136 executions in total, mean 0.68 per trace; repeated in about 14 in 100 traces). In the later window, it runs in only about 6 in 100 traces (about 26 executions, mean 0.13). [OCC003]
- **A17 is new.** A17 was not observed in the earlier window; in the later window it runs in about 13 in 100 traces (about 51 executions, so often more than once per trace), sits about seven-tenths into the trace (median position ~0.71), and every observed execution is followed by A09. [OCC004, POS002, DIR008]
- **Transitions around this segment were rewired.** A16, previously always followed by A05, is now always followed by A08. A08, previously split between A13 (about 53 in 100) and A04 (about 47 in 100), now goes to A16 (about 51) and A17 (about 49), with A08's observed executions rising from about 32 to about 104. A09, previously mainly to A06 (about 47) and A08 (about 43), now goes mainly to A05 (about 51) and A06 (about 33). A05 itself, previously followed by A01 (about 54) and A16 (about 46), is now followed by A13 (about 54) and A04 (about 46). - **Two further activities participate less.** A02 fell from about 50 to 38 in 100 traces, and A03 from about 29 to 16 in 100. [OCC001, OCC002]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

One possibility supported by the evidence is a structural reorganisation of the cyclic segment around A05. In the earlier window this segment formed a cycle: A05 handed over to A01 or A16, A01 led to A09, A09 to A08, A08 to A13/A04, and A13/A04 led back to A05, with A05⇄A16 as a side loop, and A05 was its frequent, repeated centre. In the later window, this A05-centred cycle is largely dismantled: A05 nearly disappears; A16 now feeds A08 instead of A05; A08 cycles with A16 and reaches A09 through the new activity A17; A09 now leads back into A05, which feeds A04/A13; and A01 has left the segment entirely, appearing at the start of traces that contain none of the segment's activities.

The signs are direct and consistent: the successor changes are observed directly on the outgoing transitions of A05, A08, A09, A16, A04, A13 and A01; A05's participation collapsed while A17 appeared; and the relational evidence shows A01's co-occurrences with the segment disappearing while A17's relations appear. With 42 qualifying items against roughly 0.39 expected for an unchanged process, chance alone cannot account for the set of differences as a whole. [OCC003, OCC004, DIR001, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, DIR007, POS001, POS002, REL001, REL008]

Limitations: the two windows are finite samples, and the evidence cannot distinguish a modification of one process from a shift in the mix of two existing behaviour variants (for example, traces with and without the old cycle occurring in different proportions). A01's empty routing profile in the later window means only that no outgoing transitions were observed, not that A01 has none; several lag comparisons rest on fewer than about 15 observations; and the many agreeing relation and lag items describe largely the same traces, so they are not independent confirmations.

Other differences: A02 (about 50 → 38 in 100 traces) and A03 (about 29 → 16 in 100) participate less often in the later window; the evidence does not connect them to the reorganised segment. [OCC001, OCC002]
