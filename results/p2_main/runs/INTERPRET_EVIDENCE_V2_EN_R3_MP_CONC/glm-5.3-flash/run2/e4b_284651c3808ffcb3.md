### Observed differences

- Work on seven activities — A01, A03, A13, A14, A16, A17 and A22 — was carried out by a different group of resources in the later window. Before, each of these activities was spread thinly: no single resource accounted for more than about 8–16 in 100 of an activity's executions, and roughly half of the executions went to a long tail of further resources. In the later window, a largely different set of resources holds the leading shares — for example, User_56 rose from about 3 in 100 to about 14 in 100 on A22 and gained about 6–7 percentage points on A03 and A17 — while resources that previously led, such as User_18 (about 5–6 in 100 on several of these activities), no longer appear among the leading shares. The same resources that gained on one activity tended to gain on the others. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN006, ASSIGN007]

- A16 changed most drastically: before, its executions were distributed across 15 distinct resources, with the two largest at about 16 in 100 each; in the later window, a single resource (User_28) performed every observed execution (top-resource share 100 in 100; concentration index from about 0.11 to 1.0). [ASSIGN005, CONC001]

- The sizes of these shifts correspond to profile dissimilarities of about 0.26–0.29 for A01, A03, A13, A14 and A17, about 0.57 for A22 and 1.0 for A16, and each exceeds the differences seen in about 999.5 of 1,000 random re-splits of the pooled data. The profiles rest on many observations in both windows (for example 200 vs 200 traces for A01, 145 vs 137 for A16, 96 vs 107 for A22). [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, ASSIGN007, CONC001]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

Main hypothesis: in the later window, the execution of these activities was reallocated between groups of resources — on A01, A03, A13, A14, A17 and A22 work shifted from the previously leading resources (for example User_18) to a largely different group (for example User_56), and on A16 work that had been shared across about 15 resources became concentrated in the single resource User_28. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, ASSIGN007, CONC001]

Supporting signs: eight resource items qualified where about 0.005 would be expected by chance in that family, so the set of findings is far beyond what an unchanged process typically produces; the direction is consistent, with the same gaining and losing groups recurring across activities; the evidence measures allocation directly through assignment profiles and, for A16, a concentration measure; and no control-flow items qualified (0 against about 0.19 expected), so the difference appears confined to resource allocation rather than structure. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, ASSIGN007, CONC001]

Limitations: A13 and A14 show identical before and after profiles and most likely reflect one underlying change rather than two independent ones; the resource codes are anonymous, so the evidence cannot distinguish a change in assignment behaviour from a change in which resources were available; and for the six moderately shifted activities only aggregate shares are observed, not how individual cases were reassigned. An important competing explanation the evidence cannot fully rule out is a shift in the mix of cases reaching these activities, which could alter aggregate resource shares without any reallocation; the absence of control-flow differences makes this less likely but does not exclude it. [ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004]
