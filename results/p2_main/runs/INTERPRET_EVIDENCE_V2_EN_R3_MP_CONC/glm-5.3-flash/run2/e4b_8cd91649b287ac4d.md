### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that resource allocation for A17 changed from work shared across many resources to work handled by a single resource: before, about 45 distinct resources each held small shares with a top share of about 14 in 100 executions; in the later window, all A17 executions went to User_84 alone. The concentration indicator shows this directly, the assignment-profile indicator agrees, A17's participation was stable across windows, and each difference was larger than in all but about 1 in 2000 random re-splits of the pooled data. [ASSIGN002, CONC001]

The screening comparison fits a real allocation change rather than chance: three resource items qualified where fewer than 0.01 would be expected without a change — a count many times that expectation — while no control-flow or timing item qualified against expectations of about 0.17 and 0.014, so the qualifying evidence is confined to resource allocation. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, CONC001]

Limitations: the two A17 items describe one underlying difference and are not independent confirmations; the resource codes are anonymous, so no roles or groups can be inferred; and the evidence cannot rule out that the recorded shift reflects a change in how assignments were captured rather than in how work was actually allocated. [ASSIGN002, CONC001]

Other differences: A01's assignment mix shifted gradually, with several resources losing share to several newcomers (including User_84) without any concentration; this rests on a single indicator item, and the evidence cannot establish whether it belongs to the same reallocation as the A17 change.
