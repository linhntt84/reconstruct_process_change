### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, A25, A26 and A31 each occurred exactly once in every trace (participation 100 in 100 traces, no repeats). In the later window, each of the three occurs about 3.5 times per trace on average, and about 68 in 100 traces contain repeated executions of them. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003]
- The behaviour after A31 changed: in the earlier window, every observed A31 execution was followed by A20; in the later window, about 71 in 100 A31 executions are followed by A25 and only about 29 in 100 by A20 (based on 697 observed outgoing transitions). [DIR001]
- The relationship between A25 and A31 changed from a fixed sequence to a mixed one: in the earlier window both always occurred together and A25 always came before A31; in the later window they still always co-occur, but only about 32 in 100 traces keep that fixed order, while about 68 in 100 show interleaved or repeated execution. [REL001]
- The tight spacing between the two dissolved: in the earlier window, A25 was followed by A31 exactly two event positions later in essentially all observed pairs; in the later window, the two-step gap accounts for only about 12 in 100 of the lag distribution and a five-step gap for about 5 in 100. [LAG001]
- The typical position of A25 and A31 within the trace barely moved (median about 0.40 to 0.43 and 0.47 to 0.48 of the trace), but their spread roughly doubled (standard deviation about 0.06 to 0.12), which fits repeated occurrences at varying positions rather than a relocation of the activities. [POS001, POS002]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that, in the later window, the segment involving A25, A26 and A31 repeats within traces: these three activities, previously executed exactly once per trace in a fixed order that ended with A31 handing over to A20, are now executed about 3.5 times per trace, with A31 passing back to A25 in about 71 in 100 of its executions and leaving to A20 in the remainder. The repetition is shown directly by the occurrence figures (mean 1.0 to about 3.5 per trace, repeats in about 68 in 100 traces, identically for all three activities), the loop mechanism is observed directly in A31's outgoing transitions, and the change from fixed A25-before-A31 order to interleaved/repeated execution together with the dissolution of the rigid two-step spacing agrees with that picture. Eight control-flow items qualified against fewer than one expected by chance among the 60 tested units, so the pattern as a whole is not what an unchanged process typically produces; the positional spread should be read as part of the same underlying behaviour, not as an independent confirmation. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, DIR001, REL001, LAG001, POS001, POS002]

Limitations: there is no relational evidence for A26, so its place within the repeated segment — and the exact order of activities inside each repetition — remains unresolved; and about 32 in 100 later traces still show the earlier single-pass A25-before-A31 order, so the evidence cannot distinguish a uniformly repeating behaviour from one present in only part of the cases. Both windows are also finite samples of 200 traces each. [REL001, OCC001]

Other differences: none beyond the above; the small forward shift of the median positions is treated as part of the same repetition behaviour, not as a separate relocation. [POS001, POS002]
