### Observed differences

- The choice of successor after A06 shifted. In the earlier window, about 52 in 100 of A06's observed outgoing transitions went to A08, with roughly a quarter each to A09 (about 26) and A07 (about 22); in the later window, A07 was the most common successor at about 42 in 100, A09 rose to about 32, and A08 fell to about 26. All three successors were well represented in both windows (90 and 96 observed outgoing transitions), so this is a redistribution among existing paths rather than a path appearing or disappearing. [DIR001]
- A07 also came directly after A06 more often. Among traces where A07 followed A06 within five steps, immediate succession (a gap of one position) accounted for about 43 in 100 cases in the earlier window but about 78 in 100 in the later window, while a two-step gap fell from about 57 to about 22 in 100 (47 and 51 such traces). [LAG001]
- Both differences were larger than in about 199 of 200 random re-splits of the pooled data, and both describe the same A06→A07 transition, so they point in the same direction rather than representing two unrelated changes. [DIR001, LAG001]

### Inference

Support level: Insufficient evidence
The evidence is insufficient to infer a change in the process. Two qualifying control-flow items is the kind of count an unchanged process still produces at these thresholds — about 0.32 items were expected by chance among the 65 control-flow units tested — so even the mutually consistent A06→A07 pattern described above cannot be separated from sampling variation between the two windows. [DIR001, LAG001]
