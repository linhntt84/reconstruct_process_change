### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, the elapsed time from the completion of A16, A17, A18 or A19 to the next completion of A26 was consistently about one hour (means 1.01–1.04 hours, medians near 1.0 hour). In the later window, all four intervals sit at about half an hour (means 0.51–0.52 hours), a drop of roughly 0.5 hours, and the spread around these times narrowed (standard deviations from about 0.13–0.16 hours to about 0.05–0.09 hours). Each interval appears in roughly 20–30 of 100 traces per window (pair counts 46→59, 49→50, 50→50 and 55→41 out of 200 traces), so the change lies in the duration of these intervals rather than in how often the pairs occur. - The outgoing transitions of A06 were redistributed: A08 accounted for about 52 in 100 of them in the earlier window and about 26 in 100 in the later window, while A07 rose from about 22 to about 42 in 100 and A09 from about 26 to about 32 in 100; A06 itself appears in about 45–48 of 100 traces in both windows. ### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

Main hypothesis: a change in timing — in the later window, the step ending at the completion of A26 occurs about half an hour sooner after the completion of A16, A17, A18 or A19 than it did before, with the interval shrinking from about one hour to about half an hour. Supporting signs: all four intervals move in the same direction with nearly the same magnitude; the time family produced 4 qualifying items where about 0.01 would be expected if nothing had changed, so the pattern as a whole is far beyond what random re-splits of stable behaviour produce, and each item's difference exceeds essentially all of its 2000 reference comparisons; support per pair is moderate (41–59 observations per window). A consistent change in these elapsed times is a hypothesis in its own right and needs no structural counterpart; none is required here. Limitations: TWAIT measures elapsed time between consecutive completions, so the evidence cannot tell whether the shorter interval reflects less waiting, a faster handover, or faster processing; and because the four intervals share only the endpoint A26, the evidence cannot separate a change at A26 from simultaneous changes at all four predecessors. The main competing explanation — a chance sampling difference between the windows — is effectively ruled out at the family level, but the evidence cannot exclude that this timing shift is one facet of a broader scheduling change extending beyond these four intervals. Other differences: the redistribution of A06's outgoing transitions (A08 down, A07 and A09 up) rests on a single control-flow item where about 0.28 would be expected by chance in an unchanged process; it is therefore within what a stable process typically produces and is not added to the main hypothesis.
