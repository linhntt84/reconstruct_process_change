### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility of a timing change around A17: in the later window, the elapsed time from the preceding completion to the completion of A17 was about twice as long as before, both when the preceding completion was A18 (mean about 0.18 versus 0.38 hours) and when it was A29 (mean about 0.91 versus 1.81 hours). Supporting signs: both interval distributions moved upward as a whole rather than only in the tail, both differences exceed more than 999 of 1,000 random re-splits, and the time family produced 2 qualifying items where about 0.007 would be expected if nothing had changed, so chance alone cannot account for the family's yield. Since TWAIT measures elapsed time between consecutive completions, this consistent shift is a timing hypothesis in its own right and needs no structural counterpart. [TWAIT001, TWAIT002]

Limitations and competing explanation: both measured transitions end at A17, so the two items may describe one underlying change around A17 rather than two independent ones — the evidence cannot distinguish a change specific to these transitions from a broader timing shift into A17. The samples behind them are also moderate (27 versus 23 and 50 versus 45 intervals), and the measure cannot show which part of each elapsed interval grew. [TWAIT001, TWAIT002]

Other differences: A43 repeated less often in the later window while remaining present in every trace (mean occurrences per trace about 1.9 versus 1.6; recurrence in about 49 versus 37 of 100 traces, a difference larger than in about 995 of 1,000 re-splits). This is the single control-flow item, in a family for which about 0.27 items would be expected by chance without any change, so on its own it is consistent with sampling variation and is not folded into the main hypothesis.
