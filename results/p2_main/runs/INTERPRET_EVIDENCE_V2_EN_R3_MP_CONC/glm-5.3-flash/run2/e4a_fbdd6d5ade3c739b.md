### Observed differences

- The elapsed time between consecutive completion events running from A11 to A32 roughly halved: in the earlier window these intervals averaged about 1.0 hours (median about 0.99 h, middle half between 0.87 and 1.09 h), while in the later window they averaged about 0.51 hours (median about 0.51 h, middle half between 0.45 and 0.58 h) — a decrease of about 0.48 hours, measured on 105 intervals before and 111 after; only about 1 in 2000 random re-splits produced a difference this large [TWAIT001].
- The same drop appears from A31 to A32: about 0.98 hours on average before (median about 0.96 h) versus about 0.48 hours after (median about 0.47 h), a decrease of about 0.50 hours on 40 versus 36 intervals, again larger than about 1999 of 2000 reference comparisons [TWAIT002].
- In both pairs the intervals also became more uniform, with the standard deviation falling from about 0.19 hours to about 0.07–0.10 hours, while the number of intervals changed only slightly (105 to 111, and 40 to 36) [TWAIT001, TWAIT002].

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a specific timing hypothesis centred on activity A32: in the later window, the elapsed time from the preceding completion event to the completion of A32 is about half of what it was earlier — roughly 0.5 hours instead of roughly 1.0 hour — and this holds both when the preceding completion is A11 and when it is A31 [TWAIT001, TWAIT002]. Supporting signs: both measured pairs move in the same direction with nearly the same magnitude (about 0.48 and 0.50 hours), the intervals became less variable, both differences exceed almost all random re-splits, and the screening comparison is decisive — two time items qualified where about 0.009 would be expected if nothing had changed, while the control-flow family produced no items (0 against about 0.26 expected) [TWAIT001, TWAIT002]. Limitations: both items end at A32, so they most likely describe one underlying change rather than two independent ones, and T_WAIT measures only elapsed time between consecutive completions, so it cannot show whether the half hour was saved in the gap before A32 or in A32's own handling [TWAIT001, TWAIT002]. A competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that the composition of consecutive completion pairs shifted — for example, another activity's completions now fall between the predecessor and A32 — which would shorten the measured gap without A32 being completed sooner after the same predecessor; no relational or positional evidence is available to exclude this, although no control-flow item qualified [TWAIT001, TWAIT002].

Other differences: none beyond the timing change; the small shifts in interval counts (105 to 111, and 40 to 36) are minor relative to the sample sizes [TWAIT001, TWAIT002].
