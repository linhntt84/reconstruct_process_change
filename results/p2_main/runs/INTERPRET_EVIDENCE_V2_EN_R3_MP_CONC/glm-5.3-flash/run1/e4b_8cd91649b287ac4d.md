### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a reallocation of work as the main hypothesis. A17 moved from being handled by a broad pool of roughly 45 resources, none holding more than about 14 in 100 of its assignments, to being handled exclusively by User_84 in the later window. A01's work was also redistributed: its previous lead resource User_104 dropped from about 16 to about 8 in 100, with the freed share absorbed by User_61 and several resources not previously involved, and User_84 also gained a small share (about 4 in 100) of A01. The signs supporting this are direct (assignment and concentration indicators measure allocation itself), mutually consistent for A17, quantitatively far beyond the chance expectation for the resource family, and based on large observation counts in both windows. Note that the two A17 items describe the same underlying behaviour, so they are one line of evidence rather than two independent confirmations. The evidence cannot show why the allocation changed, and it cannot rule out that a changed mix of cases in the later window happens to be routed to User_84 rather than a deliberate reassignment; A01's shift is also only a redistribution within a still-broad pool.

Other differences: none — no control-flow or timing item reached the thresholds, so structure and event timing show no qualifying change.
