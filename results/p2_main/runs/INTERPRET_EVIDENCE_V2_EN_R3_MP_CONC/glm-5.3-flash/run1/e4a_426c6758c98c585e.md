### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, A15, A17, A18, A19, A20 and A21 each had several observed successors: about 62% of A15's observed outgoing transitions went to A18 with about 12–14% each to A19, A20 and A21; A18 sent about 53% to A27; A19 sent about 55% to A21 but also about 28% to A17 and 10% to A15; A26 split about 50/50 between A19 and A17. In the later window, each of these activities has essentially one successor: about 99–100% of observed transitions go from A15 to A18, from A17 to A15, from A18 to A19, from A19 to A21, from A21 to A20, from A20 to A27, and from A26 to A17. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006, DIR007]
- In the later window these transitions form a single fixed chain, A26 → A17 → A15 → A18 → A19 → A21 → A20 → A27: adjacent pairs occur exactly one step apart in about 99–100 in 100 cases (for example A17→A15 in about 995 in 1,000, previously about 56 in 100; A20→A27 in about 995 in 1,000, previously about 47 in 100). [LAG001, LAG006, LAG017, LAG020, LAG022, LAG025]
- Pair orders changed from mixed to fixed. Earlier, A17 and A19 occurred in both orders in equal shares, A15 and A21 about 50/50, and A19 ran before A15 in about 75 in 100 cases; in the later window one order holds in essentially every trace (A15 before A19 in about 995 in 1,000, A18 before A19 in about 995 in 1,000, A17 before A19 in 100 in 100). Both activities of each pair still occur together in every trace in both windows, so participation did not change — only the order did. [REL001, REL003, REL004, REL006, REL008]
- Distances became fixed as well. Earlier, gaps spread over one to five positions (A15 to A20 over one to four steps; A17 to A20 over one to five); in the later window each pair sits at a single gap matching its chain position (A15→A19 always two steps, A15→A21 always three, A15→A27 and A17→A20 always five). Chain-direction pairs also went from being observable in only about a quarter to half of traces to nearly all (A18 before A21 within five steps: 61 of 200 traces earlier, 199 of 200 later). [LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG005, LAG009, LAG010, LAG012, LAG029]
- Relative positions moved consistently with this: A15, A17 and A18 shifted earlier (mean relative position about 0.64→0.59, 0.59→0.56, 0.68→0.62) while A19, A20 and A21 shifted later (about 0.59→0.65, 0.68→0.71, 0.64→0.68). [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the ordering constraints among A15, A17, A18, A19, A20 and A21 — entered from A26 and ending at A27 — changed from a mixed, case-dependent order to one fixed sequence, A26 → A17 → A15 → A18 → A19 → A21 → A20 → A27. Earlier, these activities co-occurred in every trace but in varying orders with two to four observed successors each; in the later window each has one observed successor, every pair follows the chain order, and every pair distance matches its position in the chain at ranges of one to five steps. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006, DIR007, REL001, REL004, REL006, LAG001, LAG002, LAG004, LAG009, LAG029]

This is beyond what an unchanged process typically produces: 50 control-flow items qualified against roughly 0.47 expected by chance, each item's difference exceeds about 995 of 1,000 random re-splits, and the items are mutually consistent, since one chain reproduces every successor profile, pair order and pair distance. [DIR001, DIR003, DIR005, REL001, REL006, LAG002, LAG004, LAG009, LAG029]

Limitations: the order and gap indicators use event positions, not clock time, so they establish sequence rather than timing; the earlier mixed order could reflect one flexible process or a mixture of several variants, which the evidence cannot distinguish; and a few reverse-direction gap profiles (for example A19→A18 and A26→A20) rest on a single after-window observation each and were not relied on. [LAG014, LAG015, LAG019, LAG021, LAG028]

Other differences: On a separate part of the process, the elapsed time between consecutive completion events roughly doubled for two pairs into A29: for A22→A29 the mean rose from about 0.9 to about 1.9 hours (98 observations in each window) and for A30→A29 from about 0.2 to about 0.4 hours (102 in each); both rank among the largest differences in 2,000 reference comparisons, and the time family produced 2 items against about 0.006 expected without change. This timing change is a hypothesis in its own right; the evidence provides no link between it and the reordering. [TWAIT001, TWAIT002]
