### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the handover out of A22 changed: in the later window A22 leads directly to A21 in a majority of its executions (about 58 in 100, up from about 18 in 100), while A25, previously the typical immediate successor (about 53 in 100), is now immediate in only about 13 in 100 and instead follows A22 two or three steps later; A23's share is essentially unchanged at about 29 in 100. The change is shown directly by A22's successor redistribution and consistently mirrored by the shifted distance distribution of the A22–A25 pair. [DIR001, LAG003]

Chance is an unlikely explanation for the evidence set as a whole: the control-flow family produced 4 qualifying items where an unchanged process would typically produce about 0.34, and each item's difference exceeds that of about 199 of 200 random re-splits; no timing items qualified (0 against 0.005 expected). [DIR001, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003]

Limitations: both A22 items rest on the same traces (identical support of 49 before and 31 after), so the hypothesis effectively hangs on one behavioural signal seen twice, and the later window contributes only 31 A22 transitions. The evidence also cannot show what now occupies the intermediate steps between A22 and A25 — an inserted A21 is a plausible reading but not an observed chain — and a sampling-driven swing in a minority branch cannot be fully ruled out, since A22 appears in only a minority of traces in both windows. [DIR001, LAG003]

Other differences: the local ordering around A16 shifted independently of the main hypothesis — A17 now typically follows A16 immediately (about 78 in 100, up from about 59), while A18 typically follows one step later (immediate succession down from about 78 to about 57 in 100). The two items move consistently with each other, but no direct successor-distribution evidence for A16 is available, so this remains a demonstrated distance shift rather than a demonstrated re-routing.
