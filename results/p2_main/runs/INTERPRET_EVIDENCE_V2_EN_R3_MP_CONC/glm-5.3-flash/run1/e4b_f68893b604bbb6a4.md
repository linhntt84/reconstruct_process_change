### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the process's resource allocation changed. Main hypothesis: work on activities A01, A03, A13, A14, A17 and A22 was reassigned from one group of resources, previously led by User_37 and User_39, to a largely different group, while activity A11, previously shared across 18 resources, became handled exclusively by User_115. Supporting signs: the resource family produced 8 qualifying items where fewer than 0.01 would be expected if nothing had changed — a count many times the chance expectation, which random splitting cannot explain as a whole; all items point in the same direction (redistribution); the same resources decline and a common new set rises across all six activities; and stable observation volumes argue against a shift in activity frequency. Limitations: the resource codes are anonymous, so no roles, teams or mechanisms (routing rule versus roster change) can be inferred, and because the items likely express one underlying reassignment they are not independent confirmations. A competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that the two windows simply sample different natural staffing periods; the cross-activity consistency makes pure sampling fluctuation unlikely, but a gradual rotation outside the observed windows cannot be excluded.

Other differences: none are reported — the control-flow and time families produced no qualifying items (0 against about 0.17 and 0.02 expected by chance), so no structural or timing difference is indicated at these thresholds.
