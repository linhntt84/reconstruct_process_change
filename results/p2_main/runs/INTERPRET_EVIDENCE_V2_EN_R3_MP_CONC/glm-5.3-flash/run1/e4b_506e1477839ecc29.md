### Observed differences

- The largest difference is in who executes five activities. In the earlier window, A01, A03, A13, A14 and A22 were each spread over a wide pool of resources — no resource above about 10%, with roughly 39–50% of assignments in a long tail of minor resources. In the later window, a group of five-to-six resources that had each held roughly 4–8% of these activities (including User_3, with about 6.5% of A01) no longer appears on them at all, while a largely distinct group of four-to-six resources that previously had no share took over a comparable combined slice (about 25–30% per activity) — for example User_56 went from none to about 7% of A01, about 7% of A03 and about 9% of A22. User_67's share grew at all five activities, most strongly at A22 (about 10% to about 17%). Each of the five differences is larger than all but about 1 in 2,000 random re-splits of the pooled data. ### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the handling of activities A01, A03, A13, A14 and A22 was handed over from one group of resources to another: in the later window, resources that previously each covered roughly 4–8% of these activities (including User_3) no longer handle them, a largely new group (including User_56) covers similar shares, and User_67's share rose at all five activities. This rests on direct assignment-profile evidence for well-observed activities; the resource family produced 5 qualifying items against roughly 0.005 expected if nothing had changed — an excess chance cannot account for as a whole; all five items agree in direction (the same resources gain everywhere, the same resources drop out); the activities' occurrence was stable, so participation is not the driver; and no control-flow item qualified (0 against about 0.18 expected), consistent with a change confined to resource allocation. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005]

Limitations: A13 and A14 show identical profiles in both windows, so the five items likely include duplicated behaviour rather than five independent confirmations; the profiles resolve only the ten largest resources, leaving about 29–50% of assignments in an unlabelled remainder; and the resource codes are anonymous, so no roles, people or teams can be inferred. An important competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that the swap is a by-product of a shift in which cases reach these activities (a changed case mix reaching different resources) rather than a reallocation of the activities themselves. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005]

Other differences: the elapsed time between consecutive completions on the A09→A23 pair increased (median from about 1 hour to about 14 hours; 29 vs 25 observed intervals). This is the time family's only qualifying item (against roughly 0.016 expected by chance), so it suggests a timing change on this connection but cannot by itself exclude alternatives such as a changed mix of cases passing through A09 and A23.
