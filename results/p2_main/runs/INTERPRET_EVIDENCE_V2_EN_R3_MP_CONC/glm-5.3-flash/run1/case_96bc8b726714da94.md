### Observed differences

- In the earlier window the process split after A26 into two mutually exclusive branches: A27 ran in about half of the traces (about 99 of 200), while A32, A33, A38, A39 and A40 each ran in about half (about 102–103 of 200) and co-occurred with one another in about 51 in 100 traces. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, REL018]
- A27 and each of those five activities were alternatives before — they shared a trace in only about 1 in 100 cases. In the later window A27 is present in about 99 in 100 traces, while the five activities appear in only about 2–3 in 100 traces each (3–5 of 200). [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, REL008, REL009, REL010, REL011, REL012]
- Earlier, the five activities formed one block: they ran together in no fixed order (each order in roughly a quarter of traces), typically after A26 (about 50 in 100) and before A28 (about 50 in 100). [REL002, REL013, REL018, REL022, REL027]
- In the later window, flow from A26 concentrates on A27: about 96 in 100 of A26's outgoing transitions lead to A27, versus about 48 in 100 earlier, and the chain A26 → A27 → A28 holds in about 98–99 in 100 traces. [DIR001, REL001, REL007]
- Minor positional shifts: A26 now sits slightly later in the trace (mean relative position about 0.46 to 0.49) and A28 slightly earlier (about 0.58 to 0.56). [POS001, POS002]
- The after-window routing, position and lag profiles of A32, A33, A38, A39 and A40 rest on only 1–5 observations each and cannot be meaningfully interpreted. [DIR002, POS003, LAG001]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the branch executing A32, A33, A38 and A39 together with A40 between A26 and A28 — previously taken in about half of the cases — is now skipped in nearly all cases (about 2–3 in 100 still run it), while A27, previously the alternative branch, now executes in nearly every case between A26 and A28. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, REL008, REL009, REL010, REL011, REL012, DIR001, REL001, REL007]

This rests on direct and mutually consistent signs: participation flips with substantial support in both windows (about 99 vs 198 traces for A27; about 103 vs 3–5 for the five activities), the exclusivity between A27 and each of the five inverts to near-universal A27-only traces, A26's outgoing flow concentrates on A27, and the A26 → A27 → A28 ordering appears in about 98–99 in 100 later traces. With 63 qualifying items against roughly 0.8 expected without change, chance sampling cannot account for the evidence as a whole. [OCC001, OCC002, REL008, DIR001, REL001, REL007]

Limitations: the evidence shows the old branch became rare but cannot distinguish whether it was removed from the process or remains a rarely-taken path, since it still occurs in about 2–3 in 100 later traces; the later-window behaviour of the five activities themselves cannot be characterised from 1–5 observations. A competing explanation that cannot be ruled out from two windows alone is that the later window captured a temporary, atypical dip in the branch's use rather than a lasting routing change, although the size and consistency of the shifts make pure sampling noise implausible. [OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, DIR002]

Other differences: the small positional shifts of A26 (later) and A28 (earlier) are consistent with the block between them no longer running, but position-based measures alone cannot confirm this. [POS001, POS002]
