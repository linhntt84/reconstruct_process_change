### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, A30, A31 and A36 each occurred about 3.5 times per trace and appeared in every trace, with repeats in about 75 in 100 traces; in the later window each occurs about once per trace (mean roughly 1.05), still in every trace, but repeats are seen in only about 1 in 100 traces. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003]
- A36's dominant successor flipped: before, about 70 in 100 of its outgoing transitions led to A30 and about 28 in 100 to A25; in the later window about 92 in 100 lead to A25 and only about 4 in 100 to A30 (based on 690 versus 210 outgoing transitions). [DIR002]
- The order between A30 and A36 changed from mixed to fixed: before, the two were interleaved or repeated in about 76 in 100 traces (A30 first in about 25 in 100); in the later window A30 precedes A36 in about 98 in 100 traces, while the two still occur together in every trace in both windows. [REL001]
- A36 now follows A30 at a short distance: a gap of two events in about 89 in 100 later traces, versus about 12 in 100 before. The reverse relation (A36 before A30) and A36 self-repetition have practically disappeared, though the later window has only 1 and 2 supporting observations for these, so they are weak on their own. [LAG001, LAG002, LAG003]
- A30's own routing is essentially unchanged: A31 remains its dominant successor (about 99 in 100 of its outgoing transitions before, about 97 in 100 after), and the relative positions of A30 and A36 barely moved; their position distributions only became tighter (spread shrinking from about 0.12 to about 0.07). [DIR001, POS001, POS002]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the repeated execution of a block containing A30, A31 and A36 stopped. In the earlier window these three activities each ran about 3–4 times per trace in an interleaved pattern in which A36 typically returned to A30; in the later window each runs once, A30 precedes A36 in a fixed order at a gap of about two events, and A36 proceeds to A25 instead of looping back to A30. This is shown directly by the collapse in occurrences and recurrence while participation stayed at every trace, and corroborated by the successor flip at A36 and the fixed A30-before-A36 order; with 11 qualifying items against roughly 0.47 expected by chance, random sampling cannot explain the pattern as a whole. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, DIR002, REL001, LAG001]

Limitations: the items largely describe one underlying trace behaviour rather than independent confirmations; the reverse-order and self-repetition items rest on only 1–2 later observations; and the evidence cannot distinguish a removed loop from the pre-existing exit A36 to A25 simply always being taken after a single pass, nor a sequential loop from stopped concurrent interleaving. [LAG002, LAG003, DIR002]

Other differences: only a minor redistribution among A30's rare outgoing edges and a tightening of the position spreads of A30 and A36 without a meaningful position shift. [DIR001, POS001, POS002]
