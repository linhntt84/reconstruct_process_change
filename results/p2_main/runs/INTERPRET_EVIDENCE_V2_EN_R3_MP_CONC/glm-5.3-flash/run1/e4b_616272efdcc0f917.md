### Observed differences

- Resource allocation shifted broadly. In the earlier window, assignments for A01, A03, A13, A14, A17 and A22 were dispersed: no single resource held more than about 13% of its activity's assignments, and roughly 40–50% went to a long tail of others. In the later window, a consistent group of resources took the largest shares — User_3 rose from about 3% to about 12–15% and User_78 from under about 4% to about 6–9% — while about seven resources that previously held roughly 3–8% each lost most or all of their share. The same direction of shift appears in all six activities. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, ASSIGN007]
- A11 moved from shared to exclusive handling: earlier, its roughly 110 assignments were spread over 23 distinct resources, with the largest share (User_100) at about 15%; later, all of its roughly 100 assignments went to User_100 alone (top-resource share 100%, concentration index 1.0). [ASSIGN003, CONC001]
- The mix of outgoing transitions from A10 shifted: A16 fell from about 57% to about 44% of observed transitions, A07 rose from about 10% to about 24%, and A11 fell from about 17% to about 11% (220 transitions earlier, 258 later). This is the only control-flow item, a count in line with its family's chance expectation, so it may be sampling variation. [DIR001]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that resource allocation was reorganised: work on A01, A03, A13, A14, A17 and A22 moved from a dispersed set of resources to a smaller gaining group (including User_3 and User_78), and A11, previously shared across 23 resources, became handled exclusively by User_100. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, ASSIGN007, CONC001]

The screening comparison supports this: the resource family produced 8 qualifying items against roughly 0.006 expected without a change — far beyond what chance would produce — while control flow (1 item against about 0.2) and time (0 against about 0.014) are at chance levels. [DIR001, ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, ASSIGN007, CONC001]

The hypothesis is direct and consistent: the allocation indicators measure the changed behaviour itself, all seven assignment profiles shift in the same direction with the same resources gaining and losing, and each profile rests on roughly 100–280 assignments per window, so the pattern is unlikely to be a rare-variant artefact. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, ASSIGN007, CONC001]

Limitations: the evidence cannot show whether the broad reallocation and A11's concentration stem from one underlying reallocation or two separate ones, nor whether the A10 routing shift is connected to them; the resource codes are anonymous, so the form of the reallocation cannot be determined from this evidence. [ASSIGN003, CONC001, DIR001]

Other differences: the shares of A10's outgoing transitions moved (A16 down about 13 points, A07 up about 14 points); as a single control-flow item at a chance-level count for its family, this difference alone is not enough to infer a structural change. [DIR001]
