### Observed differences

- Before, ten activities appeared in every trace exactly once: A28, A29, A30, A32 and A33 (first group) and A34–A38 (second group). In the later window each of them appears in only about half of the traces — the first group in about 46 in 100 cases (92 of 200) and the second group in about 55 in 100 cases (109 of 200). [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, OCC007, OCC008, OCC009, OCC010]

- Before, the two groups always ran together in the same trace, with every first-group activity preceding every second-group activity. In the later window they are mutually exclusive: they co-occur in about 1 in 200 traces, about 45 in 100 traces contain only first-group activities, and about 54 in 100 contain only second-group activities. Within each group the order stays mixed (both orders in similar proportions) in both windows. [REL025, REL033, REL040, REL046, REL051, REL021, REL056]

- A25 and A26 still occur in every trace in both windows, with A25 before the block and the block before A26 wherever they co-occur. Before, A25's observed outgoing transitions went only to the five first-group activities (about 18–24% each); in the later window they spread over all ten block activities (about 7–13% each), so A25 now enters either group directly. [DIR001, REL001, REL006, REL011, REL016]

- First-group activities gained a direct link to A26: A28, A29 and A33 now send about 22–26% of their observed outgoing transitions to A26, which was not among their observed successors before, when their flow instead continued into second-group activities. [DIR002, DIR003, DIR004]

- Relative event positions: A25's mean position moved later, from about 0.55 to about 0.64 of the trace; first-group activities, when they occur, now sit later (mean about 0.63 to about 0.72–0.73) and second-group activities slightly earlier (about 0.76 to about 0.72–0.74), so both groups now occupy the same mid-late region; A26 is nearly unchanged (about 0.84 to about 0.82). [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS008, POS012]

- Pairs of a first-group activity followed within five steps by a second-group activity, previously spread over lags 1–5 in roughly 110–130 traces, appear in only 1 later trace; the after-side distributions of these items rest on that single observation. [LAG001, LAG003, LAG009, LAG015]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the ten-activity block A28–A38, previously executed in full in every case (first A28, A29, A30, A32 and A33 in mixed order, then A34–A38 in mixed order, then A26), became conditional: in the later window roughly 45 in 100 cases execute only the first group and then pass directly to A26, roughly 54 in 100 execute only the second group and then A26, and the two groups now behave as alternatives rather than sequential stages, while A25 still opens and A26 still closes every case. [REL025, OCC001, OCC006, DIR001, DIR002, REL011]

This hypothesis is directly and consistently supported across indicator families: participation of all ten activities fell from 100% to about 46–55%; cross-group co-occurrence collapsed from 100% to about 0.5%; A28, A29 and A33 show new direct transitions to A26 and A25 now feeds both groups; the two groups' positions converge. Chance is an unlikely explanation for the evidence as a whole, since 106 items qualified against about 1 expected without change, and every item ranks above about 199 of 200 random re-splits — though the many relation items describe one underlying trace behaviour rather than independent confirmations. The change is one of participation and routing (each group became skippable, with the remaining group connecting straight to A26), not a reordering: within-group order stays mixed and the A25-before-block and block-before-A26 relations are preserved. [OCC001, OCC006, REL025, DIR002, DIR001, POS003, POS008]

An important competing explanation remains: the later window may mix two distinct case populations (for example two process variants running in parallel) rather than a single process with a routing choice, since the before window shows only the full-block behaviour. In addition, the second group's onward connection to A26 is inferred from co-occurrence order because no direction item with a second-group source qualified, and the lag items rest on a single after observation. [REL025, REL016, LAG001]
