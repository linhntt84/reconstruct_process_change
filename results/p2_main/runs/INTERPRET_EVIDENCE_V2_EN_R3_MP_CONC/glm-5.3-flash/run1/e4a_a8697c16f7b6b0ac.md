### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

A change in timing is supported: in the later window, the elapsed time between consecutive completions linking A16, A17, A18 and A19 to A26 is roughly halved, from about one hour to about half an hour. The signs are direct and consistent — all four pair-level items show the same reduction in mean, median and quartiles with essentially maximal empirical strength, the profiles rest on reasonably many observations (about 41–59 gaps per pair per window), and the time family's 4 qualifying items are hundreds of times its chance expectation, so the pattern as a whole is not what random re-splits produce. Limitations: all four items share A26, so they may reflect one underlying difference rather than four independent confirmations; TWAIT measures elapsed time between consecutive completion events, not pure waiting or service time; and the evidence cannot show whether the faster pace originates on the A16–A19 side or on the A26 side of the pairs. A competing explanation the evidence cannot fully rule out is a small structural reordering around A26 that changed which completions count as consecutive, although the roughly stable gap counts argue against a large structural shift. Other differences: A06's outgoing transitions shifted from A08 towards A07, with A09 gaining slightly (A08 from about half to about a quarter of transitions, A07 from about 22 to about 42 in 100). Because this is a single control-flow item at roughly its family's chance expectation, it may equally be a sampling fluctuation of an unchanged process, and it is not folded into the main hypothesis.
