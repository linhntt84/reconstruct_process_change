### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that resource allocation changed: A11 moved from being shared across about 21 resources (top share about 13 in 100 cases) to being assigned exclusively to User_123, and the work on A03 and A21 shifted toward User_3, whose share roughly doubled for A03 (about 8 to 19 in 100) and almost tripled for A21 (about 11 to 30 in 100). The signs are direct and consistent: assignment shares and concentration are measured on 96–200 traces per activity, A11's presence in traces was similar in both windows (about 48 vs 57 in 100), so this is a change in who performs the work rather than in how often the activity runs, and the resource family produced 4 qualifying items where about 0.006 would be expected without a change — far beyond what a stable process yields; each item's difference also exceeds all but about 1 in 2,000 random re-splits. Limitations: the resource codes are anonymous, so the evidence cannot show whether the concentration reflects a new assignment rule, changed availability of other resources, or a change in how assignments were recorded; the two A11 items describe the same traces and therefore form one line of evidence rather than two, and the exact sizes of the milder A03 and A21 shifts each rest on a single indicator. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, CONC001]

Other differences: the A04-to-A01 elapsed time shortened (median about 70 hours to about 12 hours on 200 traces per window; the time family produced 1 item against about 0.017 expected by chance). This is a timing difference in its own right; nothing in the evidence links it to the reallocation, since it concerns different activities, and elapsed time between completions cannot separate a shorter wait from faster work in between. [TWAIT001, screening]
