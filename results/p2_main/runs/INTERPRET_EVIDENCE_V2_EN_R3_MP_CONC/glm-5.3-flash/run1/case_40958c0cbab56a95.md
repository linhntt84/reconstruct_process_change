### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the mandatory segment between A44 and A29 changed structurally: instead of always executing the three activities A26, A27 and A28 together (in varying order) between A44 and A29, traces in the later window execute exactly one of the three mutually exclusive alternatives A19, A20 or A21, which then leads on to A29 [OCC005, OCC006, OCC007, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, REL013, REL014, REL021, DIR007, DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, REL018, REL019, REL025, REL026, REL032, REL033, LAG009]. The signs are direct and consistent: A44 now points to A19/A20/A21 instead of A26/A27/A28; the new activities lead to A29 in about 95 in 100 of their observed transitions; A44 precedes and A29 follows the chosen activity in essentially every trace where it occurs; the A44-to-A29 distance contracted from 4–5 to 2–3 steps; and A29's and A44's mean positions shifted only slightly (about 0.44 to 0.40 and 0.31 to 0.33), as expected for a shorter intermediate segment [POS007, POS008]. With 81 qualifying items against fewer than 1 expected for an unchanged process (control-flow family), the overall volume also far exceeds what random re-splits produce.

Limitations: the earlier-window behaviour of A19, A20 and A21 rests on only 2–4 traces, so their pre-change routing is essentially unobserved; the evidence cannot distinguish a genuine replacement of activities from the same work appearing under different labels; and the old block still shows up in about 2 in 100 later traces, so it may persist as a rare path [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, OCC005, OCC006, OCC007].
