- In the earlier window, the elapsed time between consecutive completion events running from each of A03, A04, A05, A06, A07 and A08 to A09 averaged about 0.94–1.00 hours (medians about 0.93–1.04 hours); in the later window, these same intervals average about 0.49–0.51 hours (medians about 0.48–0.53 hours) — roughly halved for every one of the six pairs. ### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a change in timing: in the later window, the elapsed time between consecutive completion events from each of the activities A03, A04, A05, A06, A07 and A08 to A09 is about half an hour instead of about one hour, and the intervals are about twice as consistent. The time family produced six qualifying items where only about 0.02 would be expected if nothing had changed — a count far above the chance expectation — and all six agree in direction and size (reductions of about 0.46–0.50 hours), each larger than in all but about 1 in 2000 random re-splits of the pooled data, with each estimate resting on 27–42 observed gaps per window. [TWAIT001, TWAIT002, TWAIT003, TWAIT004, TWAIT005, TWAIT006]

Limitations: TWAIT measures elapsed time between consecutive completion events, not pure waiting or service time, so the evidence cannot fully separate a genuine shortening of these intervals from a change in which completions are adjacent (for example, other events falling in between more or less often); the moderate shifts in pair frequency (A05→A09 down from 40 to 27, A07→A09 up from 33 to 42) leave open a small change in event adjacency. No structural counterpart was found — the control-flow family yielded no qualifying items (0 among 56 tested units, against about 0.28 expected by chance) — which is consistent with a pure timing change but does not rule out structural differences below the configured thresholds. [TWAIT001, TWAIT002, TWAIT003, TWAIT004, TWAIT005, TWAIT006]
