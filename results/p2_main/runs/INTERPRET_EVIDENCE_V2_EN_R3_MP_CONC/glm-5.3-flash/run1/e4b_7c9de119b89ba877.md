### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a change in resource allocation for activities A01, A03, A13, A14, A17 and A22: in the later window, work on these activities is concentrated on a smaller set of resources led by User_46 (about 15–19 in 100 assignments, up from 4–6 in 100), while User_39 and a group of other earlier handlers dropped out of the leading shares and newer resources such as User_61 took up about 5–9 in 100. The resource family produced 6 qualifying items where about 0.006 would be expected without change — far above what chance produces — and all six profiles move in the same direction with similar size (dissimilarity about 0.35–0.43, each larger than all but about 1 in 2000 random re-splits of the pooled traces). Participation in the activities was stable, so the change is in assignment, not in how often the activities run. Limitations: A13 and A14 show identical profiles, so the six items are not fully independent; the codes are anonymous, so only the reallocation between resource groups is established; and a shift in the mix of cases reaching these activities, rather than a change in assignment itself, cannot be ruled out with this evidence. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006]

Other differences: A21 was executed in fewer traces in the later window (about 8 in 100 versus about 17 in 100). This is the single control-flow item, against roughly 0.17 items expected by chance in that family, so it is consistent with sampling variation and is not part of the main hypothesis. [OCC001]
