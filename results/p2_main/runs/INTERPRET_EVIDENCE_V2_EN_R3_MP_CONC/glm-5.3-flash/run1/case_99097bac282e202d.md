### Observed differences

- The largest difference is a reversal between two groups of activities. In the earlier window, A21, A22 and A23 each took part in about 75 in 100 traces, while A24, A25 and A26 each took part in about 25 in 100; in the later window, A21, A22 and A23 each take part in about 25 in 100 traces and A24, A25 and A26 in about 75 in 100. Repetition is negligible for all six in both windows. [OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, OCC007, OCC008]
- The two groups behave as either-or alternatives whose shares flipped. For A21 and A24, about 75 in 100 earlier traces contained A21 but not A24 and about 25 in 100 contained A24 but not A21, with both together in at most about 1 in 200; in the later window, about 25 in 100 contain only A21 and about 75 in 100 only A24, with no joint occurrence. The same flipped pattern holds for A22 with A25 and for A23 with A26. [REL015, REL016, REL017, REL022, REL023, REL024, REL028, REL029, REL030]

Within each group, both orders occur in similar proportions in both windows, so there is no fixed sequence inside either group. [REL013, REL014, REL021, REL034, REL035, REL039]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the choice between two existing alternative branches flipped. In the earlier window, roughly 3 in 4 cases executed the branch containing A21, A22 and A23 — typically all three together, in no fixed order — and roughly 1 in 4 executed the branch containing A24, A25 and A26; in the later window, these shares are reversed, about 1 in 4 versus about 3 in 4. A20, executed in virtually every case, is the visible routing point: its outgoing transitions shifted from predominantly the A21–A23 group to predominantly the A24–A26 group, while neither branch disappeared and repetition did not change. [OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, OCC007, OCC008, DIR003, REL015, REL022, REL028]

This is unlikely to be a sampling artefact: 62 evidence items qualified in total, many times the fewer than 1 expected for an unchanged process across the 183 tested control-flow units, and the participation, routing and relation indicators all show the same flip directly. Every activity involved is observed in a substantial share of traces in both windows (at least about 25 in 100), the one-of-two structure per trace is preserved, and only the shares between the existing alternatives shift, which fits a change in how often existing paths are taken rather than activities appearing, disappearing or being bypassed. A30, present in every trace, co-occurs with whichever group is active, consistent with it lying downstream of the branch point. [DIR003, REL015, OCC003, OCC006, REL018, REL036]

An important competing explanation remains: the two windows may draw on different case mixes — for example, different process variants being more common in one period — rather than the routing itself having changed; the evidence also cannot show why the shares flipped. All ordering and position statements rest on event positions, not clock time. [REL015, POS001, LAG001]

Other differences: A10 becomes about three times as frequent (about 21 in 100 later traces versus about 6 in 100 earlier), appears later in traces by event position, and now leads almost exclusively to A33, with A32 feeding into it in about 20 in 100 of A32's outgoing transitions versus about 1 in 100 earlier — a coherent minority-path change resting on 42 later traces. A02 falls to about 1 in 100 later traces from about 9 in 100 earlier, based on only 2 later traces, so this may be a rare variant appearing in one window. Event spacing shifted slightly for some pairs: A20–A24 and A25–A30 gaps moved towards larger distances in the later window, and A32–A33 are immediately consecutive in about 39 in 100 later cases versus about 56 in 100 earlier.
