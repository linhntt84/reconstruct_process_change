### Observed differences

- The starkest difference concerns A16. In the earlier window, its assignments were spread across 19 different resources, with the most used resource handling about 18 in 100 assignments (concentration index 0.09). In the later window, all 143 observed A16 assignments went to a single resource, User_64 (top-resource share 100 in 100, concentration index 1.0). [ASSIGN005, CONC001]

- Across A01, A03, A13, A14 and A17, the later window shows a consistent handover between two groups of resources. A group that together handled roughly 30–35 in 100 assignments in the earlier window — led by User_3 with about 8–9 in 100 — is at or near zero afterwards, while a largely different group — led by User_56, up from essentially zero to about 10–12 in 100 — took over. The long tail of occasional resources kept a similar share (about 44–49 in 100 before, about 42–46 in 100 after), so the overall spread of work across many resources did not change; only which resources did the work changed. ### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the allocation of work to resources changed. In the later window, A16 — previously shared across about 19 resources — is assigned entirely to User_64, and on A01, A03, A13, A14 and A17 a consistent handover moved roughly a third of the assignments from a former group of resources (led by User_3, about 8–9 in 100 before, near zero after) to a largely new group (led by User_56, near zero before, about 10–12 in 100 after), leaving the overall dispersion of work essentially unchanged. This rests on direct allocation evidence: the resource family produced seven qualifying items where about 0.005 would be expected if nothing had changed — an excess chance cannot account for — and every item's difference was larger than in about 1999 of 2000 random re-splits of the pooled data. The A16 shift is corroborated by two indicators computed on the same 143 assignments per window, so they are consistent but not independent confirmations. Limitations: A13 and A14 have identical profiles and likely reflect one underlying behaviour rather than two; the resource codes are anonymous, so no roles or teams can be inferred; and an important competing explanation — that the two windows simply cover a different mix of cases that happen to be routed to different resources — cannot be ruled out, although the same direction of change on five activities and the complete concentration of A16 on one resource make pure sampling noise implausible. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, CONC001]

Other differences: Elapsed time between consecutive completion events on the A04/A01 pair increased (median from about 5.6 to about 17.6 hours; 75th percentile from about 18 to about 45 hours). This is the only time item, in a family where about 0.01 items would be expected by chance without a change, and a single item — however unusual it looks — is also what an unchanged process occasionally produces; the evidence therefore cannot separate a genuine timing change from a chance fluctuation. No control-flow item qualified (0 against about 0.15 expected), so no structural difference is indicated at these thresholds. [TWAIT001]
