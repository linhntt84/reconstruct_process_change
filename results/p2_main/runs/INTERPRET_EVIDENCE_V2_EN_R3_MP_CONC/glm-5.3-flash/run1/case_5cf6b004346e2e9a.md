### Observed differences

- The routing out of A08 changed substantially. In the earlier window, about 50% of A08's observed outgoing transitions went to A10 and about 48% to A11 (A04 marginal, about 2%); in the later window, about 53% went directly to A06 (29%) or A07 (24%), while A10 (27%) and A11 (19%) together accounted for only about 46%. [DIR001]
- A10 and A11 rerouted completely. Earlier, A10's observed outgoing transitions split about evenly between A06 (51%) and A07 (49%), and A11's between A06 (58%) and A07 (42%); in the later window, every observed outgoing transition of both activities went to A08. [DIR002, DIR003]
- Event-position distances match this rerouting. Earlier, A10 and A11 were typically followed by A06/A07 one event later (for example A10→A07 at distance 1 in about 58% of observed pairs); in the later window these gap-1 adjacencies are unobserved and the pairs appear at distances of 2–5. Conversely, gap-1 A10→A08 (about 13%) and A11→A08 (about 21%) appear only in the later window, as do gap-1 A08→A06 (about 13%) and A08→A07 (about 19%), whereas earlier A08 reached A06/A07 mainly at distance 2 (about 23% and 41%). Gap-1 A08→A10 and A08→A11 became rarer (about 37%→13% and 47%→24%). [LAG007, LAG008, LAG010, LAG011, LAG012, LAG013, LAG014, LAG015, LAG016]
- Repetitions of A08 occur closer together in the later window: A08→A08 shifted from mostly distance 3 (about 38%) to distance 2 (about 14%). [LAG009]
- Within the A06/A07 group and for A07→A08, distances spread from one dominant value to several (for example A06→A07 from about 74% at distance 3 to a spread over distances 2–5; A07→A07 from about 92% at distance 5 to distances 4–5), some resting on few observations (about 15 vs 7 and 12 vs 6 pairs). [LAG001, LAG002, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

A structural rerouting around A08, A10 and A11 is supported: in the later window, A10 and A11 no longer hand control over to A06 or A07 but return to A08, so that A08, A10 and A11 form a cycle, while A08 proceeds directly to A06 or A07 in about half of its observed outgoing transitions — a step that earlier ran only indirectly through A10 or A11 (at distance 2 rather than 1). [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, LAG007, LAG008, LAG012, LAG013, LAG014, LAG015, LAG016]

The signs are direct and consistent: the three DIR items show the successor changes on the transitions themselves, each with an empirical strength of about 0.995 (differences larger than in roughly 199 of 200 random re-splits) and based on 123 vs 203 observed transitions for A08, 63 vs 55 for A10 and 60 vs 39 for A11; the LAG distances shift exactly as this rerouting predicts, including A08→A08 moving closer, consistent with a short cycle; and the screening produced 20 qualifying control-flow items where an unchanged process would typically produce about 0.24, so the ensemble is far beyond what chance sampling produces. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006, LAG007, LAG008, LAG009, LAG010, LAG011, LAG012, LAG013, LAG014, LAG015, LAG016, LAG017]

Limitations: the evidence cannot show whether the A08–A10/A11 cycle is optional or how many times it repeats per case, nor whether the new edges are newly inserted or rare pre-existing ones that are now traversed far more often; LAG uses event positions rather than clock time, and many LAG items re-describe the same rerouting rather than confirming it independently; the modest fall in observed A10/A11 transitions (63→55, 60→39) also leaves open that these activities occur somewhat less often overall. [DIR002, DIR003, LAG012, LAG013, LAG015]

Other differences: the distance redistributions within the A06/A07 group, for A07→A08 and for A11→A10 (from mostly distance 3 to distances 2 and 4) are not part of the main hypothesis; they may be downstream effects of the rerouting or separate ordering changes, and several rest on fewer than about 20 observations. [LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006, LAG017]
