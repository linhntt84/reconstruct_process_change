### Observed differences

- The elapsed time from a completion of A40 to the next completion of A15 roughly doubled: in the earlier window these intervals averaged about 2.0 hours (median about 2.0, middle half about 1.9–2.1 hours), while in the later window they averaged about 3.9 hours (median about 3.9, middle half about 3.6–4.3 hours) — an increase of about 1.9 hours per occurrence. [TWAIT001]
- The shift affects the whole distribution rather than a few long cases: every quartile rose by roughly 1.7–2.1 hours and the spread about doubled (standard deviation from about 0.25 to about 0.54 hours). [TWAIT001]
- The difference is far outside what random re-splits of the pooled data produce: only about 10 of 2000 random re-splits showed a larger difference. [TWAIT001]
- Slightly fewer A40→A15 consecutive completion intervals were observed in the later window (61 vs 69, from 200 traces per window). [TWAIT001]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility of a timing change: the interval between consecutive completions of A40 and A15 lengthened from about 2 hours per occurrence in the earlier window to about 3.9 hours in the later window. The sign is direct (the indicator measures exactly this interval), the shift is consistent across the entire distribution, it rests on a substantial number of observations (69 and 61 intervals), and it exceeds about 999.5 in 1000 random re-splits. The screening comparison also fits a pure timing change: the time family produced 1 qualifying item where about 0.009 were expected without change (roughly a hundredfold excess), while the control-flow family produced 0 items where about 0.28 were expected, so no structural counterpart is needed and none was observed. [TWAIT001]

Limitations: TWAIT is elapsed time between consecutive completion events, so the evidence cannot show whether the extra ~1.9 hours is waiting, processing or idle time, and since only the A40–A15 pair was measured it cannot show whether the slowdown is specific to this interval or part of a broader timing shift. A competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is a changed mix of cases between the windows; the distribution-wide, consistent shift makes pure sampling variation unlikely, but a single measured pair limits how far the conclusion generalises. [TWAIT001]

Other differences: none beyond the slightly lower number of observed A40–A15 intervals in the later window (61 vs 69), which, given no qualifying control-flow items, may reflect sampling variation rather than a participation change. [TWAIT001]
