### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that resource allocation changed in the later window while the activities themselves continued at similar rates. A13 moved from being shared across about 45 resources to being performed entirely by User_3, and on A01, A03, A17 and A22 the handler mix shifted, with User_91 taking a roughly doubled share and a group of earlier handlers (for example User_37) giving way to new codes [ASSIGN003, CONC001, ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005].

This goes beyond what chance sampling would produce: 11 resource-allocation units were screened and 6 items qualified, where about 0.005 would be expected if nothing had changed. All six items point to redistribution or concentration of work, the two A13 items agree with each other, and each difference was larger than in all but about 1 of 2000 random re-splits of the pooled data [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, CONC001].

The evidence shows who performed the work, not why it moved; in particular, a shift in the mix of cases — for example variants handled by particular resources becoming more common — could contribute to the redistribution on A01, A03, A17 and A22, and the anonymous resource codes allow no inference about roles or teams [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005].

Other differences: none qualified; the control-flow and timing families produced no items (0 against roughly 0.17 and 0.014 expected without change), so within this screening the qualifying differences are confined to resource allocation [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, CONC001].
