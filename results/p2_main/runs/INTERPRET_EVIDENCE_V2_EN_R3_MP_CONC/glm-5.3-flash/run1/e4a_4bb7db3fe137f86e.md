### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The main hypothesis is a change in timing: in the later window, the elapsed time between consecutive completions of A01 and A03 appears to be about twice as long as before (mean about 0.87 h versus 1.78 h; median about 0.86 h versus 1.83 h), with the entire distribution shifted up by roughly 0.8–1.0 h. Supporting signs: the time family produced one qualifying item where only about 0.012 are expected without change (roughly one per 80 analyses), the difference exceeds about 999.5 in 1000 random re-splits of the pooled data, the shift is consistent across mean, median and both quartiles, and it rests on 42 versus 26 observations. [TWAIT001]

Limitations: this rests on a single indicator for a single activity pair, so nothing can be said about timing elsewhere in the process; the indicator measures elapsed time between consecutive completion events, so the extra ~0.9 h cannot be attributed specifically to waiting or to processing; and the number of consecutive A01→A03 pairs also fell (42 to 26), so the pair occurred in a somewhat different context — the evidence cannot rule out that a structural change around A01 and A03 contributed to both the fewer pairs and the longer gaps rather than a pure slowdown. [TWAIT001]

Other differences: the control-flow items show A06's outgoing transitions shifting away from A08 (about 52 in 100 to 26 in 100) toward A07 (22 to 42) and A09 (26 to 32), with A07 following A06 immediately more often (about 43 in 100 to 78 in 100); both items concern how A07 relates to A06 and may reflect one underlying behaviour. The control-flow family produced 2 items where about 0.3 are expected without change — a count an unchanged process typically produces — so these shifts may be sampling variation and are not established as a change.
