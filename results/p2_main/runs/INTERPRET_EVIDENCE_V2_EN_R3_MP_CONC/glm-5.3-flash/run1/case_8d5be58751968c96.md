### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, A33, A35, A36, A37, A38 and A39 each ran exactly once in every case (participation 100 in 100, essentially never repeated). In the later window, A33, A35 and A36 each appear in about 46–47 in 100 cases and A37, A38 and A39 in about 55–57 in 100, still at most once per case. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006]

- The two trios changed from always running one after the other to being near-mutually exclusive: A33 co-occurred with A37, A38 or A39 in 100 in 100 earlier cases (with A33 first in about 99 in 100), but in only about 2–3 in 100 later cases; in the later window the first trio runs without the second in about 44 in 100 cases, and the second runs without the first in about 53 in 100. The same pattern holds for A35 and A36 against A37/A38/A39. [REL015, REL016, REL017, REL019, REL020, REL021, REL022, REL023, REL024]

- Togetherness and loose internal order within each trio are preserved: A33, A35 and A36 still occur together in about 47 in 100 later cases (in about 53 in 100 none of them occurs) with no fixed order among them, as before; A37, A38 and A39 occur together in about 55–56 in 100 (none in about 43–44), also without a fixed order. [REL013, REL014, REL018, REL025, REL026, REL027]

- A30 and A31 keep their anchor roles: both appear in every case in both windows; A30 still precedes each of the six activities in essentially every case where they co-occur (now about 47 in 100 cases with the first trio and 56 in 100 with the second, versus 100 in 100 before), and the trio members still precede A31 whenever they co-occur (about 45–56 in 100 of later cases). Routing around A30 changed: its observed outgoing transitions went from almost only A33/A35/A36 (about 995 in 1000) to roughly 45 in 100 towards the first trio and 53 in 100 towards the second, and A33, A35 and A36 now lead directly to A31 in about 24–34 in 100 of their outgoing transitions, which was not observed earlier. [REL001, REL004, REL007, REL012, DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, POS001, POS002]

- Event distances shrank accordingly: A30 to A37/A38/A39 spans were always 4–5 steps earlier and are 1–3 steps in about 95–98 in 100 pairs; A33/A35/A36 to A31 spans were always 4–5 steps and are 1–3 steps in about 95–97 in 100 pairs. [LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG007, LAG010]

- Typical event positions moved as traces shortened: A30's mean position shifted later (about 0.62 to 0.67) and A31's slightly earlier (about 0.82 to 0.80); within the cases where they run, A33/A35/A36 sit later (about 0.68 to 0.73–0.74) and A37/A38/A39 slightly earlier (about 0.76–0.77 to 0.74). These are event-position shifts, not clock times. [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006, POS007, POS008]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that, in the later window, the six-activity stretch between A30 and A31 became an exclusive choice between two trios: where every case previously ran A33, A35 and A36 followed by A37, A38 and A39 (each trio in varying internal order), a case now runs either the A33/A35/A36 trio (about 47 in 100 cases) or the A37/A38/A39 trio (about 56 in 100), rarely both (about 2–3 in 100), with A30 before and A31 after unchanged and present in every case. [REL015, REL022, OCC001, OCC004, DIR001, LAG001, LAG004]

The support is direct and consistent: the relational evidence shows the exclusivity itself (cross-trio co-occurrence collapsing from 100 in 100 to about 2–3 in 100 while one-sided occurrence rises to about 44 and 53 in 100, with within-trio co-occurrence and mixed internal order persisting); routing and distance evidence agree (A30 now reaches both trios directly, the executed trio is followed directly by A31, and 4–5-step spans became 1–3 steps); participation drops match the split. Screening qualified 57 control-flow items against fewer than 1 (0.79) expected for an unchanged process, and every item's difference exceeds about 995 of 1000 random re-splits, so the ensemble is far beyond what chance re-splits produce. [REL015, REL019, REL022, REL025, DIR001, DIR002, DIR004, LAG001, LAG004, OCC001, OCC004]

Limitations: the evidence cannot show where or how the either-or choice is made — an upstream routing change before A30 would look identical at the observed level — and the roughly 2–3 in 100 cases that still run both trios rest on only about 4–5 observed pairs, too few to characterise; positional findings concern event positions, not clock time. [REL015, LAG005, LAG006, LAG008, LAG009, LAG011, LAG012, POS001]

Other differences: a few rare outgoing transitions from A30, A33, A35 and A36 to single low-frequency activities appear only in the later window (each about 0.5–1 in 100 of that source's outgoing transitions, i.e. one or two observations) and are too sparse to interpret. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004]
