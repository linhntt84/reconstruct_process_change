### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the allocation of work to resources changed. A16, previously shared across a wide pool of about 18 resources with a top share of about 14 in 100, was in the later window assigned exclusively to User_123. Alongside this, on A01, A03, A13, A14 and A17, assignments moved from one group of resources (together about 25–35 in 100 per activity, including User_77) to a largely different group (together about 30–37 in 100, led by User_49 at about 8 in 100). [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, CONC001]

Supporting signs: the resource family produced seven qualifying items where about 0.006 were expected if nothing had changed — more than a thousand times the chance expectation, which chance cannot explain as a whole — and every item's difference is larger than in about 9995 of 10000 random re-splits. The same direction of turnover repeats across five activities, and the A16 concentration is measured directly (distinct resources falling from 18 to 1). [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, CONC001, screening]

Limitations: the resource codes are anonymous, so nothing beyond the observed shares can be inferred about them; the two A16 items describe the same behaviour and are not independent confirmations; A13 and A14 show identical profiles in both windows and likely reflect one underlying allocation, so they should not be counted twice. The evidence cannot show whether the A16 concentration and the group-level turnover are one underlying change or two, and an important competing explanation it cannot fully rule out is sampling noise in the top-share composition of any single activity — unlikely for the family as a whole, but not excluded item by item. [ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, CONC001]

Other differences: the A04-to-A01 completion-to-completion time fell by about 19 hours on average (median about 8 hours lower, upper quartile about 33 hours lower). The time family yielded one qualifying item against about 0.01 expected without change, so a single such item is still what an unchanged process typically produces; the evidence therefore cannot establish a timing change on its own, nor whether it is connected to the reallocation.
