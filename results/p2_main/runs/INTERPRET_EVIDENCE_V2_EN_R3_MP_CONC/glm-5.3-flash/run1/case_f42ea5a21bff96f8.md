### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that, in the later window, the share of cases taking the A21–A23 branch after A19 and A20 fell from about 75 in 100 to about 25 in 100, while the alternative A24–A26 branch rose from about 25 in 100 to about 75 in 100. The flip is visible directly in A20's outgoing distribution and consistently in the participation of all six activities and their co-occurrence with A19 and A20, whose order before the active group is preserved; both branches are observed substantially in both windows and remain mutually exclusive, so this is a change in how often existing paths are taken rather than a replacement of one path by another. The volume of qualifying items (92 versus about 1 expected for an unchanged process) makes chance sampling unlikely as an overall explanation. What the evidence cannot rule out is that the two windows draw on different case mixes rather than a changed routing rule, and the outgoing-transition shares at A20 are not case-level probabilities.
