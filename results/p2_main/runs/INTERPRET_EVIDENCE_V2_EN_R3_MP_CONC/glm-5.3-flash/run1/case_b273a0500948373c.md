### Observed differences

- The two windows show a mirror-image swap in participation. In the later window, A16, A17 and A18 each appear in about 79 in 100 traces (158 of 200), up from about 19 in 100 (37 of 200) before; A19, A20 and A21 each fall from about 82 in 100 traces (163 of 200) to about 21 in 100 (42 of 200). Each of these activities occurs at most once per trace in both windows. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006]
- The outgoing transitions of A15 redistributed in the same direction. In the later window, A15 is followed by A16 in about 29% of its observed outgoing transitions (before about 7%), by A17 in about 22% (before 8.5%) and by A18 in about 29% (before 3%), while A19 falls to about 7% (before 29%), A20 to about 8% (before 24%) and A21 to about 6% (before 28.5%). [DIR001]
- The six activities form two mutually exclusive branches. Cross-group pairs such as A16–A19 never occur in the same trace in either window, while the three activities within a branch almost always occur together (about 82 in 100 traces for A19–A21 pairs before; about 79 in 100 for A16–A18 pairs after). Within a branch, both orders occur in similar proportions, so the three activities have no fixed order among themselves in either window. [REL007, REL008, REL009, REL013, REL018, REL022, REL025]
- A15 precedes the A19–A21 branch in about 82 in 100 traces before and the A16–A18 branch in only about 19 in 100; in the later window this reverses, with A15 preceding A16, A17 or A18 in about 79 in 100 traces. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006]
- A25 occurs in every trace in both windows. Before it usually ran together with the A19–A21 branch (A19 before A25 in about 46 in 100 traces, interleaved in about 36); in the later window it usually runs with the A16–A18 branch (A16 before A25 in about 45 in 100, interleaved in about 34). [REL012, REL017, REL021, REL024, REL026, REL027]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the choice after A15 between two existing branches changed in frequency: before, roughly 4 in 5 traces ran the branch A19–A20–A21 (the three activities executed together in no fixed order, followed by A25) and roughly 1 in 5 ran A16–A17–A18; in the later window these shares are roughly 1 in 5 and 4 in 5, while each branch's internal structure and its relation to A25 stay the same. [DIR001, OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, REL009, REL022, REL024]

This reads as a shift in how often existing paths are taken rather than paths appearing or disappearing: every activity involved is observed in a substantial share of traces in both windows (at least about 19 in 100), the branches remain mutually exclusive, and the within-branch mixed ordering is preserved. The change is also visible directly in A15's outgoing transitions, not only in participation counts, and the screening comparison supports the ensemble: 34 control-flow items qualified against about 0.52 expected if nothing had changed, far beyond what an unchanged process typically produces. [DIR001, OCC001, OCC004, REL007, REL009, REL022]

A competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is a change in the mix of cases reaching A15 — for example, case variants that already favoured one branch becoming more common — which would look identical here, since nothing in the evidence shows what precedes A15 or what drives the choice. All items describe one coherent difference, so they should be read as aspects of a single behavioural shift rather than independent confirmations. [DIR001, REL001, REL004]
