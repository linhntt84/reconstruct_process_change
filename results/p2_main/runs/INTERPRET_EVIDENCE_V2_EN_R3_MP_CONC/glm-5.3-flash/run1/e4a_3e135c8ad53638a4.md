### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

Screening flagged 47 control-flow items against roughly 0.5 expected without a change, and 1 time item against roughly 0.01 expected, so the volume of flagged items far exceeds what an unchanged process typically produces [DIR001, REL011, OCC001, TWAIT001]. The evidence supports the hypothesis that, in the later window, the three-activity block A31/A32/A37 that previously always executed between A20 and A23 is bypassed on the direct path — A20 now leads almost always straight to A23 — and instead runs in about 47% of traces after A21 and before A26, where it is mutually exclusive with A22, which itself is skipped in about 46% of later traces. This is shown directly and consistently by participation drops, successor changes at A20, A21 and the block, flipped precedence relations with A23, A21 and A26, and the A20→A23 distance collapsing from 4 positions to adjacent [DIR001, LAG001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, REL006, REL011, REL014, REL017, OCC001, OCC002].

Limitations: the internal ordering of the three block activities and the placement of A24 are not resolved by this evidence, and six lag items about the block's new distances rest on a single after-window case each [LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG006, LAG007, LAG008]. An important competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that the roughly 53/47 split between the A22 path and the block path reflects a changed mix of case types taking different existing paths rather than a new routing rule, since no case attributes are available [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004].

Other differences: The elapsed time between consecutive completions of A23 and A24 roughly doubled (about 1.0 h to about 1.9 h on average) — a timing change in its own right for which the evidence shows no structural counterpart [TWAIT001].
