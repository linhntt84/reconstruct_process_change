### Observed differences

- Two groups of activities exchanged their places in the flow around the unchanged middle activities A29 and A33. In the earlier window, the group A19, A23, A26, A27, A28 (each present in only about 17–24 in 100 traces) ran in the first third of the trace (mean relative position about 0.33–0.35), while the group A20, A21, A22, A24, A25 (present in every trace) ran late, at about 0.62–0.63. In the later window the positions are reversed: A20, A21, A22, A24, A25 sit at about 0.39–0.40 and A19, A23, A26, A27, A28 at about 0.67–0.71, while A29 (about 0.36 to 0.48) and A33 (about 0.54 to 0.66) stayed between the two groups. [POS001, POS002, POS005, POS008, POS010, POS011, POS012]

- Pairwise order relations flipped coherently. Before, A29 preceded A20, A21, A22, A24 and A25 in about 99 in 100 traces (these pairs co-occur in every trace); in the later window A20 precedes A29 in about 99 in 100. Likewise, A19 preceded A33 in the roughly 22 in 100 traces where both occurred before, whereas now A33 precedes A19 in the roughly 17 in 100 traces where both occur; the same reversal holds for A23, A26 and A27 against the other group and against A29 and A33. [REL003, REL007, REL011, REL016, REL023, REL026, REL029, REL035, REL037]

- Successors changed hands. Before, A19, A23, A26, A27 and A28 sent about 91–100 in 100 of their observed outgoing transitions to A29, and A20, A21, A22, A24, A25 exited to A34 in about 13–26 in 100 (the rest going to other members of their own group); in the later window the first group sends about 90–98 in 100 to A34 and the second about 16–25 in 100 to A29. A33's outgoing transitions moved from the A20–A25 group (about 16–26 in 100 each) to the A19–A28 group (about 16–22 in 100 each), and A44's moved the opposite way. - Close adjacencies moved with the blocks. Before, A33 was followed within five steps by each of A20, A21, A22, A24, A25 in about 193–199 of 200 traces, A44 directly followed A19 in about 42 of 44, and A24 reached A34 within five steps in about 194 of 200; in the later window these ordered pairs occur in only 1–2 traces, while A33 → A23 (about 41 of 44 traces containing A23), A23 → A34 (about 42 of 44) and A20 → A29 within five steps (about 198 of 200) appeared. - Participation stayed essentially stable, so the change is one of placement rather than frequency: A20–A25, A29 and A33 still occur in every trace in both windows, and the optional activities keep similar shares (for example A19 in about 22% of traces before vs 17% after, A26 about 21% vs 24%, A28 about 17% vs 21%). ### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that two blocks of activities exchanged their order in the sequence. In the earlier window the flow read A44 → the optional block A19, A23, A26, A27, A28 → A29 → A33 → the mandatory block A20, A21, A22, A24, A25 → A34; in the later window it reads A44 → A20, A21, A22, A24, A25 → A29 → A33 → A19, A23, A26, A27, A28 → A34. Accordingly, the mandatory block now flows into A29 instead of A34, the optional block into A34 instead of A29, and A33 and A44 each now feed the block the other previously fed. [DIR001, DIR005, DIR011, DIR012, REL011, REL012, LAG003, LAG008]

The hypothesis is shown directly by relational and positional evidence rather than by frequency shifts: roughly two dozen activity pairs reverse their order with supports near 200 traces per window, the successor profiles of A33, A44 and the block exits change hands, and the adjacency gains and losses match the same exchange. Participation of every involved activity is essentially unchanged, which argues against work being added, removed or repeated differently. With 73 qualifying control-flow items against roughly 0.9 expected for an unchanged process, chance sampling cannot account for the pattern as a whole. [REL016, REL022, DIR012, LAG005, LAG009, POS004]

Limitations: the evidence establishes the exchange at the level of the two groups but does not resolve how the reordering is implemented internally — transitions among members within each block exist in both windows and their internal order is not pinned down — so a single coordinated block exchange cannot be fully distinguished from five individual relocations that happen to align, even though the uniform pairwise reversals favour the block reading. Positions are event positions, not clock times, so no statement about timing is implied. [DIR002, DIR007, POS002]

Other differences: small participation shifts among the optional activities (A19 from about 22% to 17% of traces, A26 from about 21% to 24%, A28 from about 17% to 21%) and a few scattered low-frequency transitions appearing or disappearing at the group boundaries (each about 1–3 in 100 of the source activity's outgoing transitions); these are minor and may reflect sampling. [POS001, POS008, POS010, DIR001, DIR002]
