### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility of a broad change in resource allocation. Work on A01, A03, A13, A14, A17 and A22 shifted from one group of resource codes, which held the largest shares earlier (about 7–9 in 100 each, e.g. User_79), to a largely different group (e.g. User_67), and A07 moved from being shared across 29 codes to being executed only by User_100. The resource family produced 8 qualifying items where about 0.006 would be expected without a change — far beyond what chance produces — the same swap pattern recurs on all six activities, A07's concentration is corroborated by two indicators over about 160 traces per window, and every item's difference exceeds about 999.5 in 1000 random re-splits. Limitations: the assignment profiles show only the ten most frequent codes plus an aggregate, so shifts inside the tail are invisible; A13 and A14 have identical profiles and supports and likely reflect one behaviour rather than two independent confirmations; and the evidence shows which codes executed which activities, not what drove the swap. A competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that the earlier codes still perform some of this work below the top-ten threshold, having only lost their top-ranked status. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, ASSIGN007, CONC001, screening]

Other differences: The elapsed time between consecutive completions of A09 and A23 increased markedly (median from about 1.5 to about 17 hours), but this rests on a single qualifying item (1 against about 0.013 expected without a change) and few observations (26 and 14 gaps), so it does not by itself establish a timing change, and the evidence cannot show whether it is connected to the reallocation. No control-flow items qualified (0 against about 0.19 expected), so no structural difference is in evidence at these thresholds. [TWAIT001, screening]
