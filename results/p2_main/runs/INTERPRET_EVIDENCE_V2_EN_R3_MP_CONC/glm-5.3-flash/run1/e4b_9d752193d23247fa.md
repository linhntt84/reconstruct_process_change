### Observed differences

- The allocation of work across resources changed for six activities: A01, A03, A13, A14, A17 and A22. In the earlier window each of these activities was spread thinly over many resources, with no single resource above about 10 in 100 assignments; in the later window the contributor mix is substantially different, with allocation-profile dissimilarity of about 0.29 to 0.42, and each difference exceeded about 1999 of 2000 random re-splits of the pooled data. The profiles rest on roughly 100 to 310 observations per window (for example about 106 before and 104 after for A22) [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006].

- The shift points the same way on all six activities. One resource (User_49) increased its share on every one of them — most sharply on A22, from about 6 in 100 assignments to about 19 in 100 — and a second resource (User_5), not among the largest contributors before, appeared at about 5–8 in 100 assignments on all six. Conversely, a group of previously frequent resources, including User_3 (about 5–8 in 100 assignments before), dropped out of the largest contributors on all six activities, falling below about 3–4 in 100; on A13, A14 and A17 a resource that had handled about 10 in 100 assignments fell below about 4 in 100. A13 and A14 show identical profiles in both windows and moved together [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006].

- Work did not become concentrated on few resources: the share of assignments outside the ten largest contributors changed by at most about 4 in 100 on each activity (for example about 49 in 100 before and after on A01), so the change is a rotation among many small shares rather than a shift onto a few resources [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006].

- All six qualifying items come from the resource family, where about 0.005 items would be expected by chance if nothing had changed; no control-flow item (0 against about 0.17 expected) and no timing item (0 against about 0.018 expected) passed the thresholds [screening].

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that work on six activities — A01, A03, A13, A14, A17 and A22 — was reallocated between groups of resources: in the later window, assignments moved away from a set of previously frequent resources (including User_3, which fell from about 5–8 in 100 assignments to below about 3–4 in 100 on every one of these activities) toward a largely different set, with User_49 gaining on all six activities (up to about 19 in 100 on A22, from about 6 in 100) and User_5 appearing at about 5–8 in 100 where it had not been a top contributor before [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006].

The supporting signs are that the resource family produced six qualifying items where about 0.005 were expected without change — far too many to attribute to chance as a whole — that all six items agree in direction (the same resources gain and lose across activities), that each difference is larger than about 1999 of 2000 random re-splits, and that the profiles rest on roughly 100–310 observations per window. The absence of qualifying control-flow and timing items is consistent with a change confined to allocation [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, screening].

Limitations: the six items describe one underlying reallocation and are not independent confirmations of each other. The indicator shows only each activity's assignment shares, so an important competing explanation remains: the declining resources may simply have become less active overall (or stopped appearing in the process) while others became more active, without any change in how work is assigned — the evidence cannot distinguish a reallocation within the same pool of resources from a change in which resources were available [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006].
