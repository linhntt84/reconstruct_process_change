### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a timing change as a possibility: in the later window, the elapsed time between consecutive completion events lengthened at two transitions — from A04 to A03 (mean about 0.09 to about 0.17 hours) and from A24 to A16 (mean about 0.9 to about 1.9 hours) — with both distributions shifting upward as a whole rather than only growing more variable. The support is direct, since T_WAIT measures exactly these intervals; consistent, since both items move in the same direction with maximal empirical strength; and the two qualifying time items are roughly seventy times the number expected by chance in that family (2 against about 0.03), so chance alone cannot account for the pattern. The complete absence of control-flow items fits a pure timing change, which needs no structural counterpart. [TWAIT001, TWAIT002]

Limitations: T_WAIT is elapsed time between consecutive completions and includes any idle or calendar time between them, so the evidence cannot separate a genuinely lengthened interval from a change in when the events are performed, such as different scheduling, or from a changed mix of cases; the number of observed intervals also rose (about 30 to 32, and about 50 to 61), so the two windows are not built on identical case mixes. Only two of 54 time units qualified, so the lengthening is demonstrated only for these two transitions, and whether they reflect one underlying change or two separate ones cannot be determined from this evidence.
