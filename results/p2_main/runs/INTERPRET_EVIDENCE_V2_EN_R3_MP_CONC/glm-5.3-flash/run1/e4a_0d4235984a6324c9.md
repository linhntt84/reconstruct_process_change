### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility of a change in timing between activities A01 and A03: in the later window, the elapsed time from the completion of A01 to the completion of A03 is about half of what it was in the earlier window (about 0.42 versus about 0.84 hours on average). The support is direct — the indicator measures exactly these elapsed times — and consistent: the mean, median and both quartiles each dropped by about 0.40–0.43 hours and the spread halved, while the frequency of the pair remained stable (34 versus 35 observations). The screening context fits: one time item qualified where about 0.01 would be expected if nothing had changed (roughly a hundredfold excess), control flow produced nothing against about 0.3 expected, and the gap difference was larger than in all but about 1 of 2,000 random re-splits of the pooled windows. Limitations: this indicator measures elapsed time between consecutive completion events, so the evidence cannot show whether the reduction lies in waiting, handover or processing, and it cannot point to any cause. The profile rests on 34–35 observations per window, so the change is established for the roughly 17 in 100 traces in which A01 is followed by A03, not necessarily for every execution. The main competing explanation — an unusual random split of an unchanged process — is made unlikely by the screening excess and the reference comparisons, but a single indicator cannot exclude it entirely.
