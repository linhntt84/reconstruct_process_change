### Observed differences

- On six activities (A01, A03, A13, A14, A17, A22), assignments moved between two largely different groups of resources. In the earlier window, about ten resources together handled roughly 42–52 in 100 assignments of each of these activities (on A01, User_19 alone about 8 in 100); in the later window, each of those resources fell below about 3 in 100, while a largely disjoint group of about eight resources rose from negligible shares to roughly 45–53 in 100 combined (on A01, User_37 about 9 in 100). Observation volumes per activity stayed similar (for example 126 vs 114 for A22), so the change is in who executed the activities, not in whether they were executed. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, ASSIGN007]
- A10 moved from concentration to dispersion. Before, about 88 in 100 of its assignments went to a small group of resources, with the largest (User_117) at about 19 in 100; after, User_117 fell to about 7 in 100 and about 57 in 100 of assignments went to a broad tail of other resources. [ASSIGN003]
- One control-flow difference: the spacing between consecutive executions of A17 within a case shifted. Before, a gap of 5 event positions was most common (about 43 in 100) against about 23 in 100 for a gap of 1; after, the gap of 1 was most common (about 39 in 100) and the gap of 5 fell to about 20 in 100. This rests on few observations (46 before, 32 after). [LAG001]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a reallocation of work between resources as the main possibility. On A01, A03, A13, A14, A17 and A22, execution shifted from one group of resources to a largely different group — illustrated by User_19 handling about 8 in 100 of A01's assignments before and dropping out of the top resources after, while User_37 rose to about 9 in 100 — and on A10 work dispersed from a small dedicated group (User_117 about 19 in 100 before) to a broad set of resources, with the same "new" resources also gaining there. The resource family produced 7 qualifying items where about 0.005 would be expected without a change, each difference exceeded about 999 in 1000 random re-splits, the gaining and losing groups recur consistently across activities, and participation levels stayed similar, so the change is in allocation rather than in whether the activities ran. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, ASSIGN007]

Limitations: the resource codes are anonymous, so the evidence cannot distinguish a change in allocation behaviour from a change in which resources were active in the later window — this is the main competing explanation it cannot rule out. A13 and A14 show identical profiles, so the seven items are not fully independent, and the hypothesis covers allocation only; it does not establish any structural or timing change. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, ASSIGN007]

Other differences: the spacing between consecutive A17 executions redistributed towards shorter gaps (gap of 1 up from about 23 to about 39 in 100; gap of 5 down from about 43 to about 20 in 100). This is a single control-flow item against a family expectation of about 0.2 — a level an unchanged process typically produces — and it is based on few observations, so it does not support a structural or timing hypothesis on its own.
