### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a timing hypothesis: in the later window, the elapsed time between consecutive completion events increased, roughly doubling, on the steps into A17 — both A18 → A17 and A29 → A17. The supporting signs are that the time family produced 2 qualifying items where fewer than 0.01 would be expected if nothing had changed, so chance alone cannot account for the family as a whole; both items point in the same direction with a similar twofold magnitude, and both show a shift across the whole distribution rather than in outliers only. Limitations: the per-pair samples are modest (23–50 observations per window), so the exact size of the increase is uncertain, and because both items involve A17 they may reflect one local change around A17 rather than two independent shifts; TWAIT measures elapsed time between consecutive completions, so the change concerns the pacing between events, not necessarily the duration of a single activity. A competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that the slowdown is confined to the immediate neighbourhood of A17 rather than being a broader timing change, since the change is observed only on the measured pairs.
