### Observed differences

- **Resource allocation shifted across five activities.** In the earlier window, A01, A03, A13, A14 and A22 were each dominated by the same group of resources; in the later window, their assignment shares moved to a largely different group. The before/after profile dissimilarity is about 0.27–0.43 on a 0–1 scale (largest for A22), and each of the five differences is larger than those seen in more than 999 of 1,000 random re-splits of the pooled data. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005]
- **The direction of the shift is the same in all five activities.** User_78, which held about 8 in 100 A01 assignments and about 16 in 100 A22 assignments before, drops out of the top holders of all five activities afterwards; User_56 loses roughly 40% of its share on each activity (for example about 20 in 100 down to about 12 in 100 on A22); User_45, not among the top holders before, takes about 5–10 in 100 assignments on each of the five activities. On A22, the combined share of all remaining resources grows from about 20 in 100 to about 33 in 100. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005]
- **The activities themselves ran at similar rates.** The observations behind the profiles barely moved (A01: 200 in both windows; A22: 96 vs 102; A13/A14: about 250 vs 260), so this is a redistribution of work, not a change in how often the activities occur. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN005]
- **Elapsed times between consecutive completions changed for several activity pairs.** For A04→A01, with 200 observations in each window, the median gap fell from about 68 hours to about 21 hours (lower quartile from about 5 hours to under 0.2 hours) while the spread widened (standard deviation about 47 to 76 hours). For A09→A23 the mean gap fell from about 13.5 to about 3 hours on fewer observations (35 vs 25). Gaps around A02→A12 and A05→A09 were fractions of a second in both windows and moved only slightly. [TWAIT002, TWAIT004, TWAIT001, TWAIT003]
- **No structural difference appears.** No control-flow indicator qualified (0 items against roughly 0.16 expected by chance). [screening]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

Screening produced 9 qualifying items — 4 time and 5 resource items — where an unchanged process would be expected to produce fewer than one in total (time: 4 vs about 0.015; resources: 5 vs about 0.005); counts this many times larger cannot be explained by random sampling as a whole. [screening]

Main hypothesis: in the later window, the execution of A01, A03, A13, A14 and A22 was reallocated from one group of resources to another — the previously dominant holders (including User_56 and User_78) lost most of their share, with User_78 leaving the top holders of all five activities, while a largely new set including User_45 took over about 5–10 in 100 assignments of each activity. This is supported directly by the assignment-share indicator, consistently across all five activities with the same losers and gainers, while activity volumes stayed stable; each item's difference exceeds those of more than 999 of 1,000 random re-splits. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005]

Limitations: the profiles are aggregate shares, so case-level handover behaviour cannot be examined; A13 and A14 show identical profiles in both windows, so the five items may describe fewer than five distinct shifts; and the evidence cannot show what drove the reallocation. An important competing explanation is a shift in the mix of cases reaching these activities, which would alter both resource profiles and elapsed times without any reallocation; the absence of control-flow items argues against a large routing change but cannot exclude subtler mix shifts. [ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004]

Other differences: the timing shifts are a separate difference, not part of the allocation hypothesis: A04→A01 gaps became shorter on average but far more variable, A09→A23 gaps shrank about fourfold, and the near-instant gaps around A02→A12 and A05→A09 moved slightly. Whether these timing changes are connected to the reallocation or independent cannot be determined from the evidence. [TWAIT002, TWAIT004, TWAIT001, TWAIT003]
