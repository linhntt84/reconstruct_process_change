### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the allocation of work to resources changed between the windows. A12 moved from distributed handling across many codes, led by User_1 with about 40 in 100 assignments, to exclusive handling by User_36; on A13, A14 and A17, assignments shifted strongly toward User_71 (from about 5–6 in 100 to about 24–25 in 100) while the previously leading code fell out of the top shares; and on A01, A03 and A22 a recurring set of codes took over the top assignment shares from a different set that had held them. The change is in who is assigned rather than in whether the activities run: assignment volumes were broadly similar across windows (A12 about 200 vs 190, A13/A14 about 343 vs 342, A17 about 305 vs 310, A22 about 121 vs 138 observations), and no control-flow item qualified. The resource family produced 8 items against about 0.005 expected without change, so chance cannot account for the family as a whole, and every item exceeds about 9995 of 10000 random re-splits. Limitations: the resource codes are anonymous, so the evidence cannot distinguish a change in assignment behaviour from a change in the composition of the resource pool; A12's result is described by two indicators of the same underlying assignments, and A13 and A14 show identical profiles in both windows, so these items are not independent confirmations. Other differences: The elapsed time between consecutive completions for the A03/A13 pair fell by roughly 100 hours at the median (about 168 to about 66 hours).
