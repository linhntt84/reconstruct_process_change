### Observed differences

- Work on A01, A03, A17 and A22 was reallocated across resources. In the earlier window, each activity's assignments were spread thinly: no single resource held more than about 9 in 100 assignments, and a long tail of other resources together held roughly half (about 47–55 in 100). In the later window, the same work is more concentrated: User_3, previously below about 4 in 100, becomes the largest single resource for all four activities at about 11–13 in 100, User_39 and User_53 also gain share in all four, and the "other" tail shrinks to about 35–45 in 100 [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004].
- The shift is consistent across the four activities: the same small group of resources gains and a largely disjoint group loses; several resources that previously held about 4–9 in 100 of the assignments drop out of the leading resources altogether. Each activity's assignment profile differs more from the earlier window than in all but about 1 in 2,000 random re-splits, with the largest shift on A22 [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004].
- A13 and A14 were repeated less often in the later window: both still appeared in every trace in both windows, but mean occurrences per trace fell from about 1.48 to about 1.26 (roughly 15% fewer), and the share of traces in which the activity appeared more than once fell from about 34 in 100 to about 20 in 100. The two activities show identical profiles, consistent with their occurring together [OCC001, OCC002].

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that resource allocation changed: in the later window, execution of A01, A03, A17 and A22 was concentrated on a smaller, consistent set of resources — including User_3, User_39 and User_53 — while a consistent group of previously active resources was assigned a smaller share, and the diffuse tail of other resources shrank from roughly half to about 35–45 in 100 of assignments [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004].

Supporting signs: the resource family produced four qualifying items where about 0.006 would be expected if nothing had changed, so sampling variation alone cannot explain the pattern as a whole; the direction of the shift and the gaining and losing resources agree across all four activities; and each profile difference exceeds all but about 1 in 2,000 random re-splits [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004].

Limitations: the four activities may reflect one underlying reallocation rather than independent changes, and the evidence cannot rule out that a shift in the mix of cases flowing through these activities, rather than a change in assignment behaviour, produced the new concentration; the resource codes are anonymous, so nothing beyond the observed shares can be inferred about them [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004].

Other differences: A13 and A14 recurred less often in the later window (mean occurrences per trace about 1.48 to about 1.26; traces with recurrence about 34 to about 20 in 100, with participation unchanged at every trace). Although each single difference looks unusual under random re-splitting (larger than in about 995 of 1,000), two control-flow items is what this family produces by chance (about 0.2 expected without a change), so the evidence does not establish a repetition change [OCC001, OCC002].
