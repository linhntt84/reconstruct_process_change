### Observed differences

- In the earlier window every measured activity had several observed successors; in the later window each has essentially one. Of the observed outgoing transitions of A15, the share going to A18 rose from about 62% to about 99.5%, while transitions to A19, A20 and A21 (about 12–14% each) disappeared; A17 now goes to A15 in about 99.5% of cases (was 56%); A18 now goes to A19 (was 12%, while A18→A27 fell from about 53% to under 1%); A19 now goes to A21 (was about 56%); A20 now goes to A27 (was about 47%); A21 now goes to A20 in every case (was about 66%); A26 now goes to A17 in every case (was a 50/50 split with A19). [DIR001-DIR007]
- The order between activity pairs changed from mixed or reversed to uniform. All measured pairs co-occur in every trace in both windows, but pairs that previously had no fixed order — A15/A21 (about 50% vs 50%), A17/A19 (50% vs 50%), A18/A20 (about 47% vs 53%) — now occur in one order in essentially all traces, and previously dominant orders flipped: A19 preceded A18 in about 88 in 100 earlier-window traces but follows it in about 995 in 1000 later-window traces; A19 before A15 fell from about 75 in 100 to about 5 in 1000, and A21 before A18 from about 70 in 100 to about 5 in 1000. [REL001, REL003, REL004, REL006, REL007, REL008]
- Step distances collapsed to single values consistent with one chain. In the later window A15 reaches A20 after 4 steps in about 995 in 1000 cases (earlier spread over 1–4 steps), A18 reaches A27 after 4 steps (earlier 1 step in about 53 in 100), A26 reaches A19 after 4 steps (earlier 1 step in about 50 in 100), and A15 reaches A27 after 5 steps; reversed-direction pairs that were common before all but vanish, e.g. A19 followed by A15 within five steps falls from about 150 of 200 traces to a single trace, and A21 followed by A18 from about 140 of 200 to one. [LAG003, LAG005, LAG013, LAG014, LAG021, LAG027]
- Event positions (positions, not clock time) shifted consistently with this reordering: the mean relative position moved earlier for A15 (0.64→0.59), A17 (0.59→0.56) and A18 (0.68→0.62), and later for A19 (0.59→0.65), A20 (0.68→0.71) and A21 (0.64→0.68), each with slightly narrower spread. [POS001-POS006]
- Participation did not change: every measured activity occurs in all traces in both windows (share of traces containing each activity is 1.0 before and after, and all measured pairs co-occur throughout), so no activity became optional, was added or removed. [POS001-POS006, REL001-REL008]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that in the later window the activities A26, A17, A15, A18, A19, A21, A20 and A27 are executed in one fixed sequence — A26 → A17 → A15 → A18 → A19 → A21 → A20 → A27 — whereas in the earlier window the same activities, all present in every trace, were executed in a variable order in which each activity had several observed successors and several pairs occurred in both orders; the change therefore concerns ordering constraints, not participation. This is shown directly and consistently: successor distributions collapsed to one dominant successor per activity, pair orders became uniform in the direction of the chain (including full reversals such as A18/A19), and pairwise step distances collapsed to exactly the chain's distances; every item's difference exceeds those seen in about 995 of 1000 random re-splits, and 50 control-flow items qualified against roughly 0.47 expected if nothing had changed, far beyond what chance would produce. [DIR001-DIR007, REL001-REL008, LAG001-LAG029]

The evidence cannot distinguish whether the earlier variable order reflects genuine within-case flexibility or a mixture of several fixed orderings across subsets of cases, and rare residual transitions (about 5 in 1000) persist in the later window. [REL003, REL004, DIR001]

Other differences: none beyond the above; the slight uniform narrowing of position spread in the later window is part of the same move to a fixed sequence. [POS001-POS006]
