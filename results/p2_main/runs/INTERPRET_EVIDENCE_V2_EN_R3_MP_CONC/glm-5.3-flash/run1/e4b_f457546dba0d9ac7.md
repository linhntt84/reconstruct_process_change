### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a change in resource allocation for activity A17: in the earlier window its work was shared across a broad pool of about 38 resources with no resource handling more than roughly 10 in 100 of assignments, while in the later window all of A17's work was handled by the single resource User_49. Supporting signs: two resource indicators on the same activity agree (assignment shares and concentration statistics), both differences are larger than in all but about 1 in 2,000 random re-splits of the pooled data, both profiles rest on 262 and 241 observations, and the resources family produced 2 qualifying items where only 0.004 were expected if nothing had changed — far beyond what an unchanged process typically yields, while the control-flow and time families produced no items. [ASSIGN001, CONC001, screening]

Limitations: the two items measure the same underlying shift on the same activity, so they are not independent confirmations of one another; the resource codes are anonymous, so no roles, people or teams can be inferred, and the evidence shows only that allocation concentrated, not why. A competing explanation the evidence cannot fully exclude is a shift in the mix of cases reaching A17 (for example, only a subset of cases occurring in the later window) that happens to always involve User_49; with no qualifying control-flow items, such a mix shift cannot be ruled out directly.
