### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a re-sequencing in which the two five-activity groups exchanged their places around the A24–A28 segment: in the earlier window a case ran A39, then one of A14/A18/A21/A22/A23, then A24 and A28, then the group A15/A16/A17/A19/A20, which handed over to A29; in the later window the group order is reversed, with A39 leading into A15/A16/A17/A19/A20, which hand over to A24, and A28 leading into A14/A18/A21/A22/A23, which hand over to A29 [DIR011, DIR012, REL012, REL013, POS011, POS012].

This is shown directly and consistently: every one of the 45 cross-group order relations flips (several from complete precedence to complete reverse), the successor profiles of A39, A28 and the group exits rewire exactly as this re-sequencing implies, and the position shifts are large and symmetric; the 69 qualifying control-flow items and 3 time items far exceed the roughly 0.65 and 0.015 items expected without change, and each item's difference exceeds at least about 995 in 1,000 random re-splits; stable occurrence shares rule out activities being added, removed or made optional as the explanation [REL001, REL012, DIR011, DIR012, POS001, POS002, DIR001]. A competing explanation the two aggregate windows cannot fully exclude is a flip in the mix of two fixed process variants (from essentially only one order to essentially only the other), though the complete, symmetric reversals make a within-case re-sequencing the more direct reading; the many agreeing items also largely describe one underlying reordering rather than independent changes [REL001, REL012, DIR012].

Other differences: the elapsed time from the completions of A30, A41 or A43 to the following completion of A40 consistently fell from about 1.0 to about 0.5 hour — a timing change that stands as a hypothesis in its own right — and the evidence shows no link between it and the re-sequencing above [TWAIT001, TWAIT002, TWAIT003].
