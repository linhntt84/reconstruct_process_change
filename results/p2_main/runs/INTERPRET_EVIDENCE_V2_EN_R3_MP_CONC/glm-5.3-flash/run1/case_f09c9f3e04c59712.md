### Observed differences

- The relative order of two activity groups reversed completely. In the earlier window, A15, A17 and A18 (each present in 100% of traces) always ran before A20 and A24 — for example, A15 preceded A20 in every trace — while A14, A16 and A19 (each present in about 28–38% of traces) always ran after A20 and A24. In the later window, A15, A17 and A18 always run after A20 and A24, and A14, A16 and A19 always run before them; every one of these pairwise relations flipped, with no mixed orders in either window. [REL001, REL004, REL005, REL006, REL008, REL010, REL012, REL013, REL015, REL020, REL021]
- Event positions moved accordingly: the mean relative position of A15, A17 and A18 moved from about 0.39 to about 0.64 of the case (later), and that of A14, A16 and A19 from about 0.68 to about 0.35 (earlier), while A20 (about 0.46 to 0.38) and A24 (about 0.65 to 0.57) shifted only slightly earlier and kept their relative order. [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006, POS007, POS008]
- Successors were rewired consistently with an exchange of the two segments: A24 led only to A16, A14 and A19 before (about 38/31/32 in 100 of its outgoing transitions) and only to A15, A17 and A18 after (about 32/35/34 in 100); A35 led only to A17, A18 and A15 before and only to A14, A16 and A19 after; the single-choice activities exited to A25 in every before-trace and to A20 in every after-trace; the block activities' exit moved from A20 (about 31–39 in 100) to A25 (about 33–35 in 100). [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006, DIR007, DIR008]
- The step distance from A24 to A25 changed from always 2 steps to always 4 steps, and from A35 to A20 from always 4 steps to always 2 steps. [LAG001, LAG002]
- The choice within the single-activity segment also shifted: A14 ran in about 31% of traces before versus about 44% in the later window, A16 in about 38% versus 29%, and A19 in about 32% versus 28%. [OCC001, DIR007, DIR008, POS003, POS006]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that two segments exchanged their order around the unchanged A20 → A24 core. In the earlier window, a case ran A35, then the three activities A15, A17 and A18 together in varying order, then A20, then A24, then exactly one of A14, A16 or A19, then A25; in the later window, the single choice among A14, A16 and A19 runs directly after A35 and before A20, while the A15/A17/A18 block runs between A24 and A25. [REL001, REL008, REL012, REL020, DIR002, DIR004, DIR005, DIR007, DIR008, LAG001, LAG002]

The change is shown directly and consistently: every cross-segment activity pair reversed its order completely, the successors of A24, A35 and of both segments' members were rewired exactly as an exchange implies, and the step gaps at the two boundaries flipped between 2 and 4 steps. Participation of the block members is unchanged (present in all traces in both windows) and substantial for A14/A16/A19 in both windows, so this is a reordering rather than activities appearing or disappearing. With 40 qualifying control-flow items against about 0.46 expected without a change, sampling noise cannot account for the pattern as a whole, although many items describe facets of the same reordering rather than independent confirmations. [REL001, REL008, DIR007, LAG001, LAG002, POS002, POS004, OCC001]

A competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that the shifted choice shares among A14, A16 and A19 are a second, independent change in routing frequencies rather than a consequence of the segment exchange; the evidence also cannot show whether the internal sequence distribution within the A15/A17/A18 block itself changed. [OCC001, DIR002, DIR007, DIR008]

Other differences: the selection among A14, A16 and A19 shifted — A14 was the chosen activity in about 31 in 100 cases before and about 44 in 100 after, while A16 (about 38 → 29 in 100) and A19 (about 32 → 28 in 100) were chosen less often. [OCC001, DIR007, DIR008, POS003, POS006]
