### Observed differences

- The only qualifying differences are in resource allocation, and they concern four activities: A13, A14, A17 and A22. In the earlier window, each of these activities was handled by a stable set of about ten regularly used resources; in the later window, most of those resources no longer appear among the ten largest codes, and a largely different set of resources has taken over. - On A13 and A14 (identical profiles, so effectively one underlying behaviour): the pooled share of long-tail resources grew from about 45 to about 54 in 100 assignments; codes that each held roughly 4–7 in 100 assignments before — for example User_58 at about 6 in 100 — no longer hold a top-ten share, while new codes such as User_18 (about 6 in 100) and several others at 3–6 in 100 appeared. - All four differences were larger than 1,999 of 2,000 random re-splits of the pooled data, they move in the same direction on every activity (the same codes lose share, the same new codes gain it), and each rests on roughly 107–282 observed assignments per window. No control-flow or timing indicators qualified (0 items against roughly 0.19 and 0.015 expected by chance), so the evidence points to no structural or timing difference. ### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a change in resource allocation: in the later window, work on activities A13, A14, A17 and A22 was reallocated away from a previously central group of regularly used resources — including User_132 and User_58 — toward a largely different group that includes User_18, and allocation became less concentrated, with the long-tail share rising by roughly 7–9 assignments in 100 on each activity. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004]

Supporting signs: the resource family produced 4 qualifying items where about 0.004 were expected without a change — far beyond what an unchanged process typically yields — and the items agree in direction across three distinct activity profiles, each difference exceeding 1,999 of 2,000 random re-splits and each backed by at least about 100 observed assignments per window. The control-flow and timing families produced no items, so the hypothesis concerns allocation only, not structure or timing. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004]

Limitations: A13 and A14 have identical profiles and therefore count as one underlying observation rather than two independent confirmations; and because resource codes are anonymous, the evidence cannot rule out that the gaining codes are re-labels of the losing resources, or that a different mix of cases in the later window changed which codes appear, rather than a genuine reallocation of the work. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002]
