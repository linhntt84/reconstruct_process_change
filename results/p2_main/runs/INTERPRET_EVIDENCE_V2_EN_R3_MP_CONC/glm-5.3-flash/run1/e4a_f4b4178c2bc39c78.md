### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the sequence A25 → A26 → A31, which ran exactly once per trace in the earlier window, is in the later window repeated within traces: all three activities run about 3.5 times per trace on average, A31 loops back to A25 in about 71 in 100 of its outgoing transitions while still exiting to A20 in the remainder, and the once-fixed A25-before-A31 order gives way to interleaved or repeated execution in about 68 in 100 traces [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, DIR001, REL001].

The signs are direct and mutually consistent: the recurrence share (about 68 in 100 traces) matches the interleaved-or-repeated share for A25 and A31, the about 29 in 100 exit share at A31 matches the average of about 3.5 block executions per trace, and the earlier fixed two-step spacing between A25 and A31 dissolves accordingly. The volume of qualifying control-flow items (8 against roughly 0.3 expected without change) is far above what an unchanged process typically produces, so chance sampling is unlikely to explain the pattern as a whole [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, DIR001, REL001, LAG001, POS001, POS002].

Limitations: the relational indicator merges interleaving with strict repetition, so the evidence cannot fully separate sequential re-execution of the segment from interleaved executions, and no relational item directly places A26 inside the repeated segment — its inclusion rests on an occurrence profile identical to that of A25 and A31; the positional broadening is consistent with repetition but, being position-based, does not itself demonstrate a move [OCC002, REL001, POS001, POS002].

Other differences: the elapsed time between consecutive completions of A34 and A14 roughly doubled (median about 1.0 hour to about 2.0 hours, on about 93 versus 95 observations) — a timing change in its own right involving activities outside the repeated segment, whose connection to the repetition change the evidence cannot establish [TWAIT001].
