- At A35, the preferred next activity flipped: in the earlier window, about 59 in 100 of A35's outgoing transitions went to A32 and about 38 in 100 to A36 (103 observed transitions, with three other targets at about 1 in 100 each); in the later window, about 62 in 100 go to A36 and about 38 in 100 to A32 (87 transitions), and the three rare targets are no longer observed. - A minor difference at A39: earlier, all 56 observed outgoing transitions went to A40; later, about 91 in 100 of 34 transitions go to A40, with one transition each (about 3 in 100) to A43, A14 and A19. The effect size is negligible and rests on few observations. [DIR003]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that routing between A35, A36 and A32 was redistributed: before, flow at A36 typically returned to A35, which then typically exited to A32, whereas in the later window flow at A35 typically proceeds to A36, which then typically exits to A32. The shift is visible directly at both source activities and the two items agree; the share changes are large (about 20–24 points on 87–103 observed transitions each), the differences are larger than in about 199 of 200 random re-splits, and three qualifying items against roughly 0.44 expected by chance means chance alone cannot account for the count. [DIR001, DIR002]

Limitations: both items describe the same A35–A36–A32 behaviour, so they are not independent confirmations; the shares are over outgoing transitions rather than cases; and with no relational or positional evidence available, the data cannot distinguish a reordering of A35 and A36 in the sequence from a change in how often the loop between them is used relative to the direct exits. An important competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is therefore that A35 and A36 swapped places on the main path, which would produce the same successor-share pattern. [DIR001, DIR002]

Other differences: the occasional successors of A39 other than A40 (A43, A14, A19) appear only in the later window, one occurrence each in 34 transitions; given the negligible effect size and small support, this is consistent with a rare variant appearing in one window only. [DIR003]
