### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The screening comparison supports taking these items seriously: 8 items qualified in total where about 0.2 would be expected without any change; by family, resources produced 6 items against about 0.005 expected and time 2 against about 0.016, while control flow produced none (0 against about 0.18), so the resource and time counts cannot be explained by chance as a whole. Main hypothesis: a change in resource allocation. In the later window, the work on activities A01, A03, A13, A14, A17 and A22 was assigned to a largely different group of resources than before: the resource that previously held the largest share on every one of these activities (about 8–13 in 100, e.g., User_65) dropped out of the frequent assignees, and a new group led by User_69 (about 10–15 in 100) and including User_84 (about 5–7 in 100) took over, with only two resources continuing at similar shares. Supporting signs: all six assignment profiles shifted in the same direction with the same resources rising and falling, each difference is large on its own scale and exceeds about 999.5 of 1,000 random re-splits, and the profiles rest on roughly 120–290 assignments per window. Limitations: the resource codes are anonymous and permit no inference about roles or teams; A13 and A14 show identical profiles, so those two items are not independent; and the evidence cannot rule out that a changed mix of cases, rather than a change in assignment behaviour, produced the new pattern. Other differences: a change in timing.
