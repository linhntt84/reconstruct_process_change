### Observed differences

- Activities A30, A31 and A36 repeated in the earlier window but typically do not in the later window: each ran about 3.4 times per trace before, against about 1.05 times per trace in the later window, while still appearing in every trace in both windows. Repeating within the same trace fell from about 75 in 100 traces to about 1–2 in 100. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003]
- The way the cycle closes changed: before, about 70 in 100 outgoing transitions of A36 led back to A30 and about 29 in 100 led to A25; in the later window, about 93 in 100 lead to A25 and only about 4 in 100 return to A30. [DIR003]
- The chain itself is stable: A30 led almost exclusively to A31 (about 98–99 in 100) and A31 to A36 (about 98–99 in 100) in both windows. The observed outgoing transitions of these activities fell from about 690 to about 210, matching the drop in occurrences. [DIR001, DIR002]
- The relation between A30 and A36 tightened: before, they were interleaved or repeated in 75 in 100 traces and A30 strictly preceded A36 in 25 in 100; in the later window, A30 strictly precedes A36 in 98 in 100 traces, with the two still co-occurring in every trace. [REL001]
- A36 now follows A30 within two steps in about 89 in 100 observed pairs, whereas before the distances were more spread (about 12 in 100 at two steps, about 5 in 100 at five). [LAG002]
- Short self-repetitions of A30 and A36 (mainly at a distance of about three steps) and quick returns from A36 to A30 were observable before and are almost unobserved in the later window; those later profiles rest on only 1–2 traces. [LAG001, LAG003, LAG004]
- The event-position spread of A30 and A36 narrowed (standard deviation from about 0.12 to about 0.06–0.07 in position units) while mean positions stayed around 0.42–0.49; these are event positions, not clock times. [POS001, POS002]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

A possibility supported by the evidence is that the cycle A30 → A31 → A36 stopped repeating: in the earlier window the block looped from A36 back to A30 and ran about three to four times per trace (each activity about 3.4 occurrences, repeats in about 75 in 100 traces, A36 returning to A30 in about 70 in 100 of its outgoing transitions), while in the later window it typically runs once per trace, with A36 leading on to A25 in about 93 in 100 transitions and back to A30 in only about 4 in 100. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, DIR003, REL001]

The signs agree across occurrence, routing, relation and distance indicators, though they largely describe one underlying trace behaviour rather than independent confirmations: the shift to a single strict A30-before-A36 order, the concentration of the A30-to-A36 distance at two steps, and the narrowed position spread all fit one pass instead of several. Thirteen control-flow items qualified against roughly 0.46 expected without a change, so the ensemble is unlikely to be a chance product of the sampling. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, POS001, POS002, REL001, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004]

The evidence cannot show whether the return edge A36→A30 was removed or only rarely taken, since it is still observed at about 4 in 100 in the later window; the near-empty later profiles for the self-repetition distances also rest on only 1–2 traces, so they corroborate but do not carry the hypothesis on their own. [DIR003, LAG001, LAG003, LAG004]

Other differences: only minor redistributions among the rare outgoing transitions of A30 and A31, each remaining below about 0.5 in 100 of those transitions, plus the narrowed event-position spread noted above. [DIR001, DIR002, POS001, POS002]
