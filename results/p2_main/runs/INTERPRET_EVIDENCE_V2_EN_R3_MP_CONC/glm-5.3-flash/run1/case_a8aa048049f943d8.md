### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, each case ran exactly one of two five-activity blocks between A25 and A26: the block A28, A29, A30, A32, A33 in about 48 in 100 cases, or the block A34–A38 in about 52 in 100. Activities from the two blocks never occurred in the same case (co-occurrence 0 in 100), and each block activity occurred at most once per case. [OCC001, OCC006, REL025, REL056]
- In the later window, both blocks run in almost every case: each of A28, A29, A30, A32, A33 appears in about 99 in 100 cases (previously 48), and each of A34–A38 in about 98 in 100 (previously 52). Cross-block co-occurrence rose from 0 to about 97–100 in 100 cases (for example, A28 and A34 from 0 to about 97 in 100). [OCC001, OCC006, REL025, REL033]
- A fixed order emerged between the blocks: every A28-group activity precedes every A34-group activity in about 97–98 in 100 later cases, which was unobservable before because they never co-occurred. A25 still precedes both blocks (about 98–100 in 100) and both blocks still precede A26. Within each block, both orders occur in similar proportions in both windows, so the internal order remains unfixed. [REL025, REL001, REL011, REL021, REL056]
- Successor profiles moved accordingly. Before, A25's observed outgoing transitions were spread over all ten block activities (roughly 8–16 per 100 each); in the later window they go almost only to the A28-group (about 15–24 per 100 each). The direct transitions from A28 and A30 to A26 (about 28 and 17 per 100 of their outgoing transitions) have effectively disappeared (at most about 0.5 per 100), replaced by small shares into A34-group activities (about 2–5 per 100 each). [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003]
- Relative event positions shifted as this structure predicts: A25's mean position moved earlier (about 0.64 to 0.55 of the case) and A26's later (about 0.82 to 0.84); the A28-group moved earlier (about 0.73 to 0.63) and the A34-group later (about 0.72 to 0.77). [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS008]
- Before, A25 was followed by an A34-group activity within five steps in the cases containing that block, with distances 1–5 roughly evenly spread; in the later window such pairs fall within five steps in only about 1 in 200 cases, and each of those after-profiles rests on a single observation, so these distance changes carry little weight on their own. [LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG005]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that two alternative activity blocks became sequentially mandatory. In the earlier window, a case executed either the block A28, A29, A30, A32, A33 or the block A34–A38 between A25 and A26 (about 48 versus 52 in 100 cases, never both); in the later window, essentially every case executes both blocks in a fixed inter-block order — all A28-group activities before all A34-group activities (about 97–98 in 100) — while the order within each block stays unfixed and A25 and A26 keep their roles as entry and exit. [OCC001, OCC006, REL025, REL021, REL001, REL011]

Screening qualified 95 control-flow items where about 1 would be expected if nothing had changed, so chance cannot explain the volume of findings. The change is shown directly by the co-occurrence and order relations (cross-block co-occurrence from 0 to about 97–100 in 100, with a consistent A28-group-first order), and it is corroborated by the successor redistribution at A25, A28 and A30 and by position shifts in exactly the direction an always-executed second block after the first would produce. [REL025, DIR001, DIR002, POS003, POS008]

Limitations: the data show the blocks as unordered groups rather than a fixed ten-step sequence, and the LAG after-profiles rest on one observation each. A competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that the new block sequence is mediated by an unobserved decision or routing point rather than by direct transitions between the blocks; the evidence also cannot separate one coordinated modification from two independent ones (each block separately made mandatory plus a new sequencing constraint). [LAG001, DIR002, REL025]
