### Observed differences

- Two mutually exclusive activity blocks swapped how often they run. In the earlier window, A26–A30 ran together as a set in about 82 in 100 traces, while A21–A25 ran together as a set in only about 19 in 100. In the later window this reversed: A21–A25 co-occur in about 77 in 100 traces and A26–A30 in about 24 in 100. An activity from one block and one from the other almost never share a trace in either window (co-occurrence about 1 in 100 or less). [OCC002, OCC007, REL021, REL025, REL036, REL071, REL078]
- The entry into the blocks flipped. A20 is present in essentially every trace in both windows; earlier its outgoing transitions went to A29 in about 81 in 100 cases and to A24 in about 18 in 100, while later they go to A24 in about 77 in 100 and to A29 in about 24 in 100. A20 and A24 are adjacent in nearly all later co-occurrences. [DIR002, REL011, REL016, LAG003]
- The internal shape of each block is preserved; only its frequency changed. Within the growing block, A24 precedes A21, A22 and A23 (about 76 in 100 co-occurrences), A21–A23 run in mixed order, and A21/A22/A23 precede A25 (about 76 in 100); the same orderings already held in the earlier window. [REL021, REL023, REL024, REL033, REL044, REL045, REL054]
- Surrounding activities switched which block they accompany. A34, present in essentially all traces in both windows, co-occurred with A26–A30 in about 82 in 100 earlier traces but with A21–A25 in about 77 in 100 later traces (interleaved with them in about 39 in 100). A36 and A37, each present in roughly 62–64 in 100 traces in both windows, moved from co-occurring with A26–A30 (about 53 in 100) to co-occurring with A21–A25 (about 52 in 100). [REL030, REL075, REL031, REL076]
- A02 became rarer and earlier: present in about 25 in 100 traces earlier versus about 10 in 100 later; its outgoing transitions shifted from mostly A37 (about 81 in 100) to a spread over many activities (largest shares about 20 in 100 each); its median position moved from about 0.86 to about 0.37 of the trace. These items rest on only about 20 later traces. [OCC001, DIR001, POS001]
- Smaller redistributions of step gaps appear for a few pairs, e.g. A22–A34 gaps stretching toward 4–5 steps and A20–A22 / A22–A25 gaps spreading out slightly. [LAG002, LAG004, LAG005, LAG011]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that two alternative paths swapped frequencies: the path through A21–A25 — entered from A20 via A24, ordered A24 before A21/A22/A23 before A25 and accompanied by A34 and A36/A37 — was taken in about 19 in 100 traces earlier and about 77 in 100 later, while the alternative through A26–A30, entered from A20 via A29, fell from about 82 in 100 to about 24 in 100. This rests on direct participation changes for all ten block activities, the flip in A20's direct successor from A29 to A24, and relational profiles showing the blocks mutually exclusive in both windows with unchanged internal order — a shift in how often existing paths are taken, since every involved activity remains observed at a substantial share in both windows and no activity appears or disappears. The 125 qualifying control-flow items compare with about 1.3 expected by chance among the 266 tested units, far beyond what random re-splits produce, though the many relation items describe one underlying swap rather than independent confirmations. An important competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that the two windows simply sampled two pre-existing case types at different rates rather than reflecting a modified routing.

Other differences: A02's reduced participation, earlier position and redistributed successors, based on few later observations [OCC001, DIR001, POS001]; minor redistributions of step gaps between A20/A22/A25 and A34 pairs [LAG002, LAG004, LAG005, LAG011, LAG012].
