### Observed differences

- **Ordering between two groups of activities flipped from unfixed to fixed.** Earlier, each pair across the two groups occurred in both orders in similar shares: A25 ran before A26 in only about 3 in 100 of their shared cases (A26 first in about 97 in 100), A20 before A26 in about 23 vs 77 in 100 the other way, and A20 before A28 in about 58 vs 42 in 100. In the later window, A20, A21, A22, A24 and A25 each precede A26–A30 in about 97–100 in 100 cases, and the reverse order is essentially not observed. [REL001, REL002, REL005, REL009, REL014, REL018, REL019]

- **Successor profiles no longer cross between the groups.** Earlier, roughly half of A20's outgoing transitions went to A21, A22 or A25 and the rest to A26–A30 and A36; in the later window about 98 in 100 go to A21, A22 or A25 (similarly for A21 and A22). A25 earlier led most often to A36 (about 45 in 100); in the later window virtually all its outgoing transitions go to A26. A26 earlier sent about a third of its outgoing transitions back to A20, A21, A22, A24 or A25; in the later window it leads only to A28, A29 and A30. A27 now leads to A36 in about 84 in 100 cases (earlier about 45 in 100), and A35 now leads only to A24 (earlier split about 53/46 between A26 and A24). [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006, DIR010]

- **Backward reach disappeared.** Pairs running from the second group back into the first within five steps (A26→A24, A28→A20, A29→A20, A30→A20, A28→A24, A29→A24, A30→A24) were observed in roughly 40–110 of 200 traces earlier and in about 1 of 200 in the later window. [LAG031, LAG037, LAG038, LAG043, LAG044, LAG049, LAG050]

- **Forward reach became universal with settled gaps.** A20, A21 and A22 each precede A25 within five steps in all 200 later traces (earlier about 162–166), at lags of 1–3 steps; they reach A26 in all 200 later traces at lags of 2–4 steps, where earlier the pair was mostly direct (lag 1, in only about 45–46 traces). A24 precedes A25 at lag 4 in all 200 later traces (earlier 50) and A26 at lag 5 in all 200 (earlier about 92, at lags 1–3); A35 precedes A24 directly in all 200 later traces (earlier lag 1 in only about half of 189), and A35 and A24 each precede A20/A21/A22 in all 200 later traces (earlier about 101–104 and 176–177). [LAG003, LAG004, LAG010, LAG011, LAG017, LAG018, LAG022, LAG023, LAG024, LAG025, LAG026, LAG055, LAG056, LAG057, LAG058]

- **A36 now follows the second group consistently.** A27→A36 occurs at lag 1 in about 84 in 100 later traces (earlier 48 in 100), and A28, A29 and A30 each precede A36 within five steps in about 197–200 of 200 later traces (earlier about 86–111). [LAG036, LAG042, LAG048, LAG054]

- **Positions moved accordingly (event positions, not clock time).** A20, A21, A22 and A25 occur earlier in the trace (mean relative position from about 0.61 to 0.55, and 0.70 to 0.60 for A25), A24 slightly earlier (0.54 to 0.50), while A26 and A27–A30 occur later (A26 from about 0.53 to 0.63; A29 and A30 from about 0.61 to 0.68). [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006, POS007, POS008, POS009, POS010]

- **The internal order of A20, A21 and A22 stayed unfixed**: both directions still occur with similar support in the later window (A20 before A21 in about 106 traces, A21 before A20 in about 95). [LAG001, LAG008, LAG009, LAG015, LAG016]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the ordering constraints between two blocks of activities changed. Earlier, the block A20, A21, A22, A24, A25 and the block A26, A27, A28, A29, A30 were executed without a fixed order between the blocks, with transitions crossing between them in both directions; in the later window, the first block runs as a unit — entered via A35 and A24, with A20/A21/A22 in varying internal order and then A25 — followed by A26, then the second block (A28/A29/A30, then A27), which now leads to A36 in about 84 in 100 cases, while A25 no longer leads directly to A36. The pairwise relations are consistent with a fixed sequence roughly A35 → A24 → A20/A21/A22 → A25 → A26 → A28/A29/A30 → A27 → A36. [REL001, REL005, REL009, REL014, REL018, DIR001, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006, LAG025, LAG026, LAG027, LAG032, LAG058]

Supporting signs: the change is shown directly by the relational evidence — all 22 cross-block pairs moved from mixed to fixed order while the two activities kept occurring together in 100 in 100 traces in both windows, so this is an ordering change rather than a participation change; the direction, lag and position items agree with it, and every item's difference exceeds those seen in about 995 of 1000 random re-splits. The number of qualifying items (100, all control-flow) is roughly a hundred times the about 1 expected if nothing had changed, so chance sampling cannot account for the pattern as a whole. [REL001, REL018, DIR001, DIR004, LAG004, LAG031, POS001, POS006]

Limitations: the evidence cannot distinguish whether the earlier mixed order was one flexible behaviour or a mixture of two strict variants (first-block-first and second-block-first) of which one disappeared. It covers direct successors and pairs within five steps, so orderings over longer spans and the few remaining marginal transitions (about 1 in 200) are not established, and the heavily overlapping indicators describe one underlying change rather than independent confirmations. [DIR001, LAG004, LAG031]
