### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a reallocation of work: for activities A01, A03, A13, A14, A17 and A21, execution shifted from one group of resources to a largely different group — in the later window User_78, User_12 and User_77 took over the leading shares, while a largely disjoint set of previously frequent resources stopped receiving work, with one resource keeping a similar share throughout. This rests on six allocation profiles that all moved in the same direction with the same gainers, on a resource-family count (6) many times its chance expectation (about 0.005), and on per-item differences larger than in about 999.5 of 1000 random re-splits. Limitations: the items are not fully independent — A13 and A14 show identical profiles, and all six may express one global reallocation rather than six separate per-activity changes; the resource codes are anonymous, so no roles or teams can be inferred. Other differences: the interval between consecutive completions of A04 and A01 roughly doubled (median about 22 h to about 45 h). As the only time item (1 against about 0.017 expected in its family), it does not by itself rule out a sampling fluke, and it is not part of the allocation hypothesis. [TWAIT001]

An important competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out: the shift could reflect a changed resource pool (resources entering or leaving) rather than reassignment of work within the same pool — both would produce the same allocation profiles.
