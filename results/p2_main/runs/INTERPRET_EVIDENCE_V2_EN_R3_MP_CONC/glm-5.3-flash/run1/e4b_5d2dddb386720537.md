### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, A13 and A14 each appeared in every trace and occurred more than once in about 33 in 100 traces, averaging about 1.4 occurrences per trace. In the later window they still appeared in every trace, but repeated in only about 19 in 100 traces, averaging about 1.2 occurrences per trace. [OCC001, OCC002]
- A13 and A14 show identical participation, occurrence and repetition values in both windows, so they behave as a single block; the difference concerns how often the block repeats, not whether its activities run at all. [OCC001, OCC002]
- In the later window, A10 tended to follow A17 at a larger positional distance: among the traces containing the pair within five steps (about 145 before, about 143 after), pairs three positions apart rose from about 25 in 100 to about 44 in 100, while directly consecutive pairs fell from about 9 in 100 to about 3 in 100; gaps of two, four and five positions stayed roughly stable. [LAG001]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the repeated execution of the block formed by A13 and A14 decreased in the later window: the block still runs in every trace, but the share of traces in which it repeats fell from about 33 in 100 to about 19 in 100 and average occurrences per trace fell from about 1.4 to about 1.2, while participation remained complete in both windows — a change in repetition rather than the activities becoming optional or disappearing. [OCC001, OCC002]

Supporting signs: three control-flow items qualified where roughly 0.2 would be expected if nothing had changed (the time and resource families produced none against even smaller expectations), the occurrence indicator measures this repetition directly, both activities move identically as one block, and each difference is larger than about 995 of 1000 random re-splits of the pooled traces. [OCC001, OCC002, LAG001]

An important competing explanation remains: because A13 and A14 behave identically, the two occurrence items describe one behaviour rather than two independent confirmations, and the evidence cannot show whether the changed gap between A17 and A10 belongs to the same change or is a separate difference. [LAG001]

Other differences: the positional gap between A17 and A10 shifted toward larger distances in the later window (three positions apart up from about 25 to about 44 in 100 observed pairs; immediate succession down from about 9 to about 3); it is kept outside the main hypothesis because no evidence links it to the A13/A14 block, and as a position-based measure it does not by itself show that an activity was moved. [LAG001]
