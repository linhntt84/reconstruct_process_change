### Observed differences

- The three activities A14, A15 and A20 each ran in about 47 in 100 cases in the earlier window; in the later window they run in essentially every case (199 of 200). Each occurs at most once per case in both windows. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003]
- These three activities always appear together and in the fixed order A14 → A20 → A15 in both windows; earlier they were jointly missing from about 53 in 100 cases, in the later window from about 1 in 200. [REL001, REL002, REL007]
- A36 and A21 themselves occur in every case in both windows. Earlier, the outgoing flow from A36 split between A21 (about 53 in 100 observed transitions) and A14 (about 47 in 100); in the later window it goes to A14 in about 995 of 1000 transitions and to A21 in about 1 in 200. [DIR001]
- In event positions, A21 followed A36 immediately (gap of one event) in about 53 in 100 earlier cases, and four positions later — with the three block activities in between — in about 47 in 100; in the later window the four-position gap holds in about 995 of 1000 cases. [LAG001]
- A21 sits slightly later within the case (mean relative position about 0.41 to 0.43) and A36 slightly earlier (about 0.33 to 0.30); these are small shifts in event positions, not clock times. [POS001, POS002]
- A37 is present in every case in both windows; in the later window it almost never appears without A14, and its order relative to A14 became less fixed (interleaved or repeated in about 17 in 100 earlier cases versus about 46 in 100 later cases). [REL005, REL010, REL014]
- A40 appeared in about 60 in 100 cases in both windows, but earlier about 33 in 100 cases had A40 without A14, whereas in the later window it virtually only occurs together with A14. [REL006, REL011, REL015]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the previously optional block A14 → A20 → A15 became mandatory. In the earlier window the block ran in about 47 in 100 cases and was skipped in the remainder, with A36 passing flow directly on to A21; in the later window the block runs in nearly every case, inserted between A36 and A21, giving the chain A36 → A14 → A20 → A15 → A21. The signs are direct and mutually consistent: participation of all three activities rose from about 47 in 100 cases to essentially all cases [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003]; the successor of A36 changed from A21 to A14 [DIR001]; the event gap from A36 to A21 moved from one to four positions [LAG001]; and co-occurrence of the block with the always-present A21 and A36 rose from about 47 in 100 cases to about 995 in 1000 [REL003, REL004, REL008, REL009, REL012, REL013]. The screening context supports this reading: 22 qualifying items against roughly 0.41 expected for an unchanged process is far beyond what sampling variation typically produces. Note that the many relation items largely restate this single change rather than confirming it independently.

A limitation the evidence cannot resolve: only control flow was tested, so a change in the mix of cases (for example, only cases of the variant containing the block arriving after the change point) would look identical to a routing change and cannot be ruled out.

Other differences: A37's ordering relative to A14 became less fixed, and A40 now occurs only alongside the block; both may be consequences of the block becoming mandatory, but the evidence does not show how they arise. [REL005, REL010, REL014, REL006, REL011, REL015]
