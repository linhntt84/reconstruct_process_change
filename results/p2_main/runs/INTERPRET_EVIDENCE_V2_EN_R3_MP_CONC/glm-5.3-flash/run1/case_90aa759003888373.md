### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, A14, A15 and A20 each appeared exactly once in every trace (participation 100%, mean 1.0 occurrence per trace). In the later window, each still appears at most once, but only in about 53 in 100 traces (106 of 200), and is absent in about 47 in 100. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003]
- In the earlier window, every observed outgoing transition of A36 led to A14. In the later window, about 53 in 100 of A36's outgoing transitions still lead to A14, while about 47 in 100 lead directly to A21. [DIR001]
- In the earlier window, A21 followed A36 four event positions later in essentially all traces. In the later window, the pair sits one position apart in about 47 in 100 traces and four apart in the remainder. [LAG001]
- In the earlier window, A14, A15 and A20 each co-occurred with A21 and with A36 in 100% of traces, always in one fixed order (A36 before A14 before A20 before A15 before A21). In the later window, co-occurrence drops to about 53 in 100, and in about 47 in 100 traces A21 and A36 appear without any of A14, A15, A20. Where the activities still run together, their relative order is unchanged — no reversed ordering appears in either window. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, REL007, REL008, REL009]
- A21's mean event position moved slightly earlier (about 0.43 to 0.40 of the trace) and became more variable in the later window. [POS001]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that, in the later window, the block A14 → A20 → A15 sitting between A36 and A21 became optional: in roughly 47 in 100 later traces all three activities are skipped and A36 passes directly to A21, while the remaining traces still execute the full fixed sequence A36 → A14 → A20 → A15 → A21 in its original order. [OCC001, DIR001, LAG001, REL003]

The supporting signs are direct and mutually consistent: the new A36→A21 transition is observed outright, and its share (about 47 in 100 of A36's outgoing transitions) matches both the share of traces in which A14, A15 and A20 are jointly absent and the share of one-step A36–A21 distances. A36 and A21 still occur in every trace in both windows, and the three bypassed activities are the only ones whose participation dropped; because a predecessor now leads directly to a successor, this is a bypass of an existing block rather than a shift in how often existing alternative paths are taken. The screening context reinforces this: 15 control-flow items qualified where only about 0.35 would be expected if nothing had changed, far beyond what chance produces. [DIR001, LAG001, OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, REL003, REL006, REL008]

Limitations: the evidence shows nothing about what determines whether a case takes the shortcut, and it cannot distinguish a single process offering both routes from a later window that pools two distinct case populations. The slightly earlier and more variable event position of A21 fits the same bypass but comes from the same traces and is not an independent confirmation. [POS001]

Other differences: none standing apart from the hypothesis above; the only remaining item, the small forward shift of A21's event position in the later window, is consistent with the same bypass rather than a separate change. [POS001]
