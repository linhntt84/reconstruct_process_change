### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis of a broad change in resource allocation. In the later window, the work on A01, A03, A13, A14, A17 and A22 was handled by a largely new group of resources led by User_98 rather than the earlier group around User_69, the work on A08 and A11 moved to another new group led by User_133, and A04, previously shared across 29 resources, became concentrated on a single resource. The support is direct and consistent: assignment shares measure allocation directly, all nine resource items point the same way with an empirical strength of about 999.5 in 1000 reference comparisons, and the resource family produced nine qualifying items where fewer than 0.01 were expected without a change; the activities also occurred at broadly similar rates in both windows, so the shift concerns which resources executed them rather than how often. Limitations: several activity pairs share identical profiles (A01 with A03, A13 with A14, A08 with A11), so the items are not fully independent, and the evidence cannot distinguish a deliberate reassignment of work from a change in which resources were available or in how the anonymous resource codes map to executors. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, ASSIGN007, ASSIGN008, CONC001]
