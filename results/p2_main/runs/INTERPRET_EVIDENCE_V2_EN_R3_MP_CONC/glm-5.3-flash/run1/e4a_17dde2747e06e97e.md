### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that, in the later window, about 47 in 100 traces skip the activity block A14 → A20 → A15: A36 is followed directly by A21 instead of the full sequence A36 → A14 → A20 → A15 → A21, which the remaining ~53 in 100 traces still execute in the original order. A36 and A21 continue to run in every trace, so the three middle activities became skippable rather than removed [DIR001, LAG001, OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, REL003, REL004, REL008, REL009].

The routing change is shown directly by A36's outgoing transitions splitting between A14 and A21, and by A21 following A36 within one step in the skipping traces; the participation of the three block activities drops in unison and the co-occurrence patterns match a shared skip. With 16 control-flow items qualifying against roughly 0.35 expected for an unchanged process, the pattern as a whole is not a sampling artefact, although the items largely mirror one underlying trace behaviour rather than independent confirmations [DIR001, LAG001, OCC001, OCC002, OCC003]. The evidence cannot show whether the direct A36→A21 route is a newly added connection or an existing path that was simply not taken before, nor what distinguishes traces that skip the block from those that do not [DIR001, LAG001].

Other differences: the timing items (2 against about 0.01 expected by chance) point to timing changes the bypass does not explain: the A23→A24 completion gap halved to about 0.5 hour with a much narrower spread, and the A36→A14 completion gap doubled to about 2.0 hours in the 106 later-window traces where both activities still occur [TWAIT001, TWAIT002].
