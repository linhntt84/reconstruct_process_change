### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, each of the ten activities A33, A34, A35, A37, A38, A39, A40, A41, A42 and A43 ran in only about half of the cases (roughly 48–53 in 100 traces, about once per case). In the later window, each of them runs in about 98–100 in 100 cases, still about once per case (recurrence stays near zero). [OCC001, OCC005, OCC006, OCC010, POS003, POS008]
- The ten activities formed two blocks that were mutually exclusive earlier: a pair such as A33 with A39 co-occurred in at most about 1–2 in 100 cases, each otherwise appearing without the other. In the later window, such cross-block pairs co-occur in about 96–98 in 100 cases, with the first-block activity (A33–A38) before the second-block activity (A39–A43) in about 96 in 100 of them. [REL025, REL029, REL033, REL040, REL051, REL055]
- Within each block, activities already co-occurred whenever their block ran (about 48–53 in 100 cases earlier) and now co-occur in about 98–100 in 100; in both windows both orders occur in similar shares, so the order inside each block stays unfixed. [REL021, REL030, REL045, REL056, REL060, REL065]
- A30 and A31 appear in all cases in both windows. In the later window, A30 precedes each of the ten activities in about 97–99 in 100 cases and A31 follows each of them in about 97–99 in 100; earlier, A30 preceded whichever single block ran, and A31 came after it (A31 co-occurred with the first block in about 48 in 100 cases and with the second in about 52 in 100). [REL001, REL006, REL010, REL011, REL016, REL020]
- Routing shifted accordingly: A30's outgoing transitions went earlier to both blocks (A34 about 15%, A42 about 13%, A41 about 11% of observed outgoing edges) and now go almost only to the first block (A38 about 24%, A37 about 21%, A34 about 20%, A33 about 19%, A35 about 15%), with A39–A43 near zero. Direct transitions from A33, A34, A35 and A37 into A31 (about 15–27% of their outgoing edges earlier) have essentially disappeared. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005]
- Relative event positions moved consistently: A30 earlier (mean about 0.64 to 0.55), first-block activities earlier (about 0.72–0.73 to 0.63), second-block activities later (about 0.72 to 0.76) and A31 slightly later (0.82 to 0.84). [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS006, POS008, POS012]
- The seven lag items each rest on only 1–4 observations in one window (for example A30–A40 has 103 supporting cases earlier but 1 later), so they carry little weight. [LAG001, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a structural change in which two activity groups that were alternative branches in the earlier window — {A33, A34, A35, A37, A38} and {A39, A40, A41, A42, A43} — are executed together in the later window, one after the other: A30, then the first group in mixed internal order, then the second group in mixed internal order, then A31. Earlier, each case ran exactly one of the two groups (about 48–53 in 100 cases each) with A31 last; in the later window essentially every case runs both groups. [REL025, REL033, REL051, OCC001, OCC006, DIR001, POS003, POS008]

This is supported directly and consistently: the alternatives-together change is shown relationally (cross-group co-occurrence rising from about 0–2 in 100 to about 96–98 in 100 with a stable first-before-second order), participation of all ten activities rose to near-universal, and the routing and position changes fit the same picture (A30 now leads only into the first group, and the first group's direct edges into A31 vanished, as expected once the second group sits between them). With 99 control-flow items qualifying against roughly 1.2 expected if nothing had changed, chance sampling cannot explain the pattern as a whole. [REL025, REL046, DIR001, DIR002, OCC001, POS001, POS008]

Limitations: the evidence cannot distinguish a change in routing logic from a shift in the case mix (for example, the later window containing only cases of the both-groups kind); positions are event positions, not clock times; and the lag items, with 1–4 observations in one window, were not load-bearing. [LAG001, LAG005, POS001]

Other differences: only minor remnants at noise level — a few new outgoing edges with about 0.5% shares (for example A30 to A08 and A30 to A10) and recurrence-support changes of at most about 1 in 100 cases. [DIR001, DIR002, OCC001, OCC003]
