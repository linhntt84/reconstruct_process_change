### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that routing after A27 changed: in the later window, A27 leads to A26 in a majority of its outgoing transitions (about 58 in 100, up from about 18), direct transitions from A27 to A30 become rare (about 13 in 100, down from about 53), and A30, when it still follows A27, does so 2–3 events later instead of immediately. The successor redistribution is observed directly, and the lag distribution of the A27–A30 pair shows the same change from the positional side; both differences are larger than in about 995 of 1,000 random re-splits, and the screening qualified 6 items from 101 tested units where about 0.5 would be expected if nothing had changed. [DIR001, LAG001, OCC001, OCC002, LAG002, LAG003]

Limitations: the later routing shares rest on only about 31 observed outgoing transitions of A27 (49 before), and DIR001 and LAG001 describe the same traces, so they are two views of one difference rather than independent confirmations; the evidence also cannot show whether A30 is now reached later by way of A26 or skipped in some traces. A competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that the mix of traces in which A27 occurs changed (A27 itself appears in fewer traces), so the rerouting could partly reflect a changed case mix rather than a changed branching alone. [DIR001, LAG001]

Other differences:
- A17 and A18 are each executed in about 14 fewer of 100 traces; the two parallel drops may reflect one underlying change, but no relational evidence links the two activities, so each could also be an isolated participation decrease. [OCC001, OCC002]
- The distance from A33 to A22 lengthens by about one event while the distance from A33 to A23 shortens by about one event; this mirror pattern is consistent with A22 and A23 exchanging their order after A33, but the direct relation between A22 and A23 was not observed, so a swap remains unconfirmed. [LAG002, LAG003]
