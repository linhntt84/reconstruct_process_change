### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, A19, A20 and A25 were not observed in any of the 200 traces; in the later window they occur in about 30, 41 and 30 in 100 traces respectively (59, 82 and 60 of 200). [OCC002, OCC003, OCC004]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that, in the later window, one of A19, A20 or A25 is executed as an optional step between A27 and A29 in roughly 30–41 in 100 traces, the three acting as mutually exclusive alternatives, whereas in the earlier window the flow passed directly from A27 via A28 to A29 and none of the three was observed. The placement is shown directly: each new activity co-occurs with A27, A28 and A29 in essentially all of its traces, always after A27 and before A29 [REL003, REL005, REL007, REL009, REL010, REL012]; A27 and A28 now route roughly 40–55 in 100 of their outgoing steps to these activities [DIR004, DIR005]; and the step distances between A27, A28 and A29 widened by exactly the amount expected if one extra step is inserted [LAG001, LAG002, LAG003]. The slight position shifts of A27 (earlier) and A29 (later) are consistent with this [POS004, POS005]. Screening qualified 29 items where roughly 0.54 would be expected by chance for 109 tested units, so the pattern as a whole is not what an unchanged process typically produces. [OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, DIR004, DIR005, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003]

Limitations: the new step's order around A28 is mixed (each order in roughly 13–23 in 100 traces), so the evidence pins the insertion only to the segment between A27 and A29, not to a single point [REL004, REL008, REL011]. A competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that A19, A20 and A25 already existed at rates too low to be sampled in 200 traces; even so, a rise to roughly a third of all traces is far beyond what sampling noise explains. [OCC002, OCC003, OCC004]

Other differences: A10 was observed in about 5 in 100 traces before and about 13 in 100 after (10 vs 27 of 200), a modest rise resting on few observations. [OCC001]
