### Observed differences

- The relative order of two groups of activities flipped. In the earlier window, A21 and A22 each came before A30, A31 and A36 in 100% of traces; in the later window, A30, A31 and A36 each come before A21 and A22 in about 995 of 1000 traces. All six activity pairs still co-occur in every trace in both windows, so participation itself did not change. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006]
- Successors changed accordingly. In the earlier window A20 always led to A22; in the later window it leads to A30, A31 or A36 (about 33 in 100 outgoing transitions each) and almost never to A22 (about 5 in 1000). A21 earlier led to A30, A31 or A36 (roughly 28–38 in 100 each); in the later window it leads to A25 in about 995 of 1000 transitions. A30, A31 and A36 earlier led to A25 in about 30 of 100 transitions each; in the later window they lead to A22 in about 30–34 of 100 and to A25 in only about 5 of 1000. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005]
- Step distances moved consistently with this: A20 to A22 was immediate (1 step) in all traces before and is 4 steps apart in about 995 of 1000 later traces; A21 to A25 was 4 steps apart before and is immediate in about 995 of 1000 later traces. [LAG001, LAG002]
- Relative positions shifted the same way: A30, A31 and A36 moved earlier (mean relative position from about 0.59 to about 0.41 of the trace), while A22 (about 0.38 to 0.47) and A21 (about 0.53 to 0.62) moved later. [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005]
- Six further lag items (A21/A30, A21/A31, A21/A36, A30/A25, A31/A25, A36/A25) show the old distances nearly vanishing in the later window, but their later-window profiles rest on a single observation each, so they agree with the picture above while being weak on their own. [LAG003, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006, LAG007, LAG008]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the group A30/A31/A36 changed its place in the sequence relative to A20, A22, A21 and A25. In the earlier window, A20 was always immediately followed by A22, A21 and A22 always preceded the group, A21 led into it, and A25 followed about four steps after A21 (i.e., after the group); in the later window, the group runs directly after A20 and before A22 and A21, A22 now follows the group (four steps after A20), and A25 immediately follows A21. This is shown directly by the order flips between A21/A22 and the group and by the successor changes at A20, A21 and the group; the distance and position shifts agree with it. Because every involved activity still occurs in essentially all traces in both windows (co-occurrence 100%, transition support 200 of 200), this is a reordering of the flow rather than activities becoming optional or paths being replaced. Every item's difference exceeds those of 199 of 200 random re-splits, and 24 items qualified against roughly 0.41 expected without change, so the pattern as a whole is not what an unchanged process typically produces. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, LAG001, LAG002, POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, LAG003, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006, LAG007, LAG008]

Limitations: all evidence is positional (event positions and step counts, not clock time), so no conclusion about timing or duration is possible. The evidence establishes the flip in relative order but cannot distinguish a single relocation of the A30/A31/A36 group from separate moves of A22/A21 and of A25, and the roughly 5 in 1000 later traces that keep the old order could be a persisting rare variant; the six weak lag items rest on one later-window observation each. [POS001, LAG001, REL001]
