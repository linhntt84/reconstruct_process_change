### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, the six activities A28, A30, A31 and A32, A33, A34 each ran in only about half of the cases (roughly 49 in 100 for A28/A30/A31 and 52 in 100 for A32/A33/A34, each once per trace); in the later window, all six run in nearly every case (about 98–99 in 100), still once per trace. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006]
- The two triples were mutually exclusive before: they never appeared in the same trace (co-occurrence 0 of 200), with one triple or the other present in each case in roughly equal shares; in the later window they co-occur in about 98 in 100 cases, always with A28/A30/A31 before A32/A33/A34. [REL015, REL016, REL017, REL019, REL020, REL021, REL022, REL023, REL024]
- Routing was rewired around this: from A25, transitions into A32, A33 and A34 (shares of about 0.15–0.19 before) fell to about 0.01 or less, while entries into A28, A30 and A31 rose to about 0.30–0.38; from A28, A30 and A31, the direct transition to A26 (about 0.30–0.36 before) nearly disappeared (about 0.005 after, or no longer observed) and new transitions into A32, A33 and A34 appeared (about 0.08–0.13 each). [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004]
- Positions and spacings shifted consistently: A25 now sits relatively earlier in the trace (mean relative position about 0.67 to 0.62) and A26 later (about 0.80 to 0.83); A28/A30/A31 moved earlier (about 0.74 to 0.68) and A32/A33/A34 later (about 0.73 to 0.77); the gap from A25 to A32/A33/A34 and from A28/A30/A31 to A26 grew from typically 1–3 steps to 4–5 steps. [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006, POS007, POS008, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006]
- Within each triple the order stayed unfixed in both windows (both orders in similar proportions), so the change concerns the blocks as wholes, not a swap of positions. [REL013, REL014, REL018, REL025, REL026, REL027]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the two three-activity blocks {A28, A30, A31} and {A32, A33, A34}, previously mutually exclusive alternatives — each case ran one block or the other in roughly equal shares, never both — are now executed together in nearly every case in a fixed sequence: A25 leads into the first block, the first block flows into the second, and A26 follows the second block. The earlier branch that skipped one of the two blocks is no longer taken, and all six activities have effectively become mandatory. [OCC001, OCC004, REL015, REL019, REL022, DIR001, DIR002, LAG001]

This is supported directly and consistently: 51 items qualified (for example OCC001, REL015, LAG001) against about 0.59 expected by chance among 118 tested units, so chance cannot account for the ensemble; the relational items show the change from alternatives to joint execution directly (co-occurrence between the blocks rising from 0 to about 98 in 100, with the first block before the second); the successor changes in DIR001–DIR004 match the same rewiring; and the participation, position and lag shifts all point the same way rather than being independent confirmations of separate changes. [OCC001, OCC006, REL015, REL024, DIR001, DIR004, POS003, POS008, LAG001, LAG006]

Limitation: the evidence is control-flow only and cannot show whether the earlier exclusivity was a hard routing constraint or a case-mix effect, nor fully rule out that the later window reflects a different case population rather than a rewiring; the near-universal participation of all six activities afterwards makes pure sampling variation unlikely. [REL015, REL019, REL022, OCC001, OCC004]
