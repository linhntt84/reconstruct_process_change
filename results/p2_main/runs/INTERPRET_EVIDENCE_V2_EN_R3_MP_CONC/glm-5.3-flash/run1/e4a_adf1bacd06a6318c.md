### Observed differences

- The choice between two alternative paths after A15 flipped. Earlier, about 75 in 100 of A15's observed outgoing transitions led to A16, A17 or A18 (roughly 27, 23 and 26 in 100 respectively) and about 25 in 100 led to A19, A20 or A21 (roughly 14, 4 and 8 in 100); in the later window this reversed, with about 76 in 100 leading to A19, A20 or A21 (25, 22 and 29 in 100) and about 24 in 100 to A16, A17 or A18 (9, 9 and 7 in 100). All six edges were observed in both windows. [DIR001]
- Participation flipped accordingly: A16, A17 and A18 each ran in about 75 in 100 traces earlier and about 25 in 100 later (150 vs 49 traces), while A19, A20 and A21 each ran in about 25 in 100 earlier and about 76 in 100 later (50 vs 151 traces). No activity repeated within a trace in either window. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006]
- The two groups behave as alternatives: they never occur in the same trace in either window. Earlier, traces typically contained one of A16–A18 and none of A19–A21 (about 75 in 100); later this reversed (about 76 in 100). [REL009, REL010, REL011, REL014, REL015, REL016, REL018, REL019, REL020]

[REL007, REL008, REL013, REL022, REL023, REL025]
- A15 kept its position as predecessor: it preceded A16–A18 whenever they co-occurred in both windows, and precedes A19–A21 in about 76 in 100 later traces. A25 ran alongside whichever group executed — with A16–A18 earlier (about 75 in 100) and with A19–A21 later (about 76 in 100). - The elapsed time between consecutive completions of A10 and A11 roughly doubled, from about 1.0 hour on average (median about 0.99; interquartile range about 0.88–1.10) to about 2.0 hours (median about 1.99; interquartile range about 1.76–2.20), on similar numbers of intervals (344 vs 365). - A smaller shift: the step distance from A15 to A19 spread from mostly one step (about 54 in 100) to a mix of one to three steps (about 33, 28 and 39 in 100). [LAG001]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the two alternative paths after A15 were taken with different frequencies in the later window: the path on which A16, A17 and A18 run together (accompanied by A25) fell from about 75 in 100 cases to about 25 in 100, while the alternative path on which A19, A20 and A21 run together rose from about 25 to about 76 in 100. The support is direct and consistent: A15's outgoing transitions redistributed between the same six, still-observed edges; participation and co-occurrence of both groups flipped in matching proportions; A15 remained the predecessor of whichever group executed; and no repetition behaviour changed. The volume of qualifying control-flow items (35 against roughly 0.53 expected if nothing had changed) and the rarity of differences this size under random re-splits (smaller in about 99.5 in 100 reference comparisons) make chance sampling implausible as an overall explanation. A competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that the mix of case types entering the process shifted, which would produce the same flip in path frequencies without any change in the routing itself. [DIR001, OCC001, OCC004, REL009, REL022]

Other differences:
- The elapsed time between consecutive completions of A10 and A11 roughly doubled (about 1.0 to about 2.0 hours on average; medians about 0.99 vs 1.99 hours; interval counts similar at 344 vs 365). This measures elapsed time between consecutive completion events, not pure waiting or service time; it is a timing hypothesis in its own right, separate from the routing hypothesis, and it is the only time item against roughly 0.005 expected without change. [TWAIT001]
- The step distance from A15 to A19 spread from mostly one step to one-to-three steps, alongside the much larger share of traces now containing that pair. [LAG001]
