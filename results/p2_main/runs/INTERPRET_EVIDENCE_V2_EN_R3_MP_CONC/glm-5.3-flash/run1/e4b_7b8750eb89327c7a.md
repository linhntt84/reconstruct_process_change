### Observed differences

- The sharpest difference concerns activity A08. In the earlier window its executions were spread across about 16 different resources, with the most frequent (User_115) handling about 22 in 100 and the next two about 13 in 100 each; in the later window every observed A08 execution was assigned to User_115 alone (100 in 100, concentration index 1.0 versus about 0.11 before). A08 still occurred at a similar rate (in about 109 of 200 traces before and 106 of 200 after), so it is the assignment, not the occurrence, that changed. [ASSIGN003, CONC001]

- Six other activities (A01, A03, A13, A14, A17, A22) show a consistent redistribution of work across resources. In the earlier window a recurring set of resources handled sizable shares (for example User_13 at about 11 in 100 on A01); in the later window those shares mostly collapsed (User_13 about 4 in 100 on A01), while a largely different set of resources, several of them not among the frequent handlers earlier, took over, and the unattributed remainder grew (on A01 from about 43 to about 51 in 100). [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN007]

- On A13, A14 and A17 the share of User_85 roughly doubled, from about 9 in 100 to about 17–19 in 100, making it the largest single named share in the later window for these activities. [ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006]

- A13 and A14 have identical allocation profiles in both windows, so their two evidence items most likely describe one underlying pattern rather than two independent changes. [ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility of a change in resource allocation between the windows, in two forms: A08 moved from being shared across about 16 resources to being assigned exclusively to User_115, and on A01, A03, A13, A14, A17 and A22 work was reassigned from a recurring set of previously dominant resources (including User_13) to a largely different set, with User_85 roughly doubling its share on A13, A14 and A17. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, ASSIGN007, CONC001]

The resource family produced 8 qualifying items where about 0.004 would be expected if nothing had changed — a count thousands of times the chance expectation — while the control-flow family (0 items against about 0.17 expected) and the time family (0 against about 0.02) produced none, so the difference is specific to allocation and is not accompanied by structural or timing differences at these thresholds. Each difference is larger than in about 1999 of 2000 random re-splits of unchanged data, and the profiles rest on substantial support (for example A01 in 200 of 200 traces per window, A08 in about 109 and 106). [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN003, CONC001]

The evidence shows which resources handled which activities, not why: a change in the composition or availability of the active resource pool itself would produce the same signature as a change in allocation rules, and this evidence cannot separate the two.
