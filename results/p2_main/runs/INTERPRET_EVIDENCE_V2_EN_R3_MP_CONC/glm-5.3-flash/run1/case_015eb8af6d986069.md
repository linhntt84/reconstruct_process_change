### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the ordering constraints among A31, A22, A20, A23, A24, A26, A25 and A32 tightened: in the earlier window these activities were executed in varying orders with varying gaps, while in the later window they run as one fixed sequence, A31 → A22 → A20 → A23 → A24 → A26 → A25 → A32, with immediate succession between consecutive activities. With 82 qualifying items against about 1 expected for an unchanged process, chance cannot explain the evidence as a whole, and the hypothesis is shown directly by relational evidence: all eight tested pairs in the group fixed their direction in the same sense, each of the seven routing sources collapsed to a single dominant target, and the lag distributions became deterministic and mutually consistent with one chain (for example A20 to A23 at gap one, A20 to A25 at gap four, A20 to A32 at gap five). Many of these items describe the same underlying shift and are not independent confirmations. [REL019, DIR009, LAG006, LAG010]

An important competing explanation remains: the windows could be sampling different mixes of pre-existing variants, with the fixed sequence already present but rare in the earlier window; the near-total collapse of each activity's successor distribution to one target in the later window makes a pure mix shift less plausible but cannot fully rule it out. The position and gap measures use event order, not clock time, so no conclusion about timing in hours is implied. [DIR009, LAG012]

Other differences: A08 participates much less often (29 in 100 traces against 72 in 100), occurs later when it does occur, and its routing shifted (A08 to A16 about 90 to 39 in 100; A15 into A08 about 42 to 7 in 100); A10 participates more often (about 7.5 to 22 in 100 traces) with redistributed successors. These participation and routing changes involve activities outside the main hypothesis. [OCC001, POS001, DIR003, DIR006, OCC002, DIR004]
