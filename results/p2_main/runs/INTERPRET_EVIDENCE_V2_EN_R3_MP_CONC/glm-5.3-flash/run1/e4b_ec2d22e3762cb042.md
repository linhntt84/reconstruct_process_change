### Observed differences

- Resource allocation shifted for three activities. For A01, a group of resources that held only about 11 in 100 assignments in the earlier window (no single one above about 6 in 100) held about 44 in 100 in the later window — including User_60 (about 6 to 10 in 100) and User_48 (under 4 to about 7 in 100) — while the group that previously held about 36 in 100 kept about 8. A22 moved toward the same gaining group: its share rose from about 5 to about 52 in 100, and the previous holders fell from about 54 to about 11 in 100. A07 passed to a largely different set of resources led by User_120 (about 6 to 13 in 100); its six previous main resources fell from about 59 in 100 combined to about 11. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003]

- The elapsed time between consecutive completions of A04 and A01 grew markedly: in the earlier window the median gap was about 20 hours (a quarter of gaps under about 7 minutes), in the later window about 66 hours, with the mean rising from about 26 to about 73 hours and even the lower quartile reaching about 17 hours; the pair occurred in essentially every trace in both windows (200 of 200). [TWAIT001]

- In the samples, A12's outgoing transitions show more repetitions of A12 itself (about 49 to 75 in 100) and fewer transitions to A13 (about 24 to 5 in 100); no transitions to A20 or A17 (about 7 in 100 each earlier) appear in the later sample. With only about 40 outgoing transitions per window, this is the kind of difference an unchanged process also produces. [DIR001]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the allocation of work to resources changed: in the later window, A01 and A22 were executed predominantly by a group of resources that had previously carried only a small share (the same group gains on both activities, while a largely disjoint group that previously held about half of A22 and about a third of A01 kept only a few assignments), and A07 moved to a largely separate set of resources. Supporting signs: all three allocation profiles shifted in the same direction, the two activities sharing resource groups changed consistently, A01 occurs in every trace in both windows so its shift cannot be an artefact of which cases contain it, and the resource family yielded 3 qualifying items where about 0.005 were expected if nothing had changed. Limitations: the resource codes are anonymous, so the evidence shows a reallocation between groups but not what drove it; for A07 and A22 the share of traces containing them also moved (about 72 to 85 in 100 and about 57 to 46 in 100), so a changed case mix could contribute to their profile shifts, though it cannot explain the A01 change. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003]

Other differences:
- Timing: the interval between consecutive completions of A04 and A01 roughly tripled at the median (about 20 to about 66 hours), with the whole distribution shifted upward; the time family produced 1 qualifying item where about 0.015 were expected without change, so this is unlikely to be sampling variation. It is a timing change in its own right; the evidence cannot show what the additional elapsed time consists of, nor whether it is connected to the changed allocation of A01. [TWAIT001]
- Control flow: the redistribution of A12's outgoing transitions described above is the only control-flow item, where about 0.17 were expected without change; it is consistent with sampling variation and does not by itself indicate a structural change. [DIR001]
