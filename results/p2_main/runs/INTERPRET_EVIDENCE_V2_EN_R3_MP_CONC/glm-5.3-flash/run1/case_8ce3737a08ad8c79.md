### Observed differences

- The two windows show essentially swapped participation for two activity blocks. A21, A22 and A23 each appeared in about 19 in 100 traces in the earlier window and about 79 in 100 in the later window, while A24, A25 and A26 each fell from about 82 in 100 traces to about 21 in 100. [OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, OCC007]
- At the branch point A20, which occurs in every trace in both windows, the outgoing transitions moved from roughly 82% leading to A24, A25 or A26 (about 19% to A21–A23) in the earlier window to roughly 79% leading to A21, A22 or A23 (about 21% to A24–A26) in the later window. [DIR003]
- The two blocks were mutually exclusive in both windows: traces containing one block almost never contain the other (co-occurrence about 1 in 100 or less). Within the block that executes, the three activities now usually occur together — A21 and A22 co-occurred in about 19 in 100 traces before versus 79 in 100 after — with no fixed order between them (both orders in roughly equal shares). [REL015, REL017, REL023, REL024]
- Downstream activities switched which block they accompany. A30 co-occurred with A24–A26 in about 82 in 100 traces before and with A21–A23 in about 19 in 100; afterwards these shares reversed (about 79 in 100 with A21–A23, about 21 with A24–A26). A32 and A33 co-occurred with A21–A23 in about 9 in 100 traces before versus about 55 in 100 after, and with A24–A26 in about 52 in 100 before versus about 15 in 100 after. [REL020, REL027, REL038, REL045, REL021, REL039]
- A10 became rarer (about 17 in 100 traces before, 7 after), appears at an earlier event position (median about 0.86 versus 0.36 of the trace), and its outgoing transitions spread from almost exclusively A33 (about 97%) across several activities; the later-window side rests on only about 14 observed transitions. [POS001, DIR002, LAG001]
- A02 rose from about 2 to 9 in 100 traces with a completely redistributed successor profile, backed by only 4 versus 18 observations. [OCC001, DIR001]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility of a large shift in how often two existing alternative paths are taken. In the earlier window, about 82 in 100 traces executed the block A24–A26 after A20, and about 19 in 100 executed the alternative block A21–A23; in the later window this reversed to about 79 in 100 for A21–A23 and about 21 in 100 for A24–A26. The shift is visible directly in A20's outgoing transitions, both blocks and all involved activities were observed in substantial shares in both windows, the blocks remained mutually exclusive, and the internal structure of the executing block (three activities together, no fixed order) is unchanged. Downstream, A30 and A32/A33 now typically run with A21–A23 instead of A24–A26, consistent with following whichever block executes. [DIR003, OCC002, OCC005, REL009, REL012, REL015, REL017, REL020, REL021, REL038]

This goes beyond what chance sampling would produce: 60 control-flow items qualified against roughly 1 expected if nothing had changed, and the qualifying items agree in direction across occurrence, routing and relation indicators, with the main activities backed by roughly 37–200 traces per window. An important competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that the routing rule itself did not change but the two windows contain different mixes of two pre-existing case types; both would produce the observed pattern. [OCC002, OCC005, DIR003, REL009, REL012, REL020, REL038]

Other differences: A10 became rarer (about 17 in 100 traces before, 7 after), appears at an earlier event position, and its outgoing transitions spread from almost exclusively A33 to several activities; A02 became slightly more frequent (about 2 to 9 in 100 traces) with redistributed successors. Both rest on few observations (A10: 33 versus 14 transitions; A02: 4 versus 18) and are not part of the main hypothesis. [POS001, DIR002, LAG001, OCC001, DIR001]
