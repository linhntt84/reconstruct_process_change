### Observed differences

- **The order between the two activity groups became fixed.** In the earlier window, pairs spanning the two groups appeared in both orders: A26 preceded A20, A21 or A22 in about 74–76 in 100 traces, A28–A30 preceded A25 in about 80–85 in 100, and several pairs (A20/A28, A21/A30, A25/A27) were split roughly 50/50. In the later window, A20, A21, A22, A24 and A25 precede A26, A27, A28, A29 and A30 in about 96–100 in 100 traces for every tested pair; A25/A26 reversed completely (A26 first in about 95 in 100 before, A25 first in about 98 in 100 after). [REL001, REL003, REL005, REL010, REL014, REL018, REL019, REL021]

- **Routing concentrated on few successors.** Earlier, A20's outgoing transitions spread over many targets (A25 about 21 in 100, A22 about 16, A21 about 14, A26 about 13, A28 about 12, plus smaller shares); later only A21, A22 and A25 remain (about 32–36 in 100 each). A25's dominant successor switched from A36 (about 40 in 100) to A26 (about 98 in 100); A27 now goes to A36 in about 77 in 100 cases and A35 to A24 in about 99 in 100. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR004, DIR006, DIR010]

- **The two groups moved into different phases.** A20, A21, A22, A24 and A25 now occur earlier in the trace (mean relative position about 0.61 to 0.55 for A20–A22, 0.70 to 0.60 for A25), while A26–A30 occur later (about 0.53 to 0.63 for A26, 0.60–0.62 to 0.67–0.68 for A28–A30, 0.70 to 0.72 for A27). [POS001, POS003, POS005, POS006, POS009, POS010]

- **Distances between the activities collapsed.** A21 now follows A20 within 1–2 steps in all traces (before, lags ranged 1–5); A27 now follows A25 exactly 5 steps later in all 200 traces (before, spread over 1–5); A26 follows A24 exactly 5 steps later; A36 follows A27 within 1–2 steps in about 94 in 100 traces. [LAG001, LAG026, LAG028, LAG040]

- Participation itself did not change: each of these activities occurs in essentially every trace in both windows (co-occurrence 1.0 for all tested pairs), so the difference lies in order and routing, not in whether the activities run. [REL001, REL018, POS001]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the ordering constraints between these activities were tightened: in the earlier window A20, A21, A22, A25, A26, A27, A28, A29 and A30 (with A24, A35 and A36 at the boundaries) executed in varying, partly interleaved orders, while in the later window they run in one fixed sequence, roughly A35 → A24 → {A20, A21, A22 in varying order} → A25 → A26 → {A28, A29, A30 in varying order} → A27 → A36. This is a change in ordering constraints (mixed order to fixed order), not an exchange of positions and not a participation change; order within each block still varies in the later window. [REL001, REL005, REL009, REL014, REL018, REL022, DIR004, DIR010, LAG025, LAG026, LAG028, LAG058]

Supporting signs: all 22 tested activity pairs flip from mixed or reversed order to the same fixed direction while co-occurrence stays at 1.0; successor distributions collapse from wide fans to one to three successors; the two groups' position shifts move in opposite directions exactly as the fixed sequence implies; and 100 evidence items qualified against about 1 expected for an unchanged process, so the pattern as a whole is far beyond what random re-splittings produce. [REL001, REL018, DIR001, DIR004, POS005, POS006]

Limitations: the aggregate profiles cannot show whether the old flexible ordering still occurs in variants outside these windows, and the few lag items on the now-rare reverse orders (e.g., A26 before A25, A28 before A25) rest on only 1–3 later-window observations, so only their near-absence is informative. A competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that the later window samples only the subset of cases that always followed this fixed sequence, while other orderings still exist in the process. [LAG033, LAG042, LAG046]
