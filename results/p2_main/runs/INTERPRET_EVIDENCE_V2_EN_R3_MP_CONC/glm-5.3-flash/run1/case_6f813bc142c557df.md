### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the ordering constraints among A31, A22, A20, A23, A24, A26 and A25 changed: in the earlier window these activities were executed in varying orders — some pairs typically reversed relative to the later order, others in both orders without a fixed sequence — while in the later window they follow one fixed chain, A31 → A22 → A20 → A23 → A24 → A26 → A25, which then continues to A32. The 58 qualifying control-flow items are many times the fewer than 1 expected by chance, so the set as a whole cannot be a sampling artefact. The hypothesis is shown directly and consistently: pair orders converge to about 98–99 in 100 cases [REL002, REL004, REL005, REL007, REL008, REL009], outgoing transitions concentrate on the chain successor for every activity involved [DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006, DIR007, DIR008, DIR009], and the observed distances between pairs match their chain distances almost exactly [LAG003, LAG004, LAG009, LAG015, LAG020, LAG025]. Because every activity involved occurs in all traces in both windows, a replacement of one path by another is an unlikely explanation. Limitations: the evidence cannot show whether the earlier mixed orders reflect one flexible behaviour or a mixture of different strict orders across cases, and positions and distances are measured in event positions, not clock time, so no claim about timing is made; individual rare components (such as A08's new occasional successors) could still be sampling noise.

Other differences: A08 participates in fewer traces (about 13 in 100, down from about 32) and, when it occurs, its successors are more spread out and it sits later and more variably within the trace; A14 now rarely leads to A08. A10 participates more often (about 13 in 100 versus about 3). These participation and routing changes are separate from the reordering hypothesis. [OCC001, OCC002, DIR001, DIR002, POS001, REL001]
