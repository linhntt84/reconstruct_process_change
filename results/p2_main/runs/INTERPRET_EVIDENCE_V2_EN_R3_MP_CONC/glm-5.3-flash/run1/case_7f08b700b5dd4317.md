### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, A33, A35, A36, A37, A38 and A39 each appeared exactly once in every one of the 200 traces (recurrence essentially zero). In the later window they appear in only about 47 in 100 traces (A33, A35, A36) and about 56 in 100 traces (A37, A38, A39), still at most once per case. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006]

- The six activities form two groups that act as units. In the later window A33, A35 and A36 occur together in about 47 in 100 traces and are jointly absent in about 53 in 100, never split; A37, A38 and A39 occur together in about 56 in 100 and are jointly absent in about 43 in 100. In the earlier window every such pair co-occurred in 100 in 100 traces. [REL013, REL014, REL018, REL025, REL026, REL027]

- The two groups became alternatives. Pairs across groups (e.g. A33 with A37, A35 with A39, A36 with A38) co-occurred in 100 in 100 earlier traces — with the A33/A35/A36 side first in about 99 in 100 — but in only about 3 in 100 later traces; instead A33/A35/A36 run without A37/A38/A39 in about 44 in 100 traces, and the reverse in about 53 in 100. [REL015, REL016, REL017, REL019, REL020, REL021, REL022, REL023, REL024]

- A30 and A31 are unchanged as anchors: both still occur in every trace, A30 still precedes whichever group runs, and the group still precedes A31. Earlier the structure was A30 → {A33, A35, A36 in mixed order} → {A37, A38, A39 in mixed order} → A31; now A30 appears without each group activity in about 44–54 in 100 traces, where before it was followed by all six. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, REL007, REL008, REL009, REL010, REL011, REL012]

- The executed group now sits directly between the anchors. The gap from A30 to A37/A38/A39 was 4–5 steps in essentially all earlier observations and is 1–3 steps in about 94 in 100 of later ones; the gap from A33/A35/A36 to A31 shrank the same way. A30's mean relative position moved from about 0.62 to 0.67 and A31's from 0.82 to 0.80; A33/A35/A36 moved later (about 0.68 to 0.74) and A39 earlier (0.77 to 0.73). Of A30's observed outgoing transitions, about 36, 34 and 28 in 100 went to A35, A33 and A36 before, while now they spread over all six activities (about 12–19 in 100 each); A33, A35 and A36 now lead to A31 in about 26–34 in 100 of their outgoing transitions, where earlier they led mainly to each other and on to A37/A38/A39. ### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the segment between A30 and A31 changed from running two three-activity groups back-to-back in every case to running one of the two groups per case: in the later window about 47 in 100 cases run the A33/A35/A36 group and about 56 in 100 run the A37/A38/A39 group, with only about 3 in 100 still running both, and the group that runs is placed directly between A30 and A31 (1–3 steps on each side) instead of the six activities spanning a 4–5-step stretch on either side. A30 still opens the segment and A31 still closes it in every case. [OCC001, OCC004, REL015, REL017, REL025, LAG001, LAG004, DIR001, DIR002, POS001, POS002]

This hypothesis is shown directly and consistently by the relational evidence: co-occurrence across the two groups collapsed from universal to about 3 in 100, participation of all six activities halved while A30 and A31 stayed universal, and the routing edges and step distances changed exactly as this reading predicts. Chance is an unlikely explanation for the evidence as a whole: 54 control-flow items qualified where about 0.8 would be expected without a change (160 units tested), roughly 68 times the chance expectation, and every item's difference exceeds about 99 in 100 random re-splits. The items overlap heavily, however — most re-describe the same two-group structure — so they corroborate rather than independently confirm it. [screening, REL015, OCC001, LAG001]

The evidence cannot show what determines which group a case runs, and it cannot distinguish a newly introduced choice from a behaviour that was previously rare (under about 2 in 100 cases, given 200 earlier traces with none observed) and rose to about half of the cases; the roughly 3 in 100 later cases that still run both groups show the earlier behaviour persisting rarely. [REL015, REL025]

Other differences: five gap items about cross-group distances (A33/A35/A36 with A38/A39) rest on only 4–5 later-window observations each and are too weak to interpret; they were not used in the hypothesis. [LAG005, LAG007, LAG008, LAG010, LAG011]
