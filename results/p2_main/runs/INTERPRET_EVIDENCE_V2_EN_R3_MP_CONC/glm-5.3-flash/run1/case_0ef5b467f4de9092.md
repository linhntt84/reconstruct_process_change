### Observed differences

- Two blocks of five activities swapped how often they run. Before, each of A16–A20 appeared in about 19 in 100 traces (37 of 200) and each of A21–A25 in about 82 in 100 (163 of 200); in the later window, A16–A20 each appear in about 77 in 100 traces (153 of 200) and A21–A25 in about 24 in 100 (47 of 200). No activity repeats within a trace in either window. [OCC001, OCC005, OCC006, OCC010]
- The routing out of A15 flipped. Before, about 82 in 100 of the observed outgoing transitions from A15 went to A24 and about 19 in 100 to A19; in the later window, about 77 in 100 go to A19 and about 24 in 100 to A24. [DIR001]
- The two blocks behave as mutually exclusive alternatives in both windows: a pair with one activity from each block never occurs in the same trace (co-occurrence 0). Before, the A21–A25 activity occurred alone in about 82 in 100 traces; in the later window, the A16–A20 activity occurs alone in about 77 in 100. [REL015, REL019, REL024, REL039, REL045]
- A15 now precedes the active block. Before, A15 ran without each of A16–A20 in about 82 in 100 traces and preceded each of A21–A25 in about 82 in 100; in the later window, A15 precedes each of A16–A20 in about 77 in 100 traces and runs without each of A21–A25 in about 77 in 100. [REL001, REL004, REL006, REL010]
- A29 runs in essentially every trace in both windows and co-occurs with whichever block is active: about 82 in 100 traces together with A21–A25 before, about 77 in 100 together with A16–A20 in the later window. [REL020, REL050, REL055, REL065]
- Ordering inside the blocks is stable across windows and only scales with how often the block runs: A19 precedes A16 and A16 precedes A20 in essentially every co-occurrence, while A16 and A17 (and A17 and A18) occur in both orders in similar shares. [REL011, REL013, REL014, REL021]
- Every flagged item is a control-flow item, and each difference is larger than in about 995 of 1000 random re-splits of the pooled traces. [OCC001, DIR001, REL001]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that, in the later window, the choice point at A15 routes cases to the other of two alternative five-activity blocks: the block A16–A20, entered from A15 via A19, rose from about 19 in 100 cases to about 77 in 100, while the block A21–A25, entered via A24, fell from about 82 in 100 to about 24 in 100. Both blocks and all ten activities are observed in substantial shares in both windows (roughly 19–82%), the blocks never run together, no activity is bypassed or repeated, and the within-block orderings are unchanged, so this reads as the same two existing alternative paths being taken with very different frequencies rather than activities being added, removed or skipped. The routing flip is shown directly by A15's outgoing-transition shares, and the participation and relation items change in exactly the coordinated way this hypothesis predicts; A29 runs throughout and simply co-occurs with whichever block is active. With 80 qualifying items against roughly 0.84 expected by chance in an unchanged process, the ensemble of differences cannot be explained by random re-splits, though the items largely describe one underlying difference rather than independent confirmations. [DIR001, OCC001, OCC006, REL001, REL006, REL015, REL020, REL055]

The evidence cannot rule out that the two windows simply sample different mixes of case types that were always routed this way, since only aggregate shares are observed; nor can entries into the blocks other than via A19 and A24 be excluded, as only transitions from A15 to those two activities are observed. [DIR001, REL015]

Other differences: positional gaps shifted only mildly — for example, the A15-to-A17 gap was mostly 4 event positions before and is spread evenly over 2–4 in the later window, the A17-to-A20 gap spread from mostly 1 position towards 1–3, and the A17-to-A29 gap moved towards 4–5 positions; these changes are small (about 0.09–0.10 on the indicator's scale) and are consistent with the block swap rather than an independent placement change. [LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004]
