### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility of a change in timing between A11 and A12: the elapsed time from A11's completion to the completion of the immediately following A12 roughly doubled, from about 0.9 hours per gap to about 1.8 hours. Supporting signs: the time family produced 1 qualifying item where about 0.006 were expected if nothing had changed — more than a hundred times the chance expectation — the shift is consistent across the mean, median and both quartiles, the number of observed gaps is unchanged at 45 per window, and the difference exceeds all but about 1 of 2000 random re-splits. Limitations: the hypothesis rests on a single activity pair with 45 observations per window; TWAIT measures only elapsed time between consecutive completions, so it cannot show whether the extra hour is waiting, processing or work inserted between the two activities, and a change in the mix of cases containing this pair cannot be ruled out. [TWAIT001]

Other differences: the successor shares of A31 and A32 reversed (earlier A31 led mostly to A28 and A32 mostly to A31; later A31 leads mostly to A32 and A32 mostly to A28, on about 90 observed transitions per window in both cases), which is consistent with the dominant order of the two activities flipping while both still lead on to A28. However, the control-flow family produced 2 qualifying items against about 0.27 expected without change — a yield an unchanged process also produces — so this routing flip cannot be separated from a chance sampling difference at the configured thresholds and is not added to the main hypothesis; the evidence likewise shows no link between it and the timing change at A11/A12.
