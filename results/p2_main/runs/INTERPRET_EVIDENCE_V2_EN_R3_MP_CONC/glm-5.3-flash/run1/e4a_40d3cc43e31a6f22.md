### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a timing change around A41: in the later window, the elapsed time from the completion of each of A29, A42 and A44 to the completion of A41 is about half an hour instead of about one hour, and these intervals are markedly more consistent than before. The change is direct (the indicator measures exactly these intervals), consistent in direction and magnitude across three predecessor activities (decreases of about 0.49–0.52 hours, each beyond more than 999 in 1000 random re-splits), and unlikely to be a sampling artefact: the time family yielded 3 items where about 0.01 were expected without change, while the control-flow family yielded none and the pair frequencies stayed roughly stable, pointing to a timing change rather than a structural one. The main limitation is that T_WAIT is the elapsed time between consecutive completion events, so the evidence cannot show whether the reduction lies in the durations of A29, A42 or A44 themselves or in the interval before A41; a competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is therefore a change in the predecessors' durations (or in how events are timestamped) rather than in the wait preceding A41. It also cannot tell whether the shift is local to A41 or part of a wider timing change, since only pairs ending at A41 qualified. [TWAIT001, TWAIT002, TWAIT003]

Other differences: the frequency of the A42→A41 pair rose from 56 to 71 of 200 traces and that of A44→A41 fell from 86 to 75, but these shifts did not qualify as control-flow differences and are within what two 200-trace samples typically produce without any change. [TWAIT002, TWAIT003]
