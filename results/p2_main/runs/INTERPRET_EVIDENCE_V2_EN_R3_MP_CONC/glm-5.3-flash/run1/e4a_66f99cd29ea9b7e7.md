### Observed differences

- The sequence around A20, A22, A21 and the group A30/A31/A36 was rearranged. In the earlier window, A20's observed outgoing transitions split across A30 (about 40 in 100), A31 (about 33) and A36 (about 28); in the later window, about 99 in 100 go to A22. Conversely, A21 was followed by A25 in essentially every earlier transition, but in the later window it leads to A30 (about 36 in 100), A31 (about 33) and A36 (about 30), with A25 almost gone (about 1 in 100). [DIR001, DIR002]
- The group members' successors changed accordingly: earlier, A30, A31 and A36 each led to A22 in roughly 28–38 in 100 of their outgoing transitions; in the later window A22 is essentially never their successor and A25 appears at about 31–36 in 100. [DIR003, DIR004, DIR005]
- All six pairs combining A21 or A22 with A30, A31 or A36 co-occur in every trace in both windows, but their order flips: earlier, A30/A31/A36 came first in all traces; in the later window, A21/A22 come first in about 99 in 100. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006]
- Step distances match: A20 to A22 was 4 steps apart in all earlier traces and 1 step apart in about 99 in 100 later traces; A21 to A25 was 1 step apart earlier and 4 steps apart later. [LAG001, LAG004]
- Event positions shifted consistently: A21 (mean relative position about 0.62 → 0.53) and A22 (about 0.47 → 0.38) occur earlier, while A30, A31 and A36 occur later (about 0.41 → 0.59). [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005]
- Two elapsed-time intervals between consecutive completions changed: A01→A04 rose from about 0.84 to about 1.75 hours on average (38 intervals in each window), and A24→A28 fell from about 1.02 hours to nearly zero (200 intervals in each window). [TWAIT001, TWAIT002]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a reordering hypothesis: the segment formed by A22 and A21 and the three-activity group A30, A31, A36 exchanged their relative order between A20 and A25. In the earlier window, traces ran A20 → one of A30/A31/A36 (in no fixed internal order) → A22 → A21 → A25; in the later window they run A20 → A22 → A21 → one of A30/A31/A36 → A25, with the group's internal order remaining mixed in both windows. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, POS001, POS002, POS003]

The change is shown directly and consistently: all six order relations flip from group-first (100 in 100) to A21/A22-first (about 99 in 100) with full co-occurrence in both windows, the step-distance changes (4→1 and 1→4) and position shifts match the same rearrangement, and the successor changes at A20, A21 and the group members agree. Chance is an unlikely explanation overall: 22 control-flow items qualified where about 0.42 would be expected without a change, and each difference exceeded about 995 of 1,000 random re-splits. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, LAG001, LAG004, POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005]

Limitations: the evidence establishes the reversal of relative order but cannot tell whether A22 and A21 moved earlier or the group moved later, and positions are event indices rather than clock times. It also cannot rule out that the two windows sample two pre-existing variants whose shares changed rather than a modified single variant. Four LAG items (A20–A31, A20–A36, A31–A22, A36–A22) rest on only 2 later-window observations and were not relied upon. [LAG002, LAG003, LAG005, LAG006]

Other differences: two timing changes stand apart from the reordering: intervals between A01 and A04 roughly doubled (mean about 0.84 → 1.75 hours, 38 intervals per window) and intervals between A24 and A28 nearly disappeared (about 1.02 → 0.01 hours, 200 per window); both are far outside random-split variation (2 time items versus about 0.01 expected), and the evidence does not link them to the structural change. [TWAIT001, TWAIT002]
