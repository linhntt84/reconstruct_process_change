### Observed differences

- **A02 became repetitive.** In the earlier window, A02 occurred exactly once in every trace (mean 1.0 per trace, no recurrences); in the later window it occurs about 2.1 times per trace, and in about 50 in 100 traces it runs two or more times. A02 and A03 still appear in every trace in both windows. [OCC001, REL001]

- **Successors were rewired at both A02 and A04.** In the earlier window, all observed outgoing transitions of A02 led to A04, and A04 was always followed by A03; in the later window, about 53 in 100 of A02's outgoing transitions go to A04 and about 47 in 100 go directly to A03, while A04 is now always followed by A02 and never by A03. [DIR001, DIR002]

- **A04 became optional.** In the earlier window, A04 appeared in all 200 traces; in the later window it appears in about 50 in 100 traces (99 of 200), and in the traces that contain it, it occurs about two times on average. [POS002, DIR002]

- **Fixed ordering gave way to mixed ordering.** In the earlier window, the three activities always co-occurred in the single fixed order A02 → A04 → A03; in the later window, A02 and A03 are strictly ordered in only about half of the traces (interleaved or repeated in the rest), A02 and A04 co-occur in about half of the traces — always in an interleaved/repeated pattern — with A04 absent in the other half, and A04 strictly precedes A03 in only about 22 in 100 traces. [REL001, REL002, REL003]

- **Positions spread and pair distances dissolved.** A02 sat at a constant relative position (0.5) in the earlier window and now varies (spread about 0.21, median about 0.67); A04's mean position moved earlier, from 0.75 to about 0.56. The A02→A03 distance was always two steps and now has its largest share at one step (about 40 in 100), with smaller shares at three and five steps; the immediate A04→A03 succession (previously all cases) now occurs at two or four steps and never immediately. [POS001, POS002, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the fixed single pass A02 → A04 → A03 was replaced by a repeatable cycle around A02 and A04 with a direct exit to A03: A02 can now run several times, alternating with A04 (A02 → A04 → A02), and its final occurrence leads straight to A03, so A04 is skipped in about half of the traces and no longer precedes A03 directly. About half of the later traces keep the single pass (one A02, no A04), while the rest run the cycle, with roughly three A02 and two A04 occurrences per trace. [OCC001, DIR001, DIR002, REL002, POS002]

The supporting signs are direct and consistent: the successor changes are observed directly at both A02 and A04; the appearance of recurrence, the halving of A04's participation, the shift from fixed to interleaved/repeated orderings, and the lag patterns (A04 reaching A03 only at two or four steps, i.e., back through A02) all describe the same rewiring; every item's difference exceeds about 995 in 1000 random re-splits of the pooled data; and 11 of 12 tested control-flow units qualified against fewer than 1 (0.06) expected if nothing had changed, so the ensemble is not what a stable process produces. [DIR001, DIR002, OCC001, POS002, REL001, REL002, REL003, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003]

Limitations: the evidence cannot show whether the direct A02 → A03 exit already existed as a rare path in the earlier window (it was merely unobserved among A02's outgoing transitions there), nor whether A03 itself can repeat; the positional shifts are based on event positions, not clock time, and are read as consequences of the cycle rather than a relocation of activities. [DIR001, POS001, POS002, REL001]

Other differences: none observed; the position and distance shifts listed above follow from the same cycle rather than being independent changes. [POS001, POS002, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003]
