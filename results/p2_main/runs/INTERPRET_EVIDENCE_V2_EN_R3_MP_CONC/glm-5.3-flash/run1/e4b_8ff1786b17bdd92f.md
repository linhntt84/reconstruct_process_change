### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that work was reallocated across resources while the control flow stayed intact. In the earlier window, A13 was handled by many different resources (about 46 distinct, the busiest with about 7 in 100 occurrences), whereas in the later window all A13 work was handled by one resource (User_134); A22 shows the same kind of reallocation in milder form, its work moving to a largely different set of resources while remaining dispersed. The signs are direct and consistent: the assignment-mix and concentration indicators show the A13 change from two angles, each backed by roughly 270 occurrences per window, and the resources family produced 3 qualifying items where about 0.006 would be expected if nothing had changed, with every item larger than in essentially all of 2,000 random re-splits. Limitations: the two A13 indicators describe one underlying behaviour rather than two independent confirmations, the evidence covers only A13 and A22, and no control-flow item qualified (0 against about 0.19 expected by chance), so the reallocation cannot be tied to any structural change. A competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is a difference in case mix between the windows: different kinds of cases could naturally use different resources and show longer A04-to-A01 gaps without any change in the process itself, and the evidence offers no case attributes to check this.
