### Observed differences

- The five activities A14, A15, A20, A21 and A22 kept roughly the same participation (about 16–25 in 100 traces in both windows) but moved much earlier in the trace: their mean event position was about 0.60 of the trace in the earlier window and about 0.31–0.32 in the later one. A23 (in every trace, position ~0.33) and A24 (in every trace, ~0.56–0.58) barely moved. [POS001–POS007]
- The order between these five activities and A23/A24 flipped. Earlier, whenever one of the five shared a trace with A23 or A24, it always came after them (e.g., A15 followed A23 in about 20 in 100 traces, never preceded it); in the later window it always came before them (e.g., A21 preceded A23 in about 25 in 100 traces). [REL001–REL008]
- Routing into and out of this group changed fundamentally. Earlier, A24 led to A25 in about 52% of its outgoing transitions and to one of the five in about 48%; each of the five split roughly half to A25 and half to A29; and A25 returned to the group in about 52% of transitions and continued to A29 in about 48% — a loop after A24. Later, A24 led to A25 in about 99.5% of transitions, A25 led to A29 in about 99.5%, and each of the five led to A23 in about 97–100% of transitions. [DIR001–DIR007]
- A42 changed its successor: earlier it was always followed directly by A23; later it is followed by one of the five group activities in about 99% of transitions and almost never by A23. [DIR008]
- Distances became fixed and short: A24→A25 and A25→A29 were immediate (one step) in about 99.5 in 100 cases, versus about 48–52 in 100 earlier; A24 reached A29 in two steps instead of three, and A42 reached A23 in two steps instead of one. [LAG007–LAG010]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the block of five activities A14, A15, A20, A21 and A22 was repositioned from a late loop after A24 into an early segment between A42 and A23, while the late path became a direct chain A24 → A25 → A29. In the earlier window, work after A24 circulated through the five activities and A25 before reaching A29; in the later window, A42 hands over to one of the five, each of the five flows into A23, and A24, A25 and A29 follow one another immediately in almost every case. [REL001–REL008, POS001–POS007, DIR001–DIR008, LAG007–LAG010]

The order flip is shown directly by the relational items, the position shift affects exactly the five activities while A23 and A24 stay in place, and six source-activity successor profiles change coherently in the same direction; the screening produced 34 qualifying items against roughly 0.5 expected without a change, far beyond what chance produces. Most items, however, describe this one underlying restructuring, so they are not independent confirmations of it. [REL001–REL008, POS001–POS005, DIR001–DIR008, LAG007–LAG010]

Limitations: the evidence cannot rule out that the late loop was removed and a different early segment inserted, rather than the same block being moved — the stable participation of the five activities makes relocation plausible but does not prove the steps are the same work. POS and LAG use event positions rather than clock time, so no statement about timing in time is possible, and a few lag items rest on a single after-window observation and were not relied upon. [LAG003–LAG006, POS001–POS007]

Other differences: A12 occurred in about 33 in 100 traces in the later window versus about 19 earlier, and its distance to A23 grew from two to three steps [OCC001, LAG002]; the distance from A11 to A23 became uniformly four steps after having been mixed at three or five steps [LAG001].
