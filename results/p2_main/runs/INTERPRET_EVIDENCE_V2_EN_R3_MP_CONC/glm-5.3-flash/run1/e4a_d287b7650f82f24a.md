### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a change in timing: in the later window, the elapsed time between the completion of A38 or A40 and the subsequent completion of A41 was roughly halved, from about 1 hour to about 0.5 hours, and became markedly more consistent across cases. This rests on two agreeing time items of similar magnitude (about 0.51 and 0.47 hours), both extreme against random re-splits, in a family that produced far more qualifying items than chance would predict; the absence of any control-flow item argues that the change is not accompanied by a structural difference at the configured thresholds. [TWAIT001, TWAIT002]

Limitations: TWAIT measures elapsed time between consecutive completion events, not pure waiting or service time, so the evidence cannot distinguish whether A41 was completed sooner after its predecessor, the predecessors completed earlier, or intervening work between the two events changed. [TWAIT001, TWAIT002]

Other differences: the frequency of the two predecessor–successor gaps moved modestly apart (A38→A41 up from 97 to 113, A40→A41 down from 103 to 87), which could hint at a slight rebalancing of which activity precedes A41; with no control-flow evidence qualifying, this cannot be separated from ordinary sampling variation between the windows. [TWAIT001, TWAIT002]

A competing explanation the evidence cannot fully rule out is a compositional shift — for example, a changed mix of predecessors or of paths between the completion events with different baseline times — although both gap types fell by nearly the same amount to a similar level, which a pure mix shift alone would not typically produce.
