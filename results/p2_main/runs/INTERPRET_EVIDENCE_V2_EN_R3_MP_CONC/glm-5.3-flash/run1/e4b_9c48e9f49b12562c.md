### Observed differences

- Work on five activities (A01, A13, A14, A17, A22) was reassigned to a largely different set of resources. In the earlier window a recurring set of resources held the visible shares — for example User_56 handled about 10 in 100 assignments of A22 and about 6 in 100 of A13 and A14. In the later window these resources lost most of that share or dropped out of the ten most frequent assignees, while resources that were not among the top ten before took over: User_3 and User_78 each held roughly 6 in 100 of the A13, A14 and A17 assignments, and User_78's share of A22 rose from about 6 in 100 to about 12 in 100. ### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that resource allocation changed: in the later window, the work on activities A01, A13, A14, A17 and A22 was assigned to a substantially different set of resources, with a group of previously minor assignees (for example User_3 and User_78) taking over share from the previously frequent ones (for example User_56). This rests on direct allocation evidence: the resource family produced 5 qualifying items against roughly 0.005 expected if nothing had changed — far beyond what chance produces — while control flow produced none (0 against about 0.16), and all five profiles moved in the same direction, each difference exceeding about 999.5 of 1000 random re-splits. The five items most plausibly describe one coherent reallocation rather than five independent shifts. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005]

An important competing explanation remains: a shift in the case mix or in which resources were available could produce the same reassignment pattern, and the evidence cannot distinguish this from a change in allocation itself; the anonymous resource codes also preclude saying anything about the nature of the gaining and losing groups. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005]

Other differences: the elapsed time between consecutive completions of A04 and A01 decreased (median about 67 hours to about 28 hours). This is the time family's only qualifying item (about 0.013 expected without change), so on its own it does not establish a timing change, and the evidence provides no link between it and the reallocation.
