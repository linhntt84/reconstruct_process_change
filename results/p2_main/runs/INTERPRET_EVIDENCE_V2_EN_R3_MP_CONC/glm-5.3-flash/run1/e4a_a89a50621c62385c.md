### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility of a timing change centred on A17: in the later window, the elapsed time between the completion of the preceding activity and the completion of A17 roughly doubled, both after A16 (median about 4 → 8 minutes) and after A28 (about 47 → 95 minutes). Supporting signs: the indicator measures this interval directly, both items show the same direction with all quartiles shifted upward, both differences exceed about 1999 of 2000 random re-splits, and the time family produced 2 qualifying items where only about 0.014 were expected if nothing had changed (control flow: 0 items against about 0.27 expected), so chance is an unlikely explanation for the items as a whole. Limitations: both items share A17, so they may reflect one underlying change rather than two independent ones; the indicator measures elapsed time between consecutive completions and cannot separate waiting from processing, nor show whether A17 started later, took longer, or the predecessors finished earlier; and a change in intervening completions could in principle stretch the measured gaps, although no control-flow item qualified.
