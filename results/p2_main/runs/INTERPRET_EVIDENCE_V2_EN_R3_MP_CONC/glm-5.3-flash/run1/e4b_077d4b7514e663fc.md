### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that resource allocation changed. In the later window, A08 is assigned entirely to one resource (User_121) where it was previously spread across about 17 resources, and across A01, A03, A13, A14, A17 and A21 the workload moved from one group of recurring resources to a largely different group, with a few resources continuing in both windows. This rests on direct allocation measurements: the resource family produced 8 qualifying items against roughly 0.005 expected without change, the same resources gain and lose across activities, and each profile is backed by roughly 108–274 traces per window. Limitations: A13 and A14 (and A01 and A03) show nearly identical profiles, so their items are not independent confirmations; the identifiers are anonymous codes, so no roles can be inferred; and the evidence cannot distinguish a change in assignment rules from a different pool of resources simply being available in the later window. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, ASSIGN007, CONC001]

Other differences: a timing change stands on its own — the A04-to-A01 completion gap's slow tail grew (mean up about 17.5 hours, 75th percentile up about 40 hours, median stable), and with 2 time items against roughly 0.02 expected without change it would qualify as a timing hypothesis in its own right; the evidence cannot show whether it is connected to the allocation change. The A20-to-A10 item is statistically unusual but practically negligible. No control-flow difference qualified, so no reordering or path change is indicated. [TWAIT001, TWAIT002]
