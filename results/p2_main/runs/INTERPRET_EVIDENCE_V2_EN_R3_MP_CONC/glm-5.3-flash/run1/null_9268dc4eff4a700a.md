### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the preferred execution order of the pair A30–A31, which in these traces runs between A26 and A27, reversed. In the earlier window A31 was usually executed before A30 (about 60 in 100 of the traces containing both), so A30 typically led directly on to A27; in the later window A30 was usually before A31 (about 62 in 100), so A31 typically led directly on to A27. Because both orderings are well represented in both windows, this is a shift in how often two existing orderings are taken, not a replacement of one path by another or an exchange of fixed positions. [DIR002, DIR003, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004]

The change is shown directly by relational evidence, and all six order-related items move coherently with the same roughly 20-point magnitude; the A26 split between A30 and A31 mirrors the same flip. Screening qualified 7 control-flow items from 66 tested units against about 0.33 expected without a change, so chance alone cannot account for the set — although the six order items describe one underlying difference rather than independent confirmations. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004]

Limitations: the windows are finite samples of 200 traces each, and A30 and A31 were observed in about 52 in 100 traces before but about 44 in 100 after (103 vs 87 traces) — a small decline the evidence cannot separate from sampling variation and that a pure order flip would not predict. The evidence also cannot rule out that the two windows sampled different case mixes rather than a changed ordering behaviour. [DIR002, DIR003]

Other differences: A26's transitions shifted toward A32, from about 22 to about 30 in 100 of its observed outgoing transitions, while the combined share leading into A30 or A31 fell from about 52 to about 44 in 100; the evidence does not tie this to the order reversal. [DIR001]
