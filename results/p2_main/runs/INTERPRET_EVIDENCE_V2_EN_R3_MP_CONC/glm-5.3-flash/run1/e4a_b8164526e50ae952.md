### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility of a timing change centred on A33: in the later window, the elapsed time from the preceding completion to A33's completion is roughly halved, whether A33 follows A27 (about 0.88 to about 0.47 hours) or A34 (about 0.20 to about 0.08 hours). This rests on direct timing evidence — 2 items in the time family where roughly 0.02 would qualify by chance alone — that is consistent in direction, shifts all quartiles of both distributions downward, and is individually larger than about 999 in 1,000 random re-splits; a consistent change in these inter-completion times is a timing hypothesis in its own right and needs no structural counterpart, and indeed no control-flow indicator qualified. [TWAIT001, TWAIT002, screening]

Limitations: both items share A33, so they may describe one underlying difference rather than two independent confirmations; the A27→A33 item rests on only 35 earlier observations; and the indicator measures the whole elapsed span between consecutive completions, so the shortening cannot be split into waiting versus processing. An important competing explanation is a sequencing change that alters which completions are adjacent (for example, a different mix of predecessors of A33), which could contribute to the shorter pooled times and which the control-flow screening cannot exclude in every form. [TWAIT001, TWAIT002, screening]

Other differences: the consecutive-completion pair A27→A33 was observed more often in the later window (35 vs 54 intervals), while A34→A33 was observed slightly less often (54 vs 48); with no qualifying control-flow items, this is reported as a change in adjacency frequency only, not as an established structural change. [TWAIT001, TWAIT002, screening]
