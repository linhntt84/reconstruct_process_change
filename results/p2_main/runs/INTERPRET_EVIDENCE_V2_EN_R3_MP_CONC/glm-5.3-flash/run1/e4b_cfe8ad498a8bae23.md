### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that work was reallocated across resources. In the earlier window, activities A01, A03, A13, A14, A16, A17 and A22 ran under an allocation in which User_105 was the leading resource; in the later window, User_105 no longer performs these activities, a group of previously minor resources led by User_72 has taken over those shares, and A16 is executed exclusively by User_17. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, ASSIGN007, CONC001]

Supporting signs: the resource family produced 8 qualifying items where about 0.005 would be expected if nothing had changed, far beyond what random re-splits produce; the assignment and concentration indicators measure allocation directly rather than indirectly; all seven activity profiles shift in the same direction (the same resources losing and gaining share), and the concentration item agrees for A16. No control-flow item qualified (0 items against about 0.17 expected), so the qualified evidence is confined to allocation. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, ASSIGN007, CONC001]

Limitations: A13 and A14 show identical profiles, so they may reflect one underlying difference rather than two independent ones; the aggregates cannot show whether every case was reassigned or only the mix of cases shifted, and the evidence cannot rule out such a case-mix explanation. Resource codes are anonymous, so no roles or teams can be inferred. [ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004]

Other differences: the A07–A13 completion gaps shortened (median about 20 hours to about 1.7 hours), but this is a single time item (about 0.014 expected by chance) resting on only 6 earlier observations, so it does not stand as a supported timing change on its own. [TWAIT001]
