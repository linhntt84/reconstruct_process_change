### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, the eight activities formed one rigid sequence: A26 → A17 → A15 → A18 → A19 → A21 → A20 → A27. Every source activity sent all of its outgoing transitions to a single successor (100% each), every activity pair always appeared in the same order, and each pair kept a constant step distance (for example A15 reached A19 always at 2 steps and A27 always at 5 steps). [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006, DIR007, LAG001, LAG005, REL001]
- In the later window, the direct successions are loosened: A15 still leads to A18 in about 68 in 100 cases but also to A19, A20 and A21 in about 11 each; A18 now leads most often to A27 (about 52 in 100) instead of always to A19; A26 splits roughly 53/47 between A17 and A19. [DIR001, DIR003, DIR007]
- The order between the two halves of the chain is no longer fixed. Pairs that previously always ran in one direction now appear in both orders: A19 precedes A15 in about 74 in 100 cases (before: never), A19 precedes A18 in about 85 in 100, A21 precedes A18 in about 68 in 100, while A15/A21 and A18/A20 are close to even splits. Both activities of each pair still occur together in essentially every trace in both windows. [REL001, REL003, REL006, REL007, REL008]
- Step distances spread out: pairs that were always a fixed number of steps apart now occur at varying distances, e.g. A15→A18 at 1 step in about 68 in 100 cases and 2–4 steps in the remainder, and A15→A27 spread over 2–5 steps instead of always 5. [LAG001, LAG005, LAG013]
- Positions shifted consistently with this reordering: A15, A17 and A18 occur slightly later within the trace (mean relative position up by roughly 0.03–0.06), while A19, A20 and A21 occur slightly earlier (down by roughly 0.03–0.06). [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006]
- What did not change: participation. Every one of these activities appears in all 200 traces of both windows, no pair shows one-activity-only or neither patterns, and no within-pair repetition or interleaving was detected. [REL001, POS001]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the fixed sequencing along the chain A26 → A17 → A15 → A18 → A19 → A21 → A20 → A27 was relaxed into a flexible order: activities that previously always followed one another in one fixed sequence — most visibly the block A15/A17/A18 relative to the block A19/A20/A21 — now occur in either order across cases, and single fixed successors gave way to several possible ones. The support is direct (relational evidence shows the fixed-to-mixed change on eight activity pairs), consistent (successor profiles, step-distance spreads and position shifts all point the same way), and the volume of qualifying items (45 control-flow items against fewer than one expected by chance when nothing changes, each difference larger than in about 99.5 in 100 random re-splits) cannot be explained by sampling variation between the two windows. Since participation is unchanged, the difference reflects reordering rather than activities appearing or disappearing. [REL001, REL006, DIR001, DIR003, LAG005, POS004]

Limitation: the aggregate profiles cannot show the exact new routing — they cannot distinguish whether each case now draws a fresh ordering of these activities or whether a few alternative fixed sequences replaced the old one — and the reversal is uneven (strongest around A18/A19, mild between A17 and A21). The items also partly describe the same underlying reordering, so they are not independent confirmations. [REL001, REL004, REL005, DIR001]

Other differences: none beyond this reordering; no change in activity participation was observed in either window. [REL001, POS001]
