### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a change in resource allocation: on A01, A03, A13, A14, A17 and A22, execution was reassigned from one group of resources to another. In the earlier window these activities were led by resources such as User_42 (about 7–8 in 100 executions each); in the later window a largely different group including User_56 holds the largest shares on all of them, with the same direction of shift on every activity. No structural or timing difference qualified, so the change is specific to allocation rather than accompanied by added, removed or reordered activities. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN006, ASSIGN007, screening]

The support is direct and consistent: the ASSIGN indicator measures which resource performed each activity, the same gaining and losing groups recur across all six activities, the resource family produced 8 qualifying items where about 0.005 would be expected if nothing had changed — a count chance cannot plausibly produce — and every item's difference is larger than in all but about 1 of 2000 random re-splits of the pooled data. Limitations: the evidence cannot distinguish a reassignment of work from a change in which resources were active in each window or in the mix of cases handled, since it contains no case-attribute or handover information and the resource codes are anonymous.
