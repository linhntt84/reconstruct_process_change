### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, A21 appeared in every trace and its outgoing flow split roughly in half: about 49 in 100 transitions went to A22, with the rest spread over A27, A28, A33, A34 and A35 (roughly 8–12 in 100 each). In the later window, about 99 in 100 transitions from A21 go to A22, with only ~0.5 in 100 each to A28 and A33. [DIR001]
- Participation flipped sides: A22 rose from about 49 in 100 traces (98 of 200) to about 99 in 100 (198 of 200), while A27, A28, A33, A34 and A35 each fell from about 51 in 100 traces (102 of 200) to about 1 in 100 (2 of 200). [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006]
- In the earlier window, the five activities A27/A28/A33/A34/A35 behaved as one alternative branch: each pair of them co-occurred in the same ~51% of traces and appeared in both orders (no fixed internal order), they always followed A21 and preceded A23, and they never appeared together with A22. In the later window they are almost never observed at all. [REL002, REL008, REL013, REL018]
- The surviving route is a fixed sequence: A21 was always before A22, and A22 before A23; earlier, A23 still appeared without A22 in about 51 in 100 traces, whereas in the later window A21 → A22 → A23 holds in about 99 in 100 traces. [REL001, REL007]
- Relative positions shifted slightly: A21 now sits a bit later in the trace (mean about 0.46 → 0.49 of trace length) and A23 a bit earlier (about 0.59 → 0.56). [POS001, POS002]
- Caveat: the later-window routing and step-distance profiles of the five branch activities themselves (e.g., "A27 leads only to A35") rest on only 1–2 traces and do not describe stable new behaviour. [DIR002, LAG001]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that, in the later window, the branch from A21 through A27, A28, A33, A34 and A35 — five activities that earlier ran together, in varying order, between A21 and A23 in about half of the cases — is almost never taken (about 1 in 100 traces), and A21 leads almost always to A22; A22 changed from an optional step (about 49 in 100 cases) to a near-universal one (about 99 in 100), always followed by A23. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, DIR001, REL001, REL007, REL008]

Supporting signs: participation, the outgoing routing of A21, and the pairwise co-occurrence and order relations all move in the same direction, and the key items rest on all 200 traces in each window; screening tested 124 control-flow units and 62 items qualified, where about 0.62 would be expected if nothing had changed, so the pattern as a whole is far beyond what random re-splits of a stable process produce. [DIR001, REL001, REL007, OCC001]

Because the five activities are nearly unobserved in the later window, this reads as a branch being skipped or its activities becoming almost never executed, not as a re-weighting between paths that all remain in use. [OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006]

Limitations and competing explanation: "not observed" is not proof of removal — the five activities still appear in about 2 of 200 later traces, so a rarely used remaining branch cannot be excluded, and the internal order of the branch in the later window cannot be characterised from 1–2 traces. [OCC002, DIR002]

Other differences: the small position shifts of A21 (later) and A23 (earlier) are consistent with the branch no longer sitting between them rather than being an independent change. [POS001, POS002]
