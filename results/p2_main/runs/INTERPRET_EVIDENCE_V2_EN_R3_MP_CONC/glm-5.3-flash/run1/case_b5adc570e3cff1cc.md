### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the alternative block A14/A15/A20 — exactly one of which executes per case — was relocated from the late stage of the process (between A22/A23 and A27) to the early stage (between A40 and A21). The change is shown directly on the relational and positional indicators: successor profiles reversed (A40 from always A21 to A14/A15/A20; A14/A15/A20 from A23/A27 to always A21; A22 and A23 from mixed successors to fixed ones), the order relative to A21 and A22 flipped from after to before, relative positions moved from about 0.6 to about 0.3 of the trace, and step distances changed consistently (A40→A21 from 1 to 2 steps, A22→A27 from 3 to 2). Participation of the three activities stayed essentially unchanged and still totals one execution per trace, which fits a move rather than activities being added or removed; with 24 qualifying items against roughly 0.39 expected by chance in a stable process, the pattern as a whole is not what sampling variation typically produces. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006, POS001, POS002, POS003, REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004]

Limitations: positions are event-based, so this shows a change in where the activities sit in the sequence, not a clock-time rescheduling. A competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that the late executions of A14/A15/A20 were removed and their early execution added as a separate insertion rather than a single relocation — behaviourally equivalent here, but not distinguishable from the logs. The small position shifts of A21, A22 and A23 (about 0.01–0.03 later) may simply be a side effect of the block now sitting earlier in the trace. [POS004, POS005, POS006]

Other differences: A10 and A11 occur in every trace in both windows but repeat less often in the later window (mean about 1.9 to about 1.6 occurrences per trace; recurrence in about 47 down to 38 in 100 traces) — a reduction in repetition that is not part of the relocation hypothesis.
