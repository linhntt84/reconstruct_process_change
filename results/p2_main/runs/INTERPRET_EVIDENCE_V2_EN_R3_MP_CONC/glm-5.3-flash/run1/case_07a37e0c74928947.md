### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, A19, A22, A23, A24 and A25 acted as mutually exclusive alternatives: each appeared in roughly 15–23% of cases (31–45 of 200), they almost never co-occurred, and together they covered essentially every case. In the later window they are nearly gone: A24 was not observed at all, and the others appear in only 0–2 of 200 cases (about 1 in 100 or fewer). [OCC001, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, OCC007, REL003, REL031, REL039, REL044]
- A20, A21, A30, A31 and A32 were not observed in the earlier window (0 of 200 cases); in the later window each of them runs in about 97.5% of cases (195 of 200). [OCC002, OCC003, OCC008, OCC009, OCC010]
- In the later window these five activities co-occur in about 97.5% of cases and appear in both orders in similar proportions (for example, A20 precedes A21 in about 46% of cases and A21 precedes A20 in about 52%), so there is no fixed order among them; in the earlier window they never shared a case. [REL012, REL017, REL026, REL053, REL054, REL057]
- In both windows this block sits between A48 and A33. Earlier, each old alternative ran after A48 and before A33 in the cases where it occurred (for A19, both relations held in about 22% of all cases). Later, A48 precedes each new activity, and each new activity precedes A33, in about 96–97% of cases. [REL010, REL011, REL020, REL021, REL048, REL055, REL060, REL061]
- A48's outgoing transitions switched accordingly: earlier they went mainly to A19, A22, A23, A24 and A25 (about 14–21% each of A48's observed outgoing transitions); later they go to A20, A21, A30, A31 and A32 (about 15–20% each). [DIR009]
- The new activities' outgoing transitions spread almost evenly over the other block members and over A33 (roughly 15–26% each), consistent with execution without a fixed internal order. [DIR002, DIR003, DIR006, DIR007, DIR008]
- Consistent with a larger block now running between them, A33's events sit later in the trace (mean position about 0.43 to 0.48) and A48's earlier (about 0.36 to 0.31); these are event positions, not clock times. [POS011, POS012]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the segment between A48 and A33 changed in composition and cardinality: in the earlier window it consisted of exactly one of five alternative activities per case (A19, A22, A23, A24 or A25, each taken in roughly 15–23% of cases), whereas in the later window a group of five activities (A20, A21, A30, A31, A32) executes together in nearly every case (about 97.5%), in no fixed order among themselves, still after A48 and before A33. The support is direct and consistent: participation swings from 0 to about 195 of 200 cases with large support counts, the relational items show the new co-occurrence and the A48-before / before-A33 ordering directly, and A48's successor profile switched from the old set to the new set; moreover, 92 control-flow items qualified against roughly 1 expected by chance, so the volume of flagged differences cannot be explained by random re-splits of a stable process. [OCC002, OCC003, OCC008, OCC009, OCC010, REL012, REL020, REL021, DIR009]

Limitations: zero participation in a window only means an activity was not observed there, so the evidence cannot show whether the old activities were removed from the process or merely no longer exercised; similar shares of both orders show the absence of a fixed sequence but cannot distinguish truly parallel execution from flexible sequencing; and whether the disappearance of the old alternatives and the appearance of the new block are one coordinated replacement or two independent changes at the same location cannot be decided from these two windows. [OCC006, REL012, DIR009]

Other differences: a few residual executions of the old activities remain in the later window (A19, A22 and A23 in about 1 in 100 cases, A25 in about 0.5 in 100), and the outgoing-transition profiles observed for A19 and A22 there rest on only 2 traces each, so they are unreliable. [OCC001, OCC004, OCC005, OCC007, DIR001, DIR004]
