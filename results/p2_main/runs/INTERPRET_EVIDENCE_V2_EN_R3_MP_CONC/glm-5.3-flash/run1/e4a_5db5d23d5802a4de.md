### Observed differences

- **Timing:** the elapsed time between consecutive completions of A41 and A18 roughly halved. In the earlier window these intervals averaged about 2.0 hours (median about 2.0; middle half between about 1.8 and 2.1 hours), while in the later window they averaged about 1.0 hour (median about 1.0; middle half between about 0.9 and 1.1 hours). The spread halved as well (standard deviation from about 0.22 to about 0.11 hours), and the interval occurred about 107 times before versus about 99 times after, so its frequency was essentially stable. - **Routing:** among the outgoing transitions of A21, the share leading to A23 fell from about 31 to about 21 in 100 and to A25 from about 27 to about 20, while the shares leading to A24 rose from about 17 to about 26, to A19 from about 13 to about 17, and to A17 from about 3 to about 7. A21 appeared in essentially all traces in both windows. [DIR001]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a change in timing as a possibility: the elapsed time between the completion of A41 and the completion of A18 decreased by about one hour, from roughly 2.0 hours to roughly 1.0 hour, so A18's completion now follows A41's about twice as quickly. Supporting signs: the item measures exactly this interval, the shift is consistent across the mean, median and quartiles (each about 0.9–1.1 hours lower) with the spread halved, about 100 intervals back each window, and chance is an unlikely explanation — the time family produced one item where about 0.007 would be expected without any change, and only about 1 in 2000 random re-splits of the pooled data showed a larger difference. [TWAIT001]

Limitations: TWAIT is elapsed time between consecutive completion events, so the evidence cannot show whether the reduction lies in waiting, processing or handover, and with no positional or relational items it cannot show whether the activities themselves moved. An important competing explanation is that something between the two completions changed (for example different intervening work) rather than either activity being executed faster; the slight decrease in how often the interval occurs (about 107 versus 99 times) is minor. [TWAIT001]

Other differences: the redistribution of A21's outgoing transitions towards A24 and A19 and away from A23 and A25 is the single control-flow item, in a family where about 0.29 items would be expected by chance in an unchanged process; it is therefore within the range an unchanged process typically produces, involves different activities, and does not corroborate the timing hypothesis.
