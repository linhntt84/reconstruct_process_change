In the earlier window, the ten most frequent resources together handled about 55 in 100 of its executions, led by one resource (User_19) with about 12 in 100; in the later window, the ten leaders together covered only about 43 in 100, User_19 fell to about 3–4 in 100, resources that were not leading before (including User_28) took visible shares, and the long tail of infrequent resources grew from about 45 to about 54 in 100. ### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

In the later window, the execution of activities A01, A03 and A07 was reallocated across resources: work moved away from a small set of previously dominant resources (most visibly User_19 on A01 and A03, and a group of about ten leaders on A07) toward a broader set, with the long tail of infrequent resources growing from about 45 to about 53–54 in 100 on A01 and A03 and from about 18 to about 31 in 100 on A07. This rests on the resource family producing 3 qualifying items where about 0.004 were expected without change, so chance cannot account for the family as a whole; each profile difference is larger than in about 999.5 of 1000 random re-splits; the activities run in nearly all traces in both windows (A01 in 200 of 200, A03 in about 198–199 of 200, A07 in 148 and 140 of 200), so the shift is not a participation effect; and the control-flow (1 item versus about 0.16 expected) and time (0 versus about 0.017) families show nothing beyond chance, pointing to a change specific to allocation rather than to structure or timing. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003]

The resource codes are anonymous, so the evidence cannot show whether the newly leading resources are genuinely different handlers or the same capacity under other codes, and it cannot rule out that a changed mix of cases reaching these activities redistributed the work without any allocation rule changing; individual minor resources shifting by a few percentage points may also partly reflect sampling variation in 200-trace windows. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003]

Other differences: A24 was observed less often in the later window (participation about 29 versus 40 in 100 traces, mean occurrences per trace 0.36 versus 0.60, recurrence support 6% versus 14%). The control-flow family produced one item where about 0.16 were expected without change — within the range a stable process produces — so this observation alone does not support a structural change, and the evidence offers no link between it and the reallocation. [OCC001]
