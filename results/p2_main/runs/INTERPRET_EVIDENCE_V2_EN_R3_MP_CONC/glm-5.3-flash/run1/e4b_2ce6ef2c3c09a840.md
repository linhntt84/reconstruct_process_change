### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis of a change in resource allocation: the work on seven activities (A01, A03, A11, A13, A14, A17, A22) was reassigned from the group of resources that performed it in the earlier window to a largely different group, with A11 changing from being shared across about 20 resources to being handled exclusively by User_115, which previously held only about 15 in 100 of its assignments. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, ASSIGN007, CONC001]

Supporting signs: all eight qualifying items come from the resource family, where 8 items were observed against roughly 0.004 expected by chance for the 8 units tested, while control flow (0 of 36 units) and timing (0 of 36) produced none — consistent with a change confined to allocation; all seven activity profiles move in the same direction; each difference exceeds about 1995 of 2000 random re-splits of the pooled data; the A11 concentration change is corroborated by a second indicator; and every profile rests on well over 100 traces containing the activity in each window. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, ASSIGN007, CONC001]

Limitations: the profiles show only the ten most-used resources plus an aggregated remainder, and that remainder grew to roughly half of all assignments, so the exact composition of the incoming group — and whether its members were genuinely new or previously hidden in the tail — cannot be read from the evidence; the identical profiles for A13 and A14 suggest the items partly reflect one underlying reassignment rather than independent confirmations; and a shift in the mix of cases reaching these activities cannot be ruled out as the driver of the reallocation.
