### Observed differences

- Work on six activities — A01, A03, A13, A14, A17 and A22 — was handed from one group of resources to a largely different group. In the earlier window, each of these activities was handled by a stable group of about ten resources holding roughly 4–9% of its assignments apiece; in the later window, most of that group no longer appears among the leading assignees (for example User_40, previously at about 6–9% on each of these activities, is gone from the top lists), while a new group took the lead, with User_84 at about 8–11% and User_96 at about 7–11% of assignments. ### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the allocation of work changed: for A01, A03, A13, A14, A17 and A22, assignments moved from the group of resources that dominated the earlier window to a largely different group in the later window, with only a few assignees persisting at reduced shares and more work going to a long tail of occasional assignees. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006]

This rests on a family-level comparison that chance cannot explain as a whole: the resource family produced 6 qualifying items where about 0.005 (roughly 1 in 200) would be expected if nothing had changed, while control flow produced 0 items against about 0.17 expected and time 1 item against about 0.017 expected. All six resource items show the same direction (redistributed) with the same incoming and outgoing groups of resources, and each difference is larger than in about 999.5 of 1000 random re-splits of the pooled data. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, TWAIT001]

Limitations: the evidence shows that a different set of resources carried out these activities in the later window, but it cannot distinguish a lasting reassignment of work from a period-specific difference in which resources were active, and the anonymous codes allow no inference about roles or teams. A13 and A14 show identical allocation profiles in both windows, so they likely reflect one shared pattern rather than two independent changes. [ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004]

Other differences: a single time item suggests the gap between consecutive completions of A05 and A09 lengthened (median up about 45%), but the time family produced one item where about 0.017 — roughly 1 in 60 — would qualify by chance, which an unchanged process produces occasionally, so this timing hypothesis remains unconfirmed.
