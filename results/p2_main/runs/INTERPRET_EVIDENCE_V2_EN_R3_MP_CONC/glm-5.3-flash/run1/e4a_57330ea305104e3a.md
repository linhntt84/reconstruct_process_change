### Observed differences

- Routing out of A27 shifted between the two branches leading into the A31/A32 pair: in the earlier window about 29 in 100 of A27's outgoing transitions went to A32 and about 16 to A31; in the later window this reversed, with about 18 to A32 and about 28 to A31, while the branches to A33 (about 29→28) and A34 (about 26→27) stayed stable. A27 occurs in all traces in both windows. [DIR001]
- The dominant successor of A31 flipped: earlier, about 64 in 100 of A31's outgoing transitions went to A28 (about 36 to A32); later, about 62 went to A32 (about 38 to A28). A31 appears in about 90 of 200 traces in each window. [DIR002]
- The mirror image holds at A32: earlier, about 64 of its outgoing transitions went to A31 (about 36 to A28); later, about 62 went to A28 (about 38 to A31), with the same roughly 90-trace support. [DIR003]
- All three routing differences are larger than in about 199 of 200 random re-splits of the pooled data. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003]
- The elapsed time between consecutive completions of A24 and A25 roughly halved: mean about 1.0 hour in the earlier window vs about 0.5 hour in the later one (median 1.01 vs 0.50 h), with the middle half of cases narrowing from roughly 0.90–1.15 h to 0.45–0.55 h; based on 200 observations per window, and larger than in about 1,999 of 2,000 random re-splits. [TWAIT001]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the dominant execution order of A31 and A32 reversed: in the earlier window the flow typically entered the pair from A27 at A32, moved A32→A31 and exited to A28 from A31, whereas in the later window it typically entered at A31, moved A31→A32 and exited to A28 from A32. This is shown directly by the flipped successor profiles of both activities, matches the swapped entry shares at A27, and occurs with unchanged participation (both activities in about 45% of traces in both windows). The control-flow family produced 3 qualifying items where about 0.29 would be expected if nothing had changed, so the pattern as a whole is unlikely under random splitting. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003]

Limitations: the two successor items describe one reversal from opposite ends rather than independent confirmations, and because DIR aggregates transitions across traces, the evidence cannot show whether the opposite order was impossible earlier or already occurred as a minority variant; a shift in the mix of two pre-existing orders is a competing explanation the data cannot rule out. [DIR002, DIR003]

Other differences: the elapsed time between consecutive completions of A24 and A25 halved and became more consistent; this is a timing change in its own right, and no structural counterpart for it appears in this evidence (the time family produced 1 item where about 0.006 would be expected by chance). Note that this measures elapsed time between consecutive completion events, so it may include any work between the two events rather than an isolated waiting or service duration. [TWAIT001]
