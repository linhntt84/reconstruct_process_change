### Observed differences

- The fixed order between two groups of activities gave way to a mixed order. Before, every trace ran A20, A21, A22, A24 and A25 to completion before any of A26–A30: in all 22 measured pairs the first-group activity came first in at least 98 in 100 traces. In the later window, A26 precedes A25 in about 93 in 100 traces (before: under 1 in 100), A26 precedes A20/A21/A22 in about 73 in 100 (before: about 1 in 100), pairs such as A20–A28 or A25–A27 are split roughly 50/50, and only pairs involving A27 keep a clear old-order majority (A21 before A27 in about 79 in 100). [REL001, REL005, REL010, REL018, REL019, REL006]
- New transitions connect the two groups in both directions. Before, A20, A21, A22 and A24 sent virtually all outgoing transitions to A20/A21/A22/A25; in the later window about 28–45 in 100 of them go to A26–A30. A27–A30, which previously pointed almost only at each other and at A36, now send about 35–47 in 100 back to A20–A25; for example, A26 is followed by A24 within five steps in about 46 in 100 later traces versus about 1 in 100 before. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR007, DIR008, DIR009, LAG032]
- Event positions moved towards each other: in the later window A20–A25 occur later in the trace (A25's mean relative position from about 0.61 to 0.70; A20/A21/A22 from about 0.56 to 0.61), while A26–A30 occur earlier (A26 from about 0.63 to 0.55; A28–A30 from about 0.68 to 0.62). [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS005, POS006, POS008, POS009, POS010]
- Distances between the groups shrank and spread: A24 to A26 was always 5 positions apart before and is now mostly 1–3; A25 to A27 was always 5 and is now mostly 1–2; A20 to A26 is now typically 1 step (before 2–4). Gaps within the first group also spread (A20 to A21 from lags 1–2 to lags 1–5). [LAG026, LAG028, LAG004, LAG001]
- Participation itself did not change: every measured pair still occurs together in all traces in both windows, so only order and routing changed. [REL001, REL010, REL014, REL018, REL022]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the strict sequencing between two groups of activities was relaxed into interleaving: the group A20, A21, A22, A24, A25, which previously always completed before A26, A27, A28, A29, A30 began, now runs mixed with it in the later window — transitions lead from each group into the other, most measured pairs show mixed or reversed order, and the two groups' position distributions moved towards each other. Because both orders occur in the later window and the new edges point both ways, this is a change in ordering constraints rather than an exchange of the two blocks. [REL001, REL018, DIR001, DIR007, POS001, POS006, LAG026]

The volume of qualifying evidence (98 control-flow items against roughly 1 expected if nothing had changed) rules out sampling noise as an explanation of the pattern as a whole, and the unchanged 100-in-100 co-occurrence of every measured pair shows the difference is not in participation. [screening, REL001]

What the evidence cannot rule out is whether each individual case genuinely alternates between the groups or whether the later window mixes cases that each follow a different fixed route; it also cannot show whether the new transitions are optional alternatives or mandatory steps, and a few lag items rest on very few observations (for example A24 to A36: 2 before, 9 after), so individual new edges should be read with care. [LAG027]

Other differences: A25's dominant successor changed — before, all of its outgoing transitions went to A26, while in the later window about 38 in 100 go to A36 and only about 7 in 100 to A26; A27's transitions to A36 fell from about 82 to 37 in 100; A35, which previously always led to A24, now splits about 53/45 between A24 and A26. [DIR005, DIR006, DIR010]
