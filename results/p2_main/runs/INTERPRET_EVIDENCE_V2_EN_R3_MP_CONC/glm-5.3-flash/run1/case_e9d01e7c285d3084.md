### Observed differences

- **Repetition of A30, A31 and A36.** In the earlier window, each of A30, A31 and A36 occurred exactly once in virtually every trace (mean about 1.0 occurrence per trace, repeats in at most about 2 in 100 traces). In the later window, each occurs about 3.5 times per trace and repeats within a trace in about 68 in 100 traces, while still appearing in every trace. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003]
- **Successors of A36.** Before, about 97 in 100 of A36's observed outgoing transitions led to A25. In the later window, about 70 in 100 lead to A30 and about 28 in 100 to A25; in absolute terms the A36 → A25 transition still fires about as often as before (roughly 195 transitions in either window), while roughly 490 transitions per 200 traces now go from A36 to A30. [DIR001]
- **Ordering among A14, A30 and A36.** These three activities co-occur in all traces in both windows. Before, the strict orders A14 before A30, A14 before A36 and A30 before A36 each held in about 98–99.5 in 100 traces; in the later window each holds in only about 32 in 100, while interleaved or repeated execution rose from about 0–2 in 100 to about 68 in 100 — a change from a fixed order to a mixed one, not an exchange of positions. [REL003, REL004, REL005]
- **Spacing.** A30 was followed by A36 exactly two steps later in about 98 in 100 cases before; in the later window only about 12 in 100 pairs remain two steps apart, with only small shares at lags three to five. The gap between A05 and A14 moved from mostly one–two steps (about 71 in 100) to mostly two–three steps (about 82 in 100). [LAG002, LAG001]
- **Positions (event positions, not clock time).** A30 and A36 now sit at more variable relative positions (spread about 0.07 → 0.12) with slightly later medians (A30 about 0.40 → 0.44; A36 about 0.47 → 0.49); A14's position shifted slightly earlier (median about 0.14 → 0.11) with more spread. [POS002, POS003, POS001]
- **Relation of A05 to A30 and A36.** A05 co-occurs with A30 and with A36 in about 52–55 in 100 traces in both windows (A30/A36 occur without A05 in about 45–49 in 100). Before, A05 came first in about 53–54 in 100 traces; in the later window it comes first in only about 16 in 100, with interleaved or repeated execution in about 35 in 100. [REL001, REL002]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that A30, A31 and A36 changed from single, strictly sequenced steps into repeated activities: in the earlier window each ran once per trace within the fixed sequence A14 → A30 → A36, after which A36 typically led to A25; in the later window each of the three runs about 3–4 times per trace, the strict order among A14, A30 and A36 survives in only about 32 in 100 traces (interleaved or repeated in about 68 in 100), and A36's most common outgoing transition now goes to A30 (about 70 in 100) while its edge to A25 persists at a similar absolute rate. The screening produced 14 qualifying control-flow items where roughly 0.5 would be expected without a change, every item shows empirical strength of about 0.995, and the occurrence, successor, relation and lag items consistently describe this same repetition change; the successor support counts (203 vs 697 outgoing transitions of A36) also match the occurrence means. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, DIR001, REL003, REL004, REL005, LAG002]

The items largely reflect the same underlying traces rather than independent confirmations, and the evidence cannot show the exact shape of the repeat: in particular whether A31 belongs to the same cycle as A30 and A36 (A31 is documented only by its occurrence counts), and whether the interleaving reflects alternating repeats of A30 and A36 or some other repeated arrangement. [OCC002]

Other differences: A05 now relates to A30 and A36 without its fixed preceding role (first in about 16 in 100 traces versus about 53–54 before, interleaved in about 35 in 100) [REL001, REL002]; the A05 → A14 gap shifted from mostly one–two steps to mostly two–three steps [LAG001]; and A14's relative position moved slightly earlier with more spread [POS001].
