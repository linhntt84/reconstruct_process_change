### Observed differences

- The most pronounced difference is in timing around A23. In the earlier window, the elapsed time between consecutive completions of A16 and A23, A17 and A23, A18 and A23, and A19 and A23 each clustered around 1.0–1.1 hours. In the later window, all four intervals sit at about 2.0 hours — roughly double — with a somewhat wider spread. The number of times each pair occurs as consecutive completions stayed similar (for example 58 to 51 for A16–A23 and 52 to 59 for A17–A23). [TWAIT001, TWAIT002, TWAIT003, TWAIT004]

- A39 and A40 occur more often in the later window: each appeared in about 60 in 100 traces before (mean about 0.74 occurrences per trace) versus about 70 in 100 traces after (mean about 0.96), and repeated occurrences within a trace rose from about 12 in 100 to about 19 in 100 traces. Both activities show identical figures, consistent with them moving together as a block. [OCC001, OCC002]

- The outgoing transitions of A05 were redistributed: before, about 40 in 100 went to A09, 37 to A04 and 23 to A03; in the later window about 27 go to A09, 28 to A04 and 45 to A03, so the share leading to A03 roughly doubled. [DIR001]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility of a timing change centred on A23: in the later window, the elapsed time between the completion of A16, A17, A18 or A19 and the next completion of A23 is about twice as long as in the earlier window (about 1 hour versus about 2 hours). Supporting signs: all four time items agree in direction and magnitude (roughly +0.9 to +1.1 hours), each difference is essentially never produced by random re-splits of unchanged windows (a difference this large occurred in only about 1 in 2,000 reference comparisons per item), the time family yielded 4 items where about 0.01 would be expected if nothing had changed, and each pair rests on a moderate number of observations (41–59 per window). The frequency with which these pairs occur as consecutive completions barely moved, so the longer intervals are not a side effect of new activities appearing between them. Limitations: all four items share A23, so they most likely describe one underlying delay rather than four independent changes, and the evidence cannot show whether the extra hour arises because A16–A19 complete later or because A23 is reached later; TWAIT measures elapsed time between consecutive completions, not pure waiting or service time. An important competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that the two sampled periods differ in general clock-time conditions, which would lengthen intervals without any change in the process itself. [TWAIT001, TWAIT002, TWAIT003, TWAIT004]

Other differences: A39 and A40 are observed in more traces and more often per trace, and A05's transitions shifted toward A03 at the expense of A09 and A04. Each of these rests on a single item (the two A39/A40 items carry identical figures and likely describe one joint behaviour), and the control-flow family produced 3 items against about 0.28 expected by chance, so they are consistent with change but not independently confirmed; the evidence does not link them to the timing change around A23.
