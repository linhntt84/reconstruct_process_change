### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a change in resource allocation: in the later window, work on A01, A03, A10, A13, A14, A17 and A22 is handled by a largely different group of resources than before — resources that previously each covered roughly 4–11 in 100 events (including User_46) largely leave the leading positions, a different group including User_39 takes over, and A10's previously concentrated handling spreads out, with about half of its events going to resources beyond the leading group. This rests on direct allocation evidence: the resources family produced 7 qualifying items where about 0.007 would qualify by chance (roughly a thousandfold excess), all items point in the same direction, each difference beats all but about 1 in 2,000 random re-splits, and 133–270 traces per window stand behind each profile while participation itself did not change. The same newly prominent resources recur across activities, suggesting one coordinated reallocation; the seven items therefore likely describe a single underlying difference (A13 and A14 even have identical profiles) rather than independent confirmations. Control-flow indicators produced no qualifying items (0 against about 0.17 expected), so no structural change accompanies the reallocation in this evidence. Limitations: the resource codes are anonymous and the evidence shows only that shares moved; a shift in the mix of cases reaching these activities, rather than a change in the allocation itself, cannot be ruled out. Other differences: the A09–A04 elapsed-time distribution has a higher mean and a much heavier tail in the later window (mean about 0.017 to 0.032 hours). As the only time item, against about 0.015 expected by chance, it is compatible with an unchanged process and does not by itself establish a change in timing. [TWAIT001]
