

### Observed differences

- A30, A31 and A36 are present in all traces in both windows, but in the later window their mean occurrences per trace fall from about 3.5 to about 1.0, and recurrence support falls from about 75 in 100 traces to about 1 to 2 in 100 traces. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003]
- In the later window, A36's outgoing transitions are dominated by A25 (about 92 in 100), whereas in the earlier window A36 was followed by A30 in about 70 in 100 cases and by A25 in about 28 in 100 cases. [DIR002]
- A30's outgoing transitions remain dominated by A31 in both windows, about 99 in 100 before and about 97 in 100 after, with only small changes among rare successors. [DIR001]
- A30 and A36 co-occur in all traces in both windows, but in the later window A30 before A36 occurs in about 98 in 100 traces and interleaved/repeated behaviour in about 2 in 100 traces, whereas in the earlier window interleaved/repeated behaviour occurred in about 76 in 100 traces and A30 before A36 in about 25 in 100 traces. [REL001]
- The event-position gap from A30 to A36 within five steps becomes concentrated at two steps in the later window (about 89 in 100), whereas in the earlier window the two-step gap was only about 12 in 100 of that distribution and the remaining observed gaps were spread over other distances. [LAG001]
- The event-position distributions of A30 and A36 become less variable in the later window, with standard deviations falling from about 0.12 to about 0.07 while their means remain roughly stable. [POS001, POS002]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

- The 11 control-flow items are far above the expected count of about 0.5 without change, so the pattern is not what an unchanged process typically produces. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, DIR001, DIR002, POS001, POS002, REL001, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003]
- The main hypothesis is that, in the later window, the repeated execution of A30, A31 and A36 was reduced to about one execution per trace, and A36 now proceeds to A25 much more often than to A30. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, DIR002, REL001, LAG001]
- Supporting signs are the large drop in occurrences and recurrence for A30, A31 and A36, the shift of A36's successors from A30 to A25, and the change from interleaved/repeated A30/A36 behaviour to A30 before A36 with a two-step gap. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, DIR002, REL001, LAG001]
- A limitation is that A31's involvement is supported mainly by its occurrence drop and by A30's successor profile, while the reverse A36-before-A30 and A36-to-A36 lag profiles are based on very few later observations. [OCC002, DIR001, LAG002, LAG003]
- Other differences: A30's rare successors are redistributed slightly, and the position distributions of A30 and A36 become less variable, but these are consistent with the reduced repetition rather than a separate hypothesis. [DIR001, POS001, POS002]
- An important competing explanation is that the later window contains a different mix of cases, but the unchanged full participation of these activities and the consistent routing/ordering shift make this less likely than a structural repetition/routing change. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, DIR002, REL001]
