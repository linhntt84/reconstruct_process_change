### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

- The main hypothesis is that, in the later window, A20 routes to the group A21, A22 and A23 in about 80 in 100 of its observed outgoing transitions, whereas in the earlier window it routed to the group A24, A25 and A26 in about 80 in 100 of its observed outgoing transitions; the A21-A23 group is executed more often and the A24-A26 group less often, while the order within each group remains mixed. [DIR003, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, OCC007, REL009, REL010, REL011, REL012, REL013, REL014, REL015, REL016, REL023, REL036, REL037, REL041]
- The signs are direct and consistent: A20's successor shares, the participation of the two groups, and their co-occurrence with A20 all move in the same direction, and A20 remains before the group when they occur together. [DIR003, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, OCC007, REL009, REL010, REL011, REL012, REL013, REL014]
- A competing explanation is that the observed shift is a change in the frequency of existing paths rather than a structural replacement of one path by another; the evidence cannot rule this out because both groups remain observed in both windows. [OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, OCC007, DIR003]
- Other differences: A10 became rarer and earlier, A32 became less often followed by A10, and A30, A32 and A33 became more associated with A21, A22 and A23 and less associated with A24, A25 and A26; A02 was rare and its successors changed.
