### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, activities A20, A22, and A23 almost always occurred before A24, A25, and A26. For example, A20 preceded A24 in 98% of traces, A23 preceded A24 in 98.5%, and A23 preceded A26 in 98.5%. In the later window, the order reversed: A24 preceded A20 in 73% of traces, A24 preceded A23 in 83.5%, and A26 preceded A23 in 67.5%. [REL001, REL006, REL008]
- The relative positions of these activities shifted accordingly: A20, A22, and A23 moved later in the trace (mean position increased by about 0.05, 0.03, and 0.06 respectively), while A24, A25, and A26 moved earlier (mean position decreased by about 0.06, 0.02, and 0.04). [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006]
- Successor distributions changed: A20's main successor dropped from A23 (99%) to 67%, with new successors A24, A25, and A26 (each about 11%). A23's successor changed from A24 (100%) to A32 (49%), A25 (16%), A26 (17%), and A24 (15%). A24's successor changed from A26 (99%) to A26 (60%), A22 (23%), and A20 (11%). [DIR001, DIR003, DIR004]
- The distance between these activities also changed: A20 and A23 were always adjacent (lag 1) before, but after only 67.5% were adjacent, with lags 2–4 appearing. A20 and A24 were always 2 steps apart before, but after 41% were adjacent and 57% were 2 steps apart. [LAG001, LAG002]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a reversal in the ordering between two groups of activities: A20, A22, and A23, which previously occurred before A24, A25, and A26, now often occur after them. This is directly shown by the reversal of order in the REL items, the shift in POS, and the changes in successor distributions. The evidence is extensive (49 items vs 0.66 expected by chance) and consistent across indicators.

Other differences: A31's successor changed from A22 (100%) to A22 (52%) and A24 (46.5%), and A32 became a more common successor of A23 and A25. These may be part of the same reordering but are not central to the main hypothesis.

A competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that the observed order change reflects a shift in the frequency of two alternative paths rather than a structural reversal, but the near-total reversal in order (e.g., from 98% A-before-B to 73% B-before-A) makes a simple frequency shift unlikely.
