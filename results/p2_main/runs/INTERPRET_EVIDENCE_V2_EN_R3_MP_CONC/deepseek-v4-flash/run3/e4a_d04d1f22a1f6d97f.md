### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, A22 occurred in every trace (100%) and A21 always led to A22, which was always followed by A23. In the later window, A22 occurs in about 51% of traces, and in the remaining 49% one of A27, A28, or A33 appears instead, each in about 49% of traces (they are mutually exclusive with A22). - In the later window, A21 leads to A22 in about 51% of cases and to A27, A28, or A33 in the rest (about 15–17.5% each). [DIR001]
- The alternatives A27, A28, A33 appear around the middle of the trace (position ~0.5) and are followed by A23 in about 49% of traces, with no fixed order among themselves. [POS001, POS002, POS003, REL009, REL010, REL011, REL012, REL013, REL014]
- The distance between A21 and A23 increased: before, A23 always followed A21 with a lag of 2 steps; in the later window, about half the cases still have lag 2, but the other half have lag 4. [LAG001]
- Timing: the elapsed time between A25 and A29 dropped from a mean of about 0.91 hours to about 0.48 hours, and between A30 and A29 from about 0.18 to about 0.094 hours. [TWAIT001, TWAIT002]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The process changed from a fixed sequence A21 → A22 → A23 to a choice between A22 and a set of alternative activities (A27, A28, A33) between A21 and A23, with the alternatives appearing in about half the traces and no fixed order among them. This is supported by the consistent drop in A22 participation, the appearance of the alternatives, the redistribution of A21's outgoing edges, the mutual exclusivity between A22 and the alternatives, and the co-occurrence of the alternatives with A23. The evidence cannot rule out that the exact internal structure of the alternative branch (e.g., whether additional activities are involved) differs from the simple sequence, but the overall pattern is clear.

Other differences: The timing between A25–A29 and A30–A29 decreased substantially, indicating a separate change in timing.
