### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a change in which a previously mandatory segment of the process (A19, A20) and a previously common segment (A25, A26, A27, A06) are now skipped in a substantial share of cases, and the routing around them changed accordingly. In the earlier window, A19 and A20 were always executed and A06 was present in about a third of traces; in the later window, these activities are absent in about half of the traces, and A06 is present in only about 10 in 100. The successor changes (A43 now often going to A28 instead of A19, A06 no longer leading to A49) and the lag changes (A43–A28 becoming immediate, A06–A49 becoming delayed) are consistent with a re-routing that bypasses this block. The number of qualifying items (59) far exceeds the 0.85 expected by chance, so the overall difference is not a sampling artefact.

Other differences: A06 moved earlier in the trace, and A48 now leads to A49 more directly (lag 1 step in about 97% of cases, up from 71%). These are consistent with the same re-routing but are not part of the main hypothesis.

A competing explanation that cannot be fully ruled out is that the later window contains a different mix of case types, which could produce the same observed participation and routing changes without a change in the underlying process model.
