### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a hypothesis that the process changed from a variant in which A10 was frequently executed and typically followed by A16, to a variant in which A10 is rarely executed, A19 and A20 are always executed, and A43 now leads to A19 instead of A28. This is directly supported by the large drop in A10 participation and co-occurrence with core activities, the rise of A19/A20 to universal participation, the shift in A43's successor, and the change in A10's relative position and lag to A16. The number of qualifying control-flow items (73) far exceeds the 0.96 expected by chance, so the overall difference is not a sampling artefact.

Other differences: A25, A26 and A27 increased in participation, and A02, A06 and A08 increased from low levels. A10's position moved later, A43 earlier, and A28 later. These are consistent with a reordering of the process but are secondary to the main shift.

A competing explanation that cannot be fully ruled out is that the two windows represent different case types or customer segments rather than a change in the underlying process; the evidence does not include case attributes to separate these.
