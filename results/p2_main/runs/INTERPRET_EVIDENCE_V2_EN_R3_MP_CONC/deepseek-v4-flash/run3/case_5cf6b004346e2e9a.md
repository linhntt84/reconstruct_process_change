### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, A08 was followed by A10 (about 50%) or A11 (about 48%), and A10/A11 were followed by A06 or A07. In the later window, A08 is followed by A06 (29%) or A07 (24%) or A10 (27%) or A11 (19%), while A10 and A11 are each followed by A08 in 100% of observed transitions. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003]

- Many lag distributions became more spread out: e.g., A06-A07 was mostly at lag 3 (74%) before, now spread across lags 2–5; A07-A07 was almost always at lag 5 (92%) before, now split between lag 4 and 5. [LAG002, LAG005]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a hypothesis that the ordering of A08 relative to A10 and A11 reversed. In the earlier window, A08 typically preceded A10/A11, which then led to A06/A07. In the later window, A10/A11 precede A08, and A08 then leads to A06/A07. This is directly shown by the successor changes: A08's successors changed from A10/A11 to A06/A07, while A10's and A11's successors changed from A06/A07 to A08. The lag shifts are consistent with this reversal (e.g., A08-A10 at lag 1 decreasing, A10-A08 at lag 1/2 appearing). The number of qualifying items (20) far exceeds the 0.24 expected by chance, so the overall pattern is unlikely to be sampling noise. A competing explanation that cannot be fully ruled out is that the process now has two alternative branches (one with A08 before A10/A11, one after), but the DIR successor distributions show a near-complete switch rather than a mixture.

Other differences: The lag distributions for repeated activities (A06-A06, A07-A07, A08-A08) also changed, with repetitions occurring at shorter distances in the later window, but these are secondary to the main reordering.
