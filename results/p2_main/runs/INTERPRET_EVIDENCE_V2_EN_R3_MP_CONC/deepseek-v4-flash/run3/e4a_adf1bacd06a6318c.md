### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, about 75 in 100 traces contained each of A16, A17, and A18; in the later window, only about 25 in 100 did. Conversely, A19, A20, and A21 were in about 25 in 100 traces earlier and about 75 in 100 later. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006]
- After A15, the outgoing transitions shifted: earlier the largest shares went to A16, A17, and A18 (together about 75% of transitions), while later the largest shares went to A19, A20, and A21 (together about 75%). [DIR001]

- A16–A18 co-occurred with each other in about 75% of traces earlier and almost never later; A19–A21 co-occurred with each other in about 25% earlier and 75% later. [REL007, REL008, REL013, REL022, REL023, REL025]
- A25 co-occurred with A16–A18 earlier (about 75%) and with A19–A21 later (about 75%), with some interleaving in both windows. [REL012, REL017, REL021, REL024, REL026, REL027]
- The elapsed time between A10 and A11 increased from about 1 hour on average to about 2 hours. [TWAIT001]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

- The process likely changed its routing after A15: instead of executing activities A16, A17, and A18, it now executes A19, A20, and A21. This is supported by the occurrence changes, the redistribution of outgoing transitions from A15, and the co-occurrence patterns among the two activity sets. The control-flow evidence is far more extensive than expected by chance (35 items vs 0.53 expected), so the shift is not a sampling artefact. [OCC001–OCC006, DIR001, REL001–REL006, REL007–REL008, REL013, REL022–REL023, REL025]
- The timing between A10 and A11 also changed, roughly doubling the average gap. This is a separate difference, not part of the routing hypothesis. [TWAIT001]
- A competing explanation that cannot be fully ruled out is that the observed shift reflects a change in case mix rather than a change in the process itself, but the consistency across many indicators makes this less likely.
