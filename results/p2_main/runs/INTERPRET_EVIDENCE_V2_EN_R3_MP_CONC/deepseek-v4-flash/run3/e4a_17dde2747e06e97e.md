### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, all 200 traces contained A14, A15 and A20, and the order was fixed: A36 → A14 → A20 → A15 → A21, with A36 always followed by A14 and a lag of 4 steps between A36 and A21. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, DIR001, REL001–REL009, LAG001]
- In the later window, only about 53 in 100 traces contain A14, A15 and A20; the other 47 in 100 do not contain them. In those 47%, A36 leads directly to A21 (lag 1 step) instead of going through A14. [OCC001–OCC003, DIR001, LAG001, REL003, REL004, REL006–REL009]
- The relative position of A21 shifted slightly earlier and A36 slightly later, consistent with the shorter variant. [POS001, POS002]
- The wait between A23 and A24 dropped from about 1 hour to about 0.5 hours. [TWAIT001]
- The wait between A36 and A14 (in the full variant) increased from about 1 hour to about 2 hours, but this edge now occurs in only 53% of traces. [TWAIT002]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that, in the later window, about 47% of cases bypass the middle activities A14, A15 and A20, going directly from A36 to A21, while the remaining 53% follow the original full sequence. This is directly shown by the drop in participation of A14, A15 and A20 from 100% to 53%, the change in A36's outgoing edge from always A14 to 53% A14 and 47% A21, the lag between A36 and A21 changing from always 4 steps to 47% at 1 step, and the co-occurrence patterns showing A21 and A36 present without the middle activities in 47% of traces. [OCC001–OCC003, DIR001, LAG001, REL003, REL004, REL006–REL009]

Other differences: The timing between A23 and A24 decreased by about half, and the timing between A36 and A14 (in the full variant) increased by about a factor of two. These are separate timing changes not directly tied to the structural bypass. [TWAIT001, TWAIT002]

A competing explanation that cannot be fully ruled out is that the short variant existed before but was too rare to be observed in the 200-trace sample; however, the consistency and magnitude of the evidence make this unlikely.
