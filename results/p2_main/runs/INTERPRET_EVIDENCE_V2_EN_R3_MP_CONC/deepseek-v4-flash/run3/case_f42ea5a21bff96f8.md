### Observed differences

- Participation of A21, A22 and A23 dropped from about 75% of traces to about 25% each (OCC004, OCC005, OCC006). In parallel, A24, A25 and A26 rose from about 25% to about 76% each (OCC007, OCC008, OCC009). A06 rose from 17% to 52.5% of traces (OCC002). A02 and A08 also decreased, but from low levels (OCC001, OCC003).
- A06 moved later in the trace: its mean relative position went from about 0.57 to 0.90 (POS001).
- A06’s outgoing behaviour changed: before it had a spread of successors (A33, A19, A16, A15, A35, etc.), after it goes to A35 in about 90% of its occurrences (DIR001). A34’s outgoing changed from 89% to A35 to 48% to A35 and 50% to A06 (DIR005). A20’s outgoing shifted from mostly A21/A22/A23 to mostly A24/A25/A26 (DIR002).
- A06 now co-occurs with A19, A20, A29, A30, A34 and A35 in about 52% of traces, up from 17% (REL001, REL002, REL009–REL012). In those co-occurrences, A06 usually follows those activities (e.g., after A19: 8.5% → 47%; after A34: 2% → 46%) (REL001, REL011). A06 now precedes A35 in 48.5% of traces, up from 17% (REL012).
- A19 and A20 co-occurred with A21–A23 in 75% of traces before, but only 25% after (REL013–REL015, REL019–REL021). A21–A23 and A24–A26 swapped which group is present: before A21–A23 were present without A24–A26 in about 75% of traces, after A24–A26 are present without A21–A23 in about 75% (REL027–REL029, REL035–REL037, REL042–REL044). A24–A26 now co-occur with A29, A30, A34 and A35 in about 76% of traces, up from about 25% (REL051–REL054, REL056–REL059, REL060–REL063).
- A06 immediately precedes A35 in 97% of their co-occurrences after, up from 25% (LAG001). A34 immediately precedes A35 in 49% after, down from 90% (LAG013).

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a hypothesis that the process changed from a path that included activities A21, A22 and A23 (together with A19 and A20) to a path that includes A24, A25 and A26 instead, and that A06 is now executed later, often between A34 and A35. Before, most traces executed A21–A23 and A24–A26 were rare; after, the opposite holds. A06, which was rare and early, is now common and late, frequently following A34 and preceding A35. This is supported by the large participation shifts, the positional move of A06, the outgoing transition changes of A06, A34 and A20, and the relational and lag evidence showing A06 now co-occurs with and follows the later activities. The number of qualifying items (92) far exceeds the 1.09 expected by chance, so the overall pattern is unlikely to be a sampling artefact.

Other differences: A02 and A08 decreased in participation, but from low levels and without clear relational context; they are not part of the main hypothesis. The evidence cannot rule out that the change reflects a different case mix or a new variant rather than a modification of the same process, but the consistency and volume of the indicators make that competing explanation unlikely.
