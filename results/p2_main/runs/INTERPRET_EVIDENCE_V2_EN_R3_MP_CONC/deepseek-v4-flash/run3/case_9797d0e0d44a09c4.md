### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that a previously optional part of the process became mandatory: in the later window, activities A19, A20, A25, A26, A41, A42, and A45 are executed in nearly all traces, whereas in the earlier window they were executed in about half. The order among these activities remains fixed (A41 before A19/A20/A25, and those before A26, with A42/A45 interleaved). Additionally, the routing from A41 changed from a split between A19 and A26 to an almost exclusive path to A19, which explains why A26 now occurs later relative to A41. The large number of qualifying items (23 vs. 0.64 expected by chance) and the consistency across participation, co-occurrence, routing, and lag indicators make this a supported hypothesis.

Other differences: The positional shifts of A26 and A41 are minor and consistent with the lag change. No other differences.

A competing explanation that cannot be fully ruled out is that the earlier window contained a different mix of case types, which would also produce the observed differences in participation and routing. However, the evidence does not allow distinguishing that from a genuine process change.
