### Observed differences

- In the later window, the outgoing transitions from activity A12 changed: the share of transitions from A12 to itself increased from about 49% to 75%, while the share to A13 decreased from about 24% to 5%. [DIR001]
- The elapsed time between activities A04 and A01 increased substantially: the mean rose from about 26.5 hours to 73.3 hours, and the median from about 20.0 to 65.7 hours. [TWAIT001]
- Resource allocation for activities A01, A07, and A22 changed: the set of resources used and their relative shares were redistributed, with some resources appearing and others disappearing. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a hypothesis that the routing from activity A12 changed: in the later window, A12 more often follows itself and less often leads to A13. The share of outgoing transitions from A12 to A12 increased from about 49% to 75%, while the share to A13 decreased from about 24% to 5%. This is directly shown by DIR001, with support from about 40 traces in each window. The change is consistent with a shift in how often A12 is repeated or how often the path to A13 is taken. A competing explanation is that the change reflects a difference in the number of repetitions of A12 per trace rather than a change in the choice between alternatives, but the evidence does not distinguish these.

Other differences: The timing between A04 and A01 increased markedly, and resource allocation for A01, A07, and A22 changed. These are separate from the control-flow change and are not part of the main hypothesis.
