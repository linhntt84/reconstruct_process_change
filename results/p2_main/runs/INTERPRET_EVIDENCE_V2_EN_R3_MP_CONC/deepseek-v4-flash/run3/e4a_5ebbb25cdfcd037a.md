### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, every trace contained A14 and A15 (participation 1.0); in the later window, each appears in about 52.5% of traces (mean 0.525). [OCC001, OCC002]
- A20 and A21 each dropped from about 50.5% to 29% of traces, and A22 from 49.5% to 23.5%. [OCC003, OCC004, OCC005]
- A38's outgoing transitions: before, all went to A14; after, about 52.5% go to A14 and 47.5% go to A23. [DIR001]
- The lag between A38 and A23: before, A23 always appeared 4 or 5 steps after A38; after, in about 47.5% of cases it appears immediately (lag 1). [LAG001]
- Relational evidence: A14 and A15 co-occurred with each other and with A23 in every trace before; after, they co-occur in about 52.5% of traces, and in the remaining 47.5% A23 appears without them. [REL001, REL005, REL010, REL014, REL017, REL019]
- Timing: the wait between A38 and A14 increased from about 1.99 to 3.03 hours (mean), while the wait between A28 and A34 decreased from 0.91 to 0.45 hours, and between A35 and A34 from 0.20 to 0.08 hours. [TWAIT003, TWAIT001, TWAIT002]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that, in the later window, a previously mandatory segment of the process (activities A14, A15, and either A20/A21 or A22) is skipped in about half the cases; in those cases, A38 leads directly to A23 instead of going through A14/A15. This is supported by the drop in participation of A14/A15/A20/A21/A22, the change in A38's successor distribution, and the appearance of A23 immediately after A38 in those cases. The timing change between A38 and A14 (longer wait) is consistent with the remaining cases taking a different path, but it is a separate timing difference.

Other differences: The waits between A28-A34 and A35-A34 decreased substantially, indicating a general speed-up in those parts, but this is not part of the structural hypothesis. Also, the position of A23 shifted slightly earlier and A38 slightly later, but these are minor.

A competing explanation the evidence cannot fully rule out is that the observed changes reflect a change in case mix or sampling, but the consistency across many indicators and the large number of items relative to chance expectation make this unlikely.
