### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a change in the routing after activity A15: the branch containing activities A16–A20 became the dominant path, while the branch containing A21–A25 became the minority. In the earlier window, about 82% of traces took the A21–A25 branch and about 19% took the A16–A20 branch; in the later window these proportions reversed (about 77% vs 24%). This is directly shown by the occurrence changes, the outgoing transition of A15, and the co-occurrence relations. The two branches remain mutually exclusive in both windows, so this is a shift in the frequency of existing alternative paths, not a replacement of one path by another. Additionally, A29 now often co-occurs with the new branch, sometimes interleaved or repeated, suggesting it is more frequently part of that branch.

Other differences: The spacing between activities within the new branch changed: A15 and A17 became closer (lag 2 increased), while A17 and A20 became more spread (lag 3 increased). These are consistent with the new branch being executed with different internal ordering, but they are not part of the main routing hypothesis.

A competing explanation that cannot be fully ruled out is that the two branches correspond to different case types and the mix of cases changed, rather than the process logic itself. However, the evidence is strong and consistent across many indicators.
