### Observed differences

- In the later window, A20's outgoing transitions changed from being spread across A30, A31, A36, A37, A38 (each about 20%) to going almost exclusively to A22 (about 99.5%). [DIR001]
- A21's outgoing transitions changed from going always to A25 (100%) to going to A30, A31, A36, A37, A38 (each about 16–23%) and almost never to A25 (0.5%). [DIR002]
- The activities A30, A31, A36, A37, A38 each used to have A22 as a successor (about 16–22% of their outgoing transitions) and no A25; in the later window they have A25 as a successor (about 18–23%) and almost no A22 (0.5% or less). [DIR003–DIR007]
- The relative order between A21/A22 and the group A30, A31, A36, A37, A38 reversed: before, each of the group activities occurred before A21 and A22 in all traces; after, A21 and A22 occur before each of the group activities in about 99.5% of traces. [REL001–REL010]
- Positions: A21 and A22 moved earlier (mean relative position decreased by about 0.13), while A30, A31, A36, A37, A38 moved later (mean increased by about 0.16). [POS001–POS007]
- LAG items show large shifts but are based on only 1 trace in the later window, so they are not reliable. [LAG001–LAG010]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a change in which A20 now leads directly to A22, A21 now leads to the group of activities A30, A31, A36, A37, A38, and that group now leads to A25, while the order between A21/A22 and that group reversed. This is directly shown by the successor changes (DIR), the reversal of ordering (REL), and the positional shifts (POS), all with full support of 200 traces in each window. The LAG evidence is too weak to add support. A competing explanation that cannot be ruled out is that the observed changes reflect a re-labelling of activities rather than a genuine behavioural change, but the appearance of new edges (A20→A22, A21→group, group→A25) that were absent before makes a simple reordering unlikely.
