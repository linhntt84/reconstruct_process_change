### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, activities A15, A16, A17, A19 and A20 had outgoing transitions only among themselves (A20 also to A21), while A21–A25 and A31 formed a separate group with transitions only among themselves. In the later window, each of A15–A20 also has outgoing transitions to A21–A25 (and A20 to A31), and each of A21–A25 also has outgoing transitions to A15–A20. For example, A15’s successors were A16, A17 and A20; after the change they are A16, A17, A20, A21, A22, A23, A24 and A25. A20’s successor was A21 in all cases; after the change A21 accounts for about 7% of transitions, A31 for about 48%, and A22–A25 for the rest. - The relative order between the two groups reversed or became mixed. In the earlier window, A15, A16, A17, A19 and A20 always occurred before A21–A25 (A_before_B = 1.0 for all such pairs). In the later window, for example A21 occurs before A15 in about 73% of traces, A21 before A20 in about 94%, and A23 before A20 in about 84%. [REL001, REL005, REL009, REL017, REL019, REL020, REL021]
- Positions shifted: A15, A16, A17, A19 and A20 moved later in the trace (mean position increased by about 0.06–0.10 of trace length), while A21–A25 moved earlier (mean position decreased by about 0.06–0.09). [POS001–POS010]
- Distances between activities from the two groups changed: for example, A15 and A21 were 2–4 steps apart before, now often 1 step apart; A15 and A23 were 3–5 steps apart before, now 1–2 steps apart. [LAG005, LAG006, LAG012, LAG019, LAG029, LAG030]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the process changed from a strict sequential flow, in which a first group of activities (A15–A20) was always completed before a second group (A21–A25, A31), to a more flexible flow in which activities from both groups can occur in either order and are interleaved. This is directly shown by the appearance of new outgoing transitions between the groups in both directions, the reversal or mixing of order in the relational evidence, the convergence of positions, and the change in distances. The number of qualifying items (93) far exceeds the 0.72 expected by chance, and the items are consistent across indicators.

Other differences: A20’s successor changed from always A21 to mostly A31 and A22–A25; A22’s successor changed from always A31 to A31 plus A15–A20; A30’s successor changed from always A19 to A19 and A21. These are consistent with the same cluster merging but are not part of the main ordering hypothesis.

A competing explanation that cannot be fully ruled out is that some activities were added or removed from the process, but the co-occurrence of all involved activities remains at 100% in both windows, so the change is better described as a reordering and interleaving of existing activities rather than a replacement of one path by another.
