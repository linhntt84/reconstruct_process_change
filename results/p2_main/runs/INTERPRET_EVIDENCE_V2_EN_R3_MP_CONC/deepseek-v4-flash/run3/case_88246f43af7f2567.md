### Observed differences

- In the later window, A02 and A04 both route directly to A12 (100% of their outgoing transitions), whereas earlier they mostly went to A03 (about 91% for A02, 67% for A04). [DIR001, DIR003]
- A03's outgoing transitions changed: earlier it went to A11 and A12 (about 45% each) and a little to A04; later it goes to A11 (55%) and A10 (45%), with no A12 or A04. [DIR002]
- A16's outgoing transitions changed: earlier it went to A10 (52%) and A09 (48%); later to A09 (50%) and A03 (50%), with no A10. [DIR004]
- A03 and A12 co-occurred in about half of traces earlier, but in none later; they became alternatives. [REL004]
- A03 and A04, and A02 and A03, also changed from co-occurring (24% and 26%) to never co-occurring. [REL002, REL001]
- Conversely, A03 and A10, and A03 and A16, which never co-occurred earlier, now co-occur in about 30% of traces. [REL003, REL005]

- The distance between A16 and A10 grew: earlier mostly adjacent (85% lag 1), later lag 2 (45%) and lag 4 (31%). [LAG003]

- A16 moved slightly earlier (mean from 0.53 to 0.43). [POS004]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a hypothesis that the process was restructured: A02 and A04 now lead directly to A12, bypassing A03; A03 is no longer followed by A12 but instead co-occurs with A10 and A16; and A16 no longer leads to A10. This is directly shown by the routing changes (DIR001-004), the co-occurrence changes (REL001-005), and the lag changes (LAG001-003). The positional shifts (POS001, POS003) are consistent with A12 occurring earlier and A03 later. The main limitation is that these are all control-flow indicators; no timing or resource changes are observed, but that is not required for this structural hypothesis.
