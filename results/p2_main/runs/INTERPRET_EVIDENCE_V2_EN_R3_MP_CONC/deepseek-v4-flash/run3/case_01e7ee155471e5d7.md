### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, A20 always led to A22 (100% of its outgoing transitions); in the later window, A20 leads to A31, A36, and A30 (about 33% each) and almost never to A22 (0.5%). [DIR001]
- In the earlier window, A21 led to A36, A30, and A31 (38%, 34%, 28%); in the later window, A21 leads to A25 in about 99.5% of cases. [DIR002]

- A20 and A22 used to be adjacent (lag 1) in all traces; in the later window they are four steps apart in about 99.5% of traces. [LAG001]

- The relative position of A21 and A22 moved later (mean position from about 0.53 to 0.62 for A21, and 0.38 to 0.47 for A22), while A30, A31, and A36 moved earlier (mean from about 0.59 to 0.41). [POS001–POS005]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a rearrangement of the process order: A20 now leads to A30/A31/A36 instead of A22; A30/A31/A36 now lead to A22 instead of A25; A21 now leads to A25 instead of A30/A31/A36; and A21 and A22 now occur after A30/A31/A36 rather than before them. This is directly shown by successor changes (DIR), order relations (REL), position shifts (POS), and lag changes (LAG). The number of qualifying control-flow items (24) far exceeds the 0.41 expected by chance, and the core items have full support (200 traces in each window). A limitation is that the lag items involving A25 with A30/A31/A36 rest on only one trace in the later window, so those specific lags are weak; however, the main pattern is independently supported by the full-support DIR, REL, and POS items. A competing explanation that cannot be fully ruled out is that some activities are repeated or that additional paths exist that are not captured by the tested pairs, but the observed changes are consistent and direct.

Other differences: none beyond the main hypothesis.
