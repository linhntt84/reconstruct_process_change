- Participation of activities A17 and A18 decreased: A17 was present in about 60% of traces before and 46% after; A18 from about 60% to 47%. [OCC001, OCC002]
- After activity A27, the immediate successor changed: A30 dropped from about 53% of A27's outgoing transitions to 13%, while A26 rose from 18% to 58%; A28 stayed near 29%. [DIR001]

- The gap between A33 and A23 narrowed: the share of pairs four steps apart rose from about 34% to 56% (five-step share fell). [LAG003]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a hypothesis that the ordering of activities around A27 changed: A30 is now executed later relative to A27, and A26 more often directly follows A27. This is directly shown by the redistribution of A27's outgoing transitions (A30's share dropping from about 53% to 13%, A26's rising from 18% to 58%) and by the increased lag between A27 and A30 (one-step pairs falling from 53% to 13%). The support is based on 49 and 31 A27 occurrences, which is moderate, and the change could also reflect A30 being skipped in some cases, but the lag increase among observed pairs points to a delay rather than mere omission.

Other differences: A17 and A18 participation decreased (OCC001, OCC002); the lag between A33 and A22 increased while the lag between A33 and A23 decreased (LAG002, LAG003). These are not part of the main hypothesis. A competing explanation is that these other changes are independent of the A27–A30 shift, but the main hypothesis is not affected by that possibility.
