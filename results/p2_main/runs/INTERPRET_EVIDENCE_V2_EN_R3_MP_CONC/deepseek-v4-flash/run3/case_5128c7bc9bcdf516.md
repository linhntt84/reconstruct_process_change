### Observed differences

- In the later window, each activity has a single dominant successor, whereas in the earlier window each had several. For example, A15 went to A18 in 62% of cases before and 99.5% after; A18 went to A19 in 12% before and 99.5% after; A20 went to A27 in 47% before and 99.5% after. [DIR001, DIR003, DIR005]
- The order between activities became fixed. For instance, A15 preceded A19 in 25.5% of traces before and 99.5% after; A18 preceded A19 in 12% before and 99.5% after; A17 preceded A19 in 50% before and 100% after. [REL001, REL006, REL004]
- Distances between activities became concentrated on a single value, consistent with a fixed chain. For example, A15 and A18 were 1 step apart in 62% before and 99.5% after; A15 and A21 were 3 steps apart in 0% before and 99.5% after; A15 and A27 were 5 steps apart in 0% before and 99.5% after. [LAG001, LAG004, LAG005]
- Positions shifted: A15, A17, A18 moved earlier (mean position decreased by about 0.05, 0.02, 0.06 respectively), while A19, A20, A21 moved later (mean increased by about 0.06, 0.03, 0.04). [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a hypothesis that the process changed from a flexible order with multiple branching options to a fixed sequential chain: A26 → A17 → A15 → A18 → A19 → A21 → A20 → A27. In the earlier window, each activity had several possible successors and pairs of activities appeared in both orders; in the later window, each activity has essentially one successor, all pairs have a fixed order, and the distances between activities match the chain. The number of qualifying items (50) far exceeds the 0.47 expected by chance, and all items are consistent with this single structural change. A competing explanation that the process still contains rare alternative paths cannot be fully excluded, but the observed shares are near zero (e.g., 0.5% or less) in the later window.

Other differences: None beyond the ordering change.
