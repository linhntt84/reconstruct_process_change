### Observed differences

- After activity A16, the next activity shifted from A18 to A17: the share of outgoing transitions to A17 rose from about 27% to 42%, while the share to A18 fell from about 38% to 23%; the share to A20 stayed near 34–35%. [DIR001]
- After activity A22, the next activity shifted from A25 to A21: the share to A21 rose from about 18% to 58%, while the share to A25 fell from about 53% to 13%; the share to A23 stayed near 29%. [DIR002]
- The distance between A22 and A25 increased: the share of traces with a lag of 1 dropped from about 53% to 13%, while lags of 2 and 3 together rose from about 47% to 87%. [LAG001]
- The elapsed time between A33 and A34 decreased from a mean of about 1.01 hours to about 0.50 hours, with all quartiles and the standard deviation also lower. [TWAIT001]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a hypothesis that the process changed its routing after activities A16 and A22. After A16, the next activity is more often A17 and less often A18; after A22, the next activity is more often A21 and less often A25, and when A25 is reached it occurs later relative to A22. This is directly shown by the successor distributions and the lag shift. The A22-related items are based on a relatively small number of traces (49 before, 31 after), so the exact magnitudes are uncertain, but the direction is consistent across both items. A competing explanation is that the observed shifts are sampling artefacts, but the screening indicates the number of such items is far above what chance would produce.

Other differences: The elapsed time between A33 and A34 decreased substantially (mean from about 1.01 to 0.50 hours). This is a separate timing change, supported by TWAIT001, and is not part of the routing hypothesis.
