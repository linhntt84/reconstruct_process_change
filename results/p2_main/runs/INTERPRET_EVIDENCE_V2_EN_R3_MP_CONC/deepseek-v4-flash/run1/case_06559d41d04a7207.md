### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, activities A19, A20, and A21 each appeared in about 30–35% of traces; in the later window each appears in only about 0.5% of traces (1 trace each). [OCC002, OCC003, OCC004]
- In the earlier window, A26, A27, and A28 were essentially absent (0% or 0.5% participation); in the later window each appears in all 200 traces. [OCC005, OCC006, OCC007]
- A10 participation dropped from about 26% to 11% of traces. [OCC001]
- In the earlier window, A19, A20, and A21 almost always led to A29 (about 97–98% of their outgoing transitions); in the later window these activities are nearly absent, and when they occur they lead to other activities (A36, A47, A26). [DIR003, DIR004, DIR005]
- In the earlier window, A10's outgoing transitions were mostly to A19, A20, A21 (about 19%, 26%, 40% respectively); in the later window they are spread across many activities including A26, A27, A15, A16, etc. [DIR001]
- In the earlier window, A19, A20, A21 co-occurred with A29 in about 30–35% of traces and usually preceded A29; in the later window they almost never co-occur with A29. Instead, A26, A27, A28 co-occur with A29 in all traces and precede A29 in about 99% of cases. [REL012, REL020, REL027, REL033, REL038, REL042]
- In the later window, A26, A27, and A28 appear together in all traces, with no fixed order between them (about half the time one precedes the other). [REL031, REL032, REL037]
- The lag between A44 and A29 increased: in the earlier window A44 was 2–3 steps before A29 in about 99% of cases; in the later window it is 4–5 steps before A29 in all cases. [LAG005]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a hypothesis that the process changed from a structure where activities A19, A20, and A21 were common and preceded A29, to a structure where these activities are essentially removed and replaced by A26, A27, and A28, which now always precede A29. The change is direct: participation, successor, and co-occurrence/order indicators all shift together. The number of qualifying items (74) far exceeds the expected 0.82, so this is not a chance sampling effect. A competing explanation that cannot be ruled out is that the old activities were renamed or merged into the new ones, but the behavioural change is clear.

Other differences: A10's participation halved and its successors changed from A19–A21 to a broader set including A26–A28. The lag between A44 and A29 increased from 2–3 to 4–5 steps. These may be part of the same restructuring but are not central to the main hypothesis.
