### Observed differences

- In the later window, activities A27, A36, A37, A42, A43, and A44 occur in 100% of traces, up from about 49–53% in the earlier window. [OCC001–OCC006]

- Relational evidence shows A25 now always precedes A27 and the group; A26, A28, and A31 now follow the group; A31 follows A27. The group activities now always co-occur with each other, with mixed order among themselves. [REL001–REL039]
- Lag evidence: A36–A28 and A43–A28 now co-occur within five steps in about 99% of traces, up from 1 trace before; A42–A31 now co-occur within five steps in only 2 traces, down from about 102. [LAG001–LAG003]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a change from an optional branch to a mandatory sequence. In the earlier window, activities A27, A36, A37, A42, A43, and A44 were present in about half the traces; in the later window they are always executed. Their position also changed: the group A36, A37, A42, A43, A44 now occurs early (after A25, before A28 and A26), and A27 now occurs after A26 and A28, before A31. A25 remains first and A31 remains last. The group activities have no fixed order among themselves in either window, so the change concerns their presence and placement, not their internal ordering.

Other differences: A25’s successor changed from A28 to the group; A26’s successor changed from a mix to A27; A28 became a successor of the group instead of A31. These are consistent with the same structural shift.

A competing explanation that cannot be fully ruled out is a change in case mix (different types of cases being processed), but the directness and consistency of the occurrence, routing, position, and ordering evidence make a process change the more plausible interpretation.
