### Observed differences

- In the later window, after activity A06, the share of outgoing transitions to A08 dropped from about 52% to 26%, while the share to A07 rose from about 22% to 42%, and to A09 from 26% to 32%. [DIR001]
- In the later window, when A07 follows A06, it does so immediately (lag 1) in about 78% of cases instead of 43%, and with one intervening event (lag 2) in 22% instead of 57%. [LAG001]
- In the later window, the elapsed time between A01 and A03 increased from a mean of about 0.87 hours to 1.78 hours (median from 0.86 to 1.83), and the number of observed such intervals dropped from 42 to 26. [TWAIT001]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The main hypothesis is that the routing from A06 changed: A07 became a more frequent and more immediate successor, while A08 became less frequent. This is supported by the redistribution of outgoing transitions from A06 and the shift in the lag between A06 and A07. The number of control-flow items (2 vs 0.3 expected) and their consistency make a real change likely, though both items involve the same pair of activities and are not independent confirmations.

Other differences: The elapsed time between A01 and A03 increased substantially (about doubled), which is a separate timing change. This is supported by one time item with high empirical strength, but the counts are small (42 and 26), so this timing shift is less robust.

A competing explanation that cannot be fully ruled out is that the observed control-flow differences reflect a rare variant in the later window, but the high empirical strength and the fact that the number of items far exceeds chance expectation argue against that.
