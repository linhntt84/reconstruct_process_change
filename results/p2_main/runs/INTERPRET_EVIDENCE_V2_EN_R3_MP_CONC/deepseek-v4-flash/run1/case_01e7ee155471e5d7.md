### Observed differences

- In the later window, activity A20 no longer leads to A22 (100% before, 0.5% after); instead it leads to A30, A31 and A36 in roughly equal shares (about 33% each). [DIR001]
- Activity A21's outgoing transitions changed from A30/A31/A36 (about 38%/34%/28%) to A25 (99.5%). [DIR002]
- Activities A30, A31 and A36 no longer lead to A25 (previously about 28–36% each); they now lead to A22 (about 31–34%) and to each other. [DIR003, DIR004, DIR005]

- A20 and A22 used to be adjacent (lag 1), now they are 4 steps apart. [LAG001]
- A21 and A25 used to be 4 steps apart, now they are adjacent. [LAG002]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a hypothesis that the relative order of two groups of activities has been reversed: activities A30, A31 and A36 now occur before A21 and A22, whereas before they occurred after them. This is directly shown by the reversal of order in all six pairs (REL001–006), the consistent position shifts (POS001–005), and the successor changes (DIR001–005) that fit the reordering. The adjacency changes for A20–A22 and A21–A25 (LAG001, LAG002) are also consistent with the new sequence. The large number of qualifying items (24 vs. 0.41 expected by chance) makes it unlikely that this is a random sampling artefact.

Other differences: The routing from A20 and A21 changed as part of the reordering; A20 now goes to the A30/A31/A36 group instead of A22, and A21 now goes to A25 instead of the A30/A31/A36 group. These are consistent with the main hypothesis.

A competing explanation that cannot be fully ruled out is that some activities became optional or that there are additional paths not captured by the strong evidence; however, the direct relational and positional evidence strongly supports the order reversal. The LAG003–008 items have very low support in the later window (only 1 trace) and are therefore not used as evidence.
