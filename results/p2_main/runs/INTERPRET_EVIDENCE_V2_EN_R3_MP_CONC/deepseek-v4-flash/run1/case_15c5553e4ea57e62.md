- In the earlier window, activities A15–A20 consistently preceded A21–A25: for every pair such as A15–A21, A16–A23, A20–A21, the first activity appeared before the second in 100% of traces. In the later window, these orders are mixed: e.g., A21 precedes A15 in about 73% of traces, A23 precedes A20 in about 84%, and A21 precedes A20 in about 94%. ### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a change in the control-flow structure: the previously fixed sequential order in which A15–A20 always preceded A21–A25 has been relaxed, so that these activities now interleave and can appear in either order, and the routing has become more branched (e.g., A20 now often leads to A31 instead of A21). This is directly shown by the reversal of order relations (REL), the convergence of positions (POS), the shortening of lags (LAG), and the redistribution of outgoing transitions (DIR). The number of qualifying items (93) far exceeds the 0.72 expected by chance, so the overall difference is not a sampling artefact. A competing explanation that cannot be fully ruled out is that the process now contains parallel branches rather than a single interleaved sequence; the available indicators show interleaving and order flexibility but do not distinguish parallel execution from alternative paths.

Other differences: A31 appears as a new successor for A20 and A22, and A30 now sometimes leads to A21 instead of only A19. These are consistent with the broader routing change but are not part of the main ordering hypothesis.
