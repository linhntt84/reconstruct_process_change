### Observed differences

- In the later window, the order between the early activities (A15, A16, A17, A19, A20) and the later activities (A21, A22, A23, A24, A25) became fixed: for every pair tested, the early activity now always precedes the later one, whereas before the order was mixed (e.g., A15 before A21 in about 23% of cases before, 100% after; A20 before A21 in about 3% before, 100% after). [REL001, REL005, REL009, REL018, REL020, REL021, REL022]
- The positions of these activities shifted accordingly: A15, A16, A17, A19, A20 moved earlier (mean relative position decreased by about 0.03–0.10), while A21, A22, A23, A24, A25 moved later (mean increased by about 0.03–0.10). [POS001–POS010]
- The routing changed: A15, A16, A17 now have outgoing edges only to A16/A17/A20 (and A15 for A16), whereas before they also led to A21–A25. A20 now leads only to A21 (before also to A31, A22–A25). A21 now leads to A23–A25 (before also to A15–A17, A19, A20). A22–A25 now connect only among themselves and to A31, no longer to A15–A17/A20. [DIR001–DIR009]
- The distance (in events) between the early and later groups increased: e.g., A15 to A21 was within 1–2 steps in about 85% of cases before, but after it is 3–4 steps in all cases; A15 to A23 was within 1–2 steps in about 46% before, after 0% (all 3–5). [LAG004, LAG005, LAG011, LAG012]
- The elapsed time between A31 and A38 increased from a mean of about 0.91 hours to 1.80 hours; between A37 and A38 from 0.19 to 0.36 hours. [TWAIT001, TWAIT002]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a hypothesis that the process changed from a flexible ordering to a strict sequential ordering, with a clear separation between an early group (A15, A16, A17, A19, A20) and a later group (A21–A25). In the later window, the early activities always occur before the later ones, and the routing no longer allows direct transitions from the early group to the later group except through A20→A21. This is directly shown by the relational order items (all pairs became one-directional), the position shifts, and the routing changes. The large number of control-flow items (93 vs 0.72 expected by chance) rules out chance as an overall explanation. A competing explanation that cannot be fully ruled out is that the observed differences reflect a change in the sample composition (e.g., different case types) rather than a change in the process itself, but the consistency across many independent pairs makes this less likely.

Other differences: The timing between A31–A38 and A37–A38 increased (mean wait roughly doubled and nearly doubled respectively), which is a separate change in timing not explained by the ordering change. [TWAIT001, TWAIT002]
