### Observed differences

- In the later window, activity A22 appears in about 99.5% of traces, up from about 51.5% in the earlier window. [OCC001]
- Activities A27, A28, and A33 each appear in about 0.5% of traces in the later window, down from about 48.5% in the earlier window. [OCC002, OCC003, OCC004]
- The outgoing transitions of A21 were redistributed: in the earlier window, about 51.5% went to A22, 16.5% to A28, 18% to A33, and 14% to A27; in the later window, about 99.5% go to A22 and 0.5% to A28. [DIR001]
- The co-occurrence of A21 with A27, A28, and A33 dropped from about 48.5% to 0.5% each. [REL002, REL003, REL004]
- The co-occurrence of A22 with A27, A28, and A33 was already zero in both windows, but the share of traces containing only A22 (not the alternatives) rose from about 51.5% to 99.5%. [REL006, REL007, REL008]
- The distance between A21 and A23 changed: in the earlier window, about 51.5% of traces had a lag of 2 and 48.5% a lag of 4; in the later window, about 99.5% have a lag of 2. [LAG001]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the process changed from having two alternative paths after A21 – one through A22 and another through a group of activities A27/A28/A33 – to almost always taking the A22 path, making A27/A28/A33 nearly absent. This is directly shown by the increase in A22 participation, the decrease in A27/A28/A33 participation, the redistribution of A21's outgoing transitions, and the shortening of the lag between A21 and A23. The large number of qualifying items (34 vs 0.45 expected) makes chance an unlikely explanation. A competing explanation that the alternative branch still exists but was not sampled is unlikely given the near-zero participation in the later window, but cannot be entirely ruled out because the later window is a finite sample.

Other differences: The position of A23 shifted slightly earlier (mean from about 0.55 to 0.53 of the trace), but this is minor and likely a consequence of the shorter path. [POS001]
