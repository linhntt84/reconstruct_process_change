### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a hypothesis that the time between completion events for these activity pairs decreased substantially in the later window. Both pairs show a consistent reduction of roughly half in mean and median elapsed time, with very high empirical strength (0.9995) and far more time-family items than expected by chance (2 items vs 0.01 expected). The change is consistent across both pairs, though they share activity A41. The main hypothesis is a change in timing: the interval between completions of A38–A41 and A40–A41 shortened. This is a timing change; no structural or resource changes are indicated by the evidence. A competing explanation that the observed difference is due to random variation is unlikely given the magnitude and consistency, but the evidence cannot rule out that the underlying process changed in a way that affects these specific pairs only.

Other differences: the frequency of observed transitions changed in opposite directions for the two pairs (one increased, one decreased), but this may be sampling variation and is not part of the timing hypothesis.
