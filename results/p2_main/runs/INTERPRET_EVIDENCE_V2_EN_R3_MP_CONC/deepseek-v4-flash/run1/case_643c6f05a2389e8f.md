### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a hypothesis that the process switched from executing a set of activities A21–A25 (together with A20, A34, A36/A37) to executing a set A26–A30 (together with the same companions). In the earlier window, A21–A25 were the common activities and A26–A30 were rare; in the later window the reverse holds. This is directly shown by occurrence counts, co-occurrence relations, and successor changes (A20 now leads to A29 instead of A24; A36 now often leads to A02). The number of qualifying evidence items (120) far exceeds the 1.27 expected by chance, and the pattern is consistent across many activity pairs.

Other differences: A02 increased in participation and appears as a new successor of A36; some lag distributions shifted (e.g., A22–A37 lag moved from 4 to 5 steps), but these are based on few traces. A27's outgoing distribution became more balanced among A26, A28, and A30.

A competing explanation that cannot be fully ruled out is that the two groups are alternative branches whose frequencies were swapped, rather than a complete replacement of one set by another; however, the near-total reversal in participation and co-occurrence strongly favours the replacement interpretation.
