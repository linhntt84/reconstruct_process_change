### Observed differences

- Participation of A17 and A18 decreased: A17 from about 60% of traces to 46%; A18 from about 60% to 47%. [OCC001, OCC002]
- The outgoing routing of A27 changed: before, about 53% of its transitions went to A30, 29% to A28, and 18% to A26; after, about 13% went to A30, 29% to A28, and 58% to A26. [DIR001]
- When A30 follows A27, it now tends to appear later: before, 53% at lag 1, 24% at lag 2, 22% at lag 3; after, 13% at lag 1, 45% at lag 2, 42% at lag 3. [LAG001]
- The distance between A33 and A22 increased: before, 69% at lag 4 and 31% at lag 5; after, 45% at lag 4 and 55% at lag 5. [LAG002]
- The distance between A33 and A23 decreased: before, 34% at lag 4 and 66% at lag 5; after, 56% at lag 4 and 44% at lag 5. [LAG003]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a change in the control-flow ordering around activities A27 and A30. In the later window, A27 is much less often followed directly by A30 (from about 53% to 13% of its outgoing transitions) and much more often followed by A26 (from about 18% to 58%). When A30 does follow A27, it tends to appear later (lag 2–3 instead of lag 1). This is directly shown by the routing change [DIR001] and the lag shift [LAG001]. The screening found 6 control-flow items against 0.5 expected by chance, so the overall difference is unlikely to be a sampling artefact. A limitation is that the A27–A30 evidence rests on 49 before and 31 after traces containing A27, which is moderate.

Other differences: A17 and A18 participation decreased from about 60% to 46–47% of traces [OCC001, OCC002]. The lag between A33 and A22 increased (more often at lag 5) [LAG002], while the lag between A33 and A23 decreased (more often at lag 4) [LAG003]. These are separate changes not covered by the main hypothesis. A competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that the A33–A22/A23 shifts reflect an independent reordering of activities, rather than a single process-wide change.
