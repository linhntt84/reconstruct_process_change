### Observed differences

- In the later window, the assignment of resources to activities A03, A13, A14, and A22 shifted away from a broad set of resources (the "OTHER" share) toward a few specific resources. For A22, the "OTHER" share fell from about 45% to 28%, while User_79 rose from about 5% to 13%, User_67 from 0% to 11%, and User_104 from 0% to 10%. For A03, "OTHER" fell from 56% to 48% and User_67 rose from 0% to 7%. For A13 and A14, "OTHER" fell from 54% to 48% and User_67 rose from 0% to 9%. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004]
- The elapsed time between completion of A22 and A03 increased slightly: the mean rose from about 2.9e-6 to 3.7e-6 hours, the upper quartile from 3.1e-6 to 3.6e-6 hours, and the number of such transitions fell from 119 to 99. [TWAIT001]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a change in resource allocation for activities A03, A13, A14, and A22, with work shifting from a wide range of resources to a smaller set of specific resources (e.g., User_67, User_79, User_104). This is directly shown by four assignment items, all showing the same pattern of a decreased "OTHER" share and increased shares for specific users, and the number of such items (4) far exceeds the expected count by chance (0.005). The timing change between A22 and A03 is a separate difference, but it is small in absolute terms and may be a secondary effect.

Other differences: The elapsed time between A22 and A03 increased slightly, but the median was unchanged and the absolute change is tiny; this could be a separate change in timing.

A competing explanation that cannot be fully ruled out is that the resource shift reflects a change in the mix of cases rather than a deliberate reallocation, but the consistency across four activities makes this less likely.
