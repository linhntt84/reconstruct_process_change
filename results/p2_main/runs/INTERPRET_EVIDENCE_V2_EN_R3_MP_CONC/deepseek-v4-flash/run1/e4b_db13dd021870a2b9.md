### Observed differences

- Resource assignment changed for seven activities (A01, A03, A10, A13, A14, A17, A22). In the earlier window, each activity was performed by a set of users including User_19, User_13, User_49, User_42, User_46, User_35, User_36, User_41, User_27, etc. In the later window, these users' shares dropped (e.g., User_19 from about 7% to 0% for A01, User_13 from 5% to 3% for A01) and new users appeared or increased, notably User_37, User_61, User_3, User_16, User_32, User_71, User_53, User_25, User_48, User_38, User_60, User_86. For A10, the share of "other" (unlisted users) rose from about 9% to 57%, indicating much more spread-out assignment. For the other activities, the "other" share fell slightly (e.g., A01 from 52% to 48%, A03 from 54% to 48%, A13/A14 from 57% to 49%, A17 from 58% to 49%, A22 from 48% to 43%). [ASSIGN001-007]

- The time between consecutive occurrences of A17 (lag) changed: in the earlier window, a gap of 5 steps was most common (43% of occurrences), while in the later window a gap of 1 step became most common (39%). So A17 repetitions became closer together. [LAG001]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a change in resource allocation: the set of resources performing several activities changed, with a shift from one group of users (e.g., User_19, User_13) to another (e.g., User_37, User_61). This is consistent across seven activities and is far more than expected by chance (7 resource items vs 0.005 expected). The change is not a simple concentration or de-concentration but a replacement of the dominant users. A competing explanation that the process itself changed (e.g., different activities) is unlikely because the activities are the same and the resource profiles changed systematically.

Other differences: The lag between consecutive A17 occurrences shortened, but this is a single control-flow item (1 vs 0.2 expected) and could be due to chance; it is not enough to infer a structural change.
