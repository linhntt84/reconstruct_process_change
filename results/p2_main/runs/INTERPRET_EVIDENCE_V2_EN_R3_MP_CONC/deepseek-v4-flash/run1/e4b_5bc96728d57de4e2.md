### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a change in resource allocation for several activities. In the later window, the work for activities A01, A03, A13, A14, and A22 was redistributed among resources: some resources (e.g., User_78, User_56) performed a smaller share, while others (e.g., User_42) performed a larger share. This pattern is consistent across all five activities and is far more frequent than expected by chance (5 items vs. 0.005 expected). [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005]

Other differences: Timing between activities also changed. The wait between A02 and A12 increased, while waits between A04–A01, A05–A09, and A09–A23 decreased. These timing changes are also well above chance (4 items vs. 0.015 expected), but they are not uniform in direction and may be independent of the resource shift. [TWAIT001, TWAIT002, TWAIT003, TWAIT004]

A competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that the resource redistribution reflects a temporary rotation of staff rather than a lasting change in allocation, and that the timing changes are separate, unrelated adjustments.
