Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a hypothesis that the timing between certain consecutive activities changed: specifically, the elapsed time between completion of A26, A35, or A37 and the subsequent completion of A34 increased substantially, from about 1 hour to about 2 hours, with greater variability. This is directly shown by three time indicators, all with very high empirical strength, and the number of such items (3) far exceeds the expected count by chance (0.01) for the time family. The change is consistent across all three pairs and is based on a large number of observations for A26→A34 (200 in each window) and moderate numbers for the other two. A competing explanation that the observed increase is a random sampling artefact is unlikely given the magnitude and consistency, though the decrease in trace counts for two of the pairs suggests that the timing change may be accompanied by a reduction in how often those paths are taken.

Other differences: The frequency of traces containing A35→A34 and A37→A34 declined in the later window, but this is not part of the main timing hypothesis and may reflect a separate change in routing or participation.
