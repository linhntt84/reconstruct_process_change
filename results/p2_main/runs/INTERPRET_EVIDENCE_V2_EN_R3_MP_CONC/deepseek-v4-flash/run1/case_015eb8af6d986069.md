### Observed differences

- Activity A08 became much less frequent: it appeared in about 72% of traces before and in about 29% after, and its mean occurrences per trace fell from 0.75 to 0.315. [OCC001]
- Activity A10 became more frequent: participation rose from about 7.5% to 22% of traces, and mean occurrences per trace from 0.075 to 0.25. [OCC002]
- A08 also moved later in the trace: its mean relative position increased from about 0.24 to 0.41. [POS001]
- A group of activities (A20, A22, A23, A24, A25, A26, A31, A32) changed from a variable order to a fixed, sequential order in the later window. For example, A20 before A24 went from about 26% to 99% of traces, A23 before A24 from 12% to 97%, and A31 before A22 from 50% to 100%. [REL014, REL019, LAG032]
- The distances between these activities became concentrated on single values, indicating a rigid sequence. For instance, A22 immediately before A20 in 99.5% of traces (was 56%), A20 immediately before A23 in 99.5% (was 62%), A22 before A23 with one step between in 99.5% (was spread), and A26 immediately before A25 in 100% (was 66%). [LAG006, LAG011, LAG012, LAG029]
- A08's co-occurrence with many other activities dropped from about 72% to 29% of traces, with those activities now occurring without A08 in about 71% of traces. [REL001–REL013]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a change in the control flow: in the later window, activities A20, A22, A23, A24, A25, A26, A31, and A32 are executed in a fixed order (A31 → A22 → A20 → A23 → A24 → A26 → A25 → A32), whereas in the earlier window the order among them was different and less constrained, with many pairs appearing in both orders. This is directly shown by the reversal of order in REL items and the concentration of lag distances on single values in LAG items, with full support (200 traces) for the key pairs. The large number of qualifying items (82 vs. 1 expected by chance) rules out random sampling variation as the explanation.

Other differences: A08 became much less frequent and moved later in the trace, while A10 became more frequent. These are separate from the reordering and are supported by OCC, POS, and REL items. A competing explanation that cannot be fully ruled out is that the observed reordering reflects a change in the mix of case types rather than a change in the underlying process logic, but the consistency and volume of the evidence make this unlikely.
