### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, every trace ran A36 then A21 immediately: A36→A21 was the only outgoing edge of A36, and the A36-to-A21 lag was 1 in all 200 traces. Activities A14, A15 and A20 did not occur at all. [DIR004, LAG001, OCC001, OCC002, OCC003]
- In the later window, A14 occurs in about 37 in 100 traces, A15 in about 29 in 100, and A20 in about 34 in 100, each once per trace and at roughly one third of the way through the trace. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, POS001, POS002, POS003]
- A36's outgoing behaviour changed: it now leads to A14 in about 37%, A20 in about 34%, A15 in about 29% of traces, and directly to A21 in only about 1%. Each of A14, A15 and A20 leads to A21. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004]
- The A36-to-A21 lag shifted from 1 to 2 in about 99% of traces, and A21's position moved slightly later (mean from about 0.37 to 0.39). [LAG001, POS004]
- A14, A15 and A20 never co-occur with each other; each appears between A36 and A21 (A36 before it, it before A21). [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, REL007, REL008, REL009]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that, in the later window, the process inserts exactly one of three alternative activities — A14, A15 or A20 — between A36 and A21, replacing the direct A36→A21 step in about 99% of traces. This is shown directly by the redistribution of A36's outgoing edges, the new A14/A15/A20→A21 edges, the shift of the A36-to-A21 lag from 1 to 2, and the mutual exclusivity of the three inserted activities. The screening count (21 items vs 0.37 expected by chance) rules out chance as an overall explanation. The main limitation is that the earlier window cannot show whether these activities existed but were unobserved; the change itself is clear and consistent.

Other differences: A21's position shifted slightly later, consistent with the inserted step; no separate hypothesis is needed.
