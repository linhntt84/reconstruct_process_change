### Observed differences

- The outgoing transitions of A20, A21 and A22 changed from a spread over many successors (including A26–A30) to only A20/A21/A22/A25. For example, A20 before went to A25 21%, A22 16%, A21 14%, A26 13%, A28 12%, etc.; after it goes to A22 31%, A21 35%, A25 31% and no A26–A30. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003]
- A25 now almost always leads to A26 (99.5% after vs 45% to A36 and 18% to A27 before). [DIR004]
- The activities A26–A30 now only go to each other (and A27 to A36 83.5%), whereas before they also went to A20–A25. [DIR005–DIR009]
- The order between the first group (A20, A21, A22, A24, A25) and the second group (A26–A30) flipped from mixed to fixed: e.g., A20 before A26 in 23% of traces before vs 97.5% after; A20 before A28 57.5% vs 97.5%; A25 before A26 2.5% vs 99.5%. [REL001, REL002, REL018]
- The distance between the groups increased: e.g., A20–A26 lag 1 dropped from 56.5% to 0%, with lags 2–4 now common; A24–A26 now always at lag 5. [LAG004, LAG026]
- Positions shifted: A20, A21, A22, A24, A25 moved earlier (mean position down by 0.03–0.10), while A26–A30 moved later (mean up by 0.03–0.10). [POS001–POS010]
- A35 now always goes to A24 (100% after vs 45.5% before) and immediately precedes it (lag 1 in 100% after vs 48.4% before). [DIR010, LAG058]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a change from an interleaved execution of two groups of activities to a strict sequential order: a first block (A20, A21, A22, A24, A25, with A35 before A24) now always precedes a second block (A26, A27, A28, A29, A30, with A27 leading to A36). This is supported by the consistent reversal of order in REL, the increase in lags, the shift in positions, and the routing changes in DIR. The number of qualifying items (100) far exceeds the 0.94 expected by chance, so the overall pattern is not a sampling artifact.

Other differences: Within the first block, A20, A21, A22 now form a tight loop with each other and A25; within the second block, A26–A30 form a loop. A27 now more often leads to A36. These are part of the same reorganization.

Limitations: The evidence is entirely control-flow; no time or resource items are present. Some LAG items have low support in one window (e.g., before support around 30–50 for some pairs), but the pattern is consistent across many high-support items. A competing explanation that the change reflects a shift in variant frequencies rather than a structural reordering cannot be fully excluded, but the near-total disappearance of the reverse order makes that unlikely.
