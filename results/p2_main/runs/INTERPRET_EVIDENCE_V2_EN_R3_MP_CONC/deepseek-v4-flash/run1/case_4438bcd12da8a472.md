### Observed differences

- Activities A14, A15, and A20 appeared in about 47% of traces before and in about 99.5% after. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003]
- The outgoing transition of A36 shifted from A21 (53% before) to A14 (99.5% after). [DIR001]
- Co-occurrence of A14 with A21, A36, A37, and A40 increased from about 47% (27.5% for A40) to about 99.5% (62.5% for A40). [REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006]
- The lag between A36 and A21 changed from 1 step in 53% of traces and 4 steps in 47% before, to 4 steps in 99.5% after. [LAG001]
- A21 shifted slightly later in the trace (mean position from 0.406 to 0.429), while A36 shifted slightly earlier (mean from 0.328 to 0.304). [POS001, POS002]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a change from two variants—one including A14, A15, and A20 and one skipping them—to a single variant that always includes these activities. Before, about half the traces contained A14/A15/A20; after, almost all do. A36 now almost always leads to A14 instead of A21, and the distance between A36 and A21 increased from 1 to 4 steps, consistent with A14/A15/A20 being inserted between them. The large number of consistent control-flow items (22 vs. 0.41 expected by chance) rules out a chance sampling effect.

Other differences: A21 and A36 shifted slightly in position, and A14–A37 and A14–A40 show increased interleaving or repetition. A competing explanation that cannot be fully ruled out is a change in the mix of case types rather than a modification of the process itself, but the consistency across participation, routing, and lag indicators makes a structural change the most plausible interpretation.
