### Observed differences

- In the later window, the elapsed time between activities A09 and A23 increased markedly: the mean rose from about 3.9 hours to about 15.3 hours, and the median from about 1.0 hour to about 14.3 hours. The number of observed transitions also dropped slightly (from 29 to 25). [TWAIT001]
- Resource allocation for activities A01, A03, A13, A14, and A22 shifted away from a broad set of resources toward a smaller set. For example, the share of work done by User_56 increased from near zero to about 7–9% across these activities, and User_66 and User_77 also gained shares, while the shares of User_3, User_18, User_59, User_60, and User_61 decreased by several percentage points each. The overall concentration on the top resources increased (the "OTHER" share fell by 1–10 percentage points depending on the activity). [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a hypothesis that resource allocation changed across several activities, with work becoming more concentrated on a few resources (notably User_56, User_66, and User_77) and away from a larger set of previously used resources. This pattern is consistent across five different activities, each with strong empirical strength and substantial support, and the number of resource-related items (5) far exceeds the chance expectation (0.005). In addition, the waiting time between A09 and A23 increased substantially, suggesting a timing change as well. The main limitation is that the time item rests on only about 25–29 observations, so the exact magnitude of the timing shift is less certain, but the direction is clear.

Other differences: The timing change (TWAIT001) is separate from the resource shift and involves activities A09 and A23; it is not part of the main resource-allocation hypothesis. A competing explanation for the resource shift could be a change in the pool of available staff, but the evidence cannot distinguish that from a deliberate reallocation.
