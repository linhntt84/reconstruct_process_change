### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, activity A20 had outgoing transitions spread across A36, A37, A30, A38 and A31 (each about 20%); in the later window, about 99.5% of A20's outgoing transitions go to A22, with only about 0.5% to A31. [DIR001]
- In the earlier window, A21's outgoing transitions went almost entirely to A25 (100%); in the later window, they go to A30, A37, A36, A38 and A31 (each about 16–23%) and almost never to A25 (0.5%). [DIR002]
- For each of A30, A31, A36, A37 and A38, the share of outgoing transitions to A22 dropped from about 17–22% to about 0.5% or less, while the share to A25 rose from 0% to about 18–22%. [DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006, DIR007]
- The mean position of A21 and A22 in the trace decreased by about 0.13 (A21 from about 0.65 to 0.52; A22 from about 0.52 to 0.39), while the mean position of A30, A31, A36, A37 and A38 increased by about 0.16 (from about 0.44 to 0.60). [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006, POS007]
- For each pair (A21, X) and (A22, X) where X is one of A30, A31, A36, A37 or A38, the order reversed: in the earlier window X almost always occurred before A21/A22 (100% B_before_A), in the later window A21/A22 almost always occurred before X (99.5% A_before_B). [REL001–REL010]
- The lag between A20 and each of A30, A31, A36, A37, A38, and between each of those and A22, changed from a spread across 1–5 steps to a single step value, but these lag items are based on only 1 trace in the later window, so they are not reliable. [LAG001–LAG010]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a structural change in which the group of activities A30, A31, A36, A37 and A38 now occurs later in the trace, after A21 and A22, and its outgoing edge changed from A22 to A25, while A20 now leads to A22 instead of to the group, and A21 now leads to the group instead of to A25. This is directly shown by the successor changes (DIR), the order reversals (REL) and the position shifts (POS), all based on 200 traces in each window. The LAG items are too weak to rely on, but they are not needed for the conclusion. The large number of qualifying items (34 vs 0.55 expected by chance) makes it unlikely that these differences are due to random sampling alone. A competing explanation that cannot be fully ruled out is that the observed pattern reflects a rare variant rather than a systematic change, but the consistency across indicators and the high empirical strength of the items argue against that.
