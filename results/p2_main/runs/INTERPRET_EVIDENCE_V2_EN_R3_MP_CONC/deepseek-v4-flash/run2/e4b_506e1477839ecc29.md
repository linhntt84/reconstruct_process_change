### Observed differences

- In the later window, the assignment of activities A01, A03, A13, A14, and A22 is redistributed across resources. The share of work done by resources such as User_56, User_66, and User_77 increases (e.g., for A22, User_56 goes from 0% to about 9% of assignments, User_66 from 0% to about 8%), while many previously used resources decrease or disappear, and the overall "OTHER" share drops (e.g., for A22 from about 39% to about 29%). [ASSIGN001-005]
- In the later window, the elapsed time between consecutive completions of activities A09 and A23 increased substantially: the mean rose from about 3.9 hours to about 15.3 hours, and the median from about 1.0 hour to about 14.3 hours. [TWAIT001]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a hypothesis that the allocation of work to resources changed for activities A01, A03, A13, A14, and A22. In the earlier window, these activities were performed by a broad set of resources with no single dominant one; in the later window, the work is more concentrated on a different set of resources, including User_56, User_66, and User_77, while many previously used resources see their shares decrease or disappear. This pattern is consistent across all five activities and is far more than expected by chance (5 items vs 0.005 expected). A competing explanation that cannot be ruled out is that the change reflects a change in the available resource pool rather than a deliberate change in assignment policy.

Other differences: The evidence also indicates a change in timing between activities A09 and A23, with the elapsed time between their consecutive completions increasing from a median of about 1 hour to about 14 hours. This is based on a single item but is highly unusual (empirical strength 0.9995) and far exceeds chance expectation (1 vs 0.016). However, it involves different activities and is not part of the resource allocation hypothesis.
