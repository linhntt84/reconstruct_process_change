### Observed differences

- After activity A22, the share of outgoing transitions to A21 increased from about 18% to 58%, while the share to A25 decreased from about 53% to 13%; the share to A23 stayed around 29%. [DIR001]
- The gap between A16 and A17 shortened: in the later window, A17 follows A16 within one step in about 78% of cases (up from 59%), and within two steps in 22% (down from 41%). [LAG001]
- The gap between A16 and A18 lengthened: A18 follows A16 within one step in about 57% (down from 78%), and within two steps in 43% (up from 22%). [LAG002]
- The gap between A22 and A25 lengthened: A25 follows A22 within one step in about 13% (down from 53%), within two steps in 45% (up from 24%), and within three steps in 42% (up from 22%). [LAG003]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a change in the control flow around A22: A25 is no longer the immediate successor of A22; instead A21 becomes the dominant successor, and A25, when it occurs, appears later (often two or three steps after A22). This is directly shown by the redistribution of A22's outgoing transitions [DIR001] and the increased lag between A22 and A25 [LAG003]. The change is consistent across both indicators and is far more than expected by chance (4 control-flow items vs 0.34 expected). The support is moderate (49 and 31 traces containing A22), so the change may be driven by a subset of cases, but the magnitude and consistency make a real shift likely.

Other differences: The relations between A16 and A17/A18 also changed – A17 now follows A16 more closely, while A18 follows less closely [LAG001, LAG002]. This suggests a separate reordering between A17 and A18 relative to A16, but it is not part of the main hypothesis. A competing explanation is that these changes reflect sampling variation in a process with high variability, but the screening comparison makes that unlikely.
