### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a timing change: the elapsed time between the completion of activities A16, A17, A18, A19 and the completion of A26 decreased by about half in the later window. This is supported by four consistent time indicators, all showing a similar reduction in mean and median times, with high empirical strength, and the number of time items (4) far exceeds the 0.013 expected by chance. A competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that the reduction reflects changes in the activities themselves rather than the gap between them, but the observed shift in elapsed times is a timing change in its own right.

Other differences: The routing after A06 shifted, with A08 becoming less frequent and A07 and A09 more frequent. However, this is based on a single control-flow item, and the expected number of such items by chance is 0.28, so this difference alone is not enough to infer a control-flow change; it could be a chance fluctuation.
