### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, activities A20, A21, A22, A24, and A25 typically occurred before A26, A27, A28, A29, and A30. In the later window, the order is reversed: A26–A30 now typically occur before A20–A25. For example, A20 preceded A26 in about 98% of traces before, but A26 preceded A20 in about 72% of traces after. [REL001, REL005, REL009, REL017]
- The relative positions of these activities shifted accordingly: A20, A21, A22, A24, and A25 moved later (mean position increased by about 0.06–0.09), while A26, A27, A28, A29, and A30 moved earlier (mean position decreased by about 0.06–0.09). [POS001–POS010]
- The outgoing transitions of the first-block activities changed: they now lead to the second-block activities more directly, with many new successors (A26–A30) appearing. For example, A20's successors before were mainly A22, A25, A21; after, it also leads to A26, A27, A28, A29, A30. [DIR001–DIR004, DIR006–DIR010]
- The lag between the two groups changed: for pairs like A20–A26, the distance decreased when the order is still A20 before A26, but the overall order reversed. [LAG004, LAG011, LAG018]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the order of two groups of activities reversed: activities A26–A30 (and possibly A36) now occur before A20–A25, whereas before they occurred after. This is directly shown by the reversal of order in many REL pairs across the two groups, the shift in POS positions, and the new direct transitions from the first group to the second group in DIR. The large number of qualifying items (102 vs. ~1 expected) rules out chance. A limitation is that the reversal is not complete for all pairs (e.g., A24 still precedes A28 in about 78% of traces after), but the overall pattern is consistent.

Other differences: The appearance of new successors like A36 in the second group, and the redistribution of outgoing transitions, are part of the same reversal rather than separate changes.
