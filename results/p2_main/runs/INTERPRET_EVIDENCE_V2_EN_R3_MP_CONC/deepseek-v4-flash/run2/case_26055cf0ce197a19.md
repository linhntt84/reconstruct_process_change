### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a hypothesis that the ordering of activities changed: A01 and A07 swapped their relative order, and A06 moved earlier while A05 moved later. In the earlier window, A01 typically preceded A07, and A07 led to A05 or A06. In the later window, A07 precedes A01, and A01 leads to A05 or A06. This is directly shown by the reversal of direct successors (A01 no longer goes to A07; A07 now goes to A01) and by the lag changes (A01–A07 lag increased from 1 to 2, A07–A01 lag decreased from 2 to 1). The position shifts of A06 (from end to middle) and A05 (later) are consistent with this reordering. The evidence is strong: 35 control-flow items qualified when 0.29 were expected by chance.

Other differences: A01 became more frequent (participation from 28% to 53%) and A07 less frequent (51% to 29%). This is a separate change in participation, not directly part of the ordering hypothesis. A competing explanation is that the process now has a different set of paths rather than a simple reordering, but the direct successor and lag evidence point to a reordering.
