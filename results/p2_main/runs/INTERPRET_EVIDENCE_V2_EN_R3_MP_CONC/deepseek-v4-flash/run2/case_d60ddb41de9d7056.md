### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, every case followed the path A21 → A22 → A23, with A22 present in all 200 traces and A23 in all traces. [OCC001, DIR001, REL001, REL007]
- In the later window, about 56.5% of cases still follow that path, while about 43.5% of cases instead go from A21 to one of five activities (A27, A28, A33, A34, A35) and then to A23, bypassing A22. [OCC001, OCC002-006, DIR001, REL001, REL007, REL013-017]
- The five activities appear only in the later window, each in 43.5% of cases, and they co‑occur together in those cases; their order among themselves is mixed, with roughly equal shares of both directions. [OCC002-006, REL018-027]
- A22 participation dropped from 100% to 56.5% of traces. [OCC001]
- A21’s position shifted slightly earlier (mean from 0.49 to 0.46 of the trace). [POS001]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that a new alternative path was introduced in the later window: after A21, cases either continue to A22 and then A23 (about 56.5%) or branch to a set of five activities (A27, A28, A33, A34, A35) that are executed in a flexible order and then lead to A23, bypassing A22 (about 43.5%). This is directly shown by the drop in A22 participation, the appearance of the five activities, the change in A21’s outgoing transitions, and the co‑occurrence and ordering relations. A competing explanation that the five activities are a rare variant is ruled out by their high frequency and the large number of consistent indicators. A limitation is that the internal structure of the new subprocess (e.g., whether it is a parallel block or a sequence with variable order) cannot be determined from the evidence, but the existence of a distinct bypassing path is well supported.

Other differences: A21’s position shifted slightly earlier, but this is minor and not part of the main hypothesis. [POS001]
