### Observed differences

- For activities A01, A03, A13, A14, A17, and A22, the distribution of resources changed substantially. In the earlier window, the most frequent resources were User_24, User_42, User_40, User_2, and User_3, each with shares around 4–9%. In the later window, these resources almost disappear, and new resources such as User_84, User_96, User_65, User_104, and User_56 appear with shares around 3–11%. The share of "OTHER" (resources not individually listed) increased for most of these activities, e.g., from about 43% to 51% for A01, from 44% to 50% for A03, from 47% to 54% for A13/A14, and from 46% to 53% for A17; for A22 it stayed around 40%. - The elapsed time between activities A05 and A09 increased. The mean time rose from about 0.000217 hours to about 0.000286 hours (about 32% higher), and the median from 0.00021 to 0.000308 hours (about 47% higher). The number of observed transitions also increased from 120 to 132. [TWAIT001]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a hypothesis that the allocation of resources to activities A01, A03, A13, A14, A17, and A22 changed: in the later window, these activities are performed by a different set of resources than in the earlier window, with the earlier resources (e.g., User_24, User_42, User_40, User_2) largely replaced by new ones (e.g., User_84, User_96, User_65, User_104, User_56). The increase in the "OTHER" share also suggests a broader or different resource pool. This is supported by six activity-level indicators showing the same pattern, and the number of such indicators far exceeds what would be expected by chance (6 vs 0.005 expected). [ASSIGN001-006]

Other differences: The time between activities A05 and A09 increased, indicating a timing change. This is based on a single indicator, but it is also far above the chance expectation (1 vs 0.017 expected) and shows a consistent increase in mean, median, and quartiles. [TWAIT001]

A competing explanation that cannot be ruled out is that the change in resource identifiers reflects a change in the pool of available resources (e.g., different people assigned to the process) rather than a deliberate reallocation of work among the same resources. The timing change could also be influenced by factors not captured here, but the evidence directly shows an increase in the elapsed time between these two activities.
