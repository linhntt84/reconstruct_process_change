### Observed differences

- In the later window, activities A25, A26 and A31 each occur on average about 3.5 times per trace instead of exactly once, and they repeat within the same trace in about 68% of traces, whereas before they never repeated. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003]
- The outgoing behaviour of A31 changed: in the earlier window it always went to A20; in the later window it goes to A25 in about 71% of its outgoing transitions and to A20 in the remaining 29%. [DIR001]
- The relation between A25 and A31 changed from a fixed order (A25 always before A31, two positions apart) to a mixed pattern: in the later window about 68% of traces show them interleaved or repeated, and the remaining 32% still have A25 before A31. The distance between them is no longer fixed at 2 steps. [REL001, LAG001]
- The relative positions of A25 and A31 within traces became more spread out (larger standard deviation), though their average positions changed only slightly. [POS001, POS002]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that a repetition loop involving activities A25, A26 and A31 was introduced or expanded. In the earlier window these activities each occurred exactly once per trace, A31 always led to A20, and A25 always preceded A31 by a fixed gap. In the later window all three activities repeat about 3.5 times per trace in most traces, A31 often leads to A25 instead of A20, and A25 and A31 are frequently interleaved or repeated rather than appearing in a fixed order. The eight control-flow items are far more than the 0.3 expected by chance, and they are mutually consistent. A competing explanation that cannot be fully ruled out is that the observed repetition is a sampling artefact of a rare variant, but the high frequency of repetition (68% of traces) and the consistent successor change make this unlikely.

Other differences: The position distributions of A25 and A31 became more variable, and the distance between A25 and A31 is no longer fixed at 2 steps; these are consistent with the repetition loop rather than independent changes.
