### Observed differences

- In the later window, the elapsed time between consecutive completions of activities A38 and A41 dropped from a median of about 0.98 hours to about 0.50 hours, a reduction of roughly half; the mean fell from about 1.01 to 0.50 hours. The number of observed transitions for this pair rose from 97 to 113. [TWAIT001]
- Similarly, the elapsed time between completions of A40 and A41 dropped from a median of about 0.96 hours to about 0.51 hours, with the mean falling from about 0.97 to 0.51 hours. The number of observed transitions for this pair fell from 103 to 87. [TWAIT002]
- Both changes are highly unusual compared with random re-splits of the data (empirical strength above 0.999 in each case). [TWAIT001, TWAIT002]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a hypothesis that the timing between completions of activities involving A41 changed: in the later window, the time between completion of A38 and A41, and between A40 and A41, decreased substantially, to about half of its earlier value. Both time indicators show the same direction and magnitude, and the number of such time items (2) far exceeds the chance expectation (0.01) for the time family. The two items share A41, so they may reflect a single underlying change around that activity rather than two independent changes. A competing explanation that cannot be fully ruled out is that the observed reduction reflects a change in the frequency or ordering of these activity pairs, since the counts of transitions also shifted (up for A38–A41, down for A40–A41), but the consistent and large reduction in inter-completion times is direct evidence of a timing change.

Other differences: The number of observed transitions for the two activity pairs changed in opposite directions (increase for A38–A41, decrease for A40–A41), but this is not part of the main timing hypothesis and may be a sampling or frequency effect.
