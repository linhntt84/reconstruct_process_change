### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, each of A15, A17, A18, A19, A20, A21, and A26 had exactly one observed outgoing transition (e.g., A15→A18, A17→A15, A18→A19, A19→A21, A20→A27, A21→A20, A26→A17). In the later window, each of these activities has several outgoing transitions; for example, A15 now leads to A18 in about 68% of cases and to A19, A20, A21 in about 11% each, and A18 now leads to A27 in about 52% and to A19, A20, A21 in smaller shares. [DIR001-DIR007]

- The mean position of A15, A17, and A18 increased (later) by about 0.05, 0.03, and 0.06 respectively, while the mean position of A19, A20, and A21 decreased (earlier) by about 0.06, 0.03, and 0.04. [POS001-POS006]

- For many activity pairs, the order reversed or became mixed. For example, A15 always preceded A19 before, but after A19 precedes A15 in 74% of traces; A18 always preceded A19 before, but after A19 precedes A18 in 85%; A15 and A21 were always A15 first, but after the two orders are about equally frequent. [REL001-REL008]

- Distances between activities became more variable. For instance, A15 and A18 were always adjacent (lag 1) before, but after they are adjacent in 68% and separated by 2–4 steps in the rest; A17 and A18 were always 2 steps apart, but after they are 2–5 steps apart; A15 and A27 were always 5 steps apart, but after they are 2–5 steps apart. [LAG001-LAG024]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a hypothesis that the process changed from a fixed sequential order to a more flexible, branching order. Activities A15, A17, A18, A19, A20, A21, A26, and A27 are involved. In the earlier window, each of these activities had a single observed successor and pairs had a fixed order (e.g., A15 before A19, A18 before A19). In the later window, each activity has multiple successors, and many pairs appear in both orders or with variable distances, indicating that the order is no longer fixed. The number of evidence items (45) far exceeds the 0.47 expected by chance, and the changes are consistent across DIR, REL, and LAG indicators. A competing explanation that the differences are due to sampling variation is unlikely given the large number of items, but the evidence cannot rule out that the process was always variable and the two windows happened to sample different variants.

Other differences: The position shifts (A15, A17, A18 later; A19, A20, A21 earlier) are consistent with the reordering and are part of the same hypothesis.
