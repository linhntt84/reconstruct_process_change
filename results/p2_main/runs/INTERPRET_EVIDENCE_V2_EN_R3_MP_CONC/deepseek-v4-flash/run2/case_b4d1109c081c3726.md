### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, activities A20, A21, A22, A24 and A25 occurred around the middle of traces (mean positions about 0.51–0.61), while A26–A30 occurred later (mean positions about 0.63–0.68). In the later window, A20–A25 shifted later (e.g. A25 mean from 0.61 to 0.70, A20 from 0.56 to 0.61), while A26–A30 shifted earlier (e.g. A26 mean from 0.63 to 0.55, A28 from 0.68 to 0.62, A30 from 0.68 to 0.62). [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006, POS007, POS008, POS009, POS010]
- The order between these two groups reversed. For example, A20 occurred before A26 in about 99% of traces earlier, but in the later window A26 occurred before A20 in about 73% of traces. Similar reversals appear for A21–A26, A22–A26, A25–A26 and A25–A28. [REL001, REL005, REL010, REL018, REL020]
- The distance between activities in the two groups changed: pairs that were close together became more spread out, and pairs that were far apart became closer. For instance, A20–A25 earlier had lags of 1–3 steps, while later lags of 4–5 steps appeared; A20–A28 earlier had lags of 4–5 steps, while later lags of 1–2 steps became common. [LAG003, LAG005]
- Outgoing transitions from A20, A21, A22, A24 and A25 were redistributed. Earlier, A20 led mainly to A22, A25 and A21; later it also led to A26–A30. A25 earlier led only to A26; later it led to many successors including A36. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a hypothesis that the ordering between two groups of activities changed: activities A20, A21, A22, A24 and A25, which earlier tended to occur before A26, A27, A28, A29 and A30, in the later window tend to occur after them, or at least no longer in a fixed order. This is directly shown by the reversal of before/after relations across many activity pairs and by the corresponding shift in mean positions. The change is consistent across relational, positional and lag indicators. A competing explanation that cannot be fully ruled out is that some activities are repeated or that the case mix changed, but the direct order reversal makes a reordering the most plausible interpretation.

Other differences: The successor distributions of A20, A21, A22, A24 and A25 became more diverse, with new successors such as A26–A30 appearing, and A25's successor changed from exclusively A26 to a wide set including A36. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005] These are consistent with the reordering but could also reflect added or alternative paths.
