### Observed differences

- In the later window, A19, A20, A25, A26 and A27 almost always go to A28 (about 93–100% of their outgoing transitions), whereas before they went to A30 or A34 (about 40–60% each). [DIR001–DIR005]
- A47, which before went to A28 in about 91% of cases, now goes to A19/A20/A25/A26/A27 (about 17–20% each) and almost never to A28 (0.5%). [DIR008]
- A30 now goes to A34 in about 98.5% of cases (up from 48.5%), and A29 now goes to A30 in about 98.5% (up from 51%). [DIR006, DIR007, LAG006, LAG008]
- The mean position of A19, A20, A25, A26 and A27 moved from about 0.60–0.64 of the trace to about 0.34–0.36, while A28 moved slightly later (from 0.355 to 0.379). [POS001–POS006]
- The order between A19/A26/A27 and A28/A29 flipped: before, A28/A29 almost always preceded them; after, they almost always precede A28/A29. [REL001–REL006]
- The lag between A47 and A28 increased from 1 step to 2 steps, while the lag between A20 and A28 decreased from 2–3 steps to 1 step. [LAG002, LAG009]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The process changed so that A28 now occurs after one of A19, A20, A25, A26 or A27, instead of directly after A47. A47 now leads to that group, and the group leads to A28. This is directly shown by the successor changes, the earlier positions of the group, the order flip with A28, and the lag changes. The evidence cannot rule out that the process was redesigned with a different sequence, but the data consistently show this reordering.

Other differences: A30 and A34 became more tightly sequential after A29, with A29→A30→A34 almost always, whereas before A29 had several alternative successors and A30 split between A34 and other activities.
