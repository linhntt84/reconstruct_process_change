### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a hypothesis that the process changed from predominantly executing a branch containing activities A21–A25 to predominantly executing a branch containing activities A16–A20. In the earlier window, about 81.5% of traces executed A21–A25 and only 18.5% executed A16–A20; in the later window these shares are reversed. The routing from A15 changed accordingly, with the majority of its outgoing transitions now going to A19 (76.5%) instead of A24 (81.5% before). A29, which previously co-occurred with the A21–A25 branch, now co-occurs with the A16–A20 branch. The lag changes are consistent with this branch switch, showing that the relative positions of activities within the new branch differ from the old branch. The evidence is direct and consistent across occurrence, routing, co-occurrence, and lag indicators. A competing explanation that the observed differences are due to random sampling is ruled out by the large number of qualifying items (80 vs 0.84 expected by chance). A limitation is that the evidence is purely control-flow; no timing or resource changes are assessed.

Other differences: The lag distributions show that the new branch has a different internal ordering: A15 is closer to A17, A17 is farther from A20, and A19 is closer to A17, compared to the old branch. These are part of the same structural change.
