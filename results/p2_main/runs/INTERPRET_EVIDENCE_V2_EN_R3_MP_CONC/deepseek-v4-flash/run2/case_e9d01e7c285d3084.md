### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a hypothesis that the process changed from a single linear execution of a sequence (A14 → A30 → A36 → A25) to a repeated loop in which A30, A31, and A36 are executed multiple times per case and the order between A14, A30, and A36 is no longer fixed. The occurrence counts of A30, A31, and A36 each rose from about 1 to about 3.5 per trace, with recurrence support rising from near 0 to about 68% [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003]. The successor of A36 shifted from A25 to A30, indicating a loop back to A30 [DIR001]. The relational evidence shows that the fixed order A14 before A30 before A36 (observed in about 98–99% of traces before) became interleaved/repeated in about 68% of traces after [REL003, REL004, REL005]. The lag between A30 and A36 increased from a tight 2-step gap to a wider distribution, consistent with repetition [LAG002]. The position shifts of A14 (earlier) and A30/A36 (later) are consistent with a longer trace due to the loop.

Other differences: The position of A14 moved earlier in the trace, and the lag between A05 and A14 shifted from mostly 1–2 steps to more 3-step gaps [POS001, LAG001]. These are consistent with the overall lengthening of traces and do not require a separate hypothesis.

A competing explanation that cannot be fully ruled out is that the process did not add a loop but instead replaced the single sequence with a different structure that happens to produce similar relational patterns; however, the direct evidence of repeated occurrences and the new A36→A30 edge strongly support the loop interpretation.
