### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, activities A15, A16 and A17 each had outgoing transitions spread over many successors (A20, A21, A22, A23, A24, A25, A31, etc.). In the later window, A15, A16 and A17 each go only to A15, A16, A17 and A20; for example, A15's successors changed from about 21% A20, 16% A17, 14.5% A16, 13% A21, 12% A23, 8.5% A25, 8% A24, 7% A22 to 36% A16, 32% A17, 32% A20. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003]
- A20's successors changed from about 50% A31, 18% A22, 13% A25, 10% A23, 6% A24, 2.5% A21 to 100% A21. A22's successors changed from a mix (A17, A31, A20, A16, A15, A19) to 100% A31. [DIR004, DIR006]
- The relative order between the two groups reversed. For example, A15 occurred before A21 in 23% of cases earlier and after A21 in 77%; in the later window A15 always precedes A21 (100%). Similarly, A20 preceded A21 in only 2.5% of cases earlier but in 100% later. The same pattern holds for A15, A16, A17, A19 and A20 versus A21, A22, A23, A24 and A25. [REL001, REL005, REL009, REL018, REL020, REL021, REL022]
- Positions shifted: A15, A16, A17 and A20 moved earlier (mean relative position dropped by about 0.06–0.10), while A21, A22, A23, A24 and A25 moved later (mean increased by about 0.03–0.10). [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS005, POS006, POS007, POS008, POS009, POS010]
- Lags within the earlier group shrank: for A15–A16, lag 1 rose from about 31% to 69% and lags 3–5 disappeared. Lags between the groups grew: for A15–A21, lag 1 fell from 57% to 0% and lags 2–4 became the norm. [LAG001, LAG004, LAG008, LAG011]
- The elapsed time between A31 and A38 increased from about 0.91 hours to 1.80 hours (roughly doubled), and between A37 and A38 from about 0.19 to 0.36 hours. [TWAIT001, TWAIT002]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a change in the process structure: activities A15, A16, A17 and A20 (together with A19) now always occur before A21–A25, whereas earlier the order between these two groups was mixed or even reversed. This is directly shown by the successor sets (A15–A17 no longer lead to A21–A25, and A20 now leads only to A21), by the relational order (all pairs between the two groups became fixed with the first group first), by the positional shifts (the first group moved earlier, the second later), and by the lag changes (within-group lags decreased, between-group lags increased). The number of control-flow items (93) far exceeds the 0.72 expected by chance, so the overall pattern is not a sampling artefact. A limitation is that the two windows are finite samples, but the consistency across dozens of indicators makes a chance explanation unlikely.

Other differences: The timing between A31–A38 and A37–A38 increased substantially (about doubled and nearly doubled respectively), indicating a separate change in elapsed times between those activities. [TWAIT001, TWAIT002]
