### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a hypothesis that the process changed from a structure in which activity A02 was a common step (in about half the cases) to one in which A02 is no longer executed, and the routing shifted towards activity A10, with A03 and A14 becoming less frequent and occurring later in the trace. This is directly shown by A02's complete disappearance across occurrence, direction, position, and relation indicators, by A10's increased participation and earlier position, and by the systematic successor changes (e.g., A08 now leading to A03 instead of A10, A10 now leading to A07/A11 instead of A02/A13, A14 now leading to A13 instead of A07). The large number of qualifying items (60 vs. 0.67 expected by chance) rules out a chance sampling effect.

Other differences: The lag distributions for several activity pairs change, e.g., A03–A09 now typically 3 steps apart instead of 1–2, A14–A09 now typically 2 steps apart instead of 1, and A13–A10 now often 1 step apart instead of 2–3. These are consistent with the reordering but are not part of the main hypothesis.

A competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that the observed differences reflect a change in the mix of cases rather than a change in the process model itself; the evidence does not distinguish between these.
