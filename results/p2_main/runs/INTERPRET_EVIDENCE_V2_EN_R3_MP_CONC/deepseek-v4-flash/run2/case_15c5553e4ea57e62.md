### Observed differences

- In the later window, the relative order of two groups of activities reversed. Activities A15, A16, A17, A19 and A20 moved later in the trace (mean position increased by about 0.03–0.10 on a 0–1 scale), while A21–A25 moved earlier (mean position decreased by about 0.03–0.09). [POS001–POS010]
- For pairs between A15/A16/A17/A19/A20 and A21–A25, the order flipped: before, the first group always preceded the second (A_before_B 100%); after, the second group precedes the first in about 44–94% of cases, e.g. A20 before A21 dropped from 100% to 6.5%, A15 before A21 dropped to 27%. [REL001–REL021]
- Successor distributions changed: A15–A20 now lead to A21–A25 more often, and A21–A25 now lead back to A15–A20, whereas before A20 went only to A21 and A21–A25 went among themselves. [DIR001–DIR010]
- The distance between the two groups shrank: e.g. A15–A21 lag was 2–4 before, now often 1; A20–A22 lag was 5 before, now 1–4. [LAG005, LAG012, LAG027, LAG028]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a change in ordering: activities A15–A20 (including A19) and A21–A25 swapped their typical order. Before, A15–A20 occurred before A21–A25; after, A21–A25 tend to occur before A15–A20. This is directly shown by the reversal of order in REL items, the position shifts in POS, the successor changes in DIR, and the reduced lags in LAG. The screening count (93 items vs 0.72 expected by chance) rules out chance as a whole. The main limitation is that the exact process structure (e.g., whether this is a parallel branch swap or a sequential reordering) is not fully specified, but the ordering change itself is clear.

Other differences: A20's successor changed from exclusively A21 to mostly A31 (47.5%) and others, and A22's successor changed from A31 to a mix including A20. [DIR005, DIR007] This suggests a new path via A31, but it is not part of the main ordering hypothesis.
