### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, activities A19, A20, and A21 each occurred in about 30–36% of traces; in the later window they occur in about 1% or fewer of traces. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003]
- In the earlier window, activities A26, A27, and A28 did not occur at all; in the later window they occur in essentially every trace (about 100%). [OCC004, OCC005, OCC006]
- The outgoing transitions of A44 changed: before, it led mainly to A19, A20, and A21 (about 30%, 25%, and 30% of its transitions); after, it leads mainly to A26, A27, and A28 (about 31%, 27%, and 26%). [DIR007]
- The order between A44 and the middle activities reversed: before A44 preceded A19/A20/A21; after A44 precedes A26/A27/A28, and these new activities always precede A29. [REL022, REL025, REL027, REL021, REL024, REL026]
- The lag between A44 and A29 increased from mostly 2–3 steps to mostly 4–5 steps. [LAG001]
- A29 remains in all traces, but its position shifts slightly later (mean from 0.40 to 0.44 of the trace). [POS007]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the process changed by replacing the middle segment between A44 and A29. In the earlier window, A44 was followed by one of A19, A20, or A21, which then led to A29. In the later window, A44 is followed by A26, A27, and A28 (in any order among themselves), which then lead to A29. The new activities appear in every trace, while the old ones almost disappear. The lag increase is consistent with an extra step (or more) in the middle. The screening comparison (48 items vs 0.65 expected by chance) makes it very unlikely that this is due to chance. A competing explanation that cannot be fully ruled out is that the two windows represent different process variants rather than a change over time, but the consistency across many indicators supports a real change.

Other differences: A29's position shifts slightly later, consistent with the longer middle segment. No other differences.
