### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a change in how work is allocated to resources for several activities (A01, A03, A13, A14, A17, A21). In the earlier window, these activities were performed by a set of resources that included User_28, User_3, User_65, User_41, User_49, User_51, User_42, User_54, User_10, User_36, User_16 and User_56; in the later window, those resources performed these activities less or not at all, and a different set of resources (User_78, User_12, User_77, User_48, User_14, User_21, User_39, User_79, User_80, User_98, User_18) took over a substantial share. The consistency across six activities and the far-above-chance number of resource items (6 items vs 0.005 expected) support this. A competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that the set of available resources changed (e.g., different people became available), rather than a deliberate reallocation of work.

Other differences: The time between A04 and A01 increased roughly twofold (mean from 29 to 56 hours, median from 22 to 45 hours). This is a single time item but far above the expected chance count for the time family (1 vs 0.017). It suggests a timing change, but with only one item we cannot rule out that it is an isolated effect.
