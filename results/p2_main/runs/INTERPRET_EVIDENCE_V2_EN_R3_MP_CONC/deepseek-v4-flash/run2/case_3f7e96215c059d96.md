### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, activities A28 through A38 each occurred in every trace (100% participation). In the later window, A28–A33 occur in about 46% of traces and A34–A38 in about 55% of traces; A25 and A26 remain in every trace. [OCC001–OCC010]
- In the earlier window, A25 co-occurred with all of A28–A38 in every trace and always preceded them; A26 always followed them. In the later window, A25 co-occurs with A28–A33 in 46% of traces and with A34–A38 in 55% of traces, and A26 co-occurs with them in the same proportions. [REL001–REL020]
- In the earlier window, A28–A33 and A34–A38 all co-occurred with each other in every trace. In the later window, A28–A33 co-occur with each other in 46% of traces, A34–A38 co-occur with each other in 55% of traces, but an activity from one group almost never co-occurs with an activity from the other group (co-occurrence about 0.5% for A28 with A34–A38). [REL021–REL065]
- The outgoing transitions of A25 changed from mainly targeting A28–A33 (shares 0.17–0.24) with small shares to A34–A38 (0.02–0.06) to a more even distribution across all ten activities (0.07–0.13 each). [DIR001]
- A28, A29, and A33 now have A26 as a direct successor in the later window, whereas before they did not; their successors from the other group (A34–A38) disappear. [DIR002–DIR004]
- The relative position of A25 moved later (mean from 0.55 to 0.64), A26 moved slightly earlier (0.84 to 0.82), A28–A33 moved later (from about 0.63 to 0.73), and A34–A38 moved earlier (from about 0.76 to 0.73). [POS001–POS012]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the process changed from executing a single sequence containing all ten activities A28–A38 (with A25 before and A26 after) to executing one of two alternative branches: either the group A28–A33 or the group A34–A38, with A25 before and A26 after. This is directly shown by the drop in participation of these activities from 100% to about half, the mutual exclusivity between the two groups in the later window, and the appearance of A26 as a direct successor of A28–A33 while the other group's successors disappear. The large number of qualifying items (106 vs 1 expected by chance) rules out random variation. A limitation is that the exact ordering within each group is not fixed (they appear in mixed order), and the LAG evidence for cross-group pairs is based on very few traces, but the main split is robust.

Other differences: The relative positions of the activities shifted as described, but these are consistent with the branch change. A competing explanation that the two groups are simply alternative paths that were always possible but not observed in the before window is contradicted by the before window showing all activities in every trace; however, the evidence cannot rule out that the change is due to a shift in the mix of cases processed.
