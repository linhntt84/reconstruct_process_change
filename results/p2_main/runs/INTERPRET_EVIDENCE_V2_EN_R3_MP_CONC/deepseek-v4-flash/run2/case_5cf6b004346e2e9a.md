### Observed differences

- In the later window, A10 and A11 each lead to A08 in about 100% of their outgoing transitions, whereas before they led to A06 and A07 in roughly equal shares (about 50/50 each). [DIR002, DIR003]
- In the later window, A08 leads to A06 and A07 in addition to A10 and A11; before it led almost exclusively to A10 and A11 (about 50% each). [DIR001]
- The lag between A08 and A10/A11 shifted: immediate succession (lag 1) decreased (e.g., A08→A10 from about 37% to 13%; A08→A11 from about 47% to 24%), while larger lags appeared. [LAG010, LAG011]
- The lag between A10/A11 and A06/A07 shifted from mostly lag 1 to lag 2 or more (e.g., A10→A06 from 29% at lag 1 to 33% at lag 2; A11→A06 from 31% at lag 1 to 47% at lag 2). [LAG012, LAG015]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a change in the ordering of A08 relative to A10 and A11: in the earlier window A08 typically preceded A10/A11, which then led to A06/A07; in the later window A10/A11 typically precede A08, which then leads to A06/A07. This is directly shown by the successor changes (A10 and A11 now go to A08 instead of A06/A07; A08 now goes to A06/A07) and by the lag shifts that place A08 between A10/A11 and A06/A07. The change is not a strict reversal, because some traces still show A08 before A10/A11, but the dominant order has switched.

Other differences: The lag distributions for many activity pairs became more spread out (e.g., A06–A06, A07–A07, A08–A08), which is consistent with the reordering but does not constitute a separate structural change. A competing explanation that cannot be fully ruled out is that A08 became optional or repeated in some traces, which could also affect lags, but the successor evidence points specifically to an order change.
