### Observed differences

- In the later window, activities A22, A31, A32, A37, A38, and A39 occur in every trace, whereas in the earlier window each occurred in about half of the traces (48–52%). [OCC001–OCC006]
- A20's outgoing transition changed from always going to A23 to being distributed among A31, A32, A37, A38, and A39 (each about 20%). [DIR001]
- A21's outgoing transition changed from a mix (A22 48%, others ~10% each) to always A22. [DIR002]
- A26 disappears as a direct successor of A31, A32, A37, A38, and A39; A23 appears as a new direct successor of these activities. [DIR003–DIR007]

- A22 now always co-occurs with A31–A39, with those activities before it (previously they were alternatives). [REL015–REL019]
- A23 now always co-occurs with A31–A39, with those activities before it (previously A23 preceded them). [REL020–REL024]
- Among A31–A39, they now always co-occur (previously about half the traces had neither). [REL030–REL039]
- The lag between A31 and A39 decreased: lag 1 increased from about 30% to 52%. [LAG001]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a change from a branching process with two alternative paths (one through A22, one through A31–A39) to a single sequential path in which A20 is followed by A31–A39, then A23, then A21, then A22, with A26 still occurring later. The OCC, DIR, POS, REL, and LAG indicators consistently show that the alternative branches are replaced by a single path where all activities occur in a fixed order. A competing explanation that the observed differences are due to random sampling is ruled out by the large number of qualifying items (62 vs 0.67 expected by chance). A limitation is that the exact position of A26 is not directly measured, but its continued co-occurrence with the group suggests it remains part of the process.

Other differences: The disappearance of A26 as a direct successor of the group and the appearance of A23 as a new successor are part of the same reordering.
