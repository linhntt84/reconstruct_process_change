### Observed differences

- The dominant difference is a flip in how often two alternative activity blocks run. In the earlier window, A21, A22, A23 and A24 each appeared in about 83 in 100 traces and typically together as one block (co-occurrence about 83 in 100), while A25–A28 each appeared in about 18 in 100 traces. In the later window the shares are reversed: A21–A24 each appear in about 27 in 100 traces and A25–A28 in about 73 in 100. [OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, OCC007, OCC008, OCC009, REL017, REL051]

- The two blocks stayed mutually exclusive in both windows: cross-block pairs such as A21/A25 occurred together in at most about 1 in 100 traces, so in essentially every trace exactly one of the two blocks ran. [REL020, REL029, REL044]

- Routing into the blocks flipped with them. A20 occurred in every trace in both windows; earlier, about 82 in 100 of its outgoing transitions led to A24 and about 18 to A28, while in the later window about 73 lead to A28 and about 27 to A24. A20 also still precedes whichever block runs (before A21 in about 83 in 100 earlier; before A25 in about 72 in 100 later). [DIR002, REL009, REL013]

- The available paths and their internal order are the same in both windows; only the shares with which they are taken changed. Within-block orders held (for example, A24 still preceded A21 and A22 whenever both ran), and the downstream activities A32 and A36 still occurred in every trace and followed the active block (A36 after A21 in about 83 in 100 earlier, after A25 in about 72 in 100 later), with A36's position near the trace end essentially unchanged. [REL019, REL028, REL024, REL054, REL055, POS002]

- A08 became rarer and less connected: it appeared in about 18 in 100 traces earlier (36 traces) but only about 6 in 100 later (12 traces); its typical position moved from near the end of the trace (mean relative position about 0.92) to mid-trace (about 0.50); and its outgoing transitions, earlier about 92 in 100 towards A37, later spread across A16 (about 31 in 100), A15, A19 and A33 (about 15 each) and others. [OCC001, POS001, DIR001]

- Smaller shifts: A36's transition to A37 became almost always immediate (about 98 in 100 at a one-step gap versus about 84 earlier) and A36's earlier transitions to A08 (about 17 in 100) no longer appear among its observed transitions; the event gaps from A20 to A22 and from A24 to A22 shifted slightly toward shorter lags. [DIR003, LAG006, LAG002, LAG004]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the frequencies with which two existing alternative paths are taken changed around the branch point A20: the path in which A20 is followed by A21–A24 (with A32 and A36 afterwards) fell from about 83 in 100 cases to about 27, while the alternative path in which A20 is followed by A25–A28 rose from about 18 in 100 to about 73. Supporting signs: the routing shares out of A20 flipped in near mirror image across about 200 traces per window; all eight block members moved by roughly 55 percentage points in opposite directions with substantial support in both windows; and the relational structure — mutual exclusivity of the blocks, A20 before the active block, A32 and A36 after it, and within-block orders such as A24 before A21 — is the same in both windows, which fits a shift in how often existing paths are taken rather than a replacement or reordering of paths. Taken together, 88 items qualified against roughly 1 expected for an unchanged process, so the pattern as a whole is unlikely to be a sampling artefact. [DIR002, OCC002, OCC006, REL020, REL019, REL055]

The evidence shows where the shift manifests (branch participation and A20's outgoing shares) but cannot show whether the branch decision itself changed at A20 or in conditions upstream of it, and the two windows are finite samples of 200 traces each. [DIR002, REL009]

Other differences:

- A08 declined (about 18 in 100 traces to about 6) and was rewired (position moved earlier, successors spread over several targets instead of mostly A37, and A36's transitions to A08 disappeared from its observed profile). This side observation is not explained by the branch flip alone and rests on few later-window observations (12–13 traces, and only 4 pair observations for the changed A08–A37 gap), so it remains weakly supported. [OCC001, POS001, DIR001, DIR003, LAG001]

- Minor gap redistributions on unchanged paths: the A20→A22 and A24→A22 gaps concentrated somewhat at shorter lags, and A36→A37 became almost always immediate. [LAG002, LAG004, LAG006]
