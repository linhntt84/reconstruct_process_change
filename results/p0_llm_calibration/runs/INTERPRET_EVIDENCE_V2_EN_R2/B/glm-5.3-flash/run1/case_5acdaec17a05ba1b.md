### Observed differences

- Eight activities became near-universal: in the earlier window, A28, A29, A31 and A32 each appeared in about 54 of 100 traces, and A33–A36 in about 46 of 100; in the later window, each of the eight appears in 199 of 200 traces, exactly once per trace, with no repetition in either window. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, OCC007, OCC008]
- The two groups of four were mutually exclusive earlier and are executed together later: in the earlier window, cross-group pairs such as A28 with A33 never shared a trace (co-occurrence 0 of 200), while in the later window they co-occur in about 99 of 100 traces, always with the first-group activity (A28, A29, A31 or A32) earlier than the second-group activity (A33–A36). [REL020, REL023, REL026, REL031, REL035, REL038]
- A25 and A26 bracket these groups: earlier, A25 preceded and A26 followed the group's activities only in the traces containing that group (about 54 of 100 for the first group, 46 of 100 for the second); later, A25 precedes and A26 follows all eight activities in 199 of 200 traces. [REL001, REL005, REL009, REL013]
- Order inside each group stays unfixed in both windows: within-group pairs occur in both orders in near-even shares before and after (for example A28 before A29 in about half of the traces containing both, and after it in about half), so the change lies between the groups, not in swapping two activities. [REL017, REL024, REL030, REL039, REL044]
- Direct routing changed accordingly: earlier, A25 led directly to all eight activities (shares about 0.09–0.15 of its outgoing transitions); in the later window its observed successors are only A28, A29, A31 and A32 (about 0.20–0.29 each). Earlier, each first-group activity passed directly on to A26 in about a quarter of its outgoing transitions (0.24–0.29); later these transitions are essentially gone (A28 to A26 about 1 in 200; unobserved for A29, A31, A32), replaced by transitions into A33–A36 (about 0.04–0.08 each). [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005]
- Event distances agree with a second block now following the first: earlier, A33–A36 followed A25 within 1–4 steps in roughly even shares (measured in 92 of 200 traces), and A28/A29/A31/A32 reached A26 within 1–4 steps (108 of 200); later, when these pairs fall within the five-step window (about 43–56 of 199 traces), the gap is 5 steps in about 98 of 100 cases. [LAG001, LAG004, LAG005, LAG008]
- Relative event positions shifted consistently: A25's mean relative position moved from about 0.66 to 0.58, the first-group activities from about 0.74 to 0.65, and the second-group activities from about 0.74 to 0.77. [POS001, POS002, POS006, POS009]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the exclusive choice between two four-activity blocks between A25 and A26 was removed, so that both blocks now run in sequence in every trace: in the earlier window a trace executed A25, then either the block A28/A29/A31/A32 (about 54 of 100 traces) or the block A33/A34/A35/A36 (about 46 of 100), then A26, with no fixed order inside the chosen block; in the later window every trace executes A25, then all of A28/A29/A31/A32, then all of A33/A34/A35/A36, then A26, still without a fixed order inside each block. [REL020, REL026, REL001, REL009, DIR001, DIR002]

Supporting signs: 74 evidence items qualify, many times the fewer than 1 expected for an unchanged process; the relational items show the change directly (cross-group pairs move from never co-occurring to co-occurring in about 99 of 100 traces with a fixed between-group order, while within-group order stays mixed); the routing items show the matching successor changes (A25 now feeds only the first block, and the first block's direct transitions to A26 gave way to transitions into the second block); and the lag and position shifts agree quantitatively with four second-block activities now following the first block. The items rest on 92–200 traces per window, and the later successor shares (about 0.25 per remaining branch) fit a block executed in varying internal order. [REL020, DIR001, DIR002, LAG001, LAG005, POS002, POS006]

Limitations and competing explanation: the windows are finite samples, so it cannot be ruled out that a rare both-blocks path already existed before below the detection limit (0 of 200 traces) or that single-block paths persist at roughly 1 in 200 after the change (for example the residual A28→A26 transition); the evidence establishes that the dominant behaviour flipped from either-or to both-in-sequence, not that the earlier single-block paths have become impossible. [DIR002, REL020]

Other differences: none beyond the above; the only residual observations are rare single-trace transitions in the later window (A25→A35, A28→A26), which fit the same picture as leftovers of the earlier routing. [DIR001, DIR002]
