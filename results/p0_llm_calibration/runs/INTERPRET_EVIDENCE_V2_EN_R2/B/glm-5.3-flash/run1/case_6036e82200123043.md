### Observed differences

- Participation itself is unchanged: A26, A27, A35, A36, A41 and A42 occur together in every trace in both windows (co-occurrence 100 in 100 for all eight measured pairs), so the differences lie in routing and order, not in whether the activities run. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, REL007, REL008]
- In the earlier window, A26's outgoing transitions spread almost evenly over A35, A36, A41 and A42 (about 24–25 in 100 each). In the later window, about 94 in 100 of A26's outgoing transitions go to A30, and A30 immediately follows A26 in about 94 in 100 of the observed pairs, whereas before the pair was always five positions apart (177 traces). [DIR002, LAG002]
- The four activities A35, A36, A41 and A42 rerouted their exits: before, roughly 21–31 in 100 of each one's outgoing transitions went to A30; in the later window this is down to about 0–1 in 100, while new transitions to A27 carry about 17–29 in 100 of each one's outgoing transitions. [DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006]

Before, A26 preceded each of A35, A36, A41, A42 in about 98 in 100 traces and A27 preceded each of them in about 96–97 in 100; in the later window each group activity precedes A26 in about 96–98 in 100 traces and precedes A27 in about 96–98 in 100 traces. ### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the group A35, A36, A41, A42 — which still always executes together, without a fixed internal order, in both windows — now runs before A27 and A26 rather than after them, with the surrounding flow rewired: in the earlier window A25 led directly to A27, A26 branched into the group, and the group exited to A30; in the later window A26 feeds A30 directly, the group exits to A27, and A25 reaches A27 only after passing through the group. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006, REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, REL007, REL008, LAG001, LAG002, LAG005, LAG006, LAG007, LAG008, LAG009, LAG010, POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006]

This is a reversal of ordering constraints plus rerouting, not merely existing paths being taken with different shares: the order fractions flip from about 96–98 in 100 to about 2 in 100 for all eight measured pairs, the successor profiles change wholesale, and participation stays complete, which rules out a pure participation or rare-variant reading. The 31 flagged items are about 40 times the number expected without a change (0.76), and each difference exceeds those arising in about 199 of 200 random re-splits; the items converge on one coherent rerouting rather than independent effects. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006, POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006, REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, REL007, REL008, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006, LAG007, LAG008, LAG009, LAG010, LAG011]

Limitations: the order between A26 and A27 themselves was not measured, so their relative position in the later sequence (A26 sits latest, at mean relative position about 0.62) is inferred only indirectly, and the evidence cannot rule out that additional reordering occurred outside the measured pairs; some vanished edges also rest on only 4–5 later observations, though the successor profiles corroborate them. [POS001, POS002, REL001, LAG003, LAG004, LAG006, LAG009, LAG010, DIR002]

Other differences: the slightly wider A42→A36 distances within the group and the scattered one-off transitions (about 1 in 200) are not part of this hypothesis and are consistent with sampling noise. [LAG011, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005]
