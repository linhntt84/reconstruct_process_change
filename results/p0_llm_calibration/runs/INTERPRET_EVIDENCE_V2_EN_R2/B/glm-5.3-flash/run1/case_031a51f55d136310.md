### Observed differences

- Activity A08 was executed in about 33 in 100 traces in the earlier window (mean of about 0.33 occurrences per trace) but in only about 11 in 100 traces in the later window (about 0.10 per trace); within-trace repetition of A08 was essentially absent in both windows. [OCC001]
- A15's outgoing transitions changed: earlier, about 82% went to A16 and about 17% to A08; in the later window, about 97% go directly to A16 and only about 1% to A08. [DIR002]
- When A08 occurred, its outgoing transitions earlier went overwhelmingly to A16 (about 83%); in the later window A16 accounts for only about 20%, with the rest spread over many activities (A44 about 15%; A15, A46 and A33 about 10% each). This rests on only about 20 observed outgoing transitions and is weak, as are the observations that A08, when it occurs, now sits later in the trace (median relative position about 0.22 earlier versus about 0.43 later, based on 21 traces) and that A08-to-A16 gaps are no longer almost always one step (about 95% at one position earlier versus about 39% later, only 7 pairs). [DIR001, POS001, LAG001]
- A08's co-occurrence with other activities fell in step with its participation: with A15, A16, A29 and A30 it dropped from about 33 in 100 traces to about 11 in 100; with A23 from about 15 to about 5 in 100 (both activities absent from the same trace in about 50 in 100 later traces versus about 29 earlier); with A24 from about 18 to about 6 in 100. - Routing shares among A21, A22, A23, A24, A29 and A30 shifted markedly. Earlier, A21 and A22 sent about half of their outgoing transitions to A30 (about 51% and 48%) and most of the rest to A24 or A23; in the later window they send only about 1–2% to A30, about 52–55% to A24 and about 38–44% to A23. ### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the existing route in which A15 hands over to A08, which then typically proceeds straight to A16, is now taken far less often, with A15 passing directly to A16 in most traces: A08 participation fell from about 33 to about 11 in 100 traces, the A15→A08 share of A15's outgoing transitions collapsed from about 17% to about 1% while A15→A16 rose from about 82% to about 97%, and A08's co-occurrence with A15, A16, A29 and A30 fell in lockstep with its participation. Thirty evidence items qualified where about 0.65 would be expected by chance alone if nothing had changed, and each item's difference exceeds about 199 of 200 random re-splits, so the pattern as a whole is unlikely to be a sampling artefact. Because both the direct and the A08-mediated routes were observed in both windows, the difference is a shift in how often existing paths are taken, not a replacement of one path by another. [OCC001, DIR002, REL001, REL002, REL005, REL006]

Limitations: the later-window picture of A08's own successors, its later trace position and its widened gap to A16 rests on roughly 20 observations and may be noise; the A08 items partly describe the same traces and are not independent confirmations; and the evidence cannot show whether A08 was skipped in favour of the direct A15→A16 route within otherwise similar cases or whether a different mix of cases reached this part of the process at all. [DIR001, POS001, LAG001]

Other differences: routing shares in the cluster A21, A22, A23, A24, A29, A30 changed consistently — A21 and A22 now rarely hand over directly to A30 (about 1–2% of their outgoing transitions versus about half earlier) and instead go to A24 or A23, A23 and A24 now go to A30 in about 95–97% of their outgoing transitions (versus about half earlier), and A29 now feeds A21 and A22 (about 98% combined) instead of A23 and A24; the lag shifts are consistent with these being frequency changes among paths that all existed in both windows, though two related lag items rest on only 2–3 later observations.
