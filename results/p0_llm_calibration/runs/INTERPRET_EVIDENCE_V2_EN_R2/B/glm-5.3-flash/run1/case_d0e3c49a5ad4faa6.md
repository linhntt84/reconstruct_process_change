### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the frequencies of two existing alternative branches were exchanged. In the earlier window, roughly 78 in 100 traces took the branch A20 → A28 followed by A25, A26 and A27 (with A32 nearby), while roughly 22 in 100 took the branch through A24 and then A21, A22, A23; in the later window the shares are roughly reversed, about 77 in 100 for the A21–A24 branch (A20 → A24, A24 before the other three, A32 about four event positions after A24) and about 24 in 100 for the A25–A28 branch. Because both branches, their internal orders and A32's position appear in both windows, this reads as a change in how often existing paths are taken, not as a replacement of one path by another. [OCC001, OCC005, DIR001, REL001, REL005, REL011, REL037, LAG001]

The pattern is unlikely to be a sampling artefact: 54 evidence items qualified in total, many times the roughly 0.79 expected by chance alone, and the participation, direction and relation items all move in the same direction with changes of about 0.55 on their own scales, each unusual in about 99.5 of 100 random re-splits. The routing change is observed directly at A20, not only inferred from frequencies. Many items describe the same trace behaviour, so they should be read as one consistent picture rather than independent confirmations. [OCC001, DIR001, REL001, LAG001]

What the evidence cannot rule out is why the shares flipped: a changed choice at A20 and a changed mix of cases arriving at A20 look identical here, and the exact shares carry sampling uncertainty in windows of 200 traces. [DIR001, OCC001]

Other differences: in the later window, A20 shows a few additional outgoing transitions to A05, A15 and A31, each accounting for about 1% or less of A20's observed outgoing transitions; they are rare and do not change the main picture. [DIR001]
