### Observed differences

- **Participation of eight activities.** In the earlier window, each of A33, A34, A36, A37, A38, A39, A40 and A41 ran in only about half of the traces (about 47–55 in 100, essentially once per trace when present). In the later window, each of them runs in 199 of 200 traces, about once per trace, with repetition still negligible (at most about 2 in 100). - **From alternatives to always together.** The eight activities form two groups of four (A33/A34/A36/A37 and A38/A39/A40/A41). In the earlier window, a cross-group pair such as A33 with A38 or A34 with A40 occurred together in only about 0.5–2 in 100 traces; about 53 in 100 traces contained only the first-group activity and about 45 in 100 only the second-group one. In the later window, such pairs occur together in about 99 in 100 traces, with the first-group activity before the second-group one in about 96–98.5 of 100. ### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the two four-activity blocks changed from mutually exclusive alternatives to a single combined sequence: in the earlier window a trace ran A30 and then either the block A33–A34–A36–A37 (about 55 in 100 traces) or the block A38–A39–A40–A41 (about 46 in 100), each followed by A31, with both blocks together in only about 1–2 in 100 traces; in the later window essentially every trace runs A30, then the A33/A34/A36/A37 block in varying order, then the A38/A39/A40/A41 block in varying order, then A31. In path-frequency terms, the combined sequence rose from about 1–2 in 100 traces to about 99 in 100, while the two single-block paths nearly disappeared. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, OCC007, OCC008, REL020, REL021, REL022, REL023, REL026, REL027, REL028, REL029, REL031, REL032, REL033, REL034, REL035, REL036, REL037, REL038, REL009, REL010, REL011, REL012, REL013, REL014, REL015, REL016, DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005]

The supporting signs are direct and consistent: 83 qualifying items against roughly 1 expected for an unchanged process; the relational items show the alternatives-together change directly with full support in both windows; the participation, successor, distance and position items all agree; every item's difference exceeds about 199 of 200 random re-splits; and the stable mixed orders inside each block rule out reading the difference as a swap or repositioning of individual activities. [REL020, REL021, REL022, REL023, REL026, REL027, REL028, REL029, REL031, REL032, REL033, REL034, REL035, REL036, REL037, REL038, DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, LAG001, LAG007, LAG010, LAG013, LAG017, REL017, REL030]

Limitations: the evidence contains no outgoing-transition profile for A38–A41, so the activity that now directly precedes A31 is not observed directly and A31's exact slot rests on the relation and distance items, some of whose earlier-window profiles rest on only 1–2 observations; and the evidence cannot distinguish a changed routing rule from a shift in the mix of case types entering the two windows. [REL013, REL014, REL015, REL016, LAG001, LAG006, LAG008, LAG012, LAG016]

Other differences: a handful of weak new successor relations appear in the later window at about 0.5–1 in 100 of outgoing transitions (for example A30 to A04, A17, A22, A27, A28 or A46, and A33 to A05, A06, A18, A21 or A50); each rests on one or two observations and may be sampling noise.
