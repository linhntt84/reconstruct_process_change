### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, A19 and A20 each took part in about half of all traces (about 50 and 51 in 100); in the later window, participation dropped to about 26 and 28 in 100 respectively. [OCC001, OCC002]
- A19 and A20 remained strict alternatives — they share a trace in about 1 in 100 cases before and never later — but traces containing neither rose from none in the earlier window to about 46 in 100 in the later window. [REL001]
- Routing out of A40 (which occurs in every trace in both windows) shifted: before, about 43% of its observed outgoing transitions went to A20 and about 40% to A19, with no transition to A25 observed; in the later window, about 40% go directly to A25, while A19 and A20 each receive about 22–24%. [DIR001, REL002]
- Co-occurrence of the branch with its surroundings roughly halved: A19 with A25 fell from about 50 to about 26 in 100 traces, A20 with A25 from about 51 to about 28, A40 with A19 from about 50 to about 26, and A40 with A20 from about 51 to about 28. Whenever A19 or A20 did run, the sequence A40 first and A25 last still held in nearly all cases in both windows. [REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005]
- The gap between A40 and A25 shortened: before, the pair was typically two events apart (about 82 in 100); in the later window it was one event apart in about 40 in 100 cases and two apart in about 51 in 100. [LAG001]
- Minor: A25 (present in every trace in both windows) sits slightly earlier in the later window (mean relative position about 0.41 versus 0.39, with a tighter spread), and the share of A43's outgoing transitions going to A44 rose from about 75% to about 89%. [POS001, DIR002]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that, in the later window, the alternative branch formed by A19 and A20 — mutually exclusive steps executed between A40 and A25 — is skipped in a large share of cases: participation in both activities roughly halved, and about 46 in 100 traces now contain neither, proceeding from A40 directly to A25, a route not observed in the earlier window. Because A19 and A20 still follow A40 and precede A25 in nearly every trace where they occur, this is a shift in how often existing routes are taken, not a reordering or relocation of activities. [OCC001, OCC002, REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, DIR001, LAG001]

Eleven items qualified in total, many times the roughly 0.5 expected by chance alone if nothing had changed, and each difference exceeds those seen in about 199 of 200 random re-splits of the pooled data. Participation, routing, co-occurrence and distance indicators all describe one and the same underlying shift consistently (they partly reflect the same traces, so they are not independent confirmations). The competing explanation that A19 and A20 merely moved elsewhere is ruled out: in the later window they occur only between A40 and A25, and in about 46 in 100 traces not at all. [OCC001, OCC002, DIR001, DIR002, POS001, REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, LAG001]

A competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that the direct A40→A25 route already existed before but was taken too rarely to appear in 200 traces; an unobserved transition does not prove an edge is absent, so the finding is best read as a large shift in path frequencies rather than a demonstrably new connection. [DIR001, REL001]

Other differences: A25's slight forward shift in position and tighter spread is consistent with the branch before it being skipped but is too small to interpret on its own, and A43's transitions concentrate further on A44 (about 75% to about 89%); neither is part of the main hypothesis. [POS001, DIR002]
