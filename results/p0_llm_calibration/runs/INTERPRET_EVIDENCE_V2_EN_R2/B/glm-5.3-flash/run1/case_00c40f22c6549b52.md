### Observed differences

- **A fixed order between two activity groups became mixed.** In the earlier window, A15, A16, A18 and A19 co-occurred with A20, A21, A22 and A23 in all 200 traces, and the early activity came first in 100 in 100 of them. In the later window co-occurrence is still universal, but the late activity now comes first in about 22–93 in 100 cases depending on the pair: A20 precedes A19 in about 92 in 100 cases (before: 0), A20 precedes A15 in about 76 in 100 (before: 0), A22 precedes A19 in about 75 in 100 (before: 0). The reversal is strongest for pairs involving A20 (about 51–93 in 100) and weakest for those involving A21 (about 22–50 in 100). [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, REL007, REL008, REL009, REL010, REL011, REL012, REL013, REL014, REL015]
- **Routing became much more branched.** Earlier, A15's observed outgoing transitions split only between A19 (about 52 in 100) and A16 (about 48 in 100); in the later window six successors appear, with about 35 in 100 to A19, 25 to A16 and 40 together to A20–A23. A19 earlier led only to A20; later about half of its outgoing transitions go to A29 and most of the rest to A21, A22 and A23. A20, A21, A22 and A23 gained outgoing edges back to A15, A16, A18 and A19 — for example about 16 in 100 of A20's outgoing transitions now go to A18, which was not observed earlier. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006, DIR007, DIR008]
- **A direct A19→A29 continuation appeared.** Earlier A19 always continued to A20 and A21 always to A29, with A19 reaching A29 five steps later in every case; in the later window about half of A19's outgoing transitions go straight to A29, and the A19→A29 distance is one step in about 50 in 100 cases. [DIR004, DIR006, LAG022, LAG027]
- **Positions and distances shifted consistently with this reordering.** The early group's mean relative position moved later (A15 from about 0.57 to 0.63, A19 from about 0.61 to 0.69 of the trace length) while the late group moved earlier (A20 from about 0.64 to 0.56, A22 and A23 from about 0.68 to 0.62), and the positional spread widened for all eight activities. Distances were redistributed: A15 is followed by A20 within five steps in 200 traces earlier but only 47 later (matching the roughly 24 in 100 order share), and A18→A20 moved from always four steps to mostly one or two. [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006, POS007, POS008, LAG003, LAG016, LAG004]
- **A28's continuation split.** Earlier A28 was always followed by A18; in the later window its outgoing transitions split about 51 in 100 to A20 and 49 to A18, and the A28→A20 distance fell from always five steps to one step in about half of the cases. [DIR009, LAG038]
- **A modest shift around A43.** A43 appears in about 139 of 200 traces earlier and 122 later; its outgoing share to A40 rose from about 34 to 51 in 100 while A44 fell from about 66 to 49, and A43→A44 distances lengthened slightly. [POS009, DIR010, LAG039]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the ordering constraints between two groups of activities changed: the fixed precedence in which A15, A16, A18 and A19 always executed before A20, A21, A22 and A23 appears to have been replaced by a mixed order in which the late-group activities — above all A20 — now frequently execute before the early-group ones, together with far more branched routing out of A19, A20, A21, A22 and A23, including a direct A19→A29 continuation that bypasses the former chain through A20 and A21. The support is direct and consistent: the relational evidence shows order shares moving from 100/0 to mixed while co-occurrence stays at 100 in 100, the successor profiles show the corresponding new edges, the distance and position shifts point the same way, 73 evidence items stand against roughly 0.64 expected for an unchanged process, and every item's difference exceeds about 199 of 200 random re-splits. [REL001, REL005, REL012, DIR001, DIR004, DIR005, LAG003, LAG022, POS001, POS005]

Limitations: with 200 traces per window, the evidence cannot rule out that these reversed orders existed as a rare variant before and merely became frequent, and the relational, directional and distance items overlap on the same traces, so they are not fully independent confirmations; directional shares also concern observed outgoing transitions only. An important competing explanation the evidence cannot fully exclude is a pure shift in how often existing paths are taken rather than a change in ordering constraints, although the uniform flip across all fifteen activity pairs and the many newly observed edges favour a genuine constraint change. [REL001, REL012, DIR005, LAG003]

Other differences: A43 occurs in fewer traces in the later window (about 122 vs 139 of 200) and its continuation shifted toward A40 (about 51 in 100, up from 34) at the expense of A44; A28 now continues to A20 about half the time instead of always to A18. These involve activities outside the main hypothesis. [POS009, DIR010, LAG039, DIR009, LAG038]
