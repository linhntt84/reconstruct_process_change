### Observed differences

- The four activities A34, A35, A36 and A37 ran in every earlier trace (participation 1.0, 200 of 200) but in only about half of the later traces: A34 and A35 in about 112 of 200, A36 and A37 in about 92–94 of 200 [OCC005, OCC006, OCC007, OCC008].
- In the later window the pairs {A34, A35} and {A36, A37} appear as mutually exclusive alternatives: before, A34/A35 co-occurred with A36 and with A37 in essentially every trace (about 98–100 in 100); afterwards such co-occurrence is only about 2–3 in 100, while A34 still always accompanies A35, and A36 always accompanies A37 [REL029, REL030, REL031, REL036, REL037, REL042].
- Successors changed accordingly: in the later window about 47% of A34's and about 45% of A35's outgoing transitions go to A31 (previously not observed), their transitions to A36/A37 nearly disappear, and A30 now leads to A36 (about 24%) and A37 (about 18%), which it did not before [DIR007, DIR008, DIR006].
- A31 now occurs much closer to its predecessors: 1–2 event positions after A34/A35 (previously 3–4) and about 3 positions after A30 (previously always 5); A30 also reaches A36/A37 within 1–2 positions (previously 3–4) [LAG009, LAG015, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004].
- A10 participation fell from about 63 in 100 traces to 15 in 100, and its position distribution widened (standard deviation about 0.32 versus 0.11); A43 now routes to A44 in about 84% of its transitions (previously 29%) and to A10 in about 3% (previously 71%); A10's own outgoing traffic shifted from A44 (about 93%) to a spread including A15, A44 and A46 (about 16–19% each) [OCC004, POS001, DIR009, DIR003].
- A10 now co-occurs with activities such as A14, A15, A30, A31, A45 and A46 in only about 10–15 in 100 later traces, down from about 62 in 100 [REL001, REL002, REL011, REL012].
- Participation of A04, A06 and A08 roughly tripled or quadrupled (for example A08 from about 7 to about 21 in 100 traces) [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003].

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the segment involving A30, A34, A35, A36, A37 and A31 was restructured so that exactly one of the pairs {A34, A35} or {A36, A37} now executes per case — each in roughly half of the later traces — instead of both running in sequence; A36/A37 are now reached from A30 rather than from A34/A35, and A31 follows A30, A34 or A35 within about 1–3 positions instead of 3–5 [OCC005, OCC006, OCC007, OCC008, REL030, REL031, REL036, REL037, REL042, DIR006, DIR007, DIR008, LAG002, LAG009, LAG015].

This rests on direct, converging signs rather than on item count: participation of all four activities halved together; within-pair co-occurrence stayed at 1.0 while between-pair co-occurrence collapsed to about 0.02–0.03, the pattern expected if one pair is now skipped; and the successor and lag profiles of A30, A34 and A35 shifted in the same direction. About 100 evidence items qualified in total, many times the roughly 1.11 expected by chance in an unchanged process, and each difference is larger than all but about 0.5% of random re-splits of the pooled data; occurrence, relation, direction and lag indicators all reflect the same underlying behaviour [REL029, DIR007, LAG002, OCC005].

Limitations: the two windows are finite samples, so a shift in the mix of pre-existing process variants could produce a similar picture and cannot be ruled out; the evidence also cannot establish whether the A10/A43 rerouting belongs to the same modification or is a separate one, and direction shares describe outgoing transitions rather than case volumes [DIR009, OCC004].
