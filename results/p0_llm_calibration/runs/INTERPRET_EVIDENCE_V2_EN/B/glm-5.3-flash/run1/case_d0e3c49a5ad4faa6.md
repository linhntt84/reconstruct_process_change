### Observed differences

- In the later window, A21, A22, A23 and A24 each appear in about 77 in 100 traces, up from about 22 in 100, while A25, A26, A27 and A28 each drop to about 24 in 100 from about 79 in 100; all eight activities still occur essentially at most once per trace (mean occurrences per trace roughly equals participation, recurrence support near zero) [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, OCC007, OCC008].
- The two activity sets remain almost mutually exclusive in both windows: before, traces typically contained A25–A28 (about 78 in 100) or A21–A24 (about 22 in 100) but almost never both (co-occurrence of A21 with A25 about 0 in 100, of A24 with A25 about 1 in 100); in the later window these proportions invert, again with co-occurrence of about 1 in 100 or less [REL012, REL019, REL025, REL030].
- The dominant successor of A20 flips: before, about 78 of 100 outgoing transitions of A20 went to A28 and about 21 to A24; in the later window about 75 go to A24 and about 22 to A28 [DIR001].
- The internal order of the running block changes accordingly: in the later window A20 precedes A21–A24 in about 74 in 100 traces, A24 precedes A21, A22 and A23 in about 76 in 100, and A21–A23 show no stable order among themselves (roughly 37–40 vs 36–38 in 100); previously A20 preceded A25–A28 in about 78 in 100, A28 preceded A25, A26 and A27 in about 77 in 100, and A25–A27 were mutually unordered [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL009, REL011, REL018, REL024, REL005, REL037, REL040, REL042, REL035].
- A32 occurs in every trace in both windows, but its companions change: co-occurrence with A25–A28 falls from about 78 in 100 to about 24, while co-occurrence with A21–A24 rises from about 22 to about 77; when A24 and A32 co-occur they stay about 4 event positions apart in about 95 in 100 cases before and essentially all cases after, a relation now covering about 154 traces instead of 43 [REL016, REL029, REL038, REL041, REL044, LAG001].

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

Fifty-four items qualified, far more than the roughly 0.8 expected by chance alone if nothing had changed, so the ensemble cannot be explained as random fluctuation [OCC001–OCC008, DIR001, REL001–REL044, LAG001]. The evidence supports the possibility that the segment running after A20, alongside A32, now usually consists of A21–A24 — A20 first, then A24, then A21, A22 and A23 in no fixed order, with A24 about four event positions before A32 — whereas it previously usually consisted of A25–A28 led by A28; in the later window A25–A28 are usually skipped and A21–A24 usually executed [DIR001, REL001, REL011, REL024, REL037, REL042, LAG001].

The support is direct and consistent: the successor flip at A20 and the co-occurrence and order profiles show the block change relationally rather than through marginal counts alone; the participation shifts are about 0.55 on a 0–1 scale for all eight activities, each backed by roughly 43–45 traces before and 154–156 after; and every item's difference exceeds those seen in about 995 of 1000 random re-splits of the pooled traces [DIR001, OCC001, OCC005, REL001, REL011, REL035].

The evidence cannot rule out that the two windows simply contain different mixes of two pre-existing execution variants, one carrying A21–A24 and one carrying A25–A28, which would produce the same participation, co-occurrence and order differences without any modification of the process itself; sampling differences in variant frequencies also remain possible for any single item, although a swing from about 22 to about 77 in 100 traces, consistent across eight activities and all their pair relations, is well beyond what the reference comparisons typically produce. The items largely describe this one underlying shift and are not independent confirmations [OCC001, OCC005, REL012, REL016].
