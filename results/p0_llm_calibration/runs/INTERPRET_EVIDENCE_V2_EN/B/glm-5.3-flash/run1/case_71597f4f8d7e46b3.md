### Observed differences

- In the later window, four activities that were not observed at all in the earlier window — A32, A33, A38 and A39 — each run in about 47–48 in 100 traces (94–96 of 200, versus 0 of 200 before), typically around the middle of the trace (median relative position about 0.5) [OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006].
- A27, previously present in every trace, now appears in about 54 in 100 (108 of 200) [OCC002].
- A26's outgoing transitions shifted: the share leading to A27 fell from about 98 in 100 to about 52 in 100, while about 46 in 100 now lead to the new activities (A39 about 14 in 100, A38 about 12, A32 about 11, A33 about 9) [DIR002].
- The new activities route mainly to one another and to A28; for example, A32's outgoing transitions go to A28 (about 33 in 100), A33 (about 30), A39 (about 20) and A38 (about 15) [DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006].
- The order relations changed accordingly: A26 now precedes the new block in about 47 in 100 traces, the block precedes A28 in about 47 in 100, A27 and the block co-occur in only about 1–2 in 100 traces, and within the block each pair occurs in both orders at similar rates (roughly 20–27 in 100 each way) [REL021, REL025, REL026, REL032, REL036, REL045].
- The spacing from A26 to A28 changed: before, about 97 in 100 co-occurrences were two steps apart; in the later window about 53 in 100 are two steps and about 45 in 100 are five steps apart, consistent with four activities now running between them [LAG002, REL020].
- A04 changed separately: it appears in about 11 in 100 later traces (22 of 200, down from 61), at a much earlier position (median relative position about 0.37 versus 0.89), now usually before A26/A28 rather than after them, and the A45→A04 transition share collapsed from about 32 in 100 to under 1 in 100; A04's own outgoing transitions, previously about 97 in 100 to A46, are now spread over many targets, and A45 now sends about 95 in 100 of its transitions to A46 (about 68 before) — though A04's later routing profile rests on only about 21 transitions [OCC001, POS001, REL001, REL003, DIR007, DIR001].

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that, in the later window, roughly 45–48 in 100 executions replace the segment A26 → A27 → A28 with A26 → a block of A32, A33, A38 and A39, run together in varying order, → A28, while the original sequence through A27 continues in about half of the executions. This is a possibility supported by the evidence, not a confirmed modification [REL020, REL021, REL025, REL026, REL032, LAG002, DIR002].

The support is direct and consistent: the placement of the block between A26 and A28, the near-exclusive split between A27 and the block, and the five-step A26→A28 spacing are all observed relationally, and the occurrence, routing, position, relation and lag indicators agree. Many qualifying items describe the same underlying trace behaviour rather than independent confirmations, but the total count (72 items against fewer than one expected by chance at these thresholds) cannot be explained as a whole by random re-splits of stable data [OCC002, OCC003, DIR002, LAG002, REL032].

The main competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is a difference in variant mix between the two samples: the block activities were absent from all 200 earlier traces, so the block could in principle be a previously unobserved or very rare path that became frequent rather than a newly introduced one — although 0 of 200 versus about 94–96 of 200 makes pure sampling variation implausible. Whether A27's reduced presence is tied to the block's appearance or is an independent shift is likewise not decidable from this evidence. A04's separate change (rarer, earlier, and reordered relative to A26/A28) is behaviourally well attested, but the detail of its new routing targets is based on few later observations and should be treated as tentative [OCC003, OCC001, POS001, DIR001, REL001].
