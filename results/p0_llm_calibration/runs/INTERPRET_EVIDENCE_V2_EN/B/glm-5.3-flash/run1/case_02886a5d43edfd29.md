### Observed differences

- In the later window, A19 and A20 — previously almost never observed (about 1 and 2 in 200 traces) — each run in roughly half of all traces: A19 in about 92 of 200 (46%) and A20 in about 111 of 200 (56%), in each case at most once per trace, at a mean relative event position of about 0.36 of the trace. [OCC001, OCC002, POS001, POS002]
- The two activities are nearly mutually exclusive in the later window: they co-occur in only about 2 in 100 traces, at least one of them is present in about 995 of 1000 traces, and neither appears in about 1 in 200; before, both were absent in about 985 of 1000 traces. [REL001]
- A40's outgoing routing shifted: before, about 90% of its observed outgoing transitions went to A25; in the later window about 53% go to A20 and about 42% to A19, with A25 down to about 0.5%. [DIR003]
- In the later window, A19's observed outgoing transitions lead to A25 about 97% of the time and A20's about 96%; the before-window successor profiles for A19 and A20 rest on only 1–2 traces and carry little weight. [DIR001, DIR002]
- Ordering changed accordingly: in the later window A40 precedes A19 in about 45% of traces and A19 precedes A25 in about 46%, while A40 precedes A20 in about 56% and A20 precedes A25 in about 55%; before, A25 appeared without either of them in about 99 or more of 100 traces. [REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005]
- The A40→A25 distance moved: before, A25 followed A40 within one step in about 90% of traces containing the pair; in the later window two steps is typical (about 93%) and one-step adjacency is almost gone (about 0.5%). [LAG001]
- A25 itself still occurs in every trace in both windows; its mean relative event position moved slightly later, from about 0.38 to about 0.40 of the trace. [POS003]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that, in the later window, an additional step now runs between A40 and A25: in roughly 45% of traces this step is A19 and in roughly 55% it is A20 (the two are almost never both executed), and it is followed by A25 — whereas before, A25 followed A40 directly in about 90% of traces. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, LAG001, DIR003]

The placement change is shown directly by relational and distance evidence rather than inferred from frequencies alone: A40→A25 adjacency fell from about 90% at one step to about 0.5% with two steps now typical, the ordering A40 before A19/A20 before A25 appears in the later window, and the routing shifts agree, with A40 now targeting A19/A20, which in turn lead to A25 about 96–97% of the time. [LAG001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, DIR001, DIR002, DIR003]

The magnitude also exceeds what sampling alone typically produces: participation rose from about 1–2 in 200 traces to about 92–111 in 200, every item's difference is larger than about 995 of 1000 random re-splits of the pooled data, and the 14 qualifying items are roughly 30 times the 0.46 expected without any change. [OCC001, OCC002, DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, POS001, POS002, POS003, REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, LAG001]

Limitations: most items describe the same underlying trace behaviour from different angles, so they are not independent confirmations; the before-window successor profiles for A19 and A20 rest on 1–2 observations; positions are event positions, so the slightly later position of A25 reflects sequence placement rather than clock time; and a small residue of the earlier behaviour persists (A40→A25 in about 0.5% of transitions and at lag 1, neither A19 nor A20 in about 1 in 200 later traces), so the new step is not universal. The main competing explanation the evidence cannot fully rule out is a sampling-driven shift in variant frequencies, though with 200 traces per window and consistent relational evidence, a pure frequency fluctuation is implausible. [DIR001, DIR002, LAG001, REL001, POS003]
