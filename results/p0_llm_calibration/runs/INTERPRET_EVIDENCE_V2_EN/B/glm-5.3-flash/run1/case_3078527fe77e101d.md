### Observed differences

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that routing around the A25/A26 segment was restructured: in the later window, about half of the executions skip the A36/A37/A42/A43 block entirely and continue A26 → A27, while the other half skip A27 and run the block after A28 and A26, exiting to A31 instead of back into A28; A25 connects directly to A28 in both patterns. This is shown directly and consistently: participation halves for A27 and the block, the routing shares of A25, A26 and the block move exactly as this rerouting predicts, co-occurrence and order relations flip coherently across dozens of activity pairs, and the affected adjacencies appear or disappear where the rerouting implies. With 96 qualifying evidence items against about 1 expected if nothing had changed, chance alone cannot explain the pattern as a whole. [OCC002, OCC003, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, DIR008, REL018, REL024, REL030, REL034, LAG002, LAG011, LAG013, LAG016]

The main competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is a shift in the mix of pre-existing trace variants rather than a modified structure — the "block-skipping" behaviour could have existed before at very low frequency and simply be absent from the earlier 200-trace sample, although a move from 100 in 100 to about 50 in 100 participation is difficult to attribute to sampling variation alone. Some lag findings also rest on very few later observations (as few as 1–3 traces), and A06's exit-profile change rests on only 17 later transitions, so those details are weaker than the core rerouting pattern. [LAG003, LAG005, LAG011, OCC001, DIR002]
