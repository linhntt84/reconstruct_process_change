### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, each of the eight activities A33, A34, A36, A37, A38, A39, A40 and A41 ran in every case (participation 100 in 100, about one occurrence per case). In the later window, each appears in only about 50 in 100 cases, with occurrences halved to about 0.5 per case [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, OCC007, OCC008].
- In the later window these eight activities form two mutually exclusive groups: pairs inside A33/A34/A36/A37 and inside A38/A39/A40/A41 still co-occur in about 50 in 100 cases (with neither present in about 48–49 in 100), while cross-group pairs such as A33–A38 or A34–A41 co-occur in only about 2 in 100 cases or fewer, one group appearing alone in about 49 in 100 cases each [REL017, REL030, REL039, REL020, REL026, REL029].
- In the earlier window all eight co-occurred in every case (100 in 100), with the first four consistently before the last four (about 97–99 in 100); in the later window the two groups essentially never share a case [REL020, REL023, REL029, REL001].
- A30 and A31 are unchanged in frequency: A30 still occurs in every case and precedes whichever activities run (A30 before A33 in about 49.5 of the 51 in 100 co-occurrences), and A31 still occurs in every case and follows them; when a block activity is absent, A30 or A31 simply appears without it [REL001, REL002, REL009, REL010].
- Successor mixes moved accordingly: in the later window A30's outgoing transitions spread over all eight block activities (roughly 9–15 in 100 each) instead of concentrating on A34, A36 and A37, and A33, A34, A36 and A37 now lead almost only to each other or to A31 [DIR001, DIR002, DIR004, DIR005].
- Step distances shrank: A30 to a second-group activity sat 5 steps away in essentially every co-occurrence before, and 1–4 steps away in the later window (e.g., A30→A40: 100 in 100 at 5 steps before; about 28, 30, 20 and 17 in 100 at 1, 2, 3 and 4 steps after); first-group activities to A31 show the same collapse from 5 steps to 1–4 [LAG001, LAG003, LAG005, LAG008, LAG017].
- Relative event positions shifted consistently with shorter cases (e.g., A34 mean position 0.65 to 0.74, A40 0.77 to 0.72); these are event ranks, not clock times [POS001, POS003, POS007].
- Several lag items on cross-group pairs rest on only 1–2 after-window observations and carry little weight [LAG006, LAG010, LAG012].

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the eight-activity block A33, A34, A36, A37, A38, A39, A40, A41 — which in the earlier window executed in full in every case, between A30 before it and A31 after it — is in the later window executed as essentially one of two halves per case, either A33/A34/A36/A37 or A38/A39/A40/A41 in roughly a 50/50 split, with A30 and A31 unchanged around the executed half [REL020, REL023, REL026, REL029, REL001, REL009].

The support is direct and consistent: 87 qualifying evidence items against about 1 expected by chance among the 207 tested units, and the relational items show the exclusivity relation itself (within-half pairs together in about 50 in 100 cases, cross-half pairs in almost none), with the participation, successor and step-distance items all matching this one behavioural difference rather than unrelated shifts [REL017, REL030, REL039, DIR001, LAG001, OCC001].

Limitations remain: the many items largely describe one underlying difference and are not independent confirmations; the evidence cannot distinguish a newly introduced exclusive choice from a shift in the shares of pre-existing execution variants, and finite 200-case windows can produce variant-frequency differences without any change — although a joint swing from 100 in 100 full-block participation to a 50/50 exclusive split across eight activities is unlikely to be a sampling artefact alone; the lag items based on 1–2 after-window observations add nothing reliable [LAG006, LAG012, REL020].
