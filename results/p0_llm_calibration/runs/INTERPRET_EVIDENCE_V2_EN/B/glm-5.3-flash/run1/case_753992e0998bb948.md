### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the flow was restructured so that the A36/A37/A42/A43 group became an unconditional step, performed in every case and placed between the A14/A25 segment and the A26/A28/A27/A31 tail: A25, which previously passed almost directly to A28, now feeds into the group, and A26 now leads almost exclusively to A27, which the group now precedes and A31 follows. A02 additionally behaves as a more frequent, early activity inserted after A14 and leading mainly to A15. [REL026, REL030, REL031, REL032, REL025, DIR003, DIR004, OCC003, OCC004, OCC001, POS001, DIR001]

The hypothesis rests on directly observed relational and positional change rather than participation alone: order reversals and co-occurrence shares, successor-profile changes, a step-distance change and position shifts all point the same way, and the defining after-window relations (the group before A26/A28, the group co-occurring with A27) were essentially absent earlier, so they are not explained by frequency shifts of already-known variants. The volume of qualifying items (82) is also roughly 80 times the about 1 expected if nothing had changed. [REL026, REL032, REL036, DIR003, DIR004, LAG004, POS008, OCC003]

Limitations: several items re-describe one underlying trace change (for example the four order flips between A26 and A36/A37/A42/A43), so they are not independent confirmations; positions and lags use event positions, not clock time; and the A02 pattern rests on fewer traces (about 16 before vs 55 after) with very thin before-window support in the lag data. A competing explanation that cannot be fully ruled out is a shift in the mix of pre-existing trace variants — a rare after-window variant displacing the common before-window ones — although the near-complete absence of the new orderings in the earlier window makes pure sampling fluctuation an unlikely account. [REL026, REL027, REL028, REL029, OCC001, LAG001, LAG002]
