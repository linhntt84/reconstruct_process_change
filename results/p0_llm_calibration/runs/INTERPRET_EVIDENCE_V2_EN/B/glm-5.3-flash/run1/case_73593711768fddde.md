### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that, in the later window, roughly half of the executions run a new segment between A21 and A23 in which the pair A27 and A28 (in either order) takes the place of A22: about 21 in 100 cases follow A21→A27→A28→A23, about 34 in 100 follow A21→A28→A27→A23, and the remaining about 45 in 100 keep the original A21→A22→A23 path. The support is direct and relational rather than merely a frequency shift: A21's successors redistributed from A22 alone to A22, A27 and A28; both new activities precede A23; A22 and the new pair are strictly mutually exclusive; and the A21→A23 distance moves from always 2 positions to 2 or 3, exactly what a two-activity replacement implies. The 19 flagged items far exceed the roughly 0.32 expected by chance at these thresholds, and the key items rest on large samples (109 versus 0 traces for the new activities). [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, REL007, REL008, REL009, LAG001, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005]

The main competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is a difference in the mix of existing variants between the two finite samples (200 traces each) rather than a structural modification, since variant frequencies can differ between windows without any change — although a behaviour present in about 55% of after-window traces being entirely absent from 200 before-window traces is hard to attribute to sampling alone. The participation shift between A18 and A19 (about 59/41 to about 40/60) is a further behavioural change, but no relational or positional evidence links it to the A21–A23 segment, so its structural relationship to the rest remains open.
