### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the segment between A14/A42 and A27, previously covered by exactly one of A19 or A20 in almost every case, is now covered by A25 and A26 running together in almost every case, in either order, before A27. The redirection is shown directly by relational and routing evidence rather than only by frequency counts: A42's outgoing transitions moved from A19/A20 to A25/A26 with roughly 200 outgoing transitions behind each window, A14-before-A19/A20 was replaced by A14-before-A25/A26, and A19/A20-before-A27 was replaced by A25/A26-before-A27, while A25/A26 took up the trace position A19/A20 used to occupy and the A42→A27 step gap widened by about one step. With 43 qualifying items against roughly 0.65 expected for an unchanged process, the pattern as a whole is unlikely to arise from sampling variation alone. [DIR008, REL005, REL006, REL007, REL008, REL012, REL013, REL016, REL017, REL019, REL020, REL021, REL022, OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, REL009, REL018, POS003, POS004, POS005, LAG004]

Limitations: the evidence cannot distinguish a genuine re-routing from a relabelling of the same work under new activity names, since behaviourally the segment is simply executed by different labels. The later-window behaviour of A19 and A20 themselves rests on only 1 and 4 traces, so the items describing their new routing, position and step gaps are weak and were not relied upon. Sampling differences in variant frequencies between 200-trace windows remain possible in principle, but a variant moving from 0 of 200 to 198 of 200 — and the reverse for A19/A20 — is far outside what random window-to-window variation typically produces. Several items describe the same underlying trace behaviour and are not independent confirmations. [DIR004, DIR005, POS001, POS002, LAG002, LAG003, OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004]
