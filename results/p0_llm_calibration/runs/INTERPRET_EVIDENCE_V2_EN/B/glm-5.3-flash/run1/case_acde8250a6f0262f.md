### Observed differences

- **Order became uniform.** In the earlier window, the relative order of A15/A16/A18/A19 versus A20/A21/A22/A23 varied from case to case: A19 preceded A20 in only about 9 in 100 traces, A15 preceded A20 in about 3 in 10, while A18 preceded A22 in about 3 in 4. In the later window, all thirteen tested pairs run in one direction — the first-group activity precedes the second-group activity in 100 in 100 traces — while every pair still co-occurs in all traces in both windows. [REL001, REL004, REL008, REL010, REL013]

- **Pairwise distances became deterministic.** In the later window, A18 reaches A19 exactly 3 positions later in every trace (before: spread over 3–5 positions), A19 reaches A29 exactly 5 positions later (before: about 84 in 100 at distance 1–3), and A28 reaches A19 exactly 4 positions later in all 200 traces (before: within five steps in only about 32 of 200 traces). A15/A16 and A22/A23 are now always immediately adjacent, in either order (about 102 vs 98 traces each). [LAG015, LAG022, LAG037, LAG001, LAG007, LAG029, LAG032]

- **Routing collapsed into a tight chain.** In the later window, A19, A21 and A28 each show a single observed successor (A20, A29 and A18 respectively), and A15, A16, A18, A20, A22 and A23 each show exactly two successors split roughly 50/50. In the earlier window, A19 and A21 each had five observed successors (shares from about 5% to 55%) and A28 two (about 54%/46%). [DIR001, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006, DIR009]

- **Position shifts with unchanged participation.** A15, A16, A18 and A19 now occur earlier in the trace (mean relative position, e.g., A19 from about 0.69 to 0.62, A15 from 0.62 to 0.58), while A20–A23 occur later (A20 from about 0.56 to 0.64). Each of these activities still appears in all traces in both windows, so the difference is placement, not participation. [POS001, POS004, POS005, POS008]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that in the later window these activities execute in a fixed sequence — A28, A18, then A15 and A16 adjacent in either order, then A19, then A20, then A22 and A23 adjacent in either order, then A21, then A29 — whereas in the earlier window the same activities appeared with case-by-case variable ordering and much broader successor sets. This is an order/placement hypothesis, and it rests on direct relational evidence: all 13 activity pairs fixed to a single order (e.g., A19 before A20 rising from about 9 in 100 to 100 in 100 traces) with co-occurrence unchanged, and the lag distributions collapsed to single distances that are mutually consistent with one chain (A18→A19 always at 3, A19→A29 always at 5, A21→A29 always at 1). The routing concentration and the position shifts point the same way, and with 68 qualifying items against roughly 0.58 expected if nothing had changed, the pattern as a whole is not what random re-splits of a stable process produce. [REL001, REL010, LAG015, LAG022, LAG027, DIR004, POS004]

The main competing explanation is a sampling difference in the mix of pre-existing variants: the earlier window could have drawn more traces with other orderings while the later window drew mostly from a fixed-order variant, without any structural change. The complete uniformity of the later window (every tested pair at 100% one order, deterministic distances across all 200 traces) makes this an unlikely fit, but with 200 traces per window it cannot be fully excluded. The evidence also cannot show whether the earlier variability reflected several stable variants or within-case randomness, and the position-based indicators say nothing about clock time. [REL001, LAG003, POS001]
