### Observed differences

- The main difference is a swap between two mutually exclusive branches. In the earlier window, 157 of 200 traces (about 79 in 100) contained A23 followed by A20, A21 and A22, while only 43 of 200 (about 22 in 100) contained A19 followed by A16, A17 and A18; in the later window the shares are reversed, with A16–A19 present in 154 of 200 traces (about 77 in 100) and A20–A23 in only 46 of 200 (about 23 in 100). None of these eight activities recurs within a trace in either window. [OCC001-OCC008]
- The routing out of A15 flipped accordingly: before, about 78 in 100 of A15's observed outgoing transitions led to A23 and about 22 to A19; in the later window, about 77 lead to A19 and about 23 to A23. A15 itself appears in every trace in both windows. [DIR001]
- A15's relations mirror this: it co-occurs with A16, A17, A18 and A19 in about 77% of later traces (always with A15 first) versus about 22% earlier, while its co-occurrence with A20–A23 fell from about 78% to about 23%. [REL001-REL008]
- The branch structure itself is unchanged: A16 and A20 never co-occur in either window, A19 still precedes A16, A17 and A18 whenever they run, and A23 still precedes A20, A21 and A22; only the order among the three trailing activities within each branch remains mixed in both windows. [REL011, REL012, REL018, REL024, REL037, REL040, REL042, REL009, REL035]
- A27 appears in every trace in both windows and simply follows the shift: it co-occurs with A16 in about 77% of later traces (A16 first in about 41 in 100, interleaved or repeated in about 36 in 100) against about 22 in 100 earlier. [REL016]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the choice after A15 now favours the other branch: in the later window, roughly 77 in 100 cases continue from A15 to A19 and then A16, A17 and A18, whereas before only about 22 in 100 did, while the path via A23 and then A20, A21 and A22 dropped from about 78 in 100 to about 23 in 100. [DIR001, OCC001, OCC005, REL001, REL005, REL011, REL037]

The signs are direct and consistent: the routing change is observed at A15 itself rather than inferred from participation counts alone, the participation, co-occurrence and ordering items all describe the same swap with matching magnitudes, and the branches stay mutually exclusive, so this reads as a change in branch shares rather than new parallel behaviour. [DIR001, OCC001-OCC008, REL001-REL008, REL012]

On volume, 53 items qualified in total against roughly 0.67 expected if nothing had changed, and each item's difference exceeded about 99.5 in 100 random re-splits of the pooled traces; a count many times that expectation is not what an unchanged process typically produces. [OCC001-OCC008, DIR001, REL001-REL044]

Limitations: the two windows are finite samples of 200 traces each, so sampling differences in variant frequencies remain a competing explanation in principle — the observed swing (43 versus 154 of 200 traces on the A19-led branch) is far larger than random re-splits produce, but it cannot be excluded with certainty. The evidence also shows which branch runs more often, not what governs that choice, and the order within each branch is variable, so the hypothesis concerns branch shares rather than a fixed internal sequence. [OCC001, OCC005, REL009, REL035]
