### Observed differences

- Participation is stable: A20, A21, A23, A24, A27 and A28 appear in essentially every trace in both windows (support 200 of 200; co-occurrence 100%), so the differences below are about order and routing, not about activities appearing or disappearing [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, REL001].
- Routing out of A20 changed sharply: before, every observed outgoing transition from A20 went to A21; in the later window, about half go to A27 and about half to A28, with A21 chosen in only about 2 in 100 transitions [DIR001].
- Routing out of A23 changed in the opposite direction: before, its transitions split between A27 (about 54 in 100) and A28 (about 46 in 100); in the later window, about 99 in 100 go directly to A24, with A27 and A28 almost unobserved as successors [DIR002].
- A24 nearly vanished as a successor of A27 and A28: before, each of them led to A24 in roughly half of its transitions; in the later window, A24 follows them in about 1 in 100 or fewer transitions, while A21 appears as their successor in about half [DIR003, DIR004].
- The order of activities flipped: before, A21 preceded both A27 and A28, and A23 preceded both, in 100% of traces; in the later window, A27 and A28 precede A21 and A23 in about 99 in 100 traces, while the pairs still co-occur in every trace [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004].
- Step distances moved consistently (event positions, not clock time): A20→A21 from always 1 step to usually 3; A20→A23 from 3 to 5; A20→A27 and A20→A28 from 4–5 steps to 1–2; A21→A24 from 5 to 3; A23→A24 from 3 to 1; A27/A28→A24 from 1–2 to 4–5 [LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG005, LAG008, LAG011, LAG012].
- Relative positions shifted in the same directions: A21 and A23 now occur later in the trace (mean relative position about 0.43→0.50 and 0.50→0.57), while A27 and A28 occur earlier (about 0.55→0.45 each) [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004].
- The ordered pairs A21→A27, A21→A28, A23→A27 and A23→A28, which were regular before, now occur within five steps in only about 3 of 200 later-window traces; the small lag shifts reported for these four pairs rest on 3 observations and are weak on their own [LAG006, LAG007, LAG009, LAG010].

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the stretch containing A27 and A28 is now executed much earlier in each case — directly after A20 and before A21 — whereas before it ran after A23 and immediately before A24; put differently, the A27/A28 pair and the A21–A23 stretch exchanged their relative order, while A20 remained at the start and A24 at the end. This is a possibility supported by the evidence, not a confirmed modification [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG005, LAG008, LAG011, LAG012].

The support is direct and coherent: the relational items show the order reversal in about 99 in 100 traces with co-occurrence unchanged; every distance change fits one relocation (A20 to A27/A28 shrinks from 4–5 steps to 1–2, A27/A28 to A24 grows from 1–2 to 4–5, and the two-step spacing between A21 and A23 is preserved in both windows); the successor changes at A20, A23, A27 and A28 match this relocation exactly; and the position shifts agree in direction. Every item reaches empirical strength about 0.995 (its difference exceeds all but about 1 in 200 random re-splits), the main items rest on 200 traces per window, and 22 qualifying items against about 0.41 expected by chance across 82 tested units is far beyond what an unchanged process typically produces — though many of these items describe the same underlying behaviour and are not independent confirmations [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG005, LAG008, LAG011, LAG012].

The main competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that both orderings existed as variants in both windows and only their frequencies crossed: the new ordering was observed in 0 of 200 earlier-window traces and the old ordering still appears in about 2 in 100 later-window traces, so a rare variant could sit below the sampling resolution of two 200-trace windows. The four rare ordered pairs add little either way because only 3 later traces contain them, and since positions and lags are measured in event steps, no claim about clock timing is possible [REL001, LAG006, LAG007, LAG009, LAG010].
