### Observed differences

- The order of A26 relative to A32 and A33 reversed. Before, A26 came before A32 in about 99 in 100 traces and before A33 in about 99.5 in 100; in the later window, A32 preceded A26 in about 97.5 in 100 traces and A33 preceded A26 in about 97.5 in 100. Both pairs still occurred together in every trace in both windows. [REL001, REL002]
- The same reversal occurred against A28: before, A28 preceded A32 and A33 in about 99 in 100 traces; in the later window, A32 and A33 preceded A28 in about 98 in 100 traces, with co-occurrence still universal. [REL003, REL004]
- Successors changed accordingly. A25 led to A26 in about 98.5 in 100 of its observed outgoing transitions before; in the later window it leads to A32 in about 48 in 100 and A33 in about 48 in 100, with A26 almost never the direct successor (about 1.5 in 100). A28, which previously led mainly to A32 (about 53 in 100) or A33 (about 45 in 100), now leads to A29 in about 96.5 in 100. A32 and A33, which previously led to each other and on to A29, are now each followed about half the time by their partner and about half the time by A26. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004]
- Step distances moved consistently with these reroutings: A25→A26 stretched from 1 step apart (about 98.5 in 100) to 3 steps (about 95.5 in 100); A25→A32 and A25→A33 shortened from 4–5 steps to 1–2 steps; A25→A28 lengthened from 3 to 5 steps; A28→A29 tightened from 3 steps to 1 step; A26→A29 shortened from 5 to 3 steps; A32→A29 and A33→A29 lengthened from 1–2 steps to 4–5 steps. [LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG005, LAG007, LAG008, LAG009]
- Event positions shifted in matching directions: the mean relative position of A26 rose from about 0.43 to 0.50 and of A28 from about 0.50 to 0.57, while A32 fell from about 0.55 to 0.46 and A33 from about 0.55 to 0.45. [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a specific reordering hypothesis: the block formed by A32 and A33, which previously ran after A28 and immediately before A29, in the later window runs directly after A25 and before A26. The segment reads A25 → A26 → … → A28 → {A32, A33} → A29 before, and A25 → {A32, A33} → A26 → … → A28 → A29 afterwards, with A29 now immediately following A28. This is a possibility supported by the evidence, not a confirmed modification. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, DIR001, DIR002, LAG001, LAG002, LAG007]

The placement change is shown directly by relational evidence rather than inferred from position shifts alone: all four activity-pair orders reverse in about 98–99 in 100 traces, the successor profiles of A25, A28, A32 and A33 change coherently, and every step-distance moves exactly as this repositioning predicts. Participation is unchanged — each pair still co-occurs in 100 in 100 traces in both windows — so the difference concerns placement, not presence or frequency of the activities. The volume of qualifying items (19, against roughly 0.58 expected if nothing had changed) and the fact that every item's difference exceeds about 99.5 in 100 random re-splits make sampling noise an unlikely explanation for the pattern as a whole. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, LAG001, LAG003, LAG004, LAG005, LAG007, LAG008, LAG009]

Limitations: the evidence cannot distinguish a reordering within one structure from a large shift in the mix of pre-existing variants — for example, a reversed-order variant present in fewer than about 1–2 in 100 traces before becoming dominant after — since both produce the same pair statistics. The indicators also overlap on the same traces and are not independent confirmations; one lag item (A26→A33) rests on only 3 after-window observations, though it is consistent with the reversal; and POS/LAG use event positions rather than clock time, so no statement about timing is possible. [LAG006, REL001, REL002]
