- In the earlier window, A25 and A26 each ran in every one of the 200 cases (about once per case); in the later window they are almost gone, appearing in about 5 of 200 cases (A25) and about 3 of 200 (A26). [OCC003, OCC004]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the A25–A26 block, which executed between A42 and A27 in every earlier case, is replaced in the later window by exactly one activity per case — A19 in roughly half the cases and A20 in the other half — with A42 leading into it and A27 directly following. This rests on direct rather than only frequency-based signs: A42's outgoing routing moved from A25/A26 (about 94 in 100) to A19/A20 (about 91 in 100) [DIR006]; A19 and A20 route to A27 almost exclusively [DIR002, DIR003]; the order A42 → A19/A20 → A27 holds in nearly every case containing the new activities [REL004, REL005, REL008, REL009]; and the A42-to-A27 distance contracts from three events to two, consistent with one activity now sitting where two did [LAG001, LAG002]. The 31 qualifying items are many times the roughly 0.6 expected by chance in an unchanged process, so chance qualification cannot explain the pattern as a whole.

The main competing explanation the evidence cannot fully rule out is sampling: with 200-trace windows, variant frequencies can differ without any change, and a rare variant can appear in one window only. That explanation is strained here — a drop from 200 of 200 cases to about 4 of 200, together with directly observed rewiring at A42 and changed event distances, goes well beyond typical sampling variation — but it cannot be excluded outright. The residual later-window behaviour of A25 and A26 rests on only 5 and 3 traces [OCC003, OCC004, DIR004, DIR005], and the evidence shows nothing about what determines whether a case runs A19 rather than A20. Note also that most items describe this single segment change, so they are not independent confirmations of it.
