### Observed differences

- The clearest difference is execution order. In the earlier window, A20, A21, A23 and A24 came before A25, A26, A27 and A28 in almost every trace — A24 preceded A25 in all 200 traces, and A20 or A21 preceded A25/A27 in about 99 in 100. In the later window this is largely reversed: A25 precedes A24 in about 91 in 100 traces and precedes A20 or A21 in about 76 in 100, while pairs with A26, A27 and A28 are now near even (for example A26 before A24 in about 49 in 100). Both activities of every tested pair still occur together in all traces, so participation itself did not change. [REL001, REL003, REL005, REL007, REL008, REL012, REL013, REL014, REL015]
- The two groups also shifted their typical position within the trace: A20, A21, A23 and A24 now occur later (mean relative position of A24 rose from about 0.61 to 0.69, A20 from 0.57 to 0.62), while A25, A26, A27 and A28 occur earlier (A25 fell from about 0.64 to 0.56, A27 from 0.68 to 0.62). [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS007]
- Successor profiles changed sharply at the boundary activities: A24's outgoing transitions, previously all to A25, now go about 49 in 100 to A34, 17 to A26, 13 to A27, 11 to A28 and 7 to A25; A26's outgoing, previously almost all to A34, now go about half to A34 with the rest mainly to A24, A21, A20 and A23; A33's outgoing, previously almost all to A23, now split roughly half to A25 and half to A23. [DIR004, DIR006, DIR009]
- Step distances changed consistently with the new order: A24 to A34 was always 5 steps apart and is now 1 step apart in about half the cases; A33 to A25 was always 5 steps apart and is now 1 step apart in about half; A24 to A27 was 2–3 steps apart and is now 1 step apart in about half. [LAG020, LAG022, LAG039]
- More broadly, the outgoing transitions of A20, A21, A23, A25, A27, A28 and A48 were redistributed, with A20, A21 and A23 now also leading into A25–A28, and A25, A27 and A28 now sometimes leading back into A20, A21, A23 and A24. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR005, DIR007, DIR008, DIR010]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that, in the later window, the activities A25, A26, A27 and A28 — together with A34, now reached early from A24 — run before A20, A21, A23 and A24, whereas they previously ran after them. All fifteen tested pairs flip in the same direction while the two activities of each pair still co-occur in every trace, which is direct relational evidence of an order change rather than a participation change. [REL001, REL005, REL012, REL013, REL014]

The corroborating signs line up: the positional shifts move the two groups in opposite directions exactly as a reversal implies, fixed long step distances collapse to short or mixed ones, and the successor redistribution at A24, A26 and A33 matches the new sequence. With 72 qualifying items against roughly 0.84 expected for an unchanged process, chance alone cannot account for the pattern as a whole. [POS001, POS004, POS005, LAG020, LAG022, LAG039, DIR004, DIR006, DIR009]

The main competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is a shift in the mix of process variants: a variant in which A25–A28 run first may simply dominate the later sample, and two 200-trace windows can differ in variant frequencies without any structural change, although the size and uniformity of the order flips make pure sampling noise an unlikely full explanation. Some after-window distance profiles also rest on fewer observations (roughly 30 to 140 traces), so those proportions are less precise, and items from different indicators partly describe the same traces rather than independent confirmations. [REL012, LAG003, LAG026]
