### Observed differences

- In the later window, A25's outgoing transitions no longer go almost exclusively to A27 (about 97 in 100 before, about 2 in 100 after) but split roughly evenly over A35, A36, A41 and A42 (about 22–25 in 100 each). [DIR001]
- A26's routing moved the opposite way: from an even split over A35, A36, A41 and A42 (about 24–25 in 100 each) to about 94 in 100 transitions going to A30. [DIR002]
- A35, A36, A41 and A42 each lost most of their transitions to A30 (from about 21–31 in 100 down to about 1 in 100 or fewer) and gained transitions to A27 (about 17–29 in 100). [DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006]

A27 shows the same flip, moving from before these four activities (about 96–97 in 100) to after them (about 96–97 in 100). [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, REL007, REL008]

- A few further lag profiles also changed, but their later-window shares rest on only 4–5 observations and are weak on their own. [LAG003, LAG004, LAG006, LAG009, LAG010]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the placement of the activity block A35/A36/A41/A42 relative to A26 and A27 was reversed: before, the flow ran from A25 to A27, then A26, then the block, with A30 about five steps after A26; in the later window it runs from A25 into the block, which now precedes A26 and A27, and A26 leads almost directly to A30. [DIR001, DIR002, REL001, REL005, LAG001, LAG002]

The support is direct and consistent: relational items show near-complete order reversals (about 96–98 in 100 traces) for all eight block-versus-A26/A27 pairs, successor profiles change coherently at five source activities, and the position and distance shifts point the same way. The number of qualifying items (31) far exceeds the roughly 0.76 expected by chance, but the items largely describe one underlying reordering, so they should be read as one consistent picture rather than independent confirmations. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, REL007, REL008, DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006, POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006, LAG001, LAG002]

The main competing explanation is a sampling difference in variant frequencies, since a rare variant can dominate one window; order reversals of this size with 200 traces per window make that unlikely, but it cannot be excluded. In addition, several lag profiles rest on only 4–5 later-window observations and were not relied upon, and routing shares are measured over outgoing transitions, so an unobserved edge does not show that an edge is absent from the process.
