### Observed differences

- The clearest difference is a near-swap in how often two groups of activities run. A21, A22, A23 and A24 each appeared in about 83 in 100 traces before, but in only about 27–28 in 100 in the later window; conversely, A25, A26, A27 and A28 each rose from about 18 in 100 traces to about 73 in 100 [OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, OCC007, OCC008, OCC009].
- The routing out of A20, which appears in essentially every trace in both windows, flipped: before, about 82 of 100 observed outgoing transitions led to A24 and about 18 to A28; in the later window, about 27 led to A24 and about 73 to A28 [DIR002].
- The two groups behave as alternatives. Before, A20 preceded A21–A24 in about 82 in 100 traces, A21–A24 co-occurred with each other in about 82 in 100, and A21 appeared without A25 in about 82 in 100; in the later window A20 preceded A25–A28 in about 72 in 100, A25–A28 co-occurred with each other in about 73 in 100, and A21–A24 co-occurrence fell to about 27 in 100 [REL009, REL013, REL017, REL020, REL023, REL051].
- The surrounding context stayed largely stable: A36 ran in every trace at a late position in both windows, with A37 following within one event in about 84 in 100 cases before and about 98 in 100 later, and the A36→A08 transition was no longer observed. A32 kept running in most traces but switched companions, co-occurring with A21–A24 in about 83 in 100 traces before and with A25–A28 in about 73 in 100 later [POS002, LAG006, DIR003, REL024, REL054].
- A08 became rarer and behaved differently: participation fell from about 18 to about 6 in 100 traces, its typical position moved from near the end of the trace to around the middle, and its outgoing transitions shifted from mostly A37 (about 92 of 100) to a spread over several targets. These items rest on few traces (about 36 before versus 12–13 after) and are weakly supported [OCC001, POS001, DIR001, LAG001, REL001].

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the use of two alternative branches essentially swapped between the windows: the branch containing A21–A24, which followed A20 in about 82 in 100 traces before, was taken in only about 27 in 100 in the later window, while the branch containing A25–A28 rose from about 18 to about 73 in 100, with A32 accompanying whichever group dominated. The routing change is visible directly in the outgoing-transition shares of A20, and the participation, co-occurrence and ordering items all shift in the mirrored direction this hypothesis predicts [DIR002, OCC002, OCC006, REL009, REL013, REL020, REL051, REL024].

Chance is an unlikely explanation for the pattern as a whole: 88 evidence items qualified, many times the roughly 1 expected for an unchanged process, and every item's difference exceeded about 995 of 1,000 random re-splits of the pooled data. The individual swings are also large relative to the sample — for A21, about 166 of 200 traces before versus 54 of 200 after — well beyond what re-splitting an unchanged process typically produces [DIR002, OCC002].

The limitations are that the evidence shows the same two outgoing transitions from A20 in both windows, so a newly added or removed path is not demonstrated — only the branch frequencies changed — and it cannot distinguish a change in routing behaviour from a shift in the mix of case variants between the two windows, since a sampling difference in variant frequencies would look identical here. The A08-related differences rest on only about 12–13 later-window traces and should be treated as tentative [DIR002, OCC001, DIR001].
