### Observed differences

- In the later window, A32, A33 and A34 each run in only about 45–55 in 100 traces (A32 about 45, A33 about 55, A34 about 55), down from running in every trace in the earlier window; each still occurs at most once per trace, and repetition stays negligible in both windows. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003]
- In the later window, A32 and the A33+A34 pair exclude each other: A32 co-occurs with A33 in about 1 in 200 traces (before, 200 in 200) and with A34 in none (before, 200 in 200), while A33 and A34 still appear together whenever they appear at all (together in about 55 in 100 traces, both absent in about 45 in 100, in either order). [REL013, REL014, REL015]
- Routing follows this split. In the later window, about 95 in 100 of A25's observed outgoing transitions go to A26 (before, they split between A33, about 45 in 100, and A34, about 52 in 100); A28's outgoing transitions now split between A32 (about 42 in 100), A33 (about 30) and A34 (about 24), where before about 95 in 100 went to A32; and A33 and A34 now lead mainly to each other and to A29 (about 44–54 in 100) rather than to A26 (about 46–52 in 100 before). [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004]
- Placement in event positions moved with the split: A26 now follows A25 after 1 step in about 95 in 100 cases (before, mostly 3 steps) and A28 follows A25 by about 3 steps (before, 5); the A33+A34 pair moved from 1–2 steps after A25 to 4–5 steps after it, and A29 now follows the pair by 1–2 steps (before, 5). - The surrounding order is otherwise preserved: A25, A26 and A28 still precede A32, and A32 still precedes A29, in the traces containing A32; A25 still precedes the pair, the pair still precedes A29, and A29 keeps roughly its earlier distance from A26 and A28. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL007, REL010, REL011, REL012, LAG005, LAG006]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that, in the later window, A32 and the A33+A34 pair have become alternatives: about 55 in 100 traces run A33 and A34 together and do not run A32, while about 45 in 100 run A32 and do not run the pair, whereas in the earlier window every trace ran both. Alongside this, A26 now directly follows A25, and the pair now runs between A28 and A29 instead of between A25 and A26. [REL013, REL014, REL015, OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, DIR001, DIR002, LAG001, LAG007]

- The exclusivity is shown directly by relational evidence, not inferred from participation alone: co-occurrence of A32 with A33 and with A34 collapses from 200 in 200 traces to about 0, while every other order relation among A25, A26, A28, A29 and either block is preserved. [REL013, REL014, REL001, REL004, REL007, REL010, REL011, REL012]
- Participation, routing and positional indicators all describe the same reallocation: the participation drops match the co-occurrence drops almost exactly, A28's outgoing flow splits exactly along the new branch, and the lag changes place the pair immediately before A29. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, LAG007, LAG008]
- The volume of qualifying items (36, against roughly 0.6 expected if nothing had changed) and the agreement in direction across all of them make chance an implausible explanation for the pattern as a whole; each item's difference also exceeds about 199 of 200 random re-splits of the pooled data. [OCC001, DIR001, POS005, REL013, LAG001]

Limitations: all items come from the same two 200-trace windows, so agreeing items are correlated rather than independent confirmations. The evidence also cannot rule out that the later window reflects a changed mix of pre-existing process variants — for example, an either/or behaviour too rare to appear among the earlier 200 traces — rather than a modification of a single process; since A32, A33 and A34 appeared in 200 of 200 earlier traces, such a variant would have had to be very rare before, and even under that reading the frequencies of the two behaviours have shifted markedly. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, REL013, REL014]
