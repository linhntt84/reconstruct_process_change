### Observed differences

- Participation of three activities collapsed. In the earlier window A32, A33 and A34 each appeared in essentially every case (about 200 in 200); in the later window they appear in only about 46, 55 and 55 in 100 cases respectively, still at most once per case when present. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003]

- Routing around A25 and A28 changed. Before, about 98 in 100 of A25's observed outgoing transitions went to A33 or A34, and about 98 in 100 of A28's went to A32. In the later window, A25 is followed almost exclusively by A26 (about 98 in 100), while A28 splits between A32 (about 45 in 100), A33 (about 30) and A34 (about 25). [DIR001, DIR002]

- A33 and A34 no longer route to A26: in the later window their observed outgoing transitions go to each other and to A29 (about 45–55 in 100 each), whereas before roughly half of them went to A26. [DIR003, DIR004]

- Order and distance changed accordingly. Before, A33 and A34 preceded A26 and A28 in about 99 in 100 cases; in the later window, whenever both occur, A26 and A28 precede them. A25 and A26 are now adjacent (gap 1 in about 98 in 100 cases, versus gap 3 before), A33/A34 now lie about 4–5 steps after A25 (versus 1–2 before), and A29 immediately follows A33 or A34 (gap 1–2, versus about 5 before). [REL005, REL006, REL008, REL009, LAG001, LAG004, LAG005, LAG010, LAG012]

- Co-occurrence restructured. Before, A32, A33 and A34 always occurred together in the same case; in the later window A32 co-occurs with A33 in about 1 in 200 cases and with A34 in none, while A33 and A34 appear together whenever either appears (about 55 in 100 cases, with neither present in about 45 in 100 — essentially the cases in which A32 runs). [REL013, REL014, REL015, OCC001]

- Positions shifted consistently with the above: when present, A33 and A34 now sit later in the case (mean relative position about 0.43 to about 0.55), while A26 and A28 sit slightly earlier (about 0.48 to 0.43 and 0.54 to 0.50). A25, A26, A28 and A29 still appear in every case in both windows. [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS006, POS007]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that, in the later window, the A33/A34 segment was relocated from the early part of the case (between A25 and A26) to the later part (between A28 and A29) and made conditional: after A28, a case now runs either through A32 (about 45 in 100 cases) or through A33 and A34 together, in either order, both alternatives leading directly to A29 — whereas before, every case contained all of A25, A33/A34, A26, A28, A32 and A29 in a fixed arrangement. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, REL005, REL013, REL014, LAG001, LAG010]

The support is direct rather than purely frequency-based: the successor profiles of A25, A28, A33 and A34 changed (routing), the relative order of A26/A28 versus A33/A34 reversed and A32 became mutually exclusive with A33/A34 (relational evidence), and the participation, position and distance indicators all move in the direction this single rearrangement predicts. With 41 qualifying items against roughly 0.64 expected for an unchanged process, the pattern as a whole is not what random re-splits of a stable process typically produce; the items are not independent confirmations, but they coherently describe one behavioural shift. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, REL005, REL006, REL008, REL009, REL013, REL014, OCC001, POS006, LAG001, LAG010]

Limitations: the two windows are finite samples, so a shift in the mix of case types or variant frequencies between the windows cannot be ruled out entirely — although the after-window patterns (A32 without A33/A34, and A33/A34 without A32) are essentially absent from all 200 before-cases, so sampling variation alone cannot plausibly account for the difference. Several lag items also rest on very few observations (LAG003, LAG007 and LAG009 have a single supporting pair in one window, LAG011 a single one in the later window) and were not relied upon; the conclusion rests on the well-supported participation, routing, order and co-occurrence items. [LAG003, LAG007, LAG009, LAG011, OCC001, REL013]
