### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The screening flagged 90 evidence items where fewer than one (about 0.84) would be expected for an unchanged process, and each difference exceeds about 995 of 1000 random re-splits of the pooled data, so the overall volume cannot be explained by chance; the items largely describe one underlying difference rather than independent changes. The evidence supports the hypothesis that the two four-activity groups became mutually exclusive alternatives: in the later window a case executes either A28, A29, A31 and A32 or A33, A34, A35 and A36 between A25 and A26, in roughly a 50/50 split, whereas earlier every case executed both groups in sequence (first group, then second group). This is shown directly by relational evidence — cross-group co-occurrence fell from all cases to about 1 in 200, with one-sided appearances splitting about 50/50 — and is consistent across participation, routing and distance indicators. [REL020, REL031, OCC001, DIR001, LAG001]

Limitations: the split is symmetric, so the evidence cannot establish which group was newly made optional versus required, and one later-window case still contains both groups, so exclusivity may not be absolute. A competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is a changed mix of case types between the windows rather than a routing change within one process. The roughly 50/50 shares are sample estimates, although sampling differences in variant frequencies cannot plausibly explain the overall difference, since a variant present in half the cases would not be missed by the 200-case earlier window. [REL020]
