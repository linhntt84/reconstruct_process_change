### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that, in the later window, the span between A21 and A23 is executed in two ways: in roughly 47 in 100 cases the former segment A21→A22→A23 is replaced by A21, then A27, A28, A33 and A34 in varying order, then A23, while the original segment via A22 continues in the remaining cases. Participation (A22 down from all traces to about 53 in 100; the four new activities up from none to about 47 in 100), routing (A21's outgoing share split in the same proportion) and relational evidence (mutual exclusivity with A22, the four activities always co-occurring and preceding A23, and the A21→A23 distance changing from always 2 to 2 or 5) show this placement directly and consistently. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, DIR002, REL011, REL017, REL021, REL025, LAG001]

The volume of flagged behaviour far exceeds what a stable process produces here: 47 evidence items against about 0.5 expected by chance from 104 tested units, and every item's difference exceeds about 99.5 of 100 random re-splits of the pooled traces. The agreeing items nevertheless largely describe the same traces, so they form one coherent difference rather than independent confirmations. [DIR002, LAG001, REL025]

Limitations: the four new activities show no fixed internal order (both directions at roughly 20–27% per pair), so no fixed sequence within the group is established; and because they appear in 0 of 200 earlier traces, a pre-existing but rare variant — present in well under about 2 in 100 earlier cases — cannot be ruled out, so the evidence cannot distinguish a newly added route from an existing rare route that rose sharply in frequency, even though a 47% share is far beyond ordinary sampling variation between two 200-trace windows. The A06/A07 redistribution is consistent with the same change (both activities precede either branch) but the evidence cannot establish whether it belongs to it. [REL025, REL026, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, DIR001, REL002]
