### Observed differences

- The relative order of two groups of activities reversed. Before, A21 and A22 came before A23 and A27, which in turn came before A19 and A20; in the later window, A19 and A20 come first and A21 and A22 come last. For example, A21 preceded A19 in about 52 in 100 traces before, while A19 precedes A21 in about 53 in 100 traces in the later window; the same reversal holds for A19 and A20 against A22, A23 and A27 [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, REL007, REL008]. The middle segment also flipped relative to the second block: A21 preceded A23 in 200 of 200 traces before, whereas A23 precedes A21 in about 199 of 200 traces in the later window, and A22 shows the same reversal against A23 and A27 [REL009, REL010, REL011, REL012].
- Event positions shifted accordingly: A19's mean relative position fell from about 0.67 to about 0.36 and A20's from about 0.67 to about 0.37, while A21 rose from about 0.38 to about 0.65 and A22 from about 0.38 to about 0.66; A23 (about 0.43 to 0.40) and A27 (about 0.64 to 0.60) stayed roughly in place [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006].
- Outgoing routing changed at the edges of the swapped groups: A19 and A20 now lead to A23 in about 96–99 in 100 of their outgoing transitions instead of A28; A21 and A22 now lead to A28 in about 47–52 in 100 instead of A23; A27 now leads to A21 and A22 instead of A19 and A20; and A38 now leads to A19 and A20 instead of A21 and A22 [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006].
- Step distances are consistent with one group swapping past the middle: A28 now follows A27 after about 3 steps (about 96 in 100 cases) where it followed after 2 steps before (about 99 in 100), and A23 now follows A38 after about 2 steps (about 90 in 100) where it followed after 3 steps before (about 89 in 100) [LAG003, LAG004]. Two other lag items rest on only 1–2 observations in one window and carry little weight [LAG001, LAG002].
- Participation is essentially unchanged: A21, A22, A23 and A27 appear in every trace in both windows, and A19 and A20 each appear in roughly half of the traces in both windows, so the difference is reordering rather than work being added, skipped or repeated [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006].

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the block formed by A19 and A20 and the block formed by A21 and A22 exchanged places around the unchanged middle segment A23 then A27: before, the flow ran A38, then A21/A22, then A23, then A27, then A19/A20, and on to A28; in the later window it runs A38, then A19/A20, then A23, then A27, then A21/A22, and on to A28. The order changes are shown directly by relational evidence that flips in essentially every trace, and they agree with the successor changes at both ends of the swapped blocks, the opposite position shifts of the two pairs, and the step-distance changes across the middle [REL001, REL005, REL009, REL011, DIR001, DIR003, DIR005, DIR006, POS001, POS003, LAG003, LAG004]. The volume of qualifying items (28, against roughly 0.56 expected for an unchanged process) is far beyond what random re-splits of two 200-trace windows typically produce [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006, POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006, REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, REL007, REL008, REL009, REL010, REL011, REL012, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004].

This is a possibility supported by the sample, not a confirmed modification. A competing explanation the evidence cannot fully rule out is a shift in the mix of process variants between the windows, since variant frequencies can differ between finite samples without any structural change; however, a near-complete reversal within activities present in every trace (A21 before A23 in 200 of 200 traces before versus A23 before A21 in about 199 of 200 in the later window) is hard to attribute to a rare variant alone [REL009, POS003]. The lag items based on 1–2 observations were not relied upon, and the evidence says nothing about whether the reordering persists beyond these windows [LAG001, LAG002].
