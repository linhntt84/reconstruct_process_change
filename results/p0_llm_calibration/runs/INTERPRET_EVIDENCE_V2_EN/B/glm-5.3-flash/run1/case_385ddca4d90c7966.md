### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that, in the later window, a block of A32 and A33 — always executed together, in either order — sits between A26 and A28 in about 55 in 100 cases, while the previous A26 → A27 → A28 segment continues in the remaining cases with A27 at about half its former participation. The rerouting is observed directly at A26 and at the new activities' outgoing edges, and the ordering relations (A26 before A32/A33, A32/A33 before A28) hold in exactly the share of traces in which the new activities run. [DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, REL007, REL008, REL014, REL015, OCC004]

The account is consistent across occurrence, routing, positional and relational indicators, though several of them describe the same traces and are not independent confirmations; the one-step increase in the A26→A28 distance (2 to 3) matches exactly one extra activity on the path. With 38 qualifying items against fewer than 1 expected if nothing had changed, and shifts of the size of A33 going from 0 to 109 traces and A26→A27 from about 98 to 44 in 100 transitions, sampling variation cannot explain the pattern as a whole. [LAG002, OCC006, DIR002]

Limitations: the evidence does not establish whether the A04 and A43 changes belong to the same structural difference — A04's destination mix in the later window rests on only about 22 outgoing transitions, and the A23/A24 participation shifts have no relational evidence behind them, so variant-frequency sampling between the two windows remains a plausible explanation for these smaller differences. A33's absence before only means it was not observed in that window, although with 200 traces per window its later presence in 109 is unlikely to be a sampling artefact.
