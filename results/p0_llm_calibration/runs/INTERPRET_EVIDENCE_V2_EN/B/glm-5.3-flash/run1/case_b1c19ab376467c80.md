### Observed differences

- The clearest change is a reversal of execution order around A20. In the earlier window, A14 and A20 occurred together in 43 of 100 traces and A20 always came first; in the later window they occur together in 52 of 100 traces, A14 comes first in about 52 of 100, and A20 comes first in only about 1 in 200. The same flip holds for A15: A20 preceded A15 in 57 of 100 traces before, while after, A15 precedes A20 in 48 of 100 and the reverse is not observed. [REL001, REL004, REL005]
- A14 and A15 also reversed order against A21 and A22. Before, A21 preceded A14 in 43 of 100 traces; after, A14 precedes A21 in about 52 of 100. Against A22 the earlier order was mixed (A14 first in about 22 of 100 and A22 first in about 22; A15 first in about 25 and A22 first in about 33), whereas after, A14/A15 precede A22 in roughly half of all traces and the reverse order has nearly disappeared. In the later window, A35 is followed by A14 in about 52 of 100 outgoing transitions and by A15 in about 48, where before it was always followed by A20; A14 and A15, which previously led to A22/A23 (about 50/50 for A14; 43/57 for A15), now lead to A20 in essentially all observed transitions; and A21 and A22, which previously split their outgoing transitions across A14/A15/A22 and A23/A15/A14, now lead almost exclusively to A22 and to A23 respectively. - Distances moved in the same direction: A35 now reaches A14/A15 in 1 step instead of 5, A14/A15 reach A22 in 5 steps instead of 1, A35 reaches A20 in 2 steps instead of 1, A35 reaches A21 in 5 steps instead of 4, and A21→A23 tightened from 3 steps to 2, with A21→A22 and A22→A23 now uniformly 1 step. - A small, separate routing shift concerns A31/A32: A31→A32 rose from about 42 to 63 of 100 outgoing transitions, and A32→A26 likewise from about 42 to 63. [DIR005, DIR006]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the alternative block formed by A14 and A15 was repositioned: in the later window, one of A14 or A15 runs directly after A35 and before A20, whereas in the earlier window it ran later in the trace, after A20 and A21, around A22 (which then led to A23). This is shown directly by the order reversals, the successor changes at A35, A14, A15, A21 and A22, and the lag shifts from 1 to 5 steps and 5 to 1 step, with the positional shifts agreeing; the picture is consistent across all four indicator families, the items rest on roughly 43 to 200 traces each, 31 items qualified against about 0.42 expected by chance, and every item's difference exceeds about 995 of 1000 random re-splits of the pooled data. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR007, POS001, POS002, REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, LAG001, LAG003, LAG005, LAG007, LAG008, LAG009, LAG010, LAG011, LAG012, LAG013]

Limitations: the evidence cannot distinguish an in-place repositioning from a wholesale replacement of the dominant variant — in the earlier window A14 preceded A20 in 0 of 200 traces and A35 was followed by A20 at lag 1 in all 200 traces, so a rare pre-existing variant with the new order that simply took over cannot be ruled out, and sampling differences in variant frequencies remain a competing explanation. The after-profiles of the vanished pairs rest on one observation each, and the small A31/A32 routing shift is not part of this reordering. [REL001, LAG002, LAG004, LAG006, LAG012, DIR005, DIR006]
