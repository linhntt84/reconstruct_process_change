### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the segment between A25 and A26 changed from running one of two alternative groups of four activities to running both groups in sequence: in the later window, cases appear to execute A28, A29, A31 and A32 in varying order, then A33, A34, A35 and A36 in varying order, between A25 at the start and A26 at the end. The support is direct and consistent: cross-group pairs that never co-occurred earlier (0 of 200 cases) co-occur in about 99 in 100 later cases with the first group always ahead, participation of all eight activities rose from about half of cases to essentially all, the routing into and out of the segment changed in line with this, and the distance-5 measurements match a fixed four-activity block between the anchors. With 74 flagged items against roughly 0.78 expected by chance across the 157 tested units, and every difference larger than in about 995 of 1000 random re-splits, chance alone cannot explain the pattern as a whole. [REL020, REL026, REL031, REL035, OCC001, OCC005, DIR001, DIR002, LAG001, LAG005]

Limitations remain: the many relation items largely restate one underlying flip and are not independent confirmations. The evidence also cannot rule out that both behaviours already existed and only their frequencies shifted — a case containing both groups appeared 0 times in 200 earlier traces, so such cases could have existed at up to roughly 1–2 in 100 cases before, and a wholesale shift in the mix of pre-existing variants would look identical here. Unobserved transitions (such as A28 to A26 in the later window) do not prove those edges are gone, and the lag items rest on only 43–56 later cases each. [REL020, DIR002, LAG001]
