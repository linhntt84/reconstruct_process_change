### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, the four activities A19, A20, A25 and A26 each ran in roughly a quarter of the traces (about 48–56 of 200 traces each, participation 24–28 in 100), and pairs of them almost never co-occurred — together they covered essentially every trace, almost always as exactly one of the four. In the later window they are near-absent: A25 was not observed at all, A26 in 1 of 200 traces, A19 and A20 in about 2 each (roughly 1 in 100). [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, REL001, REL010]

- Routing into this block collapsed. Earlier, about 93 of 100 of A42's outgoing transitions led to one of the four (roughly evenly: A19 26, A20 22, A25 22, A26 22 in 100), and A42 essentially never led directly to A27. In the later window, about 92 of 100 of A42's outgoing transitions go straight to A27, and only about 2 in 100 still reach one of the four; this rests on all outgoing transitions in both windows (200 vs 201). [DIR004]

- Routing out of the block also disappeared: earlier, A19, A20, A25 and A26 each led almost always to A27 (94–98 of 100 of their outgoing transitions); later, A25 and A26 have no observed outgoing transitions, and A20's two observed transitions split between A27 and A15. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003]

- The block's embedding between A42 and A27 is visible relationally: earlier, A42 came before the block activity and the block activity before A27 in about 24–28 of 100 traces for each of the four (e.g., A19 with A27 in 28 of 100 earlier traces, about 1 in 100 later, with A27 now appearing alone in nearly every trace). [REL004, REL005, REL008, REL009, REL011, REL012, REL013, REL014]

- Pairs within the block confirm the joint disappearance: pairs such as A19–A20 or A25–A26 showed one of the two in about half of the earlier traces and neither in the other half; later, neither member appears in about 99 of 100 traces. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL006, REL007, REL010]

- The distance from A42 to A27 shortened: earlier, A27 typically followed A42 two positions later (93 of 100 cases); later, typically one position later (91 of 100). [LAG002]

- The remaining positional and gap differences (A19 and A20 appearing slightly earlier; A26's single later occurrence at the first position; the A20–A27 gap seen only twice) each rest on one or two later observations. [POS001, POS002, POS004, LAG001]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that, in the later window, the choice step formed by A19, A20, A25 and A26 — which in the earlier window sat between A42 and A27, with exactly one of the four executed in essentially every trace — is skipped in nearly all executions: A42 now passes almost immediately to A27, and each of the four appears in only about 1–2 of 100 later traces, A25 not at all. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, DIR004, LAG002, REL004]

The supporting signs are direct and consistent across occurrence, routing, relational and lag indicators: the rerouting at A42 and the shortened A42-to-A27 distance rest on all 200 traces per window, the earlier block was common (48–56 of 200 traces per activity, so it was not a rare variant of the earlier window), and the roughly 28 qualifying evidence items far exceed the about 0.55 expected by chance if nothing had changed. [DIR004, LAG002, OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004]

Limitations remain: the evidence cannot rule out that both behaviours always existed and only the mix of trace variants shifted between the windows, which finite samples cannot separate from a structural change. The block still fires occasionally (A19 and A20 in about 1–2 of 100 later traces), so the data show it is skipped in most cases rather than certainly removed, and thinly supported later details such as A20's outgoing split towards A15 (two transitions) or A26's single occurrence at the first position should not be read as new routes or placements. [DIR001, POS004, OCC001, OCC002]
