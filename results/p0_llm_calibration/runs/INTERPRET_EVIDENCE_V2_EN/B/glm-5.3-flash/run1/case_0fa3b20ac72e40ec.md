### Observed differences

- Before, A20 was followed by A22 in essentially all of its observed outgoing transitions (100%); in the later window A22 accounts for only about 2 in 100, while A30, A31, A36 and A37 each take roughly a quarter (about 22–26 in 100). [DIR001]
- Before, A21's outgoing transitions were spread almost evenly over A30, A31, A36 and A37 (about 24–26 in 100 each); in the later window about 98 in 100 go directly to A25. [DIR002]
- Before, each of A30, A31, A36 and A37 led to A25 in roughly 21–32 in 100 of its outgoing transitions; in the later window A25 has nearly disappeared as a successor (about 1 in 100 or less) and A22 has appeared as a successor (about 19–30 in 100). [DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006]
- The pairwise order flipped between A21 (and likewise A22) and the four activities A30/A31/A36/A37: before, A21 and A22 preceded all four in every co-occurrence; in the later window they precede them in only about 2 in 100, while the four come first in about 98 in 100. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, REL007, REL008]
- Step distances changed accordingly: A20→A22 went from immediate (distance 1 in all cases) to about 5 steps apart (98 in 100), and A21→A25 went from 5 steps apart (all cases) to immediate (98 in 100). [LAG001, LAG002]
- Relative positions moved in the same directions: A30, A31, A36 and A37 each occur earlier (mean relative position about 0.58 before versus about 0.41 in the later window), while A21 (about 0.51 to 0.62) and A22 (about 0.36 to 0.48) occur later. [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006]
- Distances within the four-activity group and towards A25/A31 shifted only modestly, and several of these profiles rest on few after-window observations (4 to 112 traces). [LAG003, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006, LAG007, LAG008]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the group of four activities A30, A31, A36 and A37 — executed in varying internal order — now runs at a different place in the sequence: previously it ran after A21 and immediately before A25, whereas in the later window it runs directly after A20 and before A22, with A22 and A21 now following the group and A21 leading straight to A25. [REL001, REL005, DIR001, DIR002, LAG001, LAG002]

The reading rests on direct relational and positional evidence, not on marginal frequencies alone: the co-occurrence order between A21/A22 and the four activities flipped from 100% to about 2% in samples of about 200 traces per window; the successor changes at A20, at A21 and at the four activities match exactly what such a reordering implies; and the step-distance changes (a gap of 1 becoming 5, and a gap of 5 becoming 1) fit four activities now sitting between A20 and A22. Every difference is larger than in about 99.5 in 100 random re-splits of the pooled data, and the flips hold in essentially all traces of both windows, so a rare variant appearing in one window is an unlikely explanation. [REL001, DIR001, DIR002, LAG001, LAG002, POS001]

Limitations: the evidence establishes the swap in relative order but cannot tell whether the four activities moved earlier or A22 and A21 moved later — the two readings are observationally equivalent — and what lies between A22 and A21 in the later window is not covered. [REL001, REL005, POS001, POS003] Occurrence counts per trace are not part of the evidence, so a change in how often the four activities repeat cannot be checked directly; four distance profiles (A21–A36, A21–A37, A36–A25, A37–A25) rest on only 4 after-window traces and were not relied on, although their thin after-samples are themselves consistent with these pairs now being more than five steps apart. [LAG003, LAG004, LAG005, LAG007] Several items describe the same underlying behaviour (for example, the four relation items for A21 all capture one flip), so agreeing items are not independent confirmations; and while sampling differences in variant frequencies between finite windows cannot be fully excluded, flips of this size across essentially all 200 traces per window are hard to attribute to sampling alone. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004]
