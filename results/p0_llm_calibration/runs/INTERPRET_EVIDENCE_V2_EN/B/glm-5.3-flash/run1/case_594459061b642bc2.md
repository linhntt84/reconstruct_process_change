### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the routing around A27 and the four-activity group A36/A37/A42/A43 was restructured: in the later window, a case appears to run A25, then A28, then A26, and then either the group A36/A37/A42/A43 or A27 before A31, with each alternative taken in about half of the cases — whereas before, every case ran the group between A25 and A28 and A27 between A26 and A31. The placement claim rests directly on relational and positional evidence (the order between the group and A26/A28 reversed, the A25→A28 gap collapsed from five steps to one, and the group's successors changed from A28 to A31), and the choice claim on the near-zero co-occurrence between A27 and the group combined with A26's split outgoing traffic. [REL006, REL007, REL012, REL013, DIR001, DIR002, DIR004, LAG001, LAG006, POS002, POS004]

Chance is difficult to sustain as an explanation: 63 evidence items qualified against roughly 1 expected for an unchanged process (0.83 over 166 tested units), every item's difference was larger than in about 995 of 1000 random re-splits, and sampling variation cannot plausibly turn a pair that co-occurred in 100 of 100 traces into one that co-occurs in 1–2 of 100 while both activities retain about 50 in 100 participation. [REL013, REL014, REL015, REL016]

Limitations remain: the evidence cannot rule out that the two windows draw on different variant mixes or populations whose boundary coincides with the split point, and it cannot show what determines which alternative a case takes, since no case attributes are available; a few lag items rest on only 1–2 observations in one window and are weak on their own; and many items describe the same underlying trace behaviour, so they are not independent confirmations. [LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG005, LAG008, LAG010, LAG011, LAG012]
