### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that, in the later window, roughly half of the executions take an alternative mid-process route: after A14, A15 or A26, the flow passes through the newly observed block A32–A33–A38–A39 and continues into A28 and A47/A48, running in place of the previously universal A27, whose participation fell from all traces to about 54 in 100 and which almost never appears together with the block. A second, related shift is that the earlier A47→A04→A48 routing is largely replaced by a direct A47→A48 handover, leaving A04 rare (about 17 in 100 traces) and early-positioned. [OCC006, OCC007, DIR007, DIR009, DIR013, REL013, REL028, REL034, REL041, LAG005]

This judgement rests on direct relational and positional evidence — the block sits at mid-trace positions, is preceded by A14/A15/A26 and precedes A28/A47/A48 in the order relations, and the successor profiles of A26, A04 and A47 shifted accordingly — and on volume: 86 qualifying items against fewer than 1 expected by chance in an unchanged process, so the pattern as a whole is unlikely to be a screening artefact. [POS003, DIR007, DIR009, REL023, REL041, DIR013]

Limitations: the two windows are finite samples, so variant frequencies can differ between them without any change and a rare variant can appear in one window only; the block's appearance in about 95 of 200 later traces after 200 earlier traces without it makes pure sampling noise implausible but cannot exclude a shift in the mix of pre-existing variants. Many items describe the same underlying traces (the block's relations with A14, A15 and A26 are near-identical) and are therefore not independent confirmations, and zero participation in the earlier window means only that A32/A33/A38/A39 were not observed there. [OCC007, REL013, REL018, REL023]
