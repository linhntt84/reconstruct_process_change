### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that routing through the A16–A19 block into A25 was restructured into a fixed sequence: in the later window, cases appear to run A24 → A16 or A17 → A18 or A19 → A25, whereas before, A25 directly followed each of A16, A17, A18 and A19 in about half of their outgoing transitions and A24 spread its flow evenly across all four. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006, LAG007, LAG008]

The change is shown directly at two levels: successor shares moved from roughly even splits to near-exclusive choices (for example, A25 fell from about 52 to about 2 in 100 of A16's outgoing transitions while rising to about 98 in 100 for A18), and the positional distances changed in exactly the pattern this chain implies, which a change in how often activities occur alone would not produce. Thirteen items qualified where only about 0.35 would be expected if nothing had changed, and every difference exceeded about 199 of 200 random re-splits of the pooled data, so chance alone cannot account for the pattern as a whole. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006, LAG007, LAG008]

Limitations remain: the profiles are aggregate, so a large shift in the mix of pre-existing execution variants — for instance a variant reaching A25 directly from A16/A17 becoming rare — could mimic this redistribution without any edge being added or removed, and sampling differences between two 200-trace windows can shift variant frequencies. The DIR and LAG items partly describe the same trace behaviour and are not independent confirmations, the aggregate shares cannot establish which of A18/A19 follows A16 versus A17, and near-zero shares over observed transitions do not prove an edge is absent from the process. [DIR001, DIR003, LAG001, LAG003]
