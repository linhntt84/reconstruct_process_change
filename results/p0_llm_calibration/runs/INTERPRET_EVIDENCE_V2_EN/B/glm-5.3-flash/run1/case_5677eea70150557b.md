### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the process was restructured in two connected ways: a repetition involving A30 and A31 was removed, and A06 is now skipped in most cases where it previously ran. In the later window, A30 and A31 each execute once per case in a fixed order (A30 immediately before A31 in about 97 in 100 traces, with the A31→A30 hand-over no longer observed), so the block A14, A19, A20, A30, A31, A38, A43, A44 — whose members still always co-occur — follows one strict order in about 97–99 in 100 traces instead of being interleaved in about 37 in 100. A06, which previously ran in about 63 in 100 cases between the A14/A15 steps and the A19/A20 steps, now runs in about 20 in 100, its remaining occurrences take different successors and sit later in the case, while A02, A04 and A10 are executed more often than before [OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, DIR001, DIR005, LAG012, POS001, REL010, REL016].

This rests on the scale and coherence of the pattern rather than on item count alone: 58 items qualify where fewer than 1 (0.77) would be expected by chance across the 155 tested units, each difference exceeds those seen in 199 of 200 random re-splits, and the participation, routing, repetition and ordering evidence point to the same few underlying shifts. The evidence cannot rule out competing explanations: many items restate the same trace behaviour (the nine A06 pairs and the block-ordering pairs overlap heavily, so they are not independent confirmations), several lag results rest on very few later-window observations (for example 1–6), and the two windows may simply sample different mixes of pre-existing variants — for example a shift in the share of cases that skip A06 or repeat A30/A31 — rather than reflecting a modified process model [OCC003, OCC005, LAG013, LAG014, REL001, REL010].
