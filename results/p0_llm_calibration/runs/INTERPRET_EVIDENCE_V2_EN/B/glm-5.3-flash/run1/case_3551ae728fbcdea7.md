- In the later window, each of the eight activities A33, A34, A36, A37, A38, A39, A40 and A41 appears in only about 50 in 100 traces, whereas before each appeared in essentially every trace (100 in 100); mean occurrences per trace halved from about 1.0 to about 0.5 [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, OCC007, OCC008].

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that, in the later window, each case executes only one of two alternative activity blocks between A30 and A31 — either {A33, A34, A36, A37} or {A38, A39, A40, A41}, in a roughly 50/50 split — whereas before, every case ran both blocks in sequence after A30 and before A31 [REL020, REL021, REL026, REL031, REL035, OCC001, OCC005, DIR001, LAG001, LAG005].

The support is direct and consistent: participation of all eight block activities halved in step; co-occurrence and mixed ordering within each block persisted while cross-block co-occurrence collapsed from 100 in 100 to about 1–2 in 100 traces; A30 and A31 retained their precedence roles; new direct edges appeared from A30 into the second block and from the first block to A31; and lag distances compressed in the way expected when one block is removed from each trace. The volume of qualifying evidence (91 items against about 1 expected without any change) also exceeds what chance screening would produce [OCC001, OCC005, REL017, REL020, REL030, REL001, REL009, DIR001, DIR005, LAG001, LAG005].

Limitations: the evidence cannot show whether the choice of block is random per case or driven by case attributes, and it cannot rule out that this exclusive-choice behaviour already existed before as a rare variant that the two windows sampled in different proportions; the coordinated halving of eight activities together with the cross-block co-occurrence collapse makes pure sampling variation unlikely, but the finite windows leave this competing explanation open [OCC001, OCC008, REL020, REL021].
