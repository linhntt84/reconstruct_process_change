### Observed differences

- In the later window, A14 and A15 appear in essentially every trace (199 of 200, about 100 in 100) versus about 48 in 100 before; neither activity repeats within a trace, so mean occurrences per trace rose identically, from about 0.48 to about 1.0. [OCC001, OCC002]
- A20 and A21 each roughly doubled: A20 from about 24 to 51 in 100 traces, A21 from about 24 to 49 in 100. The two never share a trace in either window, so in the later window almost every trace contains exactly one of them, whereas before they appeared only within the minority of traces that also contained A14 and A15. [OCC003, OCC004, REL002, REL003, REL010]
- Routing out of A37 was redistributed: before, the next activity after A37 was A22 in about 52 in 100 observed transitions and A14 in about 48 in 100; in the later window it is A14 in about 99.5 in 100 and A22 in only about 0.5 in 100. [DIR001]
- The event distance from A37 to A22 changed accordingly: before, A22 immediately followed A37 in about 52 in 100 traces and sat four positions later in about 48 in 100; in the later window the four-position gap holds in about 99.5 in 100 traces. [LAG001]
- In the later window the full chain holds in essentially every trace: A37 before A14, A14 before A15, A14 before A20 and A21, A20 and A21 before A15, and A15 (and A20/A21) before A22. Before, each of these relations was observed only in the roughly 24 to 48 in 100 traces that took the longer route. - A22's mean relative position moved slightly later (about 0.40 to 0.43 of the trace) and A37's slightly earlier (about 0.33 to 0.31); both are small shifts consistent with more activities now sitting between A37 and A22. [POS001, POS002]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a specific rerouting hypothesis: in the later window, the direct hop from A37 to A22, previously taken in about half of the cases, has all but disappeared, and virtually every case runs the full sequence A37 → A14 → exactly one of A20 or A21 → A15 → A22. Before, that sequence appeared in about 48 in 100 traces and the direct A37–A22 hop in about 52 in 100; in the later window the split is about 99.5 to 0.5 in 100. [DIR001, LAG001, REL004, REL005, REL010]

The hypothesis rests on direct relational and positional evidence, not on participation counts alone: A37's outgoing transitions shifted from a near-even split to almost exclusively A14, and the A37-to-A22 event distance moved from immediate to four positions apart — exactly the distance produced when A14, one of A20/A21, and A15 sit between them. Participation, ordering and distance indicators all describe the same rerouting consistently, and the 21 evidence items are many times the roughly 0.39 items expected by chance across the 79 tested units, so the ensemble is not what an unchanged process typically produces — although the many relation items largely restate one underlying rerouting rather than constituting independent confirmations. [DIR001, LAG001, OCC001, OCC003, REL001, REL010]

Limitations remain: the direct A37–A22 transition is still observed in about 0.5 in 100 later traces, so that route is rare rather than strictly absent; all position and distance statements concern event order, not clock time; and the evidence cannot fully rule out a shift in the mix of two pre-existing route variants rather than a structural modification — with 200 traces per window a swing from about 52 to about 99.5 in 100 is far beyond ordinary sampling noise, but the reference comparisons only show the difference is unusual for random re-splits, not that the structure itself changed. [DIR001, LAG001, POS001, POS002]
