### Observed differences

- The placement of two activity groups reversed between the windows. Before, A14 and A15 each ran in about half the traces (54 and 47 in 100) at an early mean relative position of about 0.36, while A16 and A17 ran in every trace at a late position of about 0.66; in the later window, A16 and A17 run in every trace at about 0.37, and A14/A15 run in about half the traces (48 and 53 in 100) at about 0.66–0.67. [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004]
- Pairwise order relations flipped almost completely. Before, A14 and A15 preceded A16, A17, A18 and A22 in every trace containing both (e.g., A14 before A16 in 54 in 100, the reverse in 0), and A18 and A22 preceded A16 and A17 in all 200 traces; in the later window, A16/A17 precede A14/A15, A18 and A22 in roughly 47–53 of 100 traces (matching how often A14/A15 occur at all), with the earlier order remaining in only about 1 in 200 traces. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, REL007, REL008, REL009, REL010, REL011, REL012]
- Routing moved with the relations. In the later window, A14 sends about 99% of its transitions to A23 (before: all to A18) and A15 sends all to A23 (before: all to A18); A22 routes about 52% to A15 and 47% to A14 (before: about 52% A16, 48% A17); A33 routes about 52% to A17 and 47% to A16 (before: about 54% A14, 47% A15); A16 and A17 now send about 52% and 47% of their transitions to A18 instead of to A23. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, DIR007]
- Step distances match: the A33→A18 gap moved from 2 steps in all traces to 3 steps in about 99.5 in 100, and A22→A23 from 3 steps in all traces to 2 steps in about 99.5 in 100; the ordered pairs A16→A23, A17→A23, A22→A16 and A22→A17, present in all 200 earlier traces, appear in only 1 of 200 later traces. [LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006]
- A18 and A22 kept nearly the same positions (mean about 0.40 to 0.42 and 0.60 to 0.64), so their surroundings changed rather than their own placement. [POS005, POS006]
- A secondary difference: observed outgoing transitions from A32 fell from about 179 to 132, and its share going to A33 rose from about 56% to 74% (A31 fell from 44% to 26%). [DIR006]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the sequential pair A16–A17, which still runs in every trace in either order, and the either/or branch A14/A15, of which exactly one runs per trace, exchanged their places around the stable A18 … A22 segment: before, the flow ran A33 → A14 or A15 → A18 → … → A22 → A16 and A17 → A23; in the later window, it runs A33 → A16 and A17 → A18 → … → A22 → A14 or A15 → A23. [REL009, REL010, REL011, REL012, POS001, POS003, DIR001, DIR002, DIR005, DIR007, LAG005, LAG006]

Supporting signs: 31 evidence items were flagged against fewer than one expected by chance across the 76 tested units, far beyond what an unchanged process typically produces; the reversal is shown directly by relational, positional, distance and successor evidence that all describe the same restructuring, with all 200 traces behind most profiles and every item at the top of its reference comparison (about 199 of 200 random re-splits show smaller differences). The many relation items describe one underlying reversal rather than independent confirmations, but the conclusion does not depend on counting them separately. [REL001, REL009, POS003, DIR001, LAG005]

Limitations: the evidence cannot distinguish a rewired control flow from an unchanged model in which the order/branch selection between these groups flipped for essentially all cases. Sampling differences in variant frequencies between two 200-trace windows are possible in principle, though a flip from 0 of 200 to 199 of 200 traces is not what random re-splits typically produce. The A32 shift and the flip in the A14/A15 preference (54/47 to 48/53 in 100) are not explained by the swap hypothesis, and the after-profiles of the four vanished ordered pairs rest on a single trace each. [DIR006, POS001, POS002, LAG001, REL009]
