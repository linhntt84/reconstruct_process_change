### Observed differences

- **Repetition of a four-activity block.** Before, A25, A26, A31 and A32 each occurred exactly once in every case (mean 1.0 occurrence per trace, present in 100% of traces, no repetitions). In the later window, each occurs about 3.1 times per case, still in every case, with repeated occurrences in about 69 in 100 cases. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004]
- **Routing out of A32 changed.** Before, all 200 observed outgoing transitions of A32 went to A20. In the later window, about 68 in 100 of A32's roughly 620 observed outgoing transitions go to A25 and about 32 in 100 go to A20 — roughly one exit to A20 per case. [DIR001]
- **Order within the block loosened.** Before, the four activities always appeared once each in a fixed order: A25 first (ahead of A26, A31 and A32 in every case), A32 last, with A26 versus A31 in either order (about 52/48). In the later window, about 69 in 100 cases show these pairs interleaved or repeated, while the strict single-pass order survives in about 31 in 100 cases. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006]
- **Positions spread out.** The relative positions of all four activities became far more dispersed in the later window: the standard deviation roughly doubled (from about 0.06 to about 0.13) and the 75th percentile moved later by 0.06–0.09, while medians shifted only slightly. [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004]
- **Gaps between the activities lengthened.** Before, consecutive occurrences of these pairs were always one or two events apart. In the later window the one- and two-step shares collapsed (for A26 followed by A31, from every observed pair one step apart to about 13 in 100), gaps of three to five steps now appear, and both directions between A26 and A31 occur in most cases (about 170 and 165 of 200, versus 104 and 96 before). [LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that, in the later window, the block A25 → A26/A31 → A32 is repeated within cases: A32, which previously always moved on to A20, now returns to A25 in about 68 in 100 of its executions, so that in roughly 69 in 100 cases the four activities run about three times each before the case continues to A20, while about 31 in 100 cases still make a single pass in the original order. None of the four activities is skipped — participation remains at 100%. [DIR001, OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, REL001]

The support is direct and quantitatively coherent: the routing change at A32 is observed on the transitions themselves (about 420 of 620 now going to A25, with roughly one exit to A20 per case, matching the 3.1 occurrences per case), the repetition of all four activities is observed directly, and the order, position and gap shifts match — the share of cases keeping the strict single-pass order (about 31.5 in 100) equals the share without repeats. With 21 qualifying items against roughly 0.4 expected if nothing had changed, chance alone cannot account for the evidence as a whole, and each item's difference exceeds those seen in about 199 of 200 random re-splits of the pooled traces. [DIR001, OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006]

Limitations: the occurrence, position, relation and gap items largely re-describe the same underlying repetition, so they are not independent confirmations; the evidence cannot show whether the number of repeats is fixed or varies from case to case, and it cannot distinguish one modified process from a later window that mixes a repeating variant with the original single-pass behaviour. Sampling differences in variant frequencies are an unlikely explanation for a pattern present in about 69 in 100 of 200 later traces but absent from 200 earlier ones, yet the windows remain finite samples. [OCC001, DIR001, REL001, LAG003]
