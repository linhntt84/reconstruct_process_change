### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the block formed by A30, A31, A36 and A37 — previously executed once per trace in a largely fixed order — now runs as a repeated, interleaved cycle in which each of the four activities occurs about three times per trace, with A37 returning to A30 between passes before a final exit to A25; A15 and A16 remain in every trace but sit relatively earlier against the longer, repeated tail. Supporting signs: the repetition increase covers essentially all 200 traces in both windows [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004]; the dissolution of the fixed order and of the one-step successions is consistent across every pair of these activities [REL009, REL012, REL014, LAG004, LAG006, LAG008]; and A37's rerouting from A25 towards A30 matches a return into the block [DIR004]. With 39 qualifying items against roughly 0.68 expected by chance across the 136 tested units, the pattern as a whole is not what an unchanged process typically produces [OCC001, DIR004, REL009, LAG006].

Limitations: the occurrence, relation, lag and position items describe overlapping aspects of the same trace behaviour, so their agreement is not independent confirmation. The evidence also cannot rule out that the process already contained a heavily repeating variant that was rare in the earlier sample and dominant in the later one — a shift in variant frequencies between two finite samples of 200 traces can look identical to a structural modification. Position results refer to event positions rather than clock time, and the A10 routing change rests on fewer observations (57 versus 37 outgoing transitions), so it is treated as secondary.
