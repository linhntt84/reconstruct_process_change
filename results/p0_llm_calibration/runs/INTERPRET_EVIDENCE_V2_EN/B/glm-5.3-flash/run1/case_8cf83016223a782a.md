### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, A14 and A15 never appear in any of the 200 traces; in the later window, A14 occurs in about 44 in 100 traces (88 of 200) and A15 in about 56 in 100 traces (111 of 200), in each case at most once per trace [OCC001, OCC002].
- In the later window, A14 and A15 are mutually exclusive: about 44 in 100 traces contain A14 only, about 56 in 100 contain A15 only, about 1 in 200 contains neither, and the two never occur together [REL001].
- Before, A35 was always followed directly by A20, one step apart in all 200 traces; in the later window, A35's observed outgoing transitions go to A15 in about 56 in 100 cases and to A14 in about 44 in 100, with a direct transition to A20 remaining in only about 1 in 200, and the A35-to-A20 distance is now two steps in about 199 of 200 traces [DIR003, LAG001].
- In the later window, all observed outgoing transitions of A14 and of A15 lead to A20 [DIR001, DIR002].
- The later-window ordering forms a consistent chain: A35 precedes A14 in about 44 in 100 traces and A15 in about 56 in 100, while A14 precedes A20 in about 44 in 100 and A15 precedes A20 in about 56 in 100; before, A20 always appeared without either of these activities [REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005].
- When present, A14 and A15 sit at roughly 36% of the trace's event positions (medians about 0.35) in the later window; no positional data exists for them before, as they did not occur [POS001, POS002].

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that, in the later window, a new step is executed between A35 and A20: A35 is now followed by either A14 (about 44 in 100 traces) or A15 (about 56 in 100 traces), and that activity is in turn followed by A20, so the previously direct A35-to-A20 transition survives in only about 1 in 200 traces. The support is direct and consistent: A35's routing shifted from entirely A20 to mostly A14 and A15, A14 and A15 lead only to A20, the ordering runs A35 before A14/A15 before A20, the two new activities never co-occur, and the A35-to-A20 distance moved from one step to two. The number of qualifying items (13) is many times the roughly 0.33 expected without a change, so chance alone cannot explain the set as a whole, although most items describe the same underlying difference rather than independent confirmations [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, LAG001, REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005].

The main competing explanation the evidence cannot fully rule out is sampling: the windows are finite samples and a variant can appear in one window only, though a behaviour present in roughly half of the later traces being missed in all 200 earlier traces makes pure frequency fluctuation implausible. The evidence also shows where the new step sits but not what determines the choice between A14 and A15, and the routing shares are computed over observed outgoing transitions rather than over cases [OCC001, OCC002, REL001].
