### Observed differences

- In the later window, A30, A31, A36 and A37 each occur about once per trace on average, down from about 3.3 times per trace earlier. All four still appear in every trace in both windows (participation 100 in 100), but the share of traces in which an activity repeats falls from about 67 in 100 to essentially zero (at most about 3 in 200). [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004]
- Ordering becomes fixed: earlier, A30 came before A31, A36 or A37 in only about 33 in 100 traces, with about 67 in 100 showing interleaved or repeated occurrences; in the later window A30 precedes each of the other three in about 97–99 in 100 traces, and A31 and A36 each precede A37 in about 99 in 100. A31 and A36 now appear in either order (about 56 vs 44 in 100), whereas earlier their relative order was mostly interleaved. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006]
- The four activities become adjacent: in the later window A31 follows A30 within one or two events in about 99 in 100 traces, A36 follows A31 within one event in about 98 in 100 of the 112 later traces where that pair occurs within five steps, and A31 follows A36 within one event in all 88 such traces. Earlier, the gaps were spread over one to five events with a visible tail at five. [LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006]
- Routing out of A37 changes: earlier about 70 in 100 of A37's outgoing transitions went to A30 and about 30 in 100 to A25; in the later window about 97 in 100 go to A25 and no A37-to-A30 transitions are observed. These are shares of outgoing transitions (668 earlier, 203 later), not of cases. [DIR001]
- The four activities now fall in a much narrower band of event positions, with the spread roughly halving (for example A30's standard deviation from about 0.14 to about 0.07) while average positions stay around 40–50% of the trace; these are event positions, not clock times. [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004]
- The later-window profiles for the pairs starting from A37 (A37→A31, A37→A36, A37→A37) rest on a single observation each and are too weak to interpret. [LAG007, LAG008, LAG009]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the block formed by A30, A31, A36 and A37, which earlier executed about three times per case in interleaved and repeated order, now executes about once per case as a fixed sequence — A30 first, then A31 and A36 in either order, then A37 — after which flow proceeds to A25 instead of returning to A30. The repetition drop is direct (mean occurrences about 3.3 down to about 1.0 per trace with participation unchanged at 100 in 100), the ordering change is shown directly by the relation and lag indicators for all six pairs, and the redirection of A37's outgoing flow from A30 to A25 fits the same picture, since the earlier repeats of A37 mostly fed back into A30. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006, DIR001]

The volume of flagged evidence also exceeds what an unchanged process typically produces: 24 items against roughly 0.55 expected by chance across the 110 tested units, and each item's difference is larger than all but about 1 in 200 random re-splits of the pooled traces. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, DIR001, POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006, LAG007, LAG008, LAG009]

An important competing explanation remains: a shift in which case variants enter the log rather than a modification of the process itself — the earlier window contains a heavily repeated variant in about 67 in 100 traces alongside a single-pass one, and the later window is almost entirely single-pass, a mix shift that finite-window sampling of variant frequencies produces in principle, though a swing of this size is hard to attribute to sampling alone. The items also all describe one underlying trace behaviour and are therefore not independent confirmations, and the after-profiles for pairs starting from A37 rest on a single observation each.
