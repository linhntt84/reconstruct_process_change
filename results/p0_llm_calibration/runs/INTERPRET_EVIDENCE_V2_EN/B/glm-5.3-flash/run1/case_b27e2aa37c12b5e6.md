### Observed differences

- The relative order of two groups of activities reversed. Before, each of A20, A21, A23 and A24 preceded A27 and A31 in about 98–100 of 100 traces; in the later window, A27 and A31 preceded each of them in about 97–100 of 100 traces. [REL010, REL011, REL015, REL016, REL023, REL024, REL027, REL028]
- The same reversal involves the rarer activities A19, A22, A25 and A26, each appearing in roughly 22–31 of 100 traces and only alongside the A20/A21/A23/A24 group. Before, A27 and A31 preceded them (for example, A27 before A25 in about 22 of 100) and A20–A24 preceded them (A20 before A22 in about 26 of 100); in the later window these relations are inverted (A25 before A27 in about 21 of 100; A22 before A23 in about 31 of 100, up from about 1). [REL001, REL005, REL007, REL013, REL017, REL021, REL029, REL031]
- Event positions shifted accordingly: the median position of A20, A21, A23 and A24 moved from about 0.39 to about 0.64 of the trace, that of A19, A22, A25 and A26 from about 0.69 to about 0.33, A27 from about 0.46 to 0.36, and A31 from about 0.66 to 0.57. Participation hardly changed: A20, A21, A23, A24, A27 and A31 still appear in every trace, and A19, A22, A25, A26 in about 22–31 of 100. [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006, POS007, POS008, POS009, POS010]
- Routing at the segment boundaries flipped. A42's outgoing transitions moved from A20/A21/A23/A24 (about 21–25 in 100 each) to A19/A22/A25/A26 (about 20–30 in 100 each), while A31's moved the opposite way. A19, A22, A25 and A26 led to A32 in about 96–100 of 100 outgoing transitions before and to A27 in about 95–98 of 100 after; A20, A21, A23 and A24 led to A27 in about 21–29 of 100 before and to A32 in about 21–27 of 100 after. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006, DIR007, DIR008, DIR009, DIR010]
- Distances between consecutive events changed consistently with this: A31 reached A32 within about 2 steps in 99 of 100 traces before and within about 5 steps in 99 of 100 after; A42 reached A27 within about 5 steps in 99 of 100 before and within about 2 steps in 91 of 100 after. Several other lag items rest on only 1–2 observations and carry little weight. [LAG007, LAG013]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that two segments exchanged their places around the unchanged A27 → A31 sequence: before, the run containing A20, A21, A23 and A24 sat between A42 and A27, and the occurrence of whichever of A19, A22, A25 or A26 was present sat between A31 and A32; in the later window these placements are exchanged, with A19/A22/A25/A26 following A42 and preceding A27, and the A20/A21/A23/A24 run following A31 and preceding A32. [DIR009, DIR010, REL010, REL011, LAG007, LAG013]

The support is direct and consistent: all 32 measured activity pairs between the two groups reversed in the same direction, the positional shifts match, the successor profiles of A42, A31 and the segment exits reroute exactly as the reversal implies, and the two well-supported distance changes fit; participation is essentially unchanged, so this is a reordering rather than activities appearing or disappearing. With 65 qualifying items against roughly 1 expected by chance in an unchanged process, the ensemble is unlikely under "no change". [REL010, REL017, REL024, REL031, POS002, POS008, DIR002, DIR009, LAG007, LAG013]

Limitations: the items are not independent, since the direction, relation and lag indicators largely describe the same traces. For pairs involving the rarer activities, which co-occur with the other segment in only about 22–31 of 100 traces, a sampling swing in variant frequencies remains a competing explanation, although it cannot account for the near-deterministic reversals in pairs that co-occur in every trace together with the rerouting and distance changes. The evidence also establishes only that the relative order reversed; it cannot tell which of the two segments moved. [REL007, REL010, DIR002, LAG007]
