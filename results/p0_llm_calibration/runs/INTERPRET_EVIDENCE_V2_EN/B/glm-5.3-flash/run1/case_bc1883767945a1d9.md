### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, every trace executed exactly one of four activities between A37 and A22: A14 in about 27 in 100 cases, A15 in about 25, A20 in about 23 and A21 in about 25, and no trace contained more than one of the four [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, REL001, REL010].
- These four activities always sat in that slot: A37 preceded each of them and each preceded A22 in every trace where they occurred, and the only observed outgoing transitions of A20 and A21 led to A22 [REL004, REL005, REL008, REL009, REL011, REL012, REL013, REL014, DIR001, DIR002].
- In the later window, this intermediate step has almost disappeared: A20 and A21 appear in none of the 200 traces, A14 in 2 traces (about 1 in 100) and A15 in 1 trace (about 0.5 in 100) [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004].
- Routing out of A37 changed accordingly: its outgoing transitions, previously split almost evenly across A14, A15, A20 and A21 (23–27% each), now go to A22 in about 98.5 in 100 cases, with only about 1 in 100 to A14 and 0.5 in 100 to A15 [DIR003].
- The distance from A37 to A22 shrank from consistently 2 event positions (all 200 traces) to 1 position in about 197 of 200 traces, i.e. A22 now immediately follows A37 [LAG001].
- A22 itself is unchanged in participation (present in every trace in both windows); its mean relative position moved only slightly earlier, from about 0.39 to about 0.38 of the trace [POS005].

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that, in the later window, the intermediate step between A37 and A22 — previously an exclusive choice of one of A14, A15, A20 or A21 in roughly equal shares — is skipped in almost all cases, so that A37 is followed directly by A22. The support is direct and consistent across indicator types: A37's outgoing transitions rerouted to A22, the A37-to-A22 distance collapsed from two positions to one, co-occurrence of each of the four activities with A22 fell from about 23–27 in 100 cases to about 0–1, and participation of the four activities fell to zero or near zero [DIR003, LAG001, REL004, REL008, REL011, REL013, OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004]. The volume of evidence (27 items against roughly 0.38 expected by chance in an unchanged process) makes a chance explanation of the pattern as a whole implausible, and each individual difference is also larger than in about 199 of 200 random re-splits of the pooled data.

Limitations remain: the items are not independent, since most restate the same underlying trace behaviour, and the two windows are finite samples in which variant frequencies can differ without any change. The evidence also cannot distinguish a removed branch from one whose execution probability dropped sharply — the later window still shows the old behaviour in about 1–2 in 100 cases (A14 twice, A15 once), and the absence of A20 and A21 only means they were not observed, not that they have left the process [OCC003, OCC004, DIR003].
