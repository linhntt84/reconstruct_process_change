### Observed differences

- In the later window, the relative order of two groups of activities became near-deterministic. Before, A20, A21, A23 and A24 preceded A25, A26, A27 and A28 in only a pair-dependent minority or half of the traces (for example, A24 preceded A25 in about 9 in 100 traces, A20 preceded A25 in about 29 in 100, A23 preceded A27 in about 76 in 100). In the later window, the first group precedes the second in about 97–99 in 100 traces for all thirteen measured pairs, while the activities still occur together in essentially every trace in both windows. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, REL007, REL008, REL009, REL010, REL011, REL012, REL013]
- In the later window, A20, A21, A23 and A24 occur earlier within the trace (mean relative position fell, e.g., A24 from about 0.69 to 0.61 and A20 from 0.62 to 0.58), while A25, A26, A27 and A28 occur later (e.g., A25 rose from about 0.56 to 0.64 and A27 from 0.63 to 0.68). [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006, POS007, POS008]
- Outgoing routing from these activities became concentrated: A24 now leads almost only to A25 (about 99 in 100 of its observed outgoing transitions, versus about 9 in 100 before, while the previous roughly 45-in-100 share to A34 is no longer observed); A26 leads only to A34; A20 and A21 each lead almost only to one another and to A24 (about 50/50) after previously spreading over six to eight successors; A25, and likewise A27 and A28, lead almost only within this pair set; A33 leads almost only to A23 (about 99 in 100, versus about 54 in 100 before). [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006, DIR007, DIR008]
- Gaps between specific pairs collapsed to single values in the later window: A20 and A21 are now always one event apart (about 55–60 in 100 before), A24 always follows A23 by exactly 3 events and A25 by 4, A26 always follows A25 by 3, A34 always immediately follows A26, A28 always immediately follows A27, and A25 always follows A33 by 5 events (previously mostly 1–3). [LAG001, LAG007, LAG015, LAG016, LAG023, LAG027, LAG029, LAG042]
- A few distance items rest on very few observations — the four A28→A20/A21/A23/A24 pairs on a single later-window trace, and several others on fewer than about 65 before-window traces — so they are not relied on here. [LAG031, LAG032, LAG033, LAG034, LAG003, LAG009, LAG020, LAG021, LAG026, LAG041]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that, in the later window, the activities A20, A21, A23 and A24 execute in a fixed order ahead of A25, A26, A27 and A28, replacing the previously mixed, pair-dependent ordering, with the hand-offs concentrated into a few fixed transitions (A24→A25, A25→A27/A28, A26→A34, A20/A21↔A24, A33→A23) occurring at largely fixed step distances. [REL010, REL001, POS004, POS005, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, LAG015, LAG016]

This rests on direct relational evidence: the order shares between the groups flipped while co-occurrence stayed at 1.0, so the activities did not appear or disappear — their relative order changed. The positional, routing and distance indicators all shift in the same direction, the 71 qualifying items far exceed the roughly 1 expected for an unchanged process, and every item's difference exceeds that seen in about 199 of 200 random re-splits of the pooled data. [REL001, REL010, POS004, POS005, DIR001, DIR003, LAG001]

Limitations: the items most likely describe one underlying change in how these activities are ordered rather than independent changes, so their number should not be read as separate confirmations. The evidence cannot rule out that the two windows simply sample different mixes of pre-existing execution variants — a fixed-order behaviour may have existed before and merely dominated the later sample — and a few distance items rest on only one or two observations. [REL002, LAG031, LAG032, LAG033, LAG034]
