### Observed differences

- The four activities around the branch point after A20 swapped roles. In the later window, A21 and A22 each appear in only about 22 in 100 traces (down from about 78 in 100), while A23 and A24 each appear in about 79–80 in 100 traces (up from about 22 in 100). [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004]
- In the later window, the routing out of A20 is reversed: about 79% of A20's observed outgoing transitions go to A23 and about 21% to A21, where before about 78% went to A21 and 22% to A23; a rare new edge from A20 to A05 (about 1 in 200 transitions) also appears. [DIR001]
- The old branch's relations dissolved: A20–A21 and A20–A22 co-occurrence fell from about 78 in 100 traces to about 21–22, with A20 now usually appearing without them; A21 and A22 now usually both absent (neither present in about 78 in 100 traces, up from 22); and A28 now occurs without A21/A22 in about 78 in 100 traces instead of with them. [REL001, REL002, REL005, REL008, REL011]
- The new branch's relations formed: A20 now co-occurs with A23 and with A24 in about 79 in 100 traces (up from about 22), preceding each in about 78 in 100; A23 and A24 co-occur in about 79 in 100 traces (up from 22) with A23 before A24 in about 78 in 100; A23 and A24 each now co-occur with A28 in about 79 in 100 traces and typically appear without A21/A22. [REL003, REL004, REL006, REL007, REL009, REL010, REL012, REL013, REL014]
- Routing inside the branches barely moved: A21 still leads to A22 in about 95% of its outgoing transitions (about 99% before), A23 still leads to A24 in about 99% (about 98% before), and A24 leads to A28 in 100% (about 98% before), with only rare new edges (about 1–2% each) from A21 to A12/A20 and from A23 to A14. [DIR002, DIR003, DIR004]
- A28 itself appears in essentially every trace in both windows; what changed is which activities accompany it. [REL008, REL013]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that, in the later window, the branch choice after A20 flipped: cases that previously mostly continued A20 → A21 → A22 (with A28 tied to that branch) now mostly continue A20 → A23 → A24 → A28, leaving the A21/A22 branch executed in about a fifth of cases and the A23/A24 branch in about four fifths. [DIR001, OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, REL003, REL012]

- The change is shown directly at the branch point: A20's successor distribution reversed from about 78% A21 / 22% A23 to about 21% A21 / 79% A23, based on roughly 200 outgoing transitions in each window. [DIR001]
- Participation moved consistently for all four activities with substantial support on both sides (A21 present in 156 of 200 traces before versus 44 of 200 after; A23 in 45 versus 159). [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004]
- Relational and order evidence matches the swap: the old branch's precedence and co-occurrence relations collapsed while the new branch's formed, including A20 before A23/A24 and A23 before A24. [REL001, REL005, REL008, REL003, REL012, REL013, REL014]
- Routing within each branch stayed essentially unchanged, which locates the difference at the branch point after A20 rather than inside the branches. [DIR002, DIR003, DIR004]
- The 22 qualifying items are many times the roughly 0.45 expected by chance across the 91 tested units, so the pattern as a whole is not what an unchanged process typically produces, although many pair items restate one underlying swap rather than independent confirmations. [OCC001, DIR001, REL001]

Limitations: the evidence cannot distinguish a changed routing rule at A20 from a shift in the mix of two pre-existing variants (one containing the A21/A22 branch, one containing the A23/A24 branch) — with 200 traces per window, variant frequencies can differ between windows, and a case-mix shift would look behaviourally identical. [DIR001, OCC001, OCC003] A21 and A22 are still observed in about 21–22 in 100 later traces, so their low participation does not mean they were removed, and the order evidence is based on event positions rather than clock time. [OCC001, OCC002, REL001]
