### Observed differences

- In the later window, A32, A33 and A34 each run in every case (200 of 200 traces, about once per case), whereas in the earlier window each appeared in only about half of the cases (roughly 48–52 in 100) [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003].
- Earlier, A32 and the A33/A34 pair were mutually exclusive: A32 never co-occurred with A33 or A34, with cases running either A32 alone (about 52 in 100) or the A33/A34 pair (about 48 in 100). In the later window all three co-occur in every case, with A33 and A34 placed before A32 in about 98–99 in 100 cases [REL013, REL014, REL010, REL011, REL012].
- A25's routing changed: earlier, about 99 in 100 of its observed outgoing transitions went to A26 and A26 followed A25 immediately; in the later window its transitions split about evenly between A33 and A34 (about 49 each), A33/A34 now run one to two steps after A25 instead of four to five, A26 follows three steps after A25, and A28 follows five steps after A25 instead of three [DIR001, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004].
- The order around A26 flipped: earlier A26 preceded A33 and A34 in the cases where they ran; in the later window A33 and A34 precede A26 in about 97–99 in 100 cases [REL005, REL006, REL001, REL004].
- A28's choice collapsed to one target and A29 moved: earlier A28's observed outgoing transitions split between A32 (about 52 in 100), A34 (about 27) and A33 (about 21), and A29 directly followed A33 or A34 (one to two steps); in the later window about 98 in 100 of A28's transitions go to A32, A33/A34 lead to each other and to A26 (about 48–50 in 100) with no observed direct step to A29, and A29 typically runs two steps after A28 and about five steps after A33/A34 (within the five-step window in roughly 90–96 of 200 later cases) [DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, LAG008, LAG011, LAG012, REL007].
- Event positions shifted accordingly: A33 and A34 moved earlier in the case (mean relative position from about 0.53 to about 0.44), while A26, A28, A29 and A32 each moved slightly later (for example A29 from about 0.58 to about 0.62) [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006].

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the exclusive branch after A28 — running either A32 alone or the A33/A34 pair (in either order) followed directly by A29 — was replaced by a single route in which A25 first leads through A33 and A34 (in either order) to A26, and A28 is followed by A32 and then A29, so that A32, A33 and A34 now execute in every case [REL013, REL014, DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, LAG001, LAG002, LAG008, LAG011, LAG012].

The support is direct rather than purely frequency-based: the co-occurrence of A32 with A33 and A34 rose from never to every case with a stable order, the observed successor sets of A25, A28, A33 and A34 changed as described, and the lag and position shifts match this repositioning. The picture is consistent across indicators, rests on about 200 traces per window (with roughly 90–202 observations behind the routing and relation profiles), and the 32 flagged items far exceed the roughly 0.65 expected for an unchanged process — though many items describe overlapping aspects of one reordering rather than independent confirmations [REL013, REL014, DIR001, DIR002, POS004, POS005, POS006].

An important competing explanation the evidence cannot fully rule out is a shift in the mix of variants between the two finite samples — for example, the later window being dominated by a variant that was rare or unsampled earlier. This is unlikely here because the later pattern (A32 running together with A33/A34 in a fixed order) never occurs in the earlier 200 traces, but it cannot be excluded with certainty. In addition, four lag findings involving A26 or A28 with A33/A34 rest on a single later-window observation each and were not relied upon [LAG006, LAG007, LAG009, LAG010].
