### Observed differences

- In the later window, activities A16–A19 appear in about 27% of traces, down from about 83% before; activities A20–A23 appear in about 73% of traces, up from about 18% before. [OCC001–OCC008]
- The outgoing transition from A15 shifts: before, about 83% of its transitions went to A19 and about 18% to A23; after, about 27% go to A19 and about 73% to A23. [DIR001]
- A15 co-occurs with A16–A19 in about 27% of traces after versus about 83% before, and with A20–A23 in about 73% after versus about 18% before; the share of traces containing A15 without those activities flips correspondingly. [REL001–REL008]
- A16–A19 and A20–A23 never co-occur in either window; the dominant group switches from A16–A19 to A20–A23. [REL012–REL015, REL019–REL022, REL025–REL028, REL030–REL033]
- A27 co-occurs with A16–A19 less often after (about 27% versus 83%) and with A20–A23 more often after (about 73% versus 18%), with more interleaving between A27 and A20–A23. [REL016, REL023, REL029, REL034, REL038, REL041, REL043, REL044]
- The lag between A15 and A17 shifts toward shorter distances, and the lag between A19 and A17 shifts toward more cases at lag 1. [LAG001, LAG003]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a structural difference in which, after A15, the process more often continues through A23 and the A20–A23 group instead of through A19 and the A16–A19 group. The successor change at A15, the mirrored participation changes, and the co-occurrence patterns all point to a switch between two mutually exclusive branches. The lag shifts are consistent with the same branch replacement. A competing explanation is that the two windows draw from different case mixes or variants of a stable process; the evidence cannot rule that out, but the number and consistency of indicators make a structural routing change the more direct interpretation.
