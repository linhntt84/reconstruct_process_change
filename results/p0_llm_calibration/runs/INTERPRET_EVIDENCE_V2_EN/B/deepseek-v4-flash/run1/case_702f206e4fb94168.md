### Observed differences

- In the later window, A21 and A22 appear in about 80% of traces, up from about 22% before; A23 and A24 appear in about 20% of traces, down from about 78% before. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004]
- A20's outgoing transitions shifted: before, about 77% went to A23 and 22% to A21; after, about 80% went to A21 and 20% to A23. [DIR001]
- A20 now co-occurs with A21/A22 in about 80% of traces (before about 22%), and with A23/A24 in about 20% (before about 78%). A21/A22 also now co-occur with A28 in about 80% of traces, with more interleaving or repetition, whereas before they rarely co-occurred with A28. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL008, REL011]
- A23 and A24 still lead onward to A28, but because their overall participation dropped sharply, the A23/A24 path is much less frequent in the later window. [DIR002, DIR003, REL012, REL013, REL014]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a structural shift in which the branch through A23 and A24 is largely replaced by a branch through A21 and A22. In the later window, A20 more often leads to A21/A22, these activities are executed in most traces, and they are followed by A28, while A23/A24 are executed in only about a fifth of traces. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, DIR001, REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL008, REL011]

The main limitation is that the two windows are finite samples, so the exact proportions could vary; however, the change is large and consistent across occurrence, routing and co-occurrence indicators, making a sampling artefact unlikely. A competing explanation is that the before window happened to contain more cases of a type that uses the A23/A24 path, but the size and consistency of the shift make this less plausible.
