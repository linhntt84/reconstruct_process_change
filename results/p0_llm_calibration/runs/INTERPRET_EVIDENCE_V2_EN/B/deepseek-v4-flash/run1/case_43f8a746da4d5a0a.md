### Observed differences

- In the later window, A25's outgoing transitions shifted from A26 (about 99% before) to A33 and A32 (about 48% each after), with A26 dropping to about 1.5%. [DIR001]
- A28's outgoing transitions shifted from A32 and A33 (about 53% and 45% before) to A29 (about 97% after). [DIR002]
- The relative order of A26/A28 versus A32/A33 reversed: before, A26 and A28 preceded A32 and A33 in about 99% of cases; after, A32 and A33 preceded A26 and A28 in about 98% of cases. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004]
- Position shifts agree: A26 and A28 moved later (mean position from about 0.43 to 0.50 and 0.50 to 0.57), while A32 and A33 moved earlier (from about 0.55 to 0.45). [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004]
- Lag distances changed accordingly: A25 to A26 went from lag 1 to lag 3, A25 to A32/A33 from lags 4–5 to lags 1–2, and A28 to A29 from lag 3 to lag 1. [LAG001, LAG003, LAG004, LAG007]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a structural change in which the order of two activity groups swapped: A32 and A33 now occur before A26 and A28, whereas before they occurred after. This is directly shown by the reversal in relative order (REL) and by the lag shifts (LAG), and it is consistent with the successor changes (DIR) and position shifts (POS). The change is large and consistent across many items (20 evidence items versus 0.58 expected by chance). A competing explanation of sampling variation is unlikely given the magnitude and the number of items, though the evidence cannot rule out that the process changed in a different way.
