### Observed differences

- In the later window, A20's outgoing transitions changed from being split roughly equally among A30, A31, A36, and A37 (each about 25%) to going entirely to A22 (100%). [DIR001]
- A21's outgoing transitions changed from going entirely to A25 (100%) to being split among A30, A31, A36, and A37 (roughly 19–30% each). [DIR002]
- A30, A31, A36, and A37 no longer have A22 as a successor; instead A25 appears as a successor for each (about 20–27% of their transitions). [DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006]
- A21 and A22 occur earlier in the trace (mean position dropped from about 0.62 to 0.51 for A21, and from 0.48 to 0.37 for A22), while A30, A31, A36, and A37 occur later (mean position rose from about 0.41 to 0.58–0.59). [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006]
- For every pair of A21 or A22 with A30, A31, A36, or A37, the order flipped: before, the latter activity preceded A21/A22; after, A21/A22 precede them. [REL001–REL008]
- The lag between A20 and A22 changed from 5 steps to 1 step, and the lag between A21 and A25 changed from 1 step to 5 steps. [LAG001, LAG002]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a reordering of the process: A22 now directly follows A20, and A25 now follows the group A30/A31/A36/A37, while A21 now precedes that group. Before, A20 led to that group, which led to A22, and A21 led to A25. The direct successor changes, the order flips, and the position shifts are all consistent with this. A competing explanation of sampling differences in variant frequencies is unlikely given the large number of consistent items (22 items vs 0.44 expected by chance) and the direct relational evidence. However, the evidence does not show whether this is a permanent structural change or a temporary shift in routing.
