### Observed differences

- In the later window, activities A19 and A20 appear in about half of all traces (48% and 51%), whereas they were completely absent in the earlier window. [OCC001, OCC002, REL001]
- Activities A25 and A26, which occurred in every trace before, appear in only about 1.5% of traces in the later window. [OCC003, OCC004, REL010]
- The outgoing transitions of A42 shifted: before, about 46% went to A26 and 39% to A25; in the later window, about 39% go to A19 and 41% to A20, with A25 and A26 almost disappearing as successors. [DIR005]
- A19 and A20, when they occur, almost always lead to A27 (about 95% of their outgoing transitions). [DIR001, DIR002]
- In the later window, A42 precedes A19 and A20 in about half the traces, and A19 and A20 precede A27 in about half the traces; these orderings did not exist before. [REL004, REL005, REL008, REL009]
- The gap between A42 and A27 shortened: before, about 82% of cases had three steps between them; in the later window, about 81% have two steps. [LAG001]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that a new path was introduced in the later window: A42 now leads to A19 or A20, which then lead to A27, largely replacing the previous path through A25 and A26. This is supported directly by the routing change at A42 (its successors shifting from A25/A26 to A19/A20), by the appearance of A19 and A20 in about half the traces, by the near-disappearance of A25 and A26, and by the relational orderings (A42 before A19/A20, and A19/A20 before A27) plus the shortened lag between A42 and A27. [DIR005, OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, REL004, REL005, REL008, REL009, LAG001]

A competing explanation the evidence cannot fully rule out is that the later window simply samples a different mix of trace variants, since the two windows are finite samples; however, the consistency across participation, routing, ordering and lag indicators makes a genuine structural change the more plausible reading. [OCC001, DIR005, REL004, LAG001]
