### Observed differences

- In the later window, A04 occurs much less often: mean occurrences per trace drop from about 0.30 to 0.10, and trace participation from about 28 in 100 to 9 in 100. A08 occurs more often: mean per trace rises from 0.04 to 0.15, and participation from 4 in 100 to about 15 in 100. [OCC001, OCC002]

- A04’s main successor A16 drops from about 80% to 42% of A04 transitions, and the A04–A16 gap spreads from mostly immediate to more varied. [DIR001, LAG001]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a structural reordering in which A19, A20, A25 and A26 moved from before A27/A28 to after them, with routing through A46, A27, A28, A29 and A33 adjusted accordingly. In the later window, A46 leads to A27, then A28/A29, and A19/A20/A25/A26 appear later and route to A29/A33, rather than the earlier pattern where A46 led to A19/A20/A25/A26, which then led to A27. This is supported directly by positional shifts, relational order flips, successor changes and lag changes, not merely by frequency differences. [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, REL007, REL008, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006, DIR007, DIR010, LAG011, LAG008, LAG009, LAG010]

A competing explanation that sampling differences in variant frequencies could produce some of the observed changes is largely ruled out by the direct relational and positional evidence, although a few individual lag items rest on very few observations and are not used as primary support.
