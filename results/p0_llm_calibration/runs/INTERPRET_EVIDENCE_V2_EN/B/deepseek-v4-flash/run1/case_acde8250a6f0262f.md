### Observed differences

- In the later window, the outgoing routing of several activities is much more constrained. A15 goes only to A16 (about 51%) and A19 (49%), whereas before it also went to A20, A22, A23 and A21 (about 15%, 14%, 8% and 7%). - In the later window, for every tested pair between the early group (A15, A16, A18, A19) and the late group (A20, A21, A22, A23), the early activity always precedes the late activity; before, the order was mixed. For example, A15 preceded A20 in about 29% of cases before and 100% after; A19 preceded A20 in about 9% before and 100% after. [REL001, REL004, REL010]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a structural change in which the process was reorganised into a more linear sequence, roughly A28 → A18 → (A15/A16) → A19 → A20 → (A22/A23) → A21 → A29, with fewer alternative routings than before. This is supported by the consistent redistribution of outgoing edges [DIR001-DIR009], the earlier placement of A15/A16/A18/A19 and later placement of A20/A21/A22/A23 [POS001-POS008], the uniform ordering of early-before-late pairs [REL001-REL013], and the lag patterns showing adjacency within the sequence and larger gaps between early and late activities [LAG001, LAG004, LAG022, LAG027, LAG036]. The number of qualifying evidence items (68) is far above the 0.58 expected by chance under no change, so the overall pattern is unlikely to be a sampling artefact [screening]. A competing explanation that cannot be fully ruled out is that the two windows represent two distinct stable variants of the process rather than a change at a single point, but the evidence clearly indicates a structural difference between the windows.
