### Observed differences

- In the later window, activities A19 and A20 almost disappear: A19 appears in about 0.5% of traces (1 in 200) versus about 45% before, and A20 in about 0.5% versus about 55% before. [OCC001, OCC002]
- The successors of A26 and A27 no longer include A19 or A20. A26 now goes to A27 in about 97% of its outgoing transitions (before: A27 about 45%, A20 about 27%, A19 about 23%); A27 now goes to A28 in about 97% (before: A28 about 51%, A20 about 27%, A19 about 21%). [DIR003, DIR004]
- Relations with A19 and A20 change from co-occurrence with A26/A27/A28 in about 45–55% of traces to those activities appearing alone in about 99% of traces. [REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, REL007]
- The lag structure between the remaining activities becomes more direct: A26–A27 are adjacent (lag 1) in about 98% of cases (before split between lag 1 and lag 2), A27–A28 are adjacent in about 98% (before split), and A26–A28 are two steps apart in about 96% (before mostly three steps apart). ### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that activities A19 and A20 were removed from the process, leaving a main path that runs A26 → A27 → A28. The near-total disappearance of A19 and A20 in occurrence and in relations with other activities, together with the successor changes of A26 and A27 and the lag pattern consistent with a direct A26–A27–A28 sequence, all point in the same direction. The main limitation is that the few later traces still containing A19 or A20 (one each) make their exact placement unreliable, and the evidence cannot rule out that these activities still occur as rare variants; however, the magnitude and consistency of the change across many indicators make a structural removal the most plausible interpretation.
