### Observed differences

- In the later window, activities A30, A31, A36 and A37 each occur on average about 1.0 times per trace instead of about 3.3 times; the share of traces with repeated occurrences drops from about 67% to about 1% or less. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004]
- For A37, the share of outgoing transitions to A30 falls from about 70% to 0%, while transitions to A25 rise from about 30% to about 97%. [DIR001]
- For pairs among A30, A31, A36 and A37, the share of traces where the two activities are interleaved or repeated falls from about 67% to about 1–3%, and the share where the first activity occurs before the second rises from about 33% to about 97–98% (for A31–A36, the before share of A-before-B was about 14% and B-before-A about 19%, rising to 56% and 44% respectively). - Lags between these activities become much shorter: for example, for A30–A31 the share of lag 1 or 2 rises from about 18% to about 99%; for A31–A36, lag 1 rises from about 10% to about 98%. [LAG001, LAG003]
- The relative position of these activities becomes more concentrated: the standard deviation of their position drops from about 0.13 to about 0.05–0.07. [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a structural change in which the repeated execution of activities A30, A31, A36 and A37 was replaced by a single execution of each, with a more fixed order: A30 tends to occur before A31, A36 and A37, and A31 before A36/A37, while A37's successor shifted from A30 to A25. The drop in mean occurrences and recurrence, the near-disappearance of interleaving, and the shortening of lags are all consistent and based on nearly all 200 traces in each window. A competing explanation of random sampling differences is unlikely given the number and consistency of the items. A limitation is that some lag items involving A37 as the source have only one trace in the later window, so the exact ordering of A37 relative to A31 and A36 is less certain, but the overall pattern is well supported.
