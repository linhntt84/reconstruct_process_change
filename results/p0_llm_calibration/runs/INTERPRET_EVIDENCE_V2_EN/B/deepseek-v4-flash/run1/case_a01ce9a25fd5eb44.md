### Observed differences

- In the later window, activities A14, A15, A20 and A21 appear in about 24%, 21%, 31% and 25% of traces respectively, each once per trace; in the earlier window none of them occurred. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004]
- These four activities are mutually exclusive alternatives: in the later window about 99.5% of traces contain exactly one of them, and no trace contains two. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL009, REL010, REL016]
- In the later window, A14, A15, A20 and A21 are positioned around the middle of the trace (mean normalized position about 0.51), and their outgoing transitions go to A24 and A25 in roughly equal shares. [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004]
- The routing of A23 and A24 changed: before, A23 always went to A24 and A24 always to A25; in the later window A23 goes to A24 in about 49% of cases and to the new activities in about 12–14% each, while A24 goes to A25 in about 52% of cases and to the new activities in about 8–17% each. [DIR005, DIR006]
- The distance between A23 and A24 increased: before they were always one step apart; in the later window about half are one step and half two steps apart. Similarly, A23–A25 changed from two steps to three steps in about 99.5% of cases, and A24–A25 from one step to two steps in about 49% of cases. [LAG001, LAG002, LAG003]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that, in the later window, a set of mutually exclusive activities A14, A15, A20 and A21 was introduced into the middle of the process, between A23/A24 and A24/A25, with branching to A24 or A25. This is supported by their appearance from zero to near-universal participation, their mutual exclusivity, their middle position, the changed outgoing transitions of A23 and A24, and the increased lags between A23–A24, A23–A25 and A24–A25. The main competing explanation is that these activities always existed but were simply not observed in the earlier 200-trace sample; the evidence cannot fully exclude that, but the scale and consistency of the changes make a structural change the most plausible interpretation.
