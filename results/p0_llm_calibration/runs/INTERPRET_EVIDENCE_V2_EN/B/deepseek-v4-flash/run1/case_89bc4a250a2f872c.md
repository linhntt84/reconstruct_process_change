### Observed differences

- In the later window, A32, A33 and A34 each appear in about 45–55% of traces instead of essentially all traces, and their mean occurrences per trace drop by roughly 0.45–0.55. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003]
- A25’s outgoing routing shifts from A33/A34 (about 97% combined) to A26 (about 96%). [DIR001]
- A28’s outgoing routing shifts from A32 (about 95%) to A33/A34 (about 54% combined), with A32 still receiving about 43%. [DIR002]
- A33 and A34 now route substantially to A29: A33 to A29 about 44%, A34 to A29 about 54%. [DIR003, DIR004]
- Ordering changes are direct: A25 now occurs one step before A26 (previously about three steps), while A25 now occurs four to five steps before A33/A34 (previously one to two). [LAG001, LAG003, LAG004]
- A26 and A28 now occur before A33/A34, whereas before they occurred after A33/A34. [REL005, REL006, REL008, REL009]
- A33/A34 now occur one to two steps before A29, whereas before the gap was about five steps. [LAG007, LAG008]
- A32 and A33/A34 essentially stop co-occurring: co-occurrence drops from near 100% to near zero, with A32 alone in about 45% of later traces and A33/A34 alone in about 55%. [REL013, REL014]
- Position shifts agree: A33 and A34 move later in the trace (mean position from about 0.43 to about 0.55), while A26 and A28 move earlier. [POS002, POS003, POS005, POS006]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a structural re-sequencing of the process: A25 now leads to A26, then A28, then A33/A34, and then A29, with A32 largely skipped rather than being a regular successor of A28. This is supported directly by successor changes, relational order reversals, lag shifts, and position shifts, all pointing to the same new ordering. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, REL005, REL006, REL008, REL009, LAG001, LAG003, LAG004, LAG007, LAG008, POS002, POS003, POS005, POS006]

The main limitation is that A32, A33 and A34 still appear in about half of later traces, so the change is not a complete removal of those activities; it is a shift in routing and ordering. Sampling differences in variant frequencies could explain some participation changes, but the consistent relational and positional evidence makes that an unlikely sole explanation.
