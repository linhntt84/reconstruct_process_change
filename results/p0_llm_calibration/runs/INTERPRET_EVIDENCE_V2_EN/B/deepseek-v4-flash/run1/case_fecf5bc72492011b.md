- In the later window, for many pairs where one of A20, A21, A23, A24 is paired with one of A25, A26, A27, A28, the first activity now almost always occurs before the second (98–100% of traces), whereas before the order was mixed (e.g., A20 before A25 in 28% before vs 98% after; A24 before A25 in 8.5% before vs 99% after). ### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a hypothesis that the process changed so that a group of activities (A20, A21, A23, A24) now consistently occur before another group (A25, A26, A27, A28), with a more fixed sequential order, whereas before the order between these groups was mixed. This is directly shown by the relational and lag indicators: for all tested pairs between these groups, the share where the first activity precedes the second rose to near 100% and the reverse order disappeared, and the lag distributions shifted to larger distances for the later group. The successor changes (A24→A25, A26→A34, A33→A23) and the position shifts are consistent with this reordering. The number of qualifying evidence items (77) is far above the 0.88 expected by chance, so the overall pattern is unlikely to be a sampling artefact. A competing explanation that cannot be fully ruled out is that the two windows come from different variant populations, but the consistency across many independent activity pairs and indicators makes this unlikely. Some individual lag items are based on very few traces (e.g., support of 1 in the after window for some pairs), but the pattern is corroborated by items with full support.
