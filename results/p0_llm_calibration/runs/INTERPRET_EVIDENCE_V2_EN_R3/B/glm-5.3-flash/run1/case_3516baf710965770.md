### Observed differences

- A19 and A20 ran in every trace in the earlier window (participation 1.0, about one occurrence per trace); in the later window each appears in only about 59 in 100 traces (participation 0.585). [OCC001, OCC002]
- A25 and A26 were mutually exclusive alternatives: earlier, exactly one of them ran in virtually every trace (A25 in about 48 in 100, A26 in about 52 in 100, both in about 1 in 200); in the later window one of them runs in about 59 in 100 traces, and in about 41 in 100 traces neither appears. [OCC003, OCC004, REL010]
- The drops are coupled into one segment. A19 and A20 still only occur together (co-occurrence 1.0 earlier, 0.585 later, with "neither" rising from 0 to 0.415), A19 precedes A20 in about 99 in 100 of those traces, and in the later window A25 and A26 were essentially never seen without A19. The whole segment A19 → (A25 or A26) → A20 is present in about 59 in 100 later traces and absent in about 41 in 100. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL006, REL007, REL010]
- A42 and A27 occur in every trace in both windows. Earlier, about 93 in 100 of A42's outgoing transitions led to A19 and none led directly to A27, with A27 following A42 about 4–5 positions later; in the later window, about 40 in 100 of A42's outgoing transitions lead directly to A27 (A19 down to about 55 in 100), and the A42-to-A27 distance is 1 position in about 40 in 100 cases versus 0 earlier. [DIR002, LAG001, REL004, REL005]
- Where the segment is retained in the later window it behaves as before: A42 still leads to A19 in roughly 94 in 100 of those traces, the A42-to-A27 distance stays at 4–5 positions, and A27's relative position moved only slightly earlier (mean 0.44 to 0.41). [DIR002, LAG001, POS001]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that, in the later window, the segment between A42 and A27 — A19, the A25/A26 alternative and A20 — is skipped in about 4 in 10 cases: A42 then leads directly to A27, and A19, A20 and the A25/A26 alternative drop out of the trace together, while in the remaining about 6 in 10 cases the earlier sequence A42 → A19 → A25/A26 → A20 → A27 is retained unchanged. This reads as the segment having become skippable rather than as a re-weighting of paths that already existed in both windows. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, REL001, REL002, REL003, REL006, REL007, REL010, DIR002, LAG001]

Supporting signs: the bypass is observed directly, not only inferred — A42 gained A27 as a direct successor (about 40 in 100 outgoing transitions, unobserved earlier) and the A42-to-A27 gap collapsed to one position in about 40 in 100 cases; the participation losses of A19/A20 (about 42 in 100 traces) and of A25/A26 (about 19–23 in 100 traces) match a single skipped fraction of about 41.5% applied to the retained structure; and 18 qualifying items against roughly 0.61 expected if nothing had changed makes chance sampling untenable as an overall explanation, though the many activity-pair items largely re-describe the same skip behaviour rather than confirming it independently. [DIR002, LAG001, OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, REL001, REL010]

Limitations: the evidence cannot show whether the direct A42→A27 step is newly introduced or a pre-existing rare path that is now taken far more often, since the earlier window contains no observation of it; nor can a shift in the mix of case variants between the windows be distinguished from a modification of a single process with these indicators. [DIR002, LAG001]

Other differences: A25's outgoing transitions show only a minor redistribution (A20 still receives about 95 in 100 later versus 99 earlier, with three new targets at about 2 in 100 each — roughly one trace each, given only 59 supporting traces after), consistent with sampling noise rather than a separate change. [DIR001]
