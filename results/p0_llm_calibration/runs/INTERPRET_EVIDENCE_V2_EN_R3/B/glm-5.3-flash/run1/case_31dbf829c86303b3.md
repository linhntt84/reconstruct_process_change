### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the ordering constraints in the segment between A24 and A25 changed from a mixed order to a fixed sequence. In the earlier window, the two stages — A16/A17 on one side and A18/A19 on the other — could run in either order: A24 entered any of the four activities (about 25 in 100 each) and A25 directly followed whichever stage ran last, so either group came first in roughly half of the cases. In the later window, the sequence is fixed as A24 → A16 or A17 → A18 or A19 → A25, with the formerly common transitions A24→A18/A19 and A16/A17→A25 reduced to about 1–2 in 100. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006, LAG007, LAG008]

Supporting signs: 13 items qualified, about 37 times the roughly 0.35 expected by chance across the 71 tested units, so sampling luck is unlikely to explain the set as a whole; the change is shown directly by successor redistributions together with matching position-gap shifts, not by a single unusual item; all items agree on one arrangement and rest on 88–200 observations per window. Note that the DIR and LAG items describe the same trace behaviour, so they are consistent rather than independent confirmations. [DIR001, DIR005, LAG001]

An important competing explanation remains: with 200 traces per window, transitions now seen at about 1–2 in 100 may still exist and simply be chosen very rarely rather than being removed, and the profiles cover only the dominant successors, so rare edges cannot be assessed; the evidence therefore shows strong suppression of the old transitions, not their certain elimination. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005]

Other differences: within the fixed sequence, choice shares also shifted — A24 now selects A17 (about 55 in 100) more often than A16 (about 44 in 100), where before all four successors were close to 25 in 100, and A19 now occurs in about 56 in 100 traces versus about 44 in 100 for A18, where before they were roughly even. [DIR005, DIR003, DIR004]
