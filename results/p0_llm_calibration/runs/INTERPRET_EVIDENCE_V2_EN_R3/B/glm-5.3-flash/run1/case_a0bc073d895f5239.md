### Observed differences

- The branch choice after A20 flipped. In the earlier window, about 78 in 100 of A20's outgoing transitions led to A21 and about 22 in 100 to A23; in the later window, about 21 in 100 lead to A21 and about 79 in 100 to A23, with a single transition to A05 newly observed. [DIR001]
- Participation of the two branch chains swapped. A21 and A22 each appeared in about 78 in 100 traces earlier (156 of 200 each) but in only about 21–22 in 100 later (44 and 42 of 200), while A23 and A24 rose from about 22 in 100 (45 and 44 of 200) to about 79–80 in 100 (159 and 158 of 200). None of the four activities repeats within a trace in either window. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004]
- The order inside each branch is preserved; only how often each branch runs changed. Earlier, A20 preceded A21 in about 78 in 100 traces and A21 preceded A22 in about 77 in 100, while A23 and A24 shared a trace in only about 22 in 100. In the later window, A20 precedes A23 in about 78 in 100 traces and A24 in about 79 in 100, and A23 precedes A24 in about 79 in 100. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL012]
- A28 still appears in essentially every trace in both windows, but its companions changed: earlier it co-occurred with A21 or A22 in about 78 in 100 traces, later it co-occurs with A23 or A24 in about 79–80 in 100. [REL008, REL011, REL013, REL014]
- Routing inside the branches stayed essentially the same: A21 still leads almost exclusively to A22 (about 99 in 100 of its outgoing transitions earlier, about 95 in 100 later), A23 to A24 (about 98 in 100 in both windows) and A24 to A28 (about 98 in 100 earlier, every observed transition later). [DIR002, DIR003, DIR004]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the two alternative paths out of A20 — A20 → A21 → A22 and A20 → A23 → A24, both continuing to A28 — are taken with very different frequencies in the later window: the A21/A22 path, previously chosen in about 78 in 100 cases, is now chosen in about 21 in 100, while the A23/A24 path rose from about 22 to about 79 in 100. This is a change in how often existing paths are taken rather than a replacement of one path by another: both paths and all four activities are observed in substantial shares in both windows (at lowest about 21–22 in 100 traces), the order and successors within each branch are unchanged, and repetition is negligible in both windows. [DIR001, OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, REL003, REL004, REL012, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004]

The flip is observed directly at the branch point A20, and the participation and relation items all describe one consistent underlying difference rather than unrelated shifts; 22 items qualified against roughly 0.45 expected for an unchanged process, and each item's difference exceeds about 995 of 1,000 random re-splits of the pooled data, so chance sampling is implausible as a whole explanation. [DIR001, REL001, REL005, REL008, REL013]

Limitation: the evidence shows that the branch shares flipped but not why — a changed branch condition at A20 and a shifted mix of case types that prefer each branch look identical here — and the later-window share of the now-rare branch rests on about 44 traces. [DIR001, OCC001]

Other differences: a few low-support edge changes accompany the flip — in the later window A21 is occasionally followed by A12 or A20 and A23 by A14 (about 1–2 in 100 of the respective outgoing transitions), the earlier rare edges A21→A29 and A24→A19 are no longer observed, and a rare A20→A05 transition appears. Each rests on only a handful of observations and may be sampling noise. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004]
