### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, every trace ran exactly one of four alternative activities between A41 and A26: A14 in about 20 of 100 traces, A16 about 26, A17 about 25 and A18 about 29 (the shares sum to 100, and no two of these activities ever occurred in the same trace). Each of the four always came after A41 and led only onward to A26. [OCC001, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL018, REL019, REL025, REL009, REL024, REL030, REL035, REL008, REL023, REL029, REL034, DIR001, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, DIR009]

- In the later window, A14, A16, A17 and A18 are not observed at all, while A15, A23, A24 and A25 — never seen earlier — each occur exactly once in every trace. [OCC001, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC002, OCC006, OCC007, OCC008, REL010, REL011, REL012]

- The four new activities run in no fixed order among themselves: every pair co-occurs in all later traces and appears in both orders in near-equal shares (for example, A15 precedes A23 in about 53 of 100 traces and follows it in about 48). [REL013, REL014, REL015, REL036, REL037, REL040]

- The new block sits between the same anchors: in the later window A41 precedes each of A15, A23, A24 and A25 in every trace, and each of them precedes A26 in every trace. A41's outgoing transitions now go only to these four (about 21–28 in 100 each), and each of the four is followed by one of the other three or by A26 in roughly equal shares (about 20–34 in 100), as an arbitrary sequencing of the four would produce. [REL017, REL039, REL042, REL044, REL016, REL038, REL041, REL043, DIR009, DIR002, DIR006, DIR007, DIR008]

- The distance from A41 to A26 grew from exactly 2 event positions in every earlier trace to exactly 5 positions in every later trace; over the same period A26's mean relative position moved later in the trace (about 0.39 to 0.45) and A41's moved earlier (about 0.33 to 0.29). [LAG001, POS009, POS010]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the step between A41 and A26 was restructured: in the earlier window each trace executed exactly one of four alternative activities (A14, A16, A17, A18) between A41 and A26, whereas in the later window that single choice is replaced by four activities (A15, A23, A24, A25) that every trace executes once, in no fixed internal order, between the same two anchors. Because each activity group is observed in only one window, this is a replacement of one path structure by another (activities no longer observed and others now always executed), not a shift in how often existing paths are taken. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, OCC007, OCC008, DIR009, LAG001, REL013, REL016]

Supporting signs: 72 evidence items qualified, against roughly 0.62 expected by chance in an unchanged process, so the pattern as a whole cannot be a sampling artefact; the participation changes are maximal (0 to 100 in 100 traces and back) on samples of 200 traces per window; routing out of A41 switched wholesale from the old four to the new four; relational evidence places the new activities after A41 and before A26; and the A41-to-A26 distance changed from 2 to 5 steps, exactly what replacing one activity by four predicts. Occurrence, routing, relation, position and lag indicators all describe the same picture, though they largely reflect the same traces and are not independent confirmations. [OCC002, DIR009, REL016, REL038, LAG001, POS009, POS010]

Limitations: zero participation only shows an activity was not observed, so the evidence cannot establish whether A14, A16, A17 and A18 were removed from the process or merely never chosen, nor whether A15, A23, A24 and A25 are genuinely new work or re-labels of the old four. That re-labelling possibility is the main competing explanation the data cannot rule out, although it would not change the described shift from a one-of-four choice to an unordered four-activity block. [OCC001, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC002, OCC006, OCC007, OCC008]
