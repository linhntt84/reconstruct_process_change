### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, A34, A35, A36 and A37 each appeared in every trace (200 of 200); in the later window, each appears in only about half of the traces — A34 and A35 about 56 in 100, A36 and A37 about 47 in 100. [OCC005, OCC006, OCC007, OCC008]
- Each pair still acts as a block in the later window: A34 and A35 co-occur in about 56 in 100 traces (essentially every trace containing one contains the other) and keep their mixed internal order, and A36 and A37 co-occur in about 46 in 100 traces, also in mixed order. [REL025, REL030]
- Earlier, A34/A35 and A36/A37 always occurred in the same trace, with A34/A35 first in 100 in 100 of them; in the later window the two blocks co-occur in only about 2–3 in 100 traces, while about 53 in 100 traces contain only A34/A35 and about 44 in 100 only A36/A37. [REL026, REL027, REL028, REL029]
- Routing moved with this: earlier, A30 led almost only to A34 (about 56% of its outgoing transitions) or A35 (about 44%), whereas in the later window it also leads directly to A36 (about 25%) and A37 (about 19%); earlier, A34 and A35 led mainly on to A36/A37 (together roughly 44–48%), whereas in the later window they lead mainly to A31 (about 46–50%). [DIR003, DIR004, DIR005]
- The A30→A31 distance shortened from 5 steps in essentially all earlier traces to 3 steps in about 93 in 100 later traces, and the distance from A34/A35 to A31 shortened from 3–4 steps to 1–2. [LAG003, LAG006, LAG008]
- A30 still precedes whichever block runs: in the later window it co-occurs with A34/A35 in about 55 in 100 traces and precedes them in essentially all of those, and with A36/A37 in about 46–47 in 100, again almost always before them. [REL017, REL018, REL019, REL020]
- A06 appeared in about 30 in 100 earlier traces but only about 10 in 100 later traces; where it occurs, its outgoing transitions shifted from about 90% towards A16 to about 45% towards A16 and 18% towards A15. [OCC003, DIR001, REL001, REL002]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the two consecutive activity blocks A34–A35 and A36–A37, which ran between A30 and A31, changed from being executed together in a fixed sequence to being alternatives of which typically exactly one is executed: in the earlier window every trace contained both blocks in the order A30, A34/A35, A36/A37, A31, while in the later window about 55 in 100 traces contain only the A34/A35 block, about 44–46 in 100 only the A36/A37 block, both blocks together appear in only about 2 in 100, and the A30→A31 distance shortened from 5 to about 3 steps. [OCC005, OCC006, OCC007, OCC008, REL026, REL027, REL028, REL029, LAG003] Supporting signs are that the participation of all four activities fell from 200 of 200 traces to about 93–112 of 200, that the rerouting at A30, A34 and A35 matches a skip of one block, that the internal order of each block and A30's precedence are unchanged, and that the 59 qualifying items far exceed the roughly 1 expected for an unchanged process, with each difference larger than in about 199 of 200 random re-splits. [DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, REL017, REL025, REL030, OCC005] The evidence cannot show where the choice between the two blocks is made or what governs it, and it cannot rule out that the later window reflects a shift in the shares of pre-existing variants — in which one block was already skipped — rather than a newly introduced skip. [REL026, DIR003]

Other differences: A06 became rarer, appearing in about 30 in 100 earlier traces and about 10 in 100 later traces, with its transitions spreading over more targets. [OCC003, DIR001, REL001, REL002] The rare activities A02, A04 and A08 became somewhat more frequent, from about 1–3 in 100 traces to about 7–13 in 100, though this rests on only about 2–26 observations per window. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC004] A15, A16 and A30 now occur slightly later within traces, with mean relative positions up by about 0.02–0.06; a position shift alone does not show that these activities were moved.
