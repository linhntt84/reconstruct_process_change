### Observed differences

- The two alternative activities A19 and A20 ran far more often in the later window: A19 appeared in about 47 of 200 traces earlier (roughly 24 in 100) versus about 97 of 200 (roughly 49 in 100) later, and A20 in about 43 of 200 (22 in 100) versus about 106 of 200 (53 in 100); neither activity repeated within a trace in either window. [OCC001, OCC002]
- In the earlier window, A19 and A20 were mutually exclusive and were usually both skipped: neither appeared in about 55 of 100 traces, and they never appeared together. In the later window, at least one of them executed in essentially every trace (neither in 0 of 200), while they still almost never ran together (about 2 in 100), so they remained alternatives whose "skip" outcome disappeared. [REL001]
- A40 and A25 each appeared in every trace in both windows. Whenever A19 or A20 did occur, it ran after A40 and before A25 in both windows; what changed is how often: A19 before A25 rose from about 23 to about 47 in 100 traces, and A20 before A25 from about 21 to about 53 in 100. [REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005]
- The routing out of A40 shifted accordingly: earlier, its outgoing transitions went most often to A25 (about 46 in 100) and to A19 or A20 together about 35 in 100; later, they went to A20 (about 47 in 100) or A19 (about 41 in 100), and A25 was no longer observed as a direct successor of A40. [DIR001]
- Consistently, the positional gap from A40 to A25 grew: earlier it was one step in about 46 of 100 traces and two steps in about 44; later it was two steps in about 86 of 100 and three steps in about 13, with a one-step gap no longer observed. [LAG001]
- Separately, A23 was observed in about 111 of 200 traces earlier (56 in 100) but about 84 of 200 later (42 in 100). [OCC003]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that A19 and A20 changed from optional, usually-skipped alternatives into steps that execute in virtually every case between A40 and A25, replacing the previously common direct transition from A40 to A25. Earlier, after A40 the process passed straight to A25 in about 46 of 100 traces and through exactly one of A19/A20 in about 35 of 100, with both skipped in about 55 of 100; in the later window, exactly one of A19/A20 ran in essentially every trace, after A40 and before A25, and a direct A40-to-A25 step was no longer observed. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, DIR001, LAG001, OCC001, OCC002]

The hypothesis is direct and consistent: ten qualifying items were found where roughly 0.5 would be expected if nothing had changed, every item exceeds about 995 of 1,000 random re-splits of the pooled traces, and the participation, routing, relational and positional indicators all describe the same coherent shift. These items partly reflect the same traces, so they corroborate one underlying change rather than providing independent confirmations. [OCC001, OCC002, DIR001, REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, LAG001]

Limitations: an unobserved edge does not prove absence, so the direct A40-to-A25 transition may still occur rarely below the visibility of the 200 later traces, and "one of the two always runs" is what this sample shows rather than proof of a mandatory construct. The main competing explanation the evidence cannot fully exclude is a strong sampling fluctuation in a stable process, although the screening count makes this unlikely for the ensemble as a whole. [DIR001, LAG001, REL001]

Other differences: A23 participation fell from about 56 to about 42 in 100 traces; the evidence contains no relational or routing detail for A23, so this decline stands outside the main hypothesis. [OCC003]
