### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that two existing path groups are taken with different frequencies: traces containing A31 and A32 became more common (each from about 40 to about 53 in 100 traces), while traces containing A33 and A34 became less common (each from about 60 to about 48 in 100 traces). Both groups remain well observed in both windows, no activity repeats within a trace in either window, and nothing suggests a group is bypassed, so the pattern fits a redistribution of shares between existing alternatives rather than activities being added, removed or repeated differently. The supporting signs are the equal-and-opposite shifts of about 12–13 percentage points, differences larger than in about 995 of 1,000 random re-splits, and five qualifying items in total against roughly 0.27 expected by chance if nothing had changed. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, LAG001]

Limitations: the four occurrence items likely describe one underlying trace behaviour rather than independent confirmations, and with no relational evidence in this set the data cannot show that A31/A32 and A33/A34 sit on mutually exclusive branches or identify the choice point separating them. A competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that the participation of the two groups changed for separate reasons rather than as a single shift between alternatives. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004]

Other differences: the A16 → A27 pair is now typically executed one position apart instead of two (about 59 in 100 pairs immediately consecutive versus about 41 before). Because this indicator uses event positions, this shows a change in ordering tightness between the two activities, not that either was moved, and as a single item it does not by itself establish a structural change.
