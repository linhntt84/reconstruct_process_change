### Observed differences

- In the later window, A32, A33 and A34 appear in far fewer cases: A32 in about 52 in 100 traces (before: all 200), A33 and A34 in about 64 in 100 (before: about 99 in 100); within-case repetition of these activities, already rare at about 1 in 100, disappears. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003]
- In the later window, about 84 in 100 outgoing transitions of A25 go directly to A26; before, they went to A33 (about 46 in 100) or A34 (about 52 in 100). [DIR001]
- In the later window, A28 leads to A32 in only about 51 in 100 of its outgoing transitions (before: about 97), with A33 (about 25) and A34 (about 22) as new frequent destinations. [DIR002]
- In the later window, A33 and A34 each lead to A29 in about 37–38 in 100 transitions (before: essentially never) and to A26 in only about 11–13 (before: about 46–53); the A33–A34 exchange between themselves persists (about 48–51 in 100). [DIR004, DIR005]
- The order between A26 and the A33/A34 pair reversed: before, A33 preceded A26 in about 99 in 100 cases; in the later window A26 precedes A33 in about 49 in 100, A33 precedes A26 in about 15, and A26 occurs alone in about 36 in 100 — the same reversal holds for A34, and A28 now precedes A33 in about 49 in 100 cases whereas A33 previously preceded A28 in about 99. [REL005, REL006, REL008]
- A32 is decoupled from the A33/A34 pair: A32–A33 co-occurrence falls from about 99 to 16 in 100 cases (A32 alone about 36, A33 alone about 48), and A29 now occurs without A32 in about 48 in 100 cases (before they essentially always co-occurred, A32 first); A33 and A34 still appear together in essentially every case where they occur. [REL013, REL014, REL010, REL015]
- Event distances shifted accordingly: A26 now follows A25 after 1 step in about 84 in 100 cases (before: mostly 3); A28 follows A25 after 3 steps (before: 5); A33 and A34 now sit 4–5 steps after A25 (before: 1–2); and A29 now follows A33 or A34 within 1–2 steps (before: about 5). [LAG001, LAG002, LAG004, LAG005, LAG008, LAG009]
- Mean event positions move in the same direction (A33 and A34 from about 0.43 to about 0.52 of the trace; A26 and A28 slightly earlier; A25 slightly later), while A32's own outgoing behaviour is unchanged, still leading to A29 in about 97 in 100 transitions in both windows. [POS004, POS005, POS001, POS002, POS003, DIR003]
- Every difference listed above is larger than about 199 of 200 random re-splits of the pooled traces, so differences of this size are unusual for two 200-trace samples. [DIR001, DIR002, REL005, REL013, LAG001, LAG008, OCC001]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the A33/A34 pair now runs in a different position in the flow: before, it typically ran between A25 and A26 and was followed by A26, A28, then A32 before A29; in the later window it typically runs after A28 and reaches A29 within 1–2 steps, so A32 is executed in only about half of the cases (about 52 in 100, down from all). [DIR001, DIR002, DIR004, DIR005, REL005, REL006, REL008, REL010, REL013, LAG001, LAG004, LAG005, LAG008, LAG009, OCC001]

The support is direct and consistent: the rerouting out of A25, A28, A33 and A34 points to exactly this arrangement, the pairwise order of A26 versus A33/A34 and of A28 versus A33 reversed, A32 and the A33/A34 pair became near-exclusive alternatives, and the distance shifts match; the drop in A32 participation follows from the same rerouting rather than being an independent confirmation. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR004, DIR005, REL005, REL008, REL013, LAG008]

Limitations: with two finite samples of 200 traces, a shift in the mix of pre-existing variants — for example, a rare routing that skips A32 and runs A33/A34 after A28 becoming the majority — could produce the same differences, and the evidence cannot separate a single structural edit from such a frequency shift; a few of the distance items also rest on roughly 87–111 observations per window. [DIR002, REL013, LAG008, LAG009]
