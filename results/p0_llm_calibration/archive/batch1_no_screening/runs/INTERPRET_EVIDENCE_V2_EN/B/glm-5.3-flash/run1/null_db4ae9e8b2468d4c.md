### Observed differences

- In the later window, about 60 in 100 of A16's observed outgoing transitions went to A25, up from about 40 in 100 in the earlier window (roughly 58 of 96 transitions versus 42 of 105). [DIR001]
- Over the same period, the share of A16's transitions going to A19 fell from about 33 in 100 to about 14 in 100, while the share going to A18 stayed essentially flat (about 27 in 100 before, 26 in 100 after). [DIR001]
- The number of observed outgoing transitions from A16 was similar in both windows (about 105 before, 96 after), so the difference lies in the destination mix rather than in how often A16's outgoing flow occurred. [DIR001]
- The overall redirection is large relative to random re-splits of the pooled traces: the difference exceeds about 199 of 200 reference comparisons. [DIR001]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the routing out of A16 changed: flow that previously went from A16 to A19 now goes to A25, while the share continuing to A18 is unchanged. The shift is measured directly on A16's outgoing transitions, the rise in A25 (about +20 in 100) mirrors the fall in A19 (about −20 in 100) with A18 stable, and the difference is larger than in about 199 of 200 random re-splits, which makes pure sampling noise an unlikely full explanation. [DIR001]

The evidence covers only the outgoing transitions of A16, so it cannot show whether other parts of the process changed, and it cannot rule out that the mix of cases reaching A16 differed between the windows — a changed case mix could shift the observed destination shares without any structural change. Sampling differences in variant frequencies also cannot be excluded with certainty, although the high empirical strength argues against them as the sole cause. [DIR001]
