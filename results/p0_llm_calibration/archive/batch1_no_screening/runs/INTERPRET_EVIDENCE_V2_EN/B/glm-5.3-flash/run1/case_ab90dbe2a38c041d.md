### Observed differences

- The clearest change is a reversal of execution order between two groups of activities. In the earlier window A26 preceded A32 in about 99 of 100 traces, and A28 preceded A32/A33 in about 99–100 of 100; in the later window the order is mostly inverted — A32 precedes A26 in about 78 of 100 traces, A33 precedes A26 in about 79, and A32/A33 precede A28 in about 77. Both activities of each pair still occur together in every trace in both windows, so this is a change in order, not in participation. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004]

- Successor routing moved accordingly. In the later window A25 is followed by A32 in about 38 of 100 outgoing transitions and by A33 in about 39, where before it was followed by A26 in about 97 of 100 (now about 18); A28 is now followed by A29 in about 74 of 100 transitions, where before it was followed mainly by A32 (about 56) and A33 (about 42); and A32 and A33 are now each followed by A26 in about 38 of 100 transitions, while their transitions to A29 fell to about 6 and 14 in 100. [DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005]

- Distances changed in the same direction: A25 to A26 now typically spans 3 events (75 in 100) instead of 1 (97 in 100 before); A25 to A32 and A25 to A33 now typically span 1–2 events instead of 4–5; A28 to A29 is now immediate (75 in 100) instead of 3 events; A32 and A33 to A29 now span 4–5 events instead of 1–2; and A26 to A29 spans 3 events instead of 5. [LAG002, LAG004, LAG005, LAG009, LAG013, LAG016, LAG006]

- Relative event positions agree: A26 and A28 now occur later in the trace (mean relative position about 0.43 to 0.49 and 0.49 to 0.55), while A32 and A33 occur earlier (about 0.54 to 0.47 and 0.54 to 0.48). [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004]

- Two changes lie outside this pattern: A23 appears in about 59 of 100 later-window traces versus about 42 of 100 before (mean occurrences per trace 0.59 versus 0.42), and A22's outgoing transitions shifted towards A30 (about 43 to 63 in 100) at the expense of A24 (about 34 to 10 in 100). [OCC001, DIR001]

- Distances from A32 and A33 to A22 and A24 also lengthened (typically 5 events in the later window versus 2–4 before), but these comparisons rest on only about 30–35 after-window observations and are weak. [LAG011, LAG012, LAG014, LAG015]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the order of the segment containing A25, A26, A28, A29, A32 and A33 changed: in the later window A32 and A33 run directly after A25 and before A26 and A28, whereas before they ran after A28 and before A29, leaving A29 to follow A28 directly. The order change is shown directly by the four relation comparisons, which draw on all 200 traces in each window and leave co-occurrence untouched, and the routing, distance and position shifts all point the same way; every difference is larger than in about 199 of 200 random re-splits. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, LAG002, LAG004, LAG005, LAG009, LAG013, LAG016, LAG006, POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004]

Limitations: the new order dominates but does not replace the old one — about 20 of 100 later-window traces still show A26/A28 before A32/A33, and A26 still follows A25 directly in about 18 of 100 A25 transitions, so both behaviours coexist. The main competing explanation is sampling: with 200 traces per window, variant frequencies can differ between windows without any change, and a variant carrying the new order could have been present but rare before; the consistency across order, routing, distance and position indicators makes this unlikely but cannot exclude it. The relation, routing, distance and position items also partly reflect the same traces, so they corroborate rather than independently confirm the reorder, and the higher participation of A23 and the routing shift at A22 remain separate changes this hypothesis does not cover.
