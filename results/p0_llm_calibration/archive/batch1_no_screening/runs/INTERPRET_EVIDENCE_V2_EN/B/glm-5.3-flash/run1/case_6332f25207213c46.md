### Observed differences

- All four activities A29, A30, A31 and A32 run far more often in the later window: A29 and A30 appear in about 90 in 100 traces, up from about 58 in 100; A31 and A32 appear in about 88 in 100, up from about 43 in 100. None of them repeats within a trace in either window. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004]

- The clearest behavioural difference is how these four activities combine. Before, a trace contained either the pair A29/A30 (about 58 in 100) or the pair A31/A32 (about 43 in 100), and no trace contained activities from both pairs. In the later window, about 79 in 100 traces contain both pairs, always with A29 or A30 before A31 or A32; about 12 in 100 still run only A29/A30 and about 10 in 100 only A31/A32. [REL009, REL010, REL011, REL012, REL013, REL014]

- Within each pair the internal order stays mixed: in the later window A29 precedes A30 in about 44 in 100 traces and follows it in about 47; A31 precedes A32 in about 51 and follows it in about 38. [REL009, REL014]

- Routing around A25 and A26 changed consistently with this. From A25 (present in every trace), outgoing flow was previously spread over A29–A32 (roughly 19–34% each) and now goes about 90% to A29 or A30, with only about 5% each to A31 or A32. From A29 and from A30, the transitions into A26 nearly vanish (from about 42–58% to about 6–8%), replaced by transitions into A31 and A32 (about 17–27%). [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003]

- Event distances grew: the gap from A25 to A26 was always 3 steps before and is 5 steps in about 79 in 100 later traces (3 steps in the remaining ~21 in 100); the gaps from A25 to A31/A32 and from A29/A30 to A26 moved from 1–2 steps to mostly 3–4 steps. Relative positions shift only slightly (A25 and A29/A30 slightly earlier, A26 and A31/A32 slightly later), consistent with more activities executing between them. [LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG005, POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006]

- Every difference above is larger than in about 995 of 1,000 random re-splits of the pooled traces, so none is typical of sampling noise alone. [OCC001, DIR002, POS003, REL010, LAG001]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the segment between A25 and A26 changed from an exclusive choice between two two-activity blocks into a sequence running both blocks: before, a trace executed either A29 and A30 (in either order) or A31 and A32 (in either order) between A25 and A26; in the later window it typically runs the A29/A30 pair first and then the A31/A32 pair, with A26 following. [REL010, REL011, REL012, REL013, LAG001]

- The relational evidence shows this directly: cross-pair co-occurrence rose from 0 of 200 traces to about 79 in 100, always ordered A29/A30 before A31/A32, which the earlier windows' complete exclusivity rules out as an alternative arrangement. [REL010, REL011, REL012, REL013]
- The successor changes match: A25 now feeds almost exclusively into A29/A30, and A29/A30 now lead to A31/A32 instead of A26. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003]
- The distance changes match quantitatively: the 5-step A25–A26 gap in about 79 in 100 traces equals the share of traces running both pairs, and the residual 3-step gap (about 21 in 100) equals the share still running a single pair. [LAG001, REL010]

Limitations: with 200 traces per window, variant frequencies can differ between windows without any change, and the exact shares (for example the earlier roughly 58/42 split between the two blocks) carry sampling uncertainty — although the complete absence of cross-pair co-occurrence before (0 of 200 traces) makes pure sampling an unlikely explanation for the main shift. The occurrence, relation, routing and lag items partly describe the same trace-level behaviour, so they corroborate one change rather than acting as independent confirmations, and the evidence covers only A25, A26 and A29–A32. [OCC001, REL010, DIR002, LAG001]
