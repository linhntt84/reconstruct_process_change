### Observed differences

- In the later window, the routing out of A15 has flipped: about 68 in 100 observed outgoing transitions of A15 go to A18 (about 23 in 100 before), while transitions to A16 fell to about 33 in 100 (about 78 in 100 before) [DIR001].
- Participation shifted with the same size: A16 and A17 each appear in about 33 in 100 later traces versus about 78 in 100 earlier (about 65 vs 155 of 200 traces), while A18 and A19 each rise to about 68 in 100 from about 23 in 100 (about 135 vs 45 of 200) [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004].
- The four activities behave as two mutually exclusive pairs in both windows: A16 and A17 always occur together and never occur together with A18 or A19, and A18 and A19 always occur together [REL005, REL006, REL007, REL009, REL010, REL012].
- A15 is present in every trace in both windows and always precedes the branch activity that follows it: A15-before-A16 and A15-before-A18 account for all of their co-occurrences before and after [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004].
- A23 is present in every trace in both windows; its co-occurrence with A16/A17 fell from about 78 to about 33 in 100 traces, while its co-occurrence with A18/A19 rose from about 23 to about 68 in 100, tracking the branch shift [REL008, REL011, REL013, REL014].
- Ordering between the branch activities and A23 is not fixed: when A16 and A23 co-occur, A16 lies entirely before A23 in roughly 6 of 10 cases in both windows, with the remainder showing interleaved or repeated order [REL008, REL011].
- Every listed difference is about 0.45 on the participation/co-occurrence scale and exceeds the difference seen in about 199 of 200 random re-splits of the pooled data, so shifts of this size are unusual for samples of 200 traces per window [OCC001, DIR001, REL006].

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the choice point after A15 shifted: in the later window, about two thirds of executions route from A15 into the A18–A19 branch, whereas before, about three quarters routed into the A16–A17 branch. A23's behaviour changed only in which branch it accompanies, since it appears in every trace throughout [DIR001, OCC001, OCC003, REL008, REL013].

The change is observed directly at the decision point in A15's outgoing-transition shares, and the surrounding indicators are consistent with a redistribution between two mutually exclusive branches rather than a change in which activities exist: each pair always co-occurs internally, never crosses, and A15 precedes whichever branch follows [DIR001, REL005, REL006, REL012]. The differences are atypical for random re-splits of this sample size, which weakens pure sampling noise as an explanation [OCC001, DIR001].

Limitations: all items describe the same underlying branch shift, so their agreement is not independent confirmation, and both branches are present in both windows — the evidence shows a change in routing frequencies, not the addition or removal of a path. An important competing explanation is a shift in the mix of trace variants between the two finite windows (for example, a different case mix) without any structural change; this cannot be ruled out, even though a difference of this magnitude is rare under random re-splits [OCC001, DIR001].
