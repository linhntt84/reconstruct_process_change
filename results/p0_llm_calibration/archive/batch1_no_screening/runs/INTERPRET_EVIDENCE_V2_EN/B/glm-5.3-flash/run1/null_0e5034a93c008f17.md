### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, routing out of A24 was dominated by A30: about 64 in 100 observed outgoing transitions of A24 went to A30, with about 17 in 100 to A21 and about 15 in 100 to A22. In the later window, A30 received only about 42 in 100, while A21 rose to about 29 in 100 and A22 to about 27 in 100. [DIR001]
- The successor profile of A24 differs far more between the windows than random re-splits of the same traces typically do: only about 1 in 200 reference comparisons showed a larger difference (empirical strength about 0.995). [DIR001]
- Rare successors of A24 — A04, A05, A06, A29 and A45, each about 1 in 100 transitions earlier — were not observed in the later window, while A01 and A10 each appear at about 1 in 100. With roughly 100 observed transitions per window, such single-observation components can appear or disappear by chance alone. [DIR001]
- Fewer outgoing transitions of A24 were observed in the later window (83 versus 102). These shares are computed over observed outgoing transitions of A24, not over all cases, so they describe routing among the cases that reached A24. [DIR001]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that routing after A24 was redirected: in the later window, flow from A24 went to A30 considerably less often (about 42 in 100 observed transitions, down from about 64) and to A21 and A22 considerably more often (A21 up from about 17 to about 29; A22 up from about 15 to about 27). The supporting signs are that this indicator measures A24's outgoing transitions directly, the redistribution is internally consistent (the share lost by A30 roughly matches the shares gained by A21 and A22 together), and the difference exceeds almost all random re-splits of the pooled data. [DIR001]

The limitations are that this rests on a single indicator backed by moderate counts (102 versus 83 observed transitions), so each share is estimated imprecisely, and the disappearance and appearance of the rare successors (about 1 in 100 each) is compatible with sampling variation. An important competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is a shift in the mix of cases reaching A24 between the windows: sampling differences in variant frequencies could make the successor shares differ even if the underlying routing structure did not change, although the high empirical strength makes pure sampling noise an unlikely full explanation.
