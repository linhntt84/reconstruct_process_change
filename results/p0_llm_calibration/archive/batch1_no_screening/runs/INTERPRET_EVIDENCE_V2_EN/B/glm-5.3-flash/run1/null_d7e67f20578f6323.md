### Observed differences

- In the later window, A45's outgoing traffic concentrates much more heavily on A46: about 89 in 100 of A45's observed outgoing transitions go to A46, up from about 75 in 100 before. The main alternatives lose share — A08 falls from about 8 to about 2 in 100 and A10 from about 5 to about 2 in 100, with the remaining targets (A02, A04, A06) each at only about 1–4 in 100 [DIR001].
- The A45→A46 pair is also positionally tighter: in the later window, A46 immediately follows A45 in about 90 in 100 occurrences of the pair (within five steps), up from about 76 in 100, while occurrences separated by one intervening event fall from about 25 to about 11 in 100 [LAG001].
- Both differences stand out against random re-splits of the pooled traces: each exceeds about 199 of 200 reference comparisons. The routing difference is small on the indicator's own cosine scale (about 0.007, because A46 dominates both profiles), but the reference comparisons show it is still unusually large for an unchanged process. Each indicator rests on roughly 200 observations per window (201 outgoing transitions of A45; 200 occurrences of the pair), so these are not small-sample proportions [DIR001, LAG001].

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that, in the later window, A45 is more often routed directly to A46, while transitions from A45 to other activities — especially A08 and A10 — are used less often. The outgoing-transition shares of A45 show this routing shift directly [DIR001], and the positional evidence is consistent with it: the A45→A46 pair is more often immediately adjacent rather than one step apart [LAG001]. Both differences are larger than in about 199 of 200 random re-splits and are backed by about 200 observations per window, so pure sampling noise is an unlikely explanation [DIR001, LAG001].

Limitations: the two indicators plausibly capture the same underlying behaviour — direct succession of A46 after A45 — so they should not be counted as independent confirmations. The evidence also cannot fully rule out a sampling difference in variant frequencies (about 1 in 200 random re-splits look this extreme), where a variant with direct A45→A46 succession happens to be more represented in the later window. Finally, only A45's outgoing transitions were profiled, so the evidence does not show whether the displaced alternative routes were rerouted elsewhere, skipped, or merely became rarer [DIR001, LAG001].
