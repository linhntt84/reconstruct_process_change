### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the ordering around A36 and A37 reversed: in the earlier window the most frequent transitions read A32 → A36 → A37 → A33, with A33 two positions after A36, whereas in the later window they read A32 → A37 → A36 → A33, with A33 immediately after A36. This is an order hypothesis, and it is shown directly by relational and positional evidence that all points the same way: A32 now leads to A37 more often and to A36 less often, A37 now leads to A36 more often and to A33 less often, A36 now leads to A33 more often and to A37 less often, and both position gaps (A32–A36 and A36–A33) shifted by one step in the matching direction. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, LAG001, LAG002]

The routing and gap items describe the same small cluster of activities and likely the same traces, so they corroborate but do not independently confirm one another; the shift is also a redistribution rather than a complete rewiring, since A32 still goes directly to A36 in about 22% of its transitions and A36 still goes to A37 in about 38%, and the rare new edges rest on only one or two observations. An important competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that both orderings already existed and only their frequencies shifted between the two 200-trace samples — sampling differences in variant frequencies could contribute to the pattern, although the size and consistency of the shifts make pure sampling noise unlikely.
