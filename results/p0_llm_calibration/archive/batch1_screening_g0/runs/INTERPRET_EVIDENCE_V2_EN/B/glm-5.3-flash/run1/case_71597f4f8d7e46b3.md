### Observed differences

- In the earlier window the process was highly uniform: A26, A27 and A28 each occurred in essentially every trace, A26 preceded A27 in 99 in 100 cases and A27 preceded A28 in 99 in 100, with A28 following A26 by two steps in about 97 in 100 cases. [REL007, REL012, LAG001]
- In the later window, A27 is skipped in about 39 in 100 cases (participation fell from 100% to 61%, i.e. from 200 to 122 of 200 traces), while A26 and A28 still occur in essentially every case. [OCC002, REL007, REL012, POS002]
- Four activities not observed earlier — A32, A33, A38 and A39 (0–1 of 200 traces before) — each now appear in about 40 in 100 later cases (79–81 of 200 traces), and they co-occur with one another at that same rate, so they run together as a block. [OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, REL023, REL028, REL032]
- The block sits between A26 and A28: A26 precedes each of the four in about 39 in 100 cases, each precedes A28 in about 40 in 100, the gap from A26 to A28 stretches from two steps (about 97 in 100 before) to five steps in about 37 in 100 later cases, and the four activities sit near the middle of the trace (mean position about 0.50). A26's outgoing transitions split from about 99 in 100 going to A27 to about 59 in 100 to A27 and 39 in 100 to the four new activities. [REL008, REL019, LAG001, POS004, DIR002]
- The new block and A27 are near-mutually exclusive (co-occurrence in at most about 2 in 100 later cases), and A27's own routing is unchanged when it does occur — about 97% of its outgoing transitions still go to A28. [REL013, REL016, DIR003]
- A second, more thinly supported difference concerns the tail of the process: A04 participation halved (27% to 13%; 54 to 26 traces) and it now occurs at earlier positions (mean position 0.84 to 0.48); the former A45 → A04 → A46 tail largely disappeared, with A45→A04 falling from 26% to under 1% of A45's outgoing transitions while A45→A46 rose from 74% to 93%, and A04's outgoing transitions, formerly 88% to A46, are now spread thinly over many targets. The new activities also precede A45/A46 in about 17–18 in 100 later cases. [OCC001, POS001, DIR001, DIR008, REL026]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that, in the later window, about 4 in 10 cases take an alternative route that skips A27 and instead runs a block of four activities — A32, A33, A38 and A39 — between A26 and A28, while the original A26 → A27 → A28 route continues in the remaining cases. This rests on direct relational and positional evidence rather than frequency shifts alone: A26's successor shares split between A27 and the new activities, the new activities occur almost only in traces without A27, they sit after A26 and before A28, and the A26-to-A28 distance grows from two to five steps in about 37 in 100 later cases, matching a four-activity detour. [DIR002, REL008, REL019, LAG001, REL013, DIR003]

The many qualifying items collapse into about two distinct behavioural differences — the alternative route around A27 and the A04/A45 tail change — which is clearly more than the 0.76 items expected by chance among the 153 tested units; each item's difference is also larger than in 199 of 200 random re-splits, though that alone cannot separate a real change from a rare variant. [OCC002, OCC004, OCC001, DIR008]

Limitations: the evidence cannot show whether the alternative route is newly added or a pre-existing variant whose case mix shifted between the windows, although zero block-cases among 200 earlier traces makes pure sampling fluctuation implausible for the main pattern; the internal order of the four new activities varies, with both directions appearing in their pairwise relations, so the block's sequence is not fixed; and the A04 change rests on only 26 later traces with a fragmented successor profile and one lag comparison based on 4 observations, so its details could partly reflect small-sample variation in variant frequencies. [OCC004, REL023, DIR001, LAG002]
