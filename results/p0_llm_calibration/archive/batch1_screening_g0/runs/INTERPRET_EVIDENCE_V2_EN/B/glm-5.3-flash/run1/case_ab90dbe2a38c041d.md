### Observed differences

- The clearest change is an order reversal around A26, A28 and the pair A32/A33. In the earlier window A26 came before A32 in about 99 of 100 traces containing both (and before A33 in about 99.5 of 100); in the later window the order is reversed, with A32 before A26 in about 78 of 100 and A33 before A26 in about 79 of 100, while the activities still always co-occur. [REL001, REL002]
- The same reversal appears against A28: A28 preceded A32 and A33 in about 99 of 100 traces before, whereas in the later window A32/A33 precede A28 in about 77 of 100. [REL003, REL004]
- Step distances moved consistently with this: A25 reached A26 after 1 step in about 97 of 100 cases before but after 3 steps in about 75 of 100 in the later window; A25 reached A32 and A33 after 4–5 steps before but after 1–2 steps after (about 80 in 100); A25 reached A28 after 3 steps before versus 5 after; A28 reached A29 after 3 steps before versus 1 step after (about 75 in 100); A26 reached A29 after 5 steps before versus 3 after. [LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006, LAG009]
- Successor shares moved the same way: A25's observed outgoing transitions went from about 97% leading to A26 to about 39% to A33, 38% to A32 and 18% to A26; A28's went from roughly 56% to A32 and 42% to A33 to about 74% to A29; A32 and A33, which previously rarely led to A26, now do so in about 38% of their observed outgoing transitions, while their transitions to A29 dropped (A32 about 41% to 6%, A33 about 57% to 14%). [DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005]
- Typical event positions shifted accordingly: A26 and A28 now occur later in the trace (mean relative position about 0.43 to 0.49 and 0.49 to 0.55), A32 and A33 earlier (about 0.54 to 0.47); these are positions among events, not clock times. [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004]
- Participation in A23 rose from about 42 in 100 traces to about 59 in 100 (83 versus 118 traces). [OCC001]
- Routing out of A22 shifted: A22→A24 fell from about 34% to 10% of observed outgoing transitions while A22→A30 rose from about 43% to 63%, and observed A22→A24 pairs within five steps fell from 35 to 13. [DIR001, LAG001]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the segment A32–A33 was relocated: in the later window it runs directly after A25 and before A26, whereas it previously ran after A28 and before A29. The order change is observed directly on four activity pairs (A26/A32, A26/A33, A28/A32, A28/A33 each flipping from roughly 99 in 100 one way to roughly 77–79 in 100 the other), and the surrounding evidence lines up with two activities being inserted between A25 and A26: A25→A26 and A25→A28 each shifted about two steps later, A26→A29 and A28→A29 shifted earlier, A28 now leads almost always to A29, and A32/A33 now typically lead to A26. Even after grouping items that describe the same behaviour, at least three distinct differences appear (the segment move, the higher participation in A23, the routing shift at A22 away from A24 towards A30), clearly more than the roughly 0.7 items expected by chance when nothing has changed, and every flagged difference is larger than in about 995 of 1000 random re-splits. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, LAG002, LAG003, LAG006, LAG009, OCC001, DIR001]

Limitations remain: the windows are finite samples, so a shift in the mix of process variants — for instance a variant with A32/A33 early that was rare before and dominant after — could produce the same picture and cannot be ruled out; the A23 increase and the A22 routing shift may be part of the same restructuring or independent changes; and some lag results about A32/A33 reaching A22/A24 rest on only about 30 observations in the later window and are weak. All distance and position statements concern event order within traces, not clock time. [LAG011, LAG012, LAG014, LAG015]
