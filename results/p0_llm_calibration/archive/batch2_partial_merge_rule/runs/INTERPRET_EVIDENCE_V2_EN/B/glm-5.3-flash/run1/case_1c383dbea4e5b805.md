### Observed differences

- In the later window, the execution order of two groups of activities is reversed relative to the earlier window. Previously, A32 and A33 came before A26 and A28 in about 97–100 in 100 traces (A32 before A28 in essentially all of them); in the later window, A26 and A28 come before A32 and A33 in about 97–98 in 100 traces. All four activities still occur together in every trace in both windows, so participation did not change — only their sequence did. Each of these items rests on about 200 traces per window. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004]
- Routing moved consistently with this reversal. In the later window, A25's outgoing transitions go almost exclusively to A26 (96 in 100), where before they went mainly to A32 (55 in 100) and A33 (45 in 100). A28 now leads to A32 or A33 (about 97 in 100) where before it led to A29 (98 in 100). A32 and A33 now lead mainly to each other and to A29 (about 47–50 in 100 each), where before roughly 44–54 in 100 of their transitions led to A26. [DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006]
- Event positions and step distances agree: A26 and A28 now appear earlier in the trace (mean relative position about 0.49 to 0.44 and 0.57 to 0.50) while A32 and A33 appear later (about 0.45 to 0.54–0.55). A25→A26 is now typically 1 step apart (96 in 100) versus 3 before, A25→A28 is 3 versus 5, A25→A32/A33 is 4–5 versus 1–2, A26→A29 is 5 versus 3, A28→A29 is 3 versus 1, and A32/A33→A29 is 1–2 versus 4–5. [POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, LAG003, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006, LAG007, LAG008, LAG009, LAG010]
- A second, weaker difference concerns A02: in the later window it appears in fewer traces (about 14 in 100 versus 23 before) and later within them, its outgoing transitions spread from mostly A15 (89 in 100) across many targets (A15 only 38 in 100), and A14's transitions to A02 fall from 20 to 5 in 100. These proportions rest on only about 26–46 traces before and 14–27 after, so they are weakly supported. [DIR001, DIR002, POS001, LAG001, LAG002]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that, in the later window, the segment containing A26 and A28 runs before the segment containing A32 and A33, whereas in the earlier window it ran after it, with A25 preceding both segments and A29 following them in both windows. The order change is shown directly by the relational indicators, which reverse almost completely while co-occurrence stays at 100 in 100, and the routing, position and step-distance items — each resting on roughly 190–200 observations per window — all point the same way; these agreeing items largely reflect one underlying difference rather than independent confirmations. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, LAG003, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006, LAG007, LAG008, LAG009, LAG010]

The distinct differences observed (the segment-order reversal and the A02 change) exceed the roughly 0.62 items expected by chance in an unchanged process, and every item's difference is larger than in 199 of 200 reference comparisons, so sampling alone is a poor explanation for the main finding. [DIR001, DIR002, POS001, LAG001, LAG002, REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004]

A competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is a shift in the mix of process variants between the windows — for example, a variant in which A26 and A28 run early could dominate the later sample — so the evidence describes how the executed traces behave, not that a single flow definition was modified. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004]

The A02-related differences rest on about 14–46 observations per window and could reflect sampling differences in variant frequencies; POS and LAG use event positions rather than clock time, so no conclusion about timing is implied. [DIR001, DIR002, POS001, LAG001, LAG002]
