### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility of a structural rearrangement in which the block of four activities A35, A36, A41 and A42, which previously ran between A25 and A27, now runs between A26 and A30: A25 leads directly to A27, A26 leads into the block, and the block exits to A30. The order reversal is shown directly — co-occurrence stays at 100 in 100 traces while the before/after order flips almost completely — and it is matched by the successor changes at A25, A26 and the four block activities, by the boundary lags flipping between immediate and five positions apart, and by the consistent position shifts. [REL010, REL011, REL012, REL013, REL014, REL015, REL016, REL017, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006, DIR007, LAG006, LAG007, LAG008, LAG009, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006, POS007]

A second, distinct difference is A10's near-disappearance: executed in about 35 in 100 traces before and about 7–8 in 100 after, consistent with that part of the process being skipped or run far less often; the evidence does not show whether this belongs to the same rearrangement or is an independent change, and the after-window lag profiles for A10 rest on only 1–5 observations. [OCC001, DIR001, DIR008, REL001, REL009, LAG003, LAG004, LAG005]

The two distinct differences clearly exceed the roughly 0.83 qualifying items expected by chance across the 166 tested units, and every item's difference is larger than in about 199 of 200 random re-splits; an important competing explanation that remains is a shift in the mix of process variants — for example, two coexisting variants with different block placements sampled in different proportions — which finite 200-trace windows cannot fully rule out, although near-complete flips on full support in both windows make pure sampling variation implausible.
