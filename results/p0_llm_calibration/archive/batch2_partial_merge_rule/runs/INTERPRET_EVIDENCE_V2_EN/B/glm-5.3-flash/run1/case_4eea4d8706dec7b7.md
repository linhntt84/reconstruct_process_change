### Observed differences

- In the later window, the four activities that run in every case (A15, A16, A18, A19) take place much earlier: their mean event position falls from about 0.63–0.64 to about 0.39–0.40 on a 0–1 scale of position within the case, while they still appear in 100% of traces in both windows. [POS002, POS003, POS005, POS006]
- The four optional activities A14, A17, A20 and A21 move the opposite way: mean position rises from about 0.33–0.34 to about 0.68–0.70. Their participation stays roughly similar (A14 about 25→23 in 100 cases, A17 about 20→29, A20 about 32→28, A21 about 24→21), and together they still appear in about 200 of 200 traces per window, consistent with one of the four running in each case. [POS001, POS004, POS007, POS008, DIR001, DIR004, DIR007, DIR008]
- Every measured order relation between the two groups reverses: A22 precedes A15 in 100% of traces before and follows it in 100% after; A26 precedes A15 before and follows it after; A14 runs before A15 in about 25 in 100 cases before and after it in about 23 in 100; A20 runs before A22 in about 32 in 100 cases before and after it in about 28 in 100. [REL010, REL011, REL001, REL029]
- The successor sets of A26 and A37 swap between the groups: A26, previously followed by A15/A16/A18/A19 (about 24–27% of its outgoing transitions each), is now followed by A17/A14/A21/A20 (about 21–29% each), while A37 switches from A20/A21/A14/A17 to A16/A18/A15/A19. [DIR009, DIR010]
- A14, A17, A20 and A21, which previously led exclusively to A22, now lead exclusively to A27; A15, A16, A18 and A19 no longer show A27 as a successor (previously about 23–29% of outgoing transitions) and now send about 18–31% to A22. [DIR001, DIR004, DIR007, DIR008, DIR002, DIR003, DIR005, DIR006]
- A22 and A26 each occur about 0.10 later in the case (mean 0.37→0.47 and 0.56→0.66), and event distances change accordingly: A26 and A27 are 2 events apart in the later window versus 5 before, while A37 and A22 are 5 events apart versus 2 before. [POS009, POS010, LAG001, LAG002]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that two blocks of activities exchanged their places in the case sequence. Before, the observed transitions and orders fit a pattern in which A37 is followed by one of A14/A17/A20/A21, then A22, then an unobserved middle segment, then A26 followed by A15/A16/A18/A19 and A27; in the later window the same case appears to run A37, then A15/A16/A18/A19, then A22, the middle segment, A26, one of A14/A17/A20/A21, and A27. [DIR010, DIR009, DIR001, DIR002, REL010, REL011, LAG001, LAG002]

The support is direct and consistent: the order reversals are complete (100% of traces for A22/A15 and A26/A15 in both directions), the successor replacements are total rather than partial, and the distance changes (5→2 and 2→5 events) are exactly what exchanging a four-activity block with a single-activity block predicts. All 54 qualifying items reflect this one restructuring rather than many independent differences; with about 0.57 items expected by chance across 115 tested units, and every item's difference exceeding about 199 of 200 random re-splits, chance is an implausible explanation for the pattern as a whole. [REL010, DIR009, LAG002, POS009]

Limitations remain: the evidence cannot rule out that both orderings already existed as process variants and only their sampling proportions shifted between the two 200-trace windows — a variant-mix shift would look identical to this reordering. The participation shifts of the optional activities (for example A17 from about 20 to about 29 in 100 cases) are within the range sampling differences can produce. The segment between A22 and A26 is not covered by any item, so accompanying changes there can be neither confirmed nor excluded, and the positional evidence refers to event order rather than clock time. [POS004, DIR004, POS009]
