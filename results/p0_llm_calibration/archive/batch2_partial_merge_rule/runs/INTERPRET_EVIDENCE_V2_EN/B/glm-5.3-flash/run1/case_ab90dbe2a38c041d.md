### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the block formed by A32 and A33 executes in a different place in the later window: it now runs directly after A25 and before A26, whereas before it ran after A28 and before A29 — in effect, the A32/A33 block and the A26–A28 segment swapped their sequence positions, and A29, which previously followed A32/A33, now follows A28 directly. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, LAG001, LAG003, LAG004, LAG005, LAG007, LAG010, LAG011, POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004]

The support is direct and consistent: the four pairwise orderings reverse almost completely (about 97–99 in 100 one way before, about 96–98 in 100 the other way after); the successor changes at A25, A28, A32 and A33 point to the same rearrangement; the step-distance and position shifts match it; every item's difference exceeded 199 of 200 random re-split comparisons, 21 items were flagged against roughly 0.57 expected without change, and each item used rests on about 200 observations per window. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG005, LAG007, LAG010, LAG011, POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004]

An important competing explanation remains: the evidence shows the dominant executed order per window but cannot distinguish a re-routing within one variant from a shift in the mix of two pre-existing variants, since variant frequencies can differ between finite windows even without any structural change — although each window is near-deterministic (old order about 99 in 100 before, new order about 96–98 in 100 after), so such a frequency shift would have to be extreme. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004] Positions and lags are measured in event steps, so the change is established in sequence placement, not in clock time.
