### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that, in the later window, the group A36, A37, A42, A43 executes between A25 and A28 near the start of the trace, whereas before it executed after A26, and that both this group and A27 now run in every trace: before, a trace ran either A27 or the group after A26 (co-occurrence about 1–2 in 100), while in the later window every trace runs the group early and A27 after A26. The distinct differences — the group's participation and placement, A27's participation, and A40's participation — clearly exceed the roughly one item expected by chance in an unchanged process. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC005, OCC006, REL013, DIR001, DIR002, LAG001]

The placement aspect is shown directly by relational and positional evidence, not only by frequency shifts: the group's order versus A26 and A28 reverses (about 53 in 100 before to about 97–99 in 100 after, in the opposite direction), A25's successor changes from A28 to the group, the group's successor changes from A31 to A28, and the A25-to-A28 distance grows from one to five positions — matching the four activities now sitting in between. The occurrence, position, routing, co-occurrence and distance items all describe one underlying difference, not independent confirmations. [REL007, REL017, DIR001, DIR003, LAG001, POS004]

Limitations: each window is a 200-trace sample, so a shift in the mix of process variants — the group-first behaviour simply appearing in all later traces while absent from the earlier sample — remains a competing explanation the evidence cannot fully exclude, although the magnitude and consistency of the order reversals make pure sampling noise unlikely. The internal order of the four activities stays mixed in both windows, so no fixed sequence within the group can be inferred, and A40's modest participation increase is not accounted for by this hypothesis.
