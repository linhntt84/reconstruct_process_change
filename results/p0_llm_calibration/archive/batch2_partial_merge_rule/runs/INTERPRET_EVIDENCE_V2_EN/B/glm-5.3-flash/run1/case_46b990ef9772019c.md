- In the later window, each of A33, A34, A36, A37, A38, A39, A40 and A41 runs in essentially every trace (199 of 200), up from roughly 47–55 in 100 traces before; each occurs about once per case with almost no repetition in either window. ### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the segment between A30 and A31, which before executed either the A33/A34/A36/A37 package (about 54 in 100 cases) or the A38–A41 package (about 46 in 100), now executes both packages back to back in nearly every case — A30 first, then A33/A34/A36/A37 in no fixed internal order, then A38–A41 likewise, then A31. [REL020, REL035, REL001, REL016, DIR001]

The judgement rests on direct relational evidence rather than frequency shifts alone: cross-package co-occurrence rose from about 1–4 of 200 traces to about 99 in 100 with a stable package order, and the routing changes (A30 now feeds almost only the first package, which now feeds the second and no longer A31 directly) plus the position and gap shifts all point the same way. The many items collapse into about four distinct behavioural differences — clearly more than the roughly 1 expected by chance at these thresholds — and the key relations are measured on all 200 traces per window. [REL020, REL027, DIR001, DIR003, LAG006, POS002]

An important competing explanation remains: the both-packages behaviour may have existed before as a rare variant (about 1–2 in 100 traces) that the later window simply selects almost exclusively; with 200-trace samples, such a wholesale switch in variant frequencies cannot be distinguished from a modified process definition. In addition, within-package order is roughly balanced in both windows, so each package's internal structure stays undetermined, and a few lag profiles rest on only 1–3 observations and were not relied on. [REL020, REL017, LAG001, LAG007]
