### Observed differences

- In the later window, A21 and A22 are executed far more often: each appears in about 80 in 100 traces, up from about 23 in 100 earlier (roughly 45 of 200 traces rising to about 160). [OCC001, OCC002]
- A23 and A24 show the mirror-image decline: from about 78 in 100 traces to about 20 in 100 (roughly 155 of 200 falling to about 40). [OCC003, OCC004]
- The routing out of A20 flipped: earlier, about 77% of A20's observed outgoing transitions led to A23 (about 22% to A21); in the later window, about 80% lead to A21 and about 20% to A23. [DIR001]
- Order relations flipped with them. Earlier, A20 preceded A23 and A23 preceded A24 in about 77 in 100 traces, and A23/A24 co-occurred with A28 in about 77 in 100; in the later window, A20 precedes A21 in about 80 in 100 traces, A21 precedes A22 in about 80 in 100, and A21/A22 co-occur with A28 in about 80 in 100 (A21 before A28 in about 48 in 100, interleaved or repeated in about 33 in 100). [REL003, REL012, REL013, REL001, REL005, REL008, REL011]
- Where A23 and A24 still occur in the later window, their routing is essentially unchanged (A23 still leads mainly to A24 at about 95%, A24 mainly to A28 at about 98%); the small profile shifts mainly reflect far fewer observations behind them (about 156 down to about 41). [DIR002, DIR003]
- A20 and A28 themselves appear in essentially every trace in both windows, so the difference is confined to the segment between them. [REL001, REL008]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the two-step segment A23 → A24, which in the earlier window ran between A20 and A28 in about three quarters of cases, was largely replaced in the later window by the segment A21 → A22, with the old branch surviving as a minority path in about 20 in 100 cases. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, DIR001, REL012, REL005]

The support is direct and consistent: the successor change at A20 is observed in the routing data rather than inferred from frequencies alone, the relational items place the new segment in the same position the old one occupied (A20 before A21 before A22, followed by A28), and the participation swaps match almost exactly at about 57–58 percentage points in each direction. Screening 94 units would be expected to yield about 0.47 qualifying items by chance if nothing had changed; here 19 items qualify and form one coherent pattern across occurrence, routing and order indicators — increased execution of A21/A22, decreased execution of A23/A24, a rerouted A20, and flipped precedence relations — together with residual A23 → A24 → A28 routing that is intact where A23/A24 still occur, which is not the isolated-hit pattern an unchanged process typically produces. [DIR001, REL001, REL005, REL008, DIR002, OCC001, OCC003]

A limitation the evidence cannot resolve: both branches may have existed throughout, with only their frequencies shifting — for example because the two windows captured a different mix of cases — rather than the process structure itself having been modified. With 200 traces per window, variant frequencies can differ markedly by sampling alone, although a shift of this size, appearing simultaneously in participation, routing and precedence, is unusual for random re-splits of unchanged behaviour. [OCC001, DIR001, REL005]
