### Observed differences

- The relative order of two groups of activities is reversed in the later window. Before, A26 and A27 each ran before each of A35, A36, A41 and A42 in about 96–99 in 100 traces; in the later window, each of those four runs before A26 and A27 in about 96–98 in 100 traces. All of these activities still occur together in essentially every trace in both windows, so participation itself is unchanged. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, REL007, REL008]
- Event positions shift accordingly: the mean relative position of A26 rises from about 0.51 to about 0.62 and A27's from about 0.37 to about 0.48, while A35, A36, A41 and A42 each fall from about 0.58 to about 0.42 (each based on about 200 traces per window). [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006]
- Routing at the boundaries changes in the same direction. A25 passed directly to A27 in about 97 in 100 of its outgoing transitions before; in the later window it passes to A35/A36/A41/A42 (about 22–25 in 100 each) and to A27 only about 2 in 100. A26, which previously spread over A35/A36/A41/A42 (about 24 in 100 each), now passes to A30 in about 94 in 100. Each of A35/A36/A41/A42, which previously passed to A30 in about 21–31 in 100, now passes to A27 (about 17–29 in 100), with the transition to A30 nearly or completely gone. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006]
- Distances change consistently: A27 now follows A25 five steps later in about 97 in 100 traces (previously immediately, about 97 in 100); A30 now immediately follows A26 (about 94 in 100, previously five steps later); and A27 now falls one to four steps after each of A35/A36/A41/A42 in about 195 of 200 traces, where such proximity previously appeared in only 1–2 traces. [LAG001, LAG002, LAG005, LAG007, LAG008]
- A few further gap items fit the same picture (A26 to A41/A42, and the four activities to A30, rarely within five steps in the later window) but rest on only 4–5 after-window observations and are weak on their own. [LAG003, LAG004, LAG006, LAG009, LAG010]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that, in the later window, the group of four activities A35, A36, A41 and A42 — apparently running as a short consecutive sequence — is executed earlier in the case, between A25 and A27, whereas it previously ran after A26; consequently A30, which previously followed that group, now immediately follows A26. This is an order/placement claim, and it is shown directly by relational evidence: all eight pairings between A26/A27 and the four activities reverse almost completely while co-occurrence stays at 100 in 100 [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, REL007, REL008]. The successor changes sit exactly at the boundaries this reordering implies (A25 into the group, the group into A27, A26 into A30) [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006], the positional shifts agree in direction [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006], and the gap changes match, including the A25-to-A27 distance growing to five steps, consistent with a four-activity sequence inserted between them [LAG001, LAG002, LAG005]. The 31 qualifying items far exceed the roughly 0.76 expected for an unchanged process, and although most of them reflect this one underlying difference rather than independent confirmations, they converge on it rather than scattering [DIR001, POS001, REL001, LAG001].

Limitations: the windows are aggregates, so the evidence cannot distinguish a single restructured flow from a shift in the mix of two pre-existing variants (group early versus group late); a change in how often each variant runs would produce the same aggregate pattern. Plain sampling variation is an unlikely explanation for a reversal from about 98 in 100 to about 2 in 100 — each item's difference exceeds about 199 of 200 random re-splits of the pooled data — but details such as the internal ordering of the four activities, and what runs between A27 and A26, are not settled by this evidence, and a few gap items rest on 4–5 observations [LAG003, LAG004, LAG006, LAG009, LAG010].
