### Observed differences

- In the later window, A19 and A20 appear in about 90 and 111 of 200 traces respectively (roughly 45 and 55 in 100), whereas before they each appeared in only 1–2 of 200 traces. They almost never occur together: in the later window about 1 in 100 traces contain both, about 44 in 100 contain only A19 and about 55 in 100 only A20, so they behave as mutually exclusive alternatives. [OCC001, OCC002, REL001]
- The direct transition from A40 to A25 largely disappears: before, about 88 in 100 of A40's observed outgoing transitions went to A25; in the later window that share is under 1 in 100, while about 44 in 100 go to A20 and about 37 in 100 to A19. [DIR004]
- In the later window, A19 and A20 lead almost exclusively to A25: about 97 in 100 of A19's outgoing transitions and about 95 in 100 of A20's go to A25. Their before-window outgoing profiles rest on only 1–2 observations each. [DIR002, DIR003]
- Order relations agree with this: in the later window, A40 precedes A19 in about 45 in 100 traces and A20 in about 55 in 100; A19 precedes A25 in about 43 in 100 and A20 precedes A25 in about 55 in 100. [REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005]
- Positions and gaps (in event positions, not clock time): A19 follows A40 within one position in about 84 in 100 later cases; the gap from A40 to A25 moves from one position (about 88 in 100 before) to two positions (about 80 in 100 later). A25 remains in every trace but its median position shifts slightly later, from about 0.37 to about 0.39 of the trace, while A19 and A20 sit around 36% of the way through the trace. [LAG001, LAG002, POS003, POS001, POS002]
- A04's outgoing routing is redistributed in the later window: new transitions to A19 and A20 (together about 19 in 100 of its roughly 40 outgoing transitions), no observed transitions to A25 or A14, and the share to A46 falling from about 27 in 100 to about 5 in 100. [DIR001]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that, in the later window, flow that previously ran directly from A40 to A25 is routed through one of two alternative activities — A40 → A19 → A25 in roughly 45 of 100 traces and A40 → A20 → A25 in roughly 55 of 100, with A19 and A20 almost never co-occurring. This is supported by direct relational and positional evidence rather than frequency counts alone: A40's outgoing share to A25 collapses while A19 and A20 take over; A19 and A20 in turn lead almost exclusively to A25; the order relations place A40 before A19/A20 and A19/A20 before A25; and the A40-to-A25 gap grows from one event position to two, with A25 shifting slightly later in the trace, consistent with a step now sitting between them. [OCC001, OCC002, REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, LAG001, LAG002, POS003]

The volume of evidence also exceeds what an unchanged process typically produces: the 15 items reduce to about two distinct behavioural differences (the A40–A19/A20–A25 rerouting and the redistribution at A04), against roughly 0.48 items expected by chance among the 96 units tested, and each item's difference exceeds 199 of 200 random re-splits of the pooled traces. [OCC001, OCC002, DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, POS001, POS002, POS003, REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, LAG001, LAG002]

Limitations: the before-window profiles of A19 and A20 rest on 1–2 traces, so their earlier routing is essentially unobserved, and a competing explanation that cannot be fully ruled out is a sampling swing in variant frequencies, with a previously rare variant dominating the later window — though such an imbalance is unusual under random re-splits. The redistribution at A04 is consistent with the same restructuring, since A04 also gains transitions to A19 and A20, but it could be a separate routing change and the evidence does not distinguish the two. The near-zero participation of A19 and A20 before the change point means only that they were hardly observed in that window, not that they were absent from the process. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, OCC001, OCC002]
