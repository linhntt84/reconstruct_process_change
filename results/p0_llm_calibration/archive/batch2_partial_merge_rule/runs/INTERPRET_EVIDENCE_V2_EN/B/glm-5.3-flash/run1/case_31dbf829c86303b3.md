### Observed differences

- **Routing out of A24.** In the earlier window, A24's observed outgoing transitions were spread almost evenly over A16, A17, A18 and A19 (roughly 23–28% each). In the later window, about 99 in 100 of these transitions go to A16 (about 44%) or A17 (about 55%), while continuations to A18 or A19 have nearly disappeared (about 1–2 in 100). [DIR005]

- **Routing out of A16 and A17.** Earlier, about half of the observed outgoing transitions from A16 (52%) and A17 (48%) went directly to A25, with most of the rest going to A18 or A19. In the later window, direct transitions to A25 have almost vanished (about 1–2 in 100) and continuations go to A18 or A19 instead (from A16: about 40% to A18 and 57% to A19; from A17: about 45% to A18 and 54% to A19). [DIR001, DIR002]

- **Routing out of A18 and A19.** Earlier, about half of the outgoing transitions from A18 (48%) and A19 (51%) went directly to A25, with the rest returning to A16 or A17. In the later window, about 98–99 in 100 go to A25, and returns to A16/A17 have practically stopped. [DIR003, DIR004]

- **Distances from A24.** Earlier, each of A16, A17, A18 and A19 followed A24 at 1 event position in about half of the traces where the pair occurs, and at 2 positions in the other half. In the later window, A16 and A17 follow A24 immediately in about 98–99 in 100 of their traces, while A18 and A19 follow at 2 positions in about 98–99 in 100. [LAG005, LAG006, LAG007, LAG008]

- **Distances to A25.** Earlier, A25 followed A16 or A17 at 1 position in about half of the co-occurring traces (52% and 48%) and A18 or A19 at 1 position in about half (48% and 51%). In the later window, A25 follows A16/A17 at 2 positions in about 98–99 in 100 cases, and follows A18/A19 immediately in about 98–99 in 100. [LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004]

- **Participation is broadly stable.** Each of A16–A19 appears in roughly 44–56 in 100 traces in both windows (for example A16 in about 52 of 100 earlier and 45 of 100 later; A19 in about 47 earlier and 56 later), and A24 appears in every trace in both windows. The A16 and A17 counts sum to the 200 traces of each window, as do the A18 and A19 counts, consistent with every trace containing one activity from each pair. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the two steps executed between A24 and A25 are now taken in one fixed order. In the earlier window, traces contained one activity from the pair A16/A17 and one from the pair A18/A19 between A24 and A25 in either order about equally often, so A25 directly followed A16 or A17 in about half of the relevant traces and A18 or A19 in the other half; in the later window, the order is essentially always A24, then A16 or A17, then A18 or A19, then A25. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006, LAG007, LAG008]

- The routing changes are complementary: the transitions that nearly disappear (A24 to A18/A19, A16/A17 to A25, and A18/A19 back to A16/A17) are exactly those a fixed order forbids, while those that become near-exclusive (A24 to A16/A17, A16/A17 to A18/A19, A18/A19 to A25) are exactly those it requires. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005]
- The distance profiles show the same reversal directly: the gap from A24 to A16/A17 changed from an even mix of 1 and 2 positions to 1 position in about 98–99 in 100 traces (and from A16/A17 to A25 from 1 to 2 positions), while the gap from A24 to A18/A19 changed to 2 positions (and to A25 to 1 position). [LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006, LAG007, LAG008]
- Participation of the activities involved barely changed, so the difference lies in ordering and routing rather than in whether the activities run; the 13 items collapse into this single coherent difference, against about 0.35 items expected by chance among the 71 tested units, and every item's difference exceeds 199 of 200 random re-splits of the pooled data. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006, LAG007, LAG008]

- A competing explanation the evidence cannot fully rule out is a shift in the mix of trace variants: the earlier window's even split between the two orders could reflect two coexisting variants rather than a per-case choice, and variant proportions can differ between finite samples even without a structural change — although a swing from about 50/50 to about 98/2 is difficult to attribute to sampling alone. [LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006, LAG007, LAG008]
- The items are not independent confirmations, since routing and distance items about the same activities rest on the same traces (for example, the A16 items both come from the roughly 103 and 89 traces containing A16); distances are measured in event positions rather than clock time, and the later-window pattern of about 98–99 in 100 cannot be distinguished from a strict rule at this sample size. [DIR001, LAG001, DIR003, DIR004]
