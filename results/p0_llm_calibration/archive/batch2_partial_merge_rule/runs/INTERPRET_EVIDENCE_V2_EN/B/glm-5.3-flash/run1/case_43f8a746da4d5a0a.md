### Observed differences

- **The relative order of two activity groups flipped.** In the earlier window, A26 came before A32 in about 99 of 100 traces and before A33 in about 99.5 of 100, and A28 likewise preceded A32 and A33 in about 99 of 100 traces. In the later window the order is reversed: A32 and A33 each precede A26 in about 97–98 of 100 traces and each precede A28 in about 98 of 100. All four activities still occur together in every trace in both windows. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004]

- **Entry and exit routing moved with the reversal.** In the later window, A25's outgoing transitions go to A32 about 48 in 100 times and to A33 about 48 in 100 times, where before they went almost only to A26 (about 98.5 in 100). A28 now leads to A29 in about 96.5 of 100 transitions, where before it led to A32 (about 53 in 100) or A33 (about 45 in 100). A32 and A33, which previously led on to A29 in roughly 45–53 of 100 transitions, now lead about half the time to A26 and half to each other. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004]

- **Step distances shifted consistently.** A25→A26 went from almost always 1 step apart (about 98.5 in 100) to 3 steps apart (about 95.5 in 100); A25→A32 and A25→A33 went from 4–5 steps to 1–2 steps; A28→A29 went from 3 steps to 1 step; A32→A29 and A33→A29 went from 1–2 steps to 4–5 steps; A26→A29 went from 5 steps to 3 steps; A25→A28 went from 3 steps to 5 steps. [LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG005, LAG007, LAG008, LAG009]

- **Positions within the trace moved accordingly.** In the later window A26 (mean relative position about 0.43 to 0.50) and A28 (about 0.50 to 0.57) sit later in the trace, while A32 (about 0.55 to 0.46) and A33 (about 0.55 to 0.45) sit earlier. [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004]

- One distance item (A26→A33) rests on only about 3 after-window observations and was not relied on. [LAG006]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that two segments of the process exchanged their relative order: the block formed by A32 and A33, which in the earlier window ran after A28 and before A29, in the later window runs directly after A25 and before A26, while A29 is now reached from A28 instead of from the A32/A33 pair — roughly A25 → A26 → … → A28 → A32/A33 → A29 before, versus A25 → A32/A33 → A26 → … → A28 → A29 after. The internal structure of each segment is preserved: A32 and A33 still follow one another in either order about half the time, and the A26–A28 spacing stays at two steps (both A25→A26 and A25→A28 distances grew by two steps). [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, LAG001, LAG002]

This rests on direct relational evidence, not on frequency shifts alone: all four cross-pairs reverse order almost completely, co-occurrence remains at 100 in 100 traces so no participation changed, and the successor, distance and position items all move in the directions the swap predicts. The 19 qualifying items far exceed the roughly 0.58 expected by chance without a change, and every item's difference is larger than about 99.5 in 100 random re-splits of the pooled data, so sampling noise alone is an unlikely explanation. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG005, LAG007, LAG008, LAG009]

The main competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that both orderings already existed as process variants and only their frequencies shifted between the windows: each window is a finite sample of 200 traces, and variant frequencies can differ between samples even without a structural change, although random re-splits rarely produce differences this large and this coherent across four order pairs, routing choices and distances. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004]
