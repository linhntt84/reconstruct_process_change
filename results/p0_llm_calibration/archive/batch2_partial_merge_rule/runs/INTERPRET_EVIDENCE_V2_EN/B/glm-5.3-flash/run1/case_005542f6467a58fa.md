### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that A10 took on a much more prominent and earlier role — executed in about 62 in 100 later-window cases versus about 23 before, typically immediately before A16, and reached from A15 in about a third of A15's outgoing transitions — while A19, A20, A25 and A26 are executed in only about 1–3 in 100 cases versus about a quarter before, leaving A28 and A29 to route almost exclusively to A29 and A30. The placement aspect is shown directly by relational and lag evidence (A10 preceding A16 and the A27–A30/A42/A47/A48 block; A10→A16 consecutive in about 97 in 100 observed pairs), not merely by frequency counts. [OCC003, POS001, DIR002, DIR003, LAG001, LAG009, REL002, REL007, REL013, DIR008, DIR009]

The pattern is clearly larger than chance screening noise: about 1.06 qualifying items are expected without change, and the items here collapse into at least two distinct, mutually consistent differences (the A10 cluster and the near-disappearance of A19/A20/A25/A26), each backed by participation, routing and relational indicators with sizable support (A10 in 45 vs 123 traces; the disappearing activities in 47–52 vs 2–5 traces). [OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, OCC007, DIR002, DIR003, LAG001, REL007]

Limitations: near-zero participation shows only that A19, A20, A25 and A26 were almost never observed, not that they were removed from the process, and their routing profiles rest on 2–5 observations and carry little weight; whether the A10 change and the disappearance of these activities are one restructuring or two independent changes cannot be determined. Several items describe the same underlying differences and are not independent confirmations, and sampling differences in variant frequencies between the two 200-trace windows cannot be ruled out, although the size and cross-indicator consistency of the shifts make a pure sampling explanation unlikely.
