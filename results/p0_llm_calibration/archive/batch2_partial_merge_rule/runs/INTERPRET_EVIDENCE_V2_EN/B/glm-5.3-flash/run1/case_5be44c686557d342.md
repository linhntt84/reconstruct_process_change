### Observed differences

- In the later window, A34, A35, A36 and A37 all became near-universal: A36 and A37 rose from about 40 to about 99 in 100 traces, and A34 and A35 from about 60 to essentially every trace; each of them now occurs about once per trace, with almost no repetition [OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005].
- Before, A36/A37 appeared instead of A34/A35 rather than alongside them: the pairs A34–A36, A34–A37, A35–A36 and A35–A37 co-occurred in only about 1 in 100 traces. In the later window they co-occur in about 99 in 100 traces, with A34 or A35 preceding A36 or A37 in about 96–98 in 100 of those cases [REL020, REL021, REL026, REL027].
- The surrounding routing moved in step: of A30's observed outgoing transitions, the share to A34/A35 grew from about 58 to about 98 in 100 while the share to A36/A37 (about 38 in 100) disappeared; A34 and A35 now send about half of their outgoing traffic to A36/A37 instead of to A31; A36/A37 now precede A31 in about 97 in 100 traces; and the gap from A30 to A31 stretched from typically 3 steps to 5 [DIR004, DIR005, DIR006, REL017, REL018, LAG003].
- A10 became far more frequent and later-placed: participation rose from about 22 to about 61 in 100 traces, and its typical relative position moved towards the trace end (lower quartile from about 31% to about 86% of the way through the trace) [OCC001, POS001].
- In the later window A10 typically appears between A43 and A44: A43's outgoing traffic switched from A44 (about 80 in 100) to A10 (about 73 in 100), A10's outgoing traffic concentrated on A44 (about 87 in 100, up from about 31), A10 is immediately followed by A44 in about 99 in 100 observed pairs, and A43 immediately precedes A10 in about 73 in 100 [DIR002, DIR007, LAG001, LAG019].
- A10 now also co-occurs with A30, A31 and A34–A37 in about 60 in 100 traces, typically after them, and precedes A45/A46 in about 55 in 100 traces [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, REL009, REL010, LAG007].
- Smaller shifts: A08 became rarer and its outgoing mix changed (37 vs 16 observed outgoing transitions), and the immediate successions A34→A35 and A45→A46 tightened to nearly all observed cases [DIR001, LAG011, LAG024].

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that, in the later window, the process runs two extended chains that it previously did not. First, A36 and A37 — formerly an alternative executed in about 40 in 100 traces and essentially never together with A34/A35 — are now executed in nearly every trace between the A34/A35 step and A31, so the sequence reads A30 → A34/A35 (in either order) → A36/A37 (in either order) → A31. Second, A10 — formerly present in about 22 in 100 traces — now appears in about 60 in 100 traces on a segment A43 → A10 → A44 (then A45/A46), where A43 previously led to A44 directly [OCC001, OCC004, OCC005, DIR002, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006, DIR007, REL020, REL026, REL027, LAG001, LAG003, LAG019].

This goes beyond a frequency shift: the relational evidence flips directly (pairs that were mutually exclusive become sequential with a stable direction), the successor profiles and step distances agree with the same chains, and the many qualifying items largely reflect these two trace-level differences rather than independent confirmations. With 197 units tested, about 1 item would qualify by chance alone, while two distinct chain-level differences are observed [REL020, REL021, REL026, REL027, DIR005, DIR007, LAG001, LAG019].

An important competing explanation remains: the windows are finite samples, so a shift in the mix of pre-existing variants — for example a full-chain variant that already existed but was rare in the earlier window — could produce the same picture. Some supporting details also rest on few observations: A08's routing on 37 vs 16 transitions, and two lag profiles with only 1 observation in the earlier window, so those specifics are weak [DIR001, LAG012, LAG015].
