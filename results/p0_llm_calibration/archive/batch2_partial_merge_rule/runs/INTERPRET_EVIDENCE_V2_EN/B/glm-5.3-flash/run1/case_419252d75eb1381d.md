### Observed differences

- In the later window, A15, A23, A24 and A25 each appear exactly once in every trace: participation rose from none to 200 of 200 traces, with a mean of 1.0 occurrence per trace and no repetitions. [OCC002, OCC006, OCC007, OCC008]
- A14, A16, A17 and A18, which in the earlier window each ran in about 21, 26, 25 and 29 of 100 traces respectively (41, 51, 50 and 58 of 200 traces), were not observed at all in the later window (0 of 200 traces). [OCC001, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005]
- In the earlier window, exactly one of A14/A16/A17/A18 ran per trace — their shares sum to 200 of 200 traces and no pair of them ever co-occurred — whereas in the later window the four new activities co-occur in every trace. [REL002, REL003, REL004, REL010, REL011, REL012, REL018, REL019, REL025, REL013, REL036, REL040]
- Direct successors changed accordingly: before, A41 was followed by one of the old four (shares about 21–29%) and each old activity led only to A26; in the later window A41 is followed by one of the new four (about 21–28% each), and each new activity is followed by one of the other new activities or by A26 (about 21–34% each). [DIR001, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, DIR009, DIR002, DIR006, DIR007, DIR008]
- Order evidence points the same way: in the later window A41 precedes each of A15/A23/A24/A25 in all traces and each of them precedes A26 in all traces, while the order within the group is split roughly 50/50 (for example, A15 precedes A23 in about 53 of 100 traces, A24 precedes A25 in about 45 of 100). [REL017, REL039, REL042, REL044, REL016, REL038, REL041, REL043, REL013, REL040]
- The event distance from A41 to A26 was 2 steps in all earlier traces and 5 steps in all later traces; A26's mean relative position moved later (about 0.39 to 0.45) and A41's moved earlier (about 0.33 to 0.29). [LAG001, POS009, POS010]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the segment between A41 and A26 was restructured from executing exactly one of four alternative activities (A14, A16, A17 or A18, selected in roughly equal shares) to executing four activities (A15, A23, A24, A25) once each in every trace, in a variable order, between the same two activities. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, OCC007, OCC008, REL013, REL016, REL038, REL039, LAG001]

The support is direct and consistent: participation, successor routing, pairwise co-occurrence and order, and the A41-to-A26 distance all change coherently; the placement conclusion rests on relational evidence (REL, LAG) rather than event positions alone, and both the old and new activity groups occupy a similar relative position in the trace (mean about 0.35–0.37). The shifts are complete rather than marginal (0 vs 200 of 200 traces), with differences larger than in about 995 of 1,000 random re-splits, and the distinct differences clearly exceed the roughly 0.62 items expected by chance among the 124 tested units — though many items describe the same underlying behaviour and are not independent confirmations. [DIR009, LAG001, REL013, POS001, POS002, POS006, OCC002]

Limitations: the two windows are finite samples, so a shift in the mix of pre-existing process variants cannot be ruled out completely, although the uniformity within each window (all 200 later traces contain all four new activities; all 200 earlier traces contain exactly one old activity) makes a pure sampling explanation implausible. Zero participation in the later window shows only that A14/A16/A17/A18 were not observed there, and a renaming or re-mapping of activity labels between the windows cannot be excluded from this evidence — although relabelling alone would not explain the move from one block activity per trace to four. [OCC001, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC002, OCC006, OCC007, OCC008]
