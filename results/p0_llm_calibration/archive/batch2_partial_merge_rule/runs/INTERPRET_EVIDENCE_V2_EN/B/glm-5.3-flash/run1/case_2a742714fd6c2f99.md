### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that, in the later window, the continuation after A15 has effectively swapped: cases now usually proceed from A15 via A23 together with A20, A21 and A22 towards A27, whereas they previously usually proceeded from A15 via A19 together with A16, A17 and A18. The A20–A23 continuation is executed in roughly 73 of 100 later cases versus roughly 18 of 100 earlier ones, while the A16–A19 continuation falls from roughly 83 to roughly 27 of 100. [DIR001, OCC001, OCC005, REL005, REL038]

The routing change is observed directly at A15 rather than inferred from frequencies alone; the participation shifts of the two four-activity groups match the changed transition shares almost exactly; and the relational items agree throughout, with the groups remaining mutually exclusive and their internal ordering unchanged. Even counting conservatively, the distinct differences — the decline of the A16–A19 continuation, the rise of the A20–A23 continuation, and the changed routing at A15 — clearly exceed the roughly 0.66 items this screening expects by chance when nothing has changed, and every item's difference is larger than about 199 of 200 random re-splits of the pooled data. Both continuations are well supported in both windows (165 vs 54 and 35 vs 146 cases), so this is not a rare variant appearing in one window only. [DIR001, OCC001, OCC005, REL012, REL016, REL035, REL038]

A competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is a shift in the mix of cases between the windows — for example, more cases of a variant that uses A20–A23 — which would look identical to a routing change on these indicators. The three distance items add little independent weight, as they rest on only 54 supporting later cases and show only small shifts. [LAG001, LAG002, LAG003]
