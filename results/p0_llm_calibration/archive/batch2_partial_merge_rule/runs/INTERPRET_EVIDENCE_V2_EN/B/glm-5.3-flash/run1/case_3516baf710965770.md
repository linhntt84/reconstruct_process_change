### Observed differences

- In the later window, A19 and A20 each appear in only about 59 in 100 traces, down from 100 in 100 before (about 1.0 down to about 0.6 occurrences per trace). The two always move together: the pair co-occurs in about 58.5 in 100 later traces and is absent from both in about 41.5 in 100, whereas before they co-occurred in every trace. [OCC001, OCC002, REL001]
- A25 and A26 also appear less often. Before, almost every trace contained exactly one of the two (A25 in about 48 in 100, A26 in about 52 in 100, joint occurrence rare); in the later window each appears in about 30 in 100 traces, and about 41 in 100 traces contain neither. [OCC003, OCC004, REL010]
- The routing after A42 changed: before, about 93 in 100 of A42's outgoing transitions led to A19; in the later window only about 55 in 100 do, while about 40 in 100 lead directly to A27, which was not observed as a successor before. A42 and A27 themselves still appear in every trace in both windows. [DIR002, REL004, REL005]
- The A42→A27 distance shortened: before, the pair sat about 4 event positions apart in about 89 in 100 cases; in the later window A27 immediately follows A42 (distance 1) in about 40 in 100 cases, while about 53 in 100 remain at distance 4. A27's relative position in the trace also shifted slightly earlier (mean about 0.44 to about 0.41). [LAG001, POS001]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that, in the later window, roughly 4 in 10 cases take a shortened path in which A42 is followed directly by A27, skipping A19, A20 and the A25/A26 step. The participation drop of A19 and A20 (about 41.5 in 100 traces) matches the share of A42's outgoing transitions now going to A27 (about 40 in 100) and the share of A42→A27 pairs at distance 1 (about 40 in 100); the earlier 4-position gap is consistent with the chain A42 → A19 → A20 → (A25 or A26) → A27, and a distance-1 gap necessarily means those intermediate activities are absent in those cases. A27 and A42 still occur in every trace, so the change lies on the path between them rather than in their execution. About three distinct behavioural differences stand behind the items, clearly more than the roughly 0.6 items the screening expects by chance when nothing has changed, and each item's difference exceeds about 199 of 200 random re-splits of the pooled data. [OCC001, OCC002, REL001, REL010, DIR002, LAG001]

Limitations: the link between the skipped activities and the new direct edge is established by matching aggregate shares, not by following individual cases, and the routing shares are measured over A42's outgoing transitions rather than over cases. A competing explanation the evidence cannot fully rule out is a sampling difference in variant frequencies: a skip variant that existed but went unsampled in the earlier window could in principle have become frequent later, although a behaviour absent from 200 before-traces yet present in about 80 of 200 after-traces is hard to explain by sampling alone. [DIR002, LAG001, OCC001]
