### Observed differences

- In the later window, A25 and A26 each occur exactly once per trace. Before, both averaged about 1.6 occurrences per trace, so roughly 0.6 repetitions per trace have disappeared; both activities still appear in 100 in 100 traces in both windows, so neither is skipped. [OCC001, OCC002]
- The transition from A26 back to A25 is no longer observed. Before, about 37 in 100 of A26's outgoing transitions led to A25 and about 63 in 100 led to A20 (out of 316 observed transitions); in the later window, all 200 observed transitions from A26 lead to A20. [DIR001]
- The execution pattern of the pair consolidated: before, A25 strictly preceded A26 in about 64 in 100 traces, while about 36 in 100 traces showed the two activities interleaved or repeated; in the later window A25 precedes A26 in 100 in 100 traces, with no interleaving or repetition, and the two still co-occur in every trace. - The pair became uniformly adjacent: before, A26 immediately followed A25 in about 36 in 100 pair observations, with the remainder at gaps of about three (5 in 100) or five (1 in 100) event positions; in the later window the gap is one position in every observation. [LAG001]
- Taken together, these items describe one underlying behaviour: before, the A25–A26 block could run more than once per trace, with A26 returning to A25; in the later window it runs exactly once, A25 directly followed by A26 and then A20. [OCC001, OCC002, DIR001, REL001, LAG001]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the repetition of the A25–A26 block is no longer executed: in the later window the flow no longer returns from A26 to A25, so A25 and A26 each run exactly once, A25 immediately followed by A26, which then always passes to A20. The routing change is shown directly (the A26→A25 transition, about 37 in 100 of A26's outgoing transitions before, is absent from all 200 after-observations), and it is consistent with the drop in mean occurrences from about 1.6 to 1.0 for both activities, the shift from "interleaved or repeated" to strict A25-before-A26 in every trace, and the position gap concentrating at one. Every item's difference exceeds about 995 in 1000 random re-splits of the pooled traces, and this single distinct difference clearly exceeds the roughly 0.29 items expected by chance across the 59 tested units. [OCC001, OCC002, DIR001, REL001, LAG001]

Limitations: the five items all describe the same trace behaviour and are not independent confirmations. The evidence cannot rule out that the return from A26 to A25 still exists but occurs so rarely that it did not appear in the 200 after-traces, or that the before-window over-represented the repeating variant; given that the return accounted for about 116 of 316 transitions before, a chance disappearance is unlikely but cannot be excluded from these finite samples. [DIR001, OCC001]
