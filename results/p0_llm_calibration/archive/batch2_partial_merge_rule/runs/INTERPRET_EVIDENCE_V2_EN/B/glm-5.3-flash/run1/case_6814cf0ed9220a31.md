### Observed differences

- The activity group A34, A35, A36, A37, which appeared together in every earlier trace, is split in the later window: A34 with A35 run in about 56 in 100 traces and A36 with A37 in about 46 in 100, the two pairs co-occur in only about 2–3 in 100 traces, and in about 98 in 100 later traces exactly one of the two pairs runs. [OCC005, OCC006, OCC007, OCC008, REL029, REL030, REL036, REL042]
- A10 dropped from about 63 in 100 traces to about 15 in 100 (125 to 30 traces), and when it still runs it sits earlier and far more variably in the case (mean relative position about 0.87 versus 0.57). [OCC004, POS001]
- A10's ties to the main flow weakened: co-occurrence with A14, A15, A30 or A31 fell from about 62 in 100 to about 15 in 100 traces, A10 preceding A45 or A46 fell from about 58–59 in 100 to about 11–14 in 100, and A43 now passes to A44 in about 84 in 100 of its observed outgoing transitions (previously about 29) and almost never to A10 (about 3, previously about 71). [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL011, REL012, DIR009]
- Routing around the split group changed: A30 now also feeds A36 and A37 (about 24 and 18 in 100 of its outgoing transitions, previously near 0), A34 and A35 send about 45–47 in 100 of their outgoing transitions to A31 (previously near 0), the successions A34/A35 → A31 and A30 → A36/A37 moved from gaps of 3–4 steps to 1–2 steps, and A30 → A31 moved from a gap of 5 steps to 3 steps. [DIR006, DIR007, DIR008, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG009, LAG015]
- Rare activities became more common: A04, A06 and A08 now appear in about 15–21 in 100 traces, versus about 4–7 in 100 earlier. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003]
- The early path A14 → A15 → A16 and the tail A45 → A46 (gap 1 in about 88–98 in 100 traces) are essentially unchanged. [DIR004, DIR005, LAG026]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the later window executes a branched version of the earlier flow: after A30, the previously inseparable group A34/A35/A36/A37 runs as two mutually exclusive pairs — A34 with A35 (about 56 in 100 traces) or A36 with A37 (about 46 in 100) — A31 now follows whichever pair runs, and A10, which previously sat between A43 and the A45 → A46 tail in about 63 in 100 traces, is mostly skipped, with A43 rerouted to A44. [REL029, REL030, REL036, REL042, DIR006, DIR007, LAG009, OCC004, DIR009]

The split and the rerouting are shown directly by relational and successor evidence, not by participation alone: co-occurrence of A34 with A36 fell from 200 of 200 traces to about 4 of 200 while each pair keeps its internal togetherness, and A43's handover moved from A10 to A44 as A10 disappeared. Counting behaviours that share the same activities as one difference, the windows show roughly three distinct changes (the pair split with its rerouting, the A10 skip, and the rise of A04/A06/A08), clearly more than the roughly one item expected for an unchanged process, and differences of this size (for example 100% to 2–3% co-occurrence with 200 traces per window) are not produced by re-splitting a stable process at random. [REL030, REL042, DIR009, OCC001, OCC002, OCC003]

Limitations: several items rest on few observations — A04's outgoing profile on 7 earlier versus 41 later traces and several gap items on 1–4 observations in one window — so details of the new adjacency pattern are weakly grounded, and items sharing activities overlap heavily, so they are not independent confirmations. The evidence also cannot fully separate a modified routing structure from a shift in the mix of pre-existing variants, since rare branching in the earlier window may simply not have surfaced in 200 traces. [DIR002, LAG012, LAG013, LAG014, LAG018]
