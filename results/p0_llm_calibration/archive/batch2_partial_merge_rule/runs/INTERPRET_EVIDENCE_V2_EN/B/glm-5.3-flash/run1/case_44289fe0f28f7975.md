### Observed differences

- In the later window, A30, A31, A36 and A37 each occur about 3 times per trace on average, up from about 1 time before; about 69 in 100 traces now contain repeats of each of these activities, versus 0–2 in 100 before. Participation is unchanged — every trace still contains each of the four activities in both windows [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004].
- The routing out of A37 shifted strongly: before, about 98 in 100 outgoing transitions of A37 went to A25 (and about 1 in 100 to A30); in the later window about 66 in 100 go to A30 and about 32 in 100 to A25, based on 621 outgoing transitions after versus 200 before [DIR001].
- The fixed order among the four activities broke down: before, A30 preceded A31, A36 and A37 in 96–99 in 100 traces (A31 and A36 appeared in either order roughly equally); in the later window, about 69 in 100 traces show each of these pairs interleaved or repeated, with the one-directional shares falling to about 31 in 100 [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006].
- Immediate adjacency largely disappeared: A31 followed A30 within one step in about 99 in 100 eligible cases before (about 13 in 100 after), and A36 followed A31 within one step in about 96 in 100 before (about 11 in 100 after), with gaps spreading to three to five steps [LAG001, LAG002, LAG005, LAG006, LAG008].
- The relative positions of all four activities became markedly more variable (spread roughly doubled, e.g. A30's standard deviation from about 0.08 to 0.13 of the trace) and slightly later on average, consistent with multiple occurrences per trace [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004].
- A caveat: several reverse-direction and self-pair gap items rest on only 1–3 observations in the earlier window (about 137 in the later), so their before-side picture is weak [LAG003, LAG004, LAG007, LAG009, LAG011, LAG012].

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the segment formed by A30, A31, A36 and A37, previously executed once per case in a largely fixed order, is now repeated about three times per trace in roughly 69 in 100 traces, with A37 routing back to A30 (about two-thirds of its outgoing transitions, versus about 1 in 100 before) instead of proceeding almost exclusively to A25. This would directly account for the observed collapse of the fixed pairwise order into interleaving, the loss of immediate adjacency, and the wider and slightly later position distributions [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, DIR001, REL001, REL002, REL003, REL005, REL006, POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004].

The support is direct and consistent: the relational items show the order change itself, the successor profile of A37 shows the new dominant A37→A30 transition, and the occurrence items show the repetition; the distinct differences (the repetition/interleaving of the four activities, plus the A37 rerouting) clearly exceed the roughly 0.57 items expected by chance in an unchanged process, and every item's difference exceeds those seen in about 995 of 1,000 random re-splits of the pooled data [DIR001, REL001, LAG005, OCC001].

Limitations: the windows are finite samples, so the later window may over-represent a repeating variant that already existed at low frequency — repetition was observed in up to 2 in 100 traces before, and variant frequencies can shift between samples; the precise repetition structure (whether all four activities always repeat together within a trace, and exactly how often) cannot be determined from these indicators, and several items describe the same underlying trace behaviour rather than independent confirmations [OCC001, OCC004, REL004].
