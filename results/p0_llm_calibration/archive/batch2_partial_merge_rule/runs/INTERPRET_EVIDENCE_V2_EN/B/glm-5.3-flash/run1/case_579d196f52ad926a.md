### Observed differences

- **A14 and A15 all but disappear.** In the earlier window A14 ran in about 46 in 100 traces (91 of 200) and A15 in about 55 in 100 (109 of 200), each at most once per trace; in the later window A14 appears in 0 of 200 traces and A15 in only 1 of 200. [OCC001, OCC002, POS001, POS002]
- **Before, the two formed a mandatory either/or.** Earlier, every trace contained exactly one of A14 or A15 (co-occurrence and "neither" both 0); in the later window about 199 of 200 traces contain neither. [REL001]
- **Their relational ties vanish.** Earlier, A21 always preceded A14/A15 and A14/A15 always preceded A23, with each sitting either before A22 (A14 in about 25 in 100 traces, A15 in about 28) or after it (about 21 and 27); in the later window A14/A15 co-occur with A21, A22 or A23 in essentially no trace. [REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, REL007]
- **Routing concentrates.** A21's outgoing transitions, previously split between A22 (48%), A14 (24.5%) and A15 (27.5%), all lead to A22 in the later window; A22's outgoing, previously A23 (52%), A15 (27%) and A14 (21%), lead to A23 in about 995 of 1000 cases. [DIR003, DIR004]
- **The chain shortens by one step.** A21 to A23 was always 3 event positions apart earlier and is 2 apart in all but one later case; A21→A22 and A22→A23, previously a mix of 1 and 2 steps, are now always directly adjacent. A23 itself remains in every trace in both windows, its mean position shifting slightly earlier (0.568 to 0.550 of the trace). [LAG003, LAG004, LAG005, POS003]
- A few items (A14's outgoing profile, A15's routing and lag profiles) rest on an empty or single-observation after-profile and carry little weight on their own. [DIR001, DIR002, LAG001, LAG002]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the alternative intermediate steps A14 and A15 — which earlier were executed exactly once in every trace, as a mutually exclusive choice inserted either between A21 and A22 or between A22 and A23 — are no longer executed in the later window, so traces run directly from A21 to A22 to A23. This is shown directly by the participation collapse (about 46 and 55 in 100 traces down to 0 and 1 of 200), the disappearance of every co-occurrence and ordering tie between A14/A15 and A21, A22 and A23, and the shortening of the A21→A23 distance from 3 steps to 2. [OCC001, OCC002, REL001, REL002, REL004, REL005, REL007, LAG004]

The pattern is one coherent difference rather than a scatter of quirks: the screening of 75 units would yield about 0.37 qualifying items by chance if nothing had changed, yet here five indicator types (participation, routing, position, relations, lags) all move in the direction this disappearance predicts, most with full 200-trace support in both windows and empirical strength 0.995 — far beyond what random re-splits of stable traces produce. [DIR003, DIR004, LAG003, LAG005, POS003, OCC001, OCC002]

Limitations remain: the evidence cannot rule out that the work previously done by A14/A15 continues under activity labels outside the analysed set, which would look identical to removal here; the single later A15 occurrence shows the behaviour can still appear, so the step is not strictly absent; and the items describing A15's new routing and lag profile rest on one observation. Sampling differences in variant frequencies cannot plausibly explain a step present in all 200 earlier traces dropping to 0–1 of 200, though they do account for minor shifts such as A23's slight position change. [OCC002, DIR002, LAG001, LAG002, POS003]
