### Observed differences

- **Four activities became near-universal.** In the earlier window, A29 and A30 each ran in about 60 in 100 traces and A31 and A32 in about 40 in 100; in the later window, A29 and A30 run in all 200 traces and A31 and A32 in about 99 in 100. No activity ever repeats within a trace, so each occurs at most once per case. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004]

- **Two activity pairs went from mutually exclusive to jointly executed.** Earlier, about 60 in 100 traces contained A29 and A30 together (without A31/A32) and about 40 in 100 contained A31 and A32 together (without A29/A30); A29 never co-occurred with A31 or A32. In the later window, every pairing across the four activities co-occurs in about 99–100 in 100 traces. [REL009, REL010, REL012, REL014]

- **A fixed cross-pair order appeared.** In the later window, A25 precedes A29/A30, which precede A31/A32, which precede A26, in essentially every trace; A29/A30 run in either order (about 47 vs 53 in 100) and so do A31/A32 (about 53 vs 46 in 100). Earlier, the only patterns were A25 → {A29, A30} → A26 or A25 → {A31, A32} → A26. [REL001, REL003, REL005, REL007, REL010, REL011, REL012, REL013, REL014]

- **Direct successors changed accordingly.** In the later window, A25 is followed only by A29 (about 47 in 100 of its outgoing transitions) or A30 (about 54 in 100), whereas before it could be followed by any of the four activities; A29 and A30 now lead mainly to A31/A32 instead of straight to A26 — the A29→A26 share fell from about 57 in 100 to under 1 in 100. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003]

- **The block sits between A25 and A26.** The distance from A25 to A26 grew from 3 event positions in virtually all traces to 5 positions in about 99 in 100, and the distances A25→A31/A32 and A29/A30→A26 grew from 1–2 positions to 3–4; in relative position, A25 and A29/A30 shifted slightly earlier (A29 mean from about 0.75 to 0.71) and A26 and A31/A32 slightly later (A31 mean from about 0.74 to 0.77). [LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG005, POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the segment between A25 and A26 changed from executing exactly one of two two-activity pairs per case — about 60 in 100 cases running only A29/A30 and about 40 in 100 running only A31/A32 — to executing both pairs in essentially every case, first A29/A30 (in either order) and then A31/A32 (in either order). In other words, A29 and A30 now also run in the cases that previously skipped them, and A31 and A32, which previously replaced A29/A30, now follow them in nearly all cases, with about 2 in 100 later traces still skipping A31/A32. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, REL009, REL010, REL014, LAG001]

Supporting signs: the participation, co-occurrence, precedence, direct-successor and positional-distance items all describe one and the same shift, so the many items act as facets of a single underlying difference rather than independent confirmations; that one difference is categorical — none of the 200 earlier traces contained both pairs while about 198–200 of 200 later traces do — and rests on large supports (121, 79 and 198–200 traces per window), with every item at the top of its reference distribution (strength about 0.995, against roughly 0.41 items expected by chance across the 82 tested units). A 0-in-200 versus about-199-in-200 contrast at these sample sizes is not plausibly produced by sampling variation in variant frequencies. [REL010, REL012, OCC003, DIR002, LAG001]

Limitations: the evidence cannot rule out that the two windows draw different case mixtures or variant populations rather than a modified process definition, and positions and lags are measured in event positions, so the shifts reflect the two additional activities inserted between A25 and A26 rather than clock-time timing. [POS001, LAG001]
