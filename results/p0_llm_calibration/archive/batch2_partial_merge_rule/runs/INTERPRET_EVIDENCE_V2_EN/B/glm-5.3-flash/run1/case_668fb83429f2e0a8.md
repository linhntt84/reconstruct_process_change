### Observed differences

- In the earlier window the two activity groups ran in a fixed order: every tested pair made up of one activity from A20/A21/A23/A24 and one from A25/A26/A27/A28 had the early-group activity first in about 96–100 of 100 traces (A24 preceded A25 in all 200 traces). In the later window this strict order is broken: A25 now usually comes first (before A24 in about 91 of 100 cases, before A20 or A21 in about 75 of 100), A27 and A28 usually precede A24 (about 73 of 100) and are roughly even with A20 and A21, while A26 still follows A20 or A21 in about 75–77 of 100 cases and is about even with A24. Both groups still occur together in essentially every trace in both windows, so participation itself did not change. [REL001, REL002, REL005, REL012, REL013, REL014, REL015]
- Event positions moved in opposite directions for the two groups: A20, A21, A23 and A24 now occur later in the trace (A24's mean position rose from about 0.61 to 0.69, A20's from 0.57 to 0.62), while A25, A26, A27 and A28 occur earlier (A25 fell from about 0.64 to 0.56, A27 and A28 from about 0.68 to 0.62). [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006, POS007, POS008]
- Outgoing routing at the group boundaries changed markedly. A24, previously followed only by A25, now continues to A34 in about 49 of 100 outgoing transitions, with most of the rest going to A25–A28. A26, previously followed by A34 in about 995 of 1000 transitions, now goes to A34 about half the time and returns to A20, A21, A23 or A24 the other half. A33 now leads to A25 in about half of its transitions, where before it led almost only to A23. A20, A21 and A23, which previously transitioned almost only among themselves and to A24, now send roughly 33–44 of 100 transitions to A25–A28. [DIR004, DIR006, DIR009, DIR001, DIR002, DIR003]
- Step distances changed consistently with a reordering. A33 to A25 was always five steps apart before and is now adjacent in about half the cases; A24 to A34 went from always five steps to about half at one step; pairs running from the early to the late group, such as A20 to A25, now occur within five steps in far fewer traces (about 47 vs 200), as expected when A25 now usually comes first. [LAG039, LAG022, LAG003]
- Spacing also loosened inside the groups: A20 to A21 was always adjacent before and is now adjacent in only about 54 of 100 cases, with the rest spread over 2–5 steps; A23 to A24 moved from always three steps apart to a spread of three to five; A27 to A28 from almost always adjacent to about 55 of 100. [LAG001, LAG015, LAG030]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that, in the later window, the group A25/A26/A27/A28 is placed before rather than after the group A20/A21/A23/A24 — most clearly for A25, and for A27/A28 relative to A24 — with the connections at the group boundaries rewired: A33 now often enters A25 directly, A24 now often continues to A34, and A26 now often leads back into A20/A21/A23/A24. This rests on direct relational evidence: all fifteen tested cross-group pairs reversed or substantially evened out, with shifts of up to about 92 in 100 cases (A24/A25), and the order change is corroborated by the opposing position shifts, the distance changes, and the successor changes at A24, A26 and A33. [REL012, REL001, POS004, POS005, LAG039, LAG022, DIR004, DIR006, DIR009]

The pattern is unlikely to be a sampling artefact. The qualifying items collapse to a small number of distinct differences — the cross-group reorder, the rewiring at A24, A26 and A33, and looser within-group spacing — which is well above the roughly 0.84 items expected by chance among the 169 tested units, and every item's difference exceeded about 199 of 200 random re-splits of the pooled traces. Most items rest on about 200 traces per window; a few lag items rest on very few observations (for example A26 to A23 with only 16 after-traces) and add little weight. [DIR004, REL012, LAG027]

Limitations: the evidence cannot distinguish a clean reversal of two sequential groups from a partial reorder in which old and new orders coexist or the groups interleave — in the later window some pairs keep the old order for a sizable minority of traces (A20 still precedes A26 in about 75 of 100 cases), and A26's position moved least. Sampling differences in variant frequencies between two 200-trace windows can also produce one-sided shifts, and although a coordinated reversal of this size across many pairs makes that explanation implausible, finite samples cannot exclude it. Positions and distances are measured in event positions rather than clock time, so the conclusion concerns execution order, not timing. [REL002, REL006, POS006]
