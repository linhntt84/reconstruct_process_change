### Observed differences

- In the later window, A25, A26, A31 and A32 each occur about 3.1 times per trace on average, up from exactly once per trace earlier, while still appearing in every trace; repetition is new — about 69 in 100 later traces contain a repeated occurrence of each of these activities, against none in the earlier 200 traces. - Routing out of A32 changed: earlier, every observed outgoing transition of A32 went to A20; in the later window about 68 in 100 go to A25 and about 32 in 100 to A20, with observed outgoing transitions rising from about 200 to about 620 across the same 200 traces. [DIR001]

- The event positions of the four activities became more dispersed — the spread roughly doubled, with slightly later typical positions — which is consistent with the repetition above rather than with the activities being moved. [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that, in the later window, the activities A25, A26, A31 and A32 are executed repeatedly within a case: A32 routes back to A25 in about 68 in 100 of its outgoing transitions instead of always proceeding to A20, so each of the four activities runs about three times per case rather than once. The signs are direct and mutually consistent — the rise from 1.0 to 3.1 mean occurrences with recurrence in about 69 in 100 traces, the new A32-to-A25 successor, the shift from fixed order to interleaved-or-repeated relations, and the appearance of longer lags all describe the same behaviour, with the same roughly 69/31 split recurring across indicators, and every item's difference exceeds about 995 in 1000 random re-splits of the pooled data. [OCC001, DIR001, REL001, LAG003]

This is unlikely to be a screening artefact: 76 units were tested and only about 0.38 qualifying items would be expected by chance if nothing had changed, whereas 21 items qualify and, although they reflect one underlying difference rather than independent confirmations, they form a single coherent pattern far beyond that expectation. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, REL001, REL006]

Limitations remain: the windows are finite samples of 200 traces each, so a repeating variant that existed earlier but was not observed in the before window cannot be fully excluded, although recurrence in about 69 in 100 later traces against 0 in 200 earlier traces makes sampling variation alone an unlikely explanation; the positional changes show greater spread, not proof that an activity was relocated; and the occurrence, relation and lag items describe the same trace behaviour, so they should be read as one difference, not several independent confirmations. [OCC001, POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004]
