### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a structural change in routing: A25 now leads to A28, A28 is no longer a successor of the A36/A37/A42/A43 group, A31 takes over that successor role, and A26 now routes to that group with a reversed order, while participation of A27 and the A36/A37/A42/A43 group is roughly halved. The consistency across occurrence, direction, relation, position and lag indicators points to a coordinated reorganisation rather than isolated fluctuations. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006, DIR007, OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, REL007, REL008, REL009, REL010, REL011, REL012, REL013, REL014, REL015, REL016, REL017, REL018, REL019, REL020, REL021, REL022, REL023, REL024, POS004, POS006, POS007, POS008, POS009, LAG001]

A competing explanation that cannot be fully ruled out is that the later window reflects a different case mix or a new variant that replaces the old one, rather than a modification of a single stable process. However, the large and mutually consistent changes in routing, order and participation make a structural change the most plausible interpretation. Some lag items are based on very few observations in the later window, but the main directional and relational evidence rests on hundreds of traces.
