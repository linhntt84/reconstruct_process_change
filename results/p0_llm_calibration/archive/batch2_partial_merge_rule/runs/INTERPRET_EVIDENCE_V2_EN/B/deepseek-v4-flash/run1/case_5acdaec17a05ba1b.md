### Observed differences

- In the later window, activities A28–A32 appear in about 99–100% of traces, up from about 54% before; A33–A36 appear in about 99–100%, up from about 46% before. [OCC001–OCC008]
- Before, A28–A32 and A33–A36 never co-occurred in the same trace; after, they co-occur in about 99% of traces. [REL020–REL023, REL026–REL029, REL031–REL038]
- A25’s outgoing transitions changed: before it led to all of A28–A36; after it leads almost exclusively to A28, A29, A31, A32, and no longer directly to A33–A36. [DIR001]
- A28–A32 now have outgoing transitions to A33–A36, which they did not have before; their direct transition to A26 almost disappears. [DIR002–DIR005]
- The relative position of A28–A32 moved earlier (mean position from about 0.74 to 0.65), while A33–A36 moved later (from about 0.74 to 0.77). [POS002–POS009]
- The distance between A25 and A33–A36 increased: before the lag was spread over 1–4 steps, after it is almost always 5 steps. [LAG001–LAG004]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the process changed from an exclusive choice between two branches after A25 – one containing A28–A32 and one containing A33–A36 – to a sequential execution of both branches, with A25 followed by A28–A32 and then A33–A36. This is directly shown by the co-occurrence of the two activity groups in the later window, the change in A25’s outgoing transitions, the new outgoing edges from A28–A32 to A33–A36, and the shift in positions and lags. The change is consistent across multiple indicators and is far larger than what would be expected by chance. A competing explanation that the difference is due to sampling variation in variant frequencies is unlikely given the magnitude and consistency of the changes, but the evidence cannot rule out that the underlying process changed in a different way that produces the same observed trace behaviour.
