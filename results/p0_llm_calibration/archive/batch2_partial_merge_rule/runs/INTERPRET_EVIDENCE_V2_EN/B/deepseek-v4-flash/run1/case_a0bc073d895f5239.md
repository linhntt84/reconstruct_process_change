### Observed differences

- In the later window, activities A21 and A22 appear in about 22% of traces instead of about 78% before; activities A23 and A24 appear in about 80% of traces instead of about 22% before. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004]
- The outgoing transition from A20 changed: before, about 78% of its occurrences went to A21 and 22% to A23; after, about 79% go to A23 and 21% to A21. [DIR001]
- Co-occurrence and ordering relations shift accordingly: A20 with A21 drops from 78% to 22% co-occurrence, while A20 with A23 rises from 22% to 80%; A21 with A22 drops from 78% to 21% co-occurrence, while A23 with A24 rises from 22% to 79%. [REL001, REL003, REL005, REL012]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a hypothesis that the process now routes from A20 to the A23–A24 path instead of the A21–A22 path. The routing change at A20 is directly shown by the successor distribution, and the participation, co-occurrence, and ordering indicators consistently reflect the same switch. The differences are large (about 56–57 percentage points) and appear across multiple indicators tied to the same activities. The main limitation is that the evidence comes from two samples of 200 traces each, but the consistency and size of the changes make sampling variation an unlikely explanation. A competing explanation that cannot be fully excluded is that both branches still exist and the observed shift reflects a major change in branch frequency rather than a removal of one branch, but the evidence points to a structural re-routing at A20.
