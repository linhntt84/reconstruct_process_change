### Observed differences

- In the later window, the relative order of two groups of activities reversed. Before, A20, A21, A23, A24 typically occurred before A25, A26, A27, A28; after, A25–A28 typically occur before A20–A24. For example, A20 preceded A25 in about 99% of cases before, but in only about 24% after, with A25 preceding A20 in about 76% of cases. [REL001, REL005, REL012]
- Position shifts confirm this: A20, A21, A23, A24 moved later (mean relative position increased by about 0.05, 0.05, 0.03, 0.08 respectively), while A25, A26, A27, A28 moved earlier (mean decreased by about 0.08, 0.03, 0.06, 0.06). [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006, POS007, POS008]
- Successor distributions changed: A20, A21, A23 now sometimes lead to A25–A28 (e.g., A20's successors to A25–A28 sum to about 40% after vs 0% before), and A24's successor changed from A25 (100%) to A34 (about 49%) and others. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004]
- Lag distributions also shifted: for pairs like A20–A25, the lag shortened from mostly 2–3 steps to mostly 1–2 steps in the cases where A20 still precedes A25. [LAG003, LAG009, LAG020]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the order of two groups of activities changed: activities A25, A26, A27, A28 (and possibly A34) now tend to occur before A20, A21, A23, A24, whereas before they occurred after. This is directly shown by the reversal in relative order for many pairs (e.g., A20–A25, A21–A25, A24–A25) and by the consistent position shifts. The successor changes are consistent with this reordering. A competing explanation of sampling variation is unlikely given the large and consistent differences across many pairs, though the evidence is based on 200 traces per window and some lag items have limited support.
