### Observed differences

In the later window, activities A29 and A30 appear in every trace (100%) and A31 and A32 in about 99% of traces, up from about 60% for A29/A30 and 40% for A31/A32 before. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004] The outgoing routing of A25 changed from four targets (A30 34.5%, A32 22.5%, A29 26%, A31 17%) to only A29 (46.5%) and A30 (53.5%). [DIR001] A29 and A30 now also route to A31 and A32, while their transitions to A26 drop from about 57% and 43% to 0.5%. [DIR002, DIR003] The relational evidence shows that A29 with A31, A29 with A32, A30 with A31, and A30 with A32 never co-occurred before (0%) but co-occur in 99% of traces after, always with the first activity before the second. [REL010, REL011, REL012, REL013] A25 now always precedes A29, A30, A31, and A32, whereas before it preceded A29/A30 in about 60% and A31/A32 in about 40% of traces. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004] The distance between A25 and A26 increased from 3 steps to 5 steps, and the distance between A29/A30 and A26 increased from 1–2 steps to 3–4 steps. [LAG001, LAG004, LAG005] Position shifts: A29 and A30 occur earlier (mean from about 0.74 to 0.71), A31 and A32 later (mean from about 0.74 to 0.77), and A26 later (mean from about 0.79 to 0.82). ### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the process changed from a choice between two alternative branches — one containing A29 and A30, the other containing A31 and A32 — to a single path that includes all four activities in sequence, with A25 first, then A29/A30, then A31/A32, then A26. The most direct sign is the co-occurrence of A29/A30 with A31/A32 rising from 0% to 99% [REL010, REL011, REL012, REL013], together with the near-universal presence of all four activities [OCC001–OCC004]. The routing changes [DIR001–DIR003] and the increased lags between A25/A29/A30 and A26 [LAG001, LAG004, LAG005] are consistent with this interpretation. A competing explanation that the differences arise from sampling variation in variant frequencies is unlikely given the magnitude and consistency of the changes, but the evidence cannot rule out that the process changed in a different way that produces the same observed trace behaviour.
