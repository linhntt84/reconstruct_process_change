### Observed differences

- In the later window, the relative order of two groups of activities reversed. Before, A20, A21, A23 and A24 almost always occurred before A25, A26, A27 and A28; after, the latter group often occurs first. For example, A20 preceded A25 in 99% of cases before but only 23% after, with A25 preceding A20 in 76% after [REL001]. A21 preceded A25 in 97.5% before vs 25% after, with A25 before A21 in 73.5% after [REL005]. A24 preceded A25 in 99% before vs 7.5% after, with A25 before A24 in 91.5% after [REL012]. Similar reversals hold for pairs with A26, A27 and A28 [REL002, REL003, REL004, REL006, REL007, REL008, REL009, REL010, REL011, REL013, REL014, REL015].

- Position shifts confirm the reversal: A20, A21, A23 and A24 moved later (mean position from about 0.57 to 0.63, 0.58 to 0.63, 0.53 to 0.56, and 0.61 to 0.68 respectively) [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004], while A25, A26, A27 and A28 moved earlier (A25 from 0.64 to 0.56, A26 from 0.71 to 0.69, A27 from 0.68 to 0.62, A28 from 0.67 to 0.62) [POS005, POS006, POS007, POS008].

- Lag distributions shifted accordingly: e.g., A20–A25 before had lag 2 or 3, after mostly lag 1 (51%) [LAG003]; A20–A26 before always lag 5, after spread across lags 1–5 [LAG004]; A24–A34 before lag 5, after lag 1 in 50% [LAG022]; A33–A25 before lag 5, after lag 1 in 50% [LAG042].

- Routing changes accompany the order shift: A24 before went to A25 in 99.5% of its outgoing transitions, after to A34 in 49.5%, A26 17%, A27 13% and A28 11% [DIR004]. A33 before went to A23 in 98%, after to A25 in 51% and A23 in 49% [DIR009]. A20 before went to A24 and A21, after also to A25, A27, A28 and A26 [DIR001]. A21 before to A20 and A24, after to A20, A28, A27, A25, A26 and A24 [DIR002].

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that, in the later window, the order of two groups of activities reversed: activities A25–A28 (and A34) now tend to occur before A20–A24, whereas before they occurred after. The supporting signs are the consistent reversal in relative order across many activity pairs, the position shifts, and the lag changes. The number of distinct differences far exceeds the expected number under no change (0.89), and they are highly consistent. A competing explanation of sampling variation in variant frequencies is unlikely given the magnitude and consistency, though the evidence cannot rule out that some rare variants appear in only one window.
