### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a change in resource allocation as the main hypothesis: in the later window, executions of activities A01, A03, A17 and A21 were reassigned from a broad pool of occasional resources to a consistent, somewhat more concentrated subset, with largely the same resources gaining share on all four activities and largely the same resources losing share. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004]

The resource family produced four qualifying items where about 0.006 would be expected if nothing had changed, a count far beyond what chance produces, while the control-flow and time families produced none against their own expectations. [screening] All four items agree in direction and in which resources gained and lost, and each difference is larger than virtually all random re-splits of the pooled data, with substantial observation counts behind every profile. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004]

Limitations: the evidence cannot rule out that the active pool of resources itself changed between the windows — resources appearing in only one window would produce a similar redistribution — and the anonymous codes allow no inference about roles or groups. The four items likely reflect one shared allocation change rather than four independent ones, though the family-level chance comparison already treats the count as a whole. Other differences: none — no control-flow or timing items qualified, so the evidence shows no structural or timing difference at the configured thresholds. [screening]
