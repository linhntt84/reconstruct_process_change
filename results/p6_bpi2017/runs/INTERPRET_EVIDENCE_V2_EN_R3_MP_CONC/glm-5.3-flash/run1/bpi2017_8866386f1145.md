### Observed differences

- Activity A09 appeared in about 60 in 100 traces in the earlier window and about 74 in 100 in the later window (mean occurrences per trace 0.60 vs 0.74; it never occurred more than once within a trace in either window). [OCC001]
- Of A05's observed outgoing transitions, about 60 in 100 led to A09 and about 40 in 100 led to A04 in the earlier window; in the later window, about 74 in 100 led to A09 and about 27 in 100 led to A04. [DIR001]
- The consecutive pair A09 followed by A04 occurred 90 times in the earlier window (about 45 in 100 traces) and 126 times in the later one (about 63 in 100). The typical gap between the two completions stayed just under one minute (median about 0.95 vs 0.88 minutes), but the earlier window had a heavy tail of much longer gaps (mean about 2.3 minutes, standard deviation about 8.7 minutes), whereas in the later window gaps were uniformly about 0.6–1.1 minutes (mean about 0.9 minutes, standard deviation about 0.3 minutes). [TWAIT001]
- A04's events were spread over 26 distinct resources in the earlier window, with the busiest resource handling about 44 in 100 of them (concentration index about 0.22); in the later window they spread over 24 distinct resources, with the busiest handling about 64 in 100 (index about 0.41). [CONC001]

### Inference

Support level: Weak hypothesis

The evidence suggests a change in the timing of the step from A09 to A04: in the earlier window, the elapsed time from the completion of A09 to the following completion of A04 was usually just under one minute but occasionally stretched to many minutes (mean about 2.3 minutes, standard deviation about 8.7 minutes across 90 observed pairs), while in the later window these gaps were uniformly around one minute (mean about 0.9 minutes, standard deviation about 0.3 minutes across 126 pairs) — long gaps of the kind seen earlier are no longer observed. [TWAIT001]

Supporting signs: this rests on a direct, like-for-like measurement of the same activity pair in both windows; the time family produced one evidence item against roughly 0.015 expected by chance (about seventy times that family's expectation), and the difference was larger than in all but about 1 of 2,000 random re-splits, so chance re-sampling rarely produces a shift of this size. [TWAIT001]

What is missing is corroboration: this is the only timing indicator, and the median gap barely moved (about 0.95 vs 0.88 minutes), so the change lies in the disappearance of the long-gap tail rather than in the typical gap. An important competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that the earlier window simply contained more cases that idled between the two completions — a difference in case mix rather than a uniform change in the step's timing; the rise in pair counts from 90 to 126 also parallels A09's higher participation, which the control-flow family's chance expectation does not establish. [TWAIT001, OCC001]

Other differences:
- Resource allocation for A04 changed: the busiest resource's share of A04's events rose from about 44 to about 64 in 100 and the concentration index from about 0.22 to 0.41, while distinct resources stayed nearly flat (26 to 24). The resource family produced one item against about 0.005 expected by chance (far above its expectation), but it is a single, uncorroborated indicator, and a case-mix shift cannot be ruled out. [CONC001]
- Control-flow shifts — A09's participation (about 60 vs 74 in 100 traces) and A05's outgoing transitions moving from A04 (about 40 vs 27 in 100) toward A09 (about 60 vs 74 in 100) — point in the same direction as the pair-count rise, but the control-flow family produced two items against about 0.19 expected by chance, which is what an unchanged process typically produces even though each item alone looks unusual (larger than in about 995 of 1,000 reference splits), and the two items likely reflect the same underlying trace behaviour; these shifts are therefore not established beyond chance sampling. [OCC001, DIR001]
