### Observed differences

- The resources handling activities A01, A03, A13, A14, A17 and A22 changed markedly between the two windows. In the earlier window, each of these activities was performed predominantly by a group of resources led by User_51, who alone handled about 7–13% of executions depending on the activity. In the later window, User_51 and most of that group no longer appear, and the work is instead carried out by a different group led by User_28 and User_24, each taking about 6–11% of executions. The share of unlisted resources ("OTHER") stayed roughly constant at about 40–50% in both windows. The same pattern appears for all six activities. - Activity A18's outgoing transitions shifted: in the earlier window about 53% of its continuations went to A22 and 33% to A10; in the later window about 69% went to A10, 13% to A02 and 13% to A16, while A22 dropped to about 6%. This rests on only 15–16 traces containing A18. [DIR001]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a hypothesis that the allocation of resources to activities A01, A03, A13, A14, A17 and A22 changed between the two windows: a group of resources led by User_51 handled these activities in the earlier window, and a different group led by User_28 and User_24 took over in the later window. The change is consistent across all six activities, is based on large numbers of traces (about 200 per window for A01 and A03, about 300 for A13/A14, about 280 for A17, about 125 for A22), and the resource family produced 6 evidence items when only 0.005 were expected by chance, far more than a stable process would typically produce. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006]

Other differences: The control flow item for A18 (DIR001) hints at a possible shift in its outgoing transitions, but it is based on only 15–16 traces and the control flow family produced 1 item when 0.17 were expected by chance, so it is within what an unchanged process typically produces and does not support a structural change. [DIR001]

A competing explanation that cannot be ruled out is that the resource codes changed (e.g., re-labelling or replacement of the resource pool) rather than a change in how the work is allocated; the evidence cannot distinguish between these.
