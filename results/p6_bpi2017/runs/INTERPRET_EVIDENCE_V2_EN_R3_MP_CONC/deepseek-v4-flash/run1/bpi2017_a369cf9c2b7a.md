### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a hypothesis that resource allocation changed systematically across several activities. In the later window, a different set of users handled activities A01, A03, A13, A14, A17 and A22: users such as User_28 and User_60 took over a substantial share of work previously done by users such as User_51. The change is consistent across six activities, each with strong support (nearly all 200 traces per window), and the number of resource-related evidence items (6) far exceeds the expected count by chance (0.005). [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006]

Other differences: The control-flow evidence for A18 suggests a shift in its successor distribution (from A22 to A10), but this rests on only about 15–16 observations per window and is a single item against an expected 0.17, so it is not strong enough to support a structural change on its own. A competing explanation that cannot be fully ruled out is that the resource shift reflects a change in case mix or workload rather than a deliberate reassignment, but the consistency across many activities makes a genuine change in resource allocation the most plausible interpretation.
