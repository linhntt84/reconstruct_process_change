### Observed differences

- In the later window, activity A09 occurs in about 74% of traces, up from about 60% in the earlier window; the mean occurrences per trace rose from 0.60 to 0.74. [OCC001]
- For activity A05, the share of outgoing transitions going to A09 increased from about 60% to about 74%, while the share going to A04 decreased from about 40% to about 27%. [DIR001]

- For activity A04, work became more concentrated on a single resource: the top resource's share rose from about 44% to about 64%, while the number of distinct resources used fell from 26 to 24. [CONC001]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a hypothesis that the process increasingly routes from A05 to A09 instead of A04, making A09 more frequent and A04 less frequent as a successor of A05. This is directly shown by the increased occurrence of A09 and the corresponding shift in A05's outgoing transition shares. The timing change (shorter intervals between A09 and A04) and the resource concentration change for A04 are consistent with this shift but could also reflect separate adjustments; they are reported as other differences.

Other differences: The wait time between A09 and A04 decreased substantially, and A04's work became more concentrated on fewer resources. These changes are not required for the routing hypothesis and could be independent.

A competing explanation that cannot be fully ruled out is that the observed differences stem from a change in the mix of cases entering the process, rather than a deliberate change in routing or resource allocation. However, the number of qualifying evidence items far exceeds what would be expected by chance in each family, and the control-flow items directly show the routing shift.
