### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility of a change in timing around A13: in the later window, the elapsed time between the completion of A11 and that of A13 roughly doubled (about 0.9 → 1.8 hours), and the elapsed time between the completion of A12 and that of A13 grew to about 2.5 times its earlier level (about 0.16 → 0.40 hours). Supporting signs: both items measure these intervals directly, both move in the same direction, the A11→A13 shift is near-uniform across the whole distribution, both shifts lie far outside what random re-splits produce, and 2 qualifying items against a chance expectation of about 0.006 cannot be explained by sampling alone. Limitations: both items share A13, so they may reflect one underlying delay rather than two independent changes; TWAIT is elapsed time between consecutive completions, so the evidence cannot show whether the extra time is waiting, service or handover; and each estimate rests on a moderate number of intervals (about 40–60 per window) [TWAIT001, TWAIT002].

An important competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is a change in the order or placement of activities around A13, which would lengthen the same intervals without any change in durations; the evidence contains no relational or positional items to separate these [TWAIT001, TWAIT002].

Other differences: the number of A12→A13 consecutive-completion intervals rose from 41 to 62 while A11→A13 intervals fell slightly from 50 to 45, and the A12→A13 intervals became more variable; with no structural evidence in this set, the source of these count changes cannot be determined [TWAIT001, TWAIT002].
