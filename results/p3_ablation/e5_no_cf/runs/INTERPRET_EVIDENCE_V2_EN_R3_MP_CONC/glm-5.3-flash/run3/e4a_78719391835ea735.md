### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a timing change as a possibility: the elapsed time between consecutive completion events lengthened for the steps ending at A08 — from A02 the median gap roughly doubled (about 0.86 to 1.72 hours) and from A06 it rose about two-and-a-half-fold (about 0.10 to 0.26 hours). Supporting signs: both items measure this timing directly, the whole distribution shifted upward (lower and upper quartiles moved with the median, not only the average), each difference exceeded all but about 1 of 1,000 random re-splits, and two items qualified where fewer than one in a hundred were expected by chance. Limitations: the intervals come from only about 13–19 in 100 traces per window; the two items share the endpoint A08 and are therefore not independent confirmations; and the evidence set contains no structural, positional or resource items, so any accompanying change in routing, participation or allocation can be neither confirmed nor excluded. TWAIT measures elapsed time between consecutive completion events, so the increase shows longer gaps between events rather than a specific waiting or processing component. A competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is a changed mix of cases contributing these intervals (the A06–A08 pair occurred somewhat less often in the later window), which could lengthen the typical gap without every case slowing; the broad, consistent upward shift makes this less likely but does not rule it out.
