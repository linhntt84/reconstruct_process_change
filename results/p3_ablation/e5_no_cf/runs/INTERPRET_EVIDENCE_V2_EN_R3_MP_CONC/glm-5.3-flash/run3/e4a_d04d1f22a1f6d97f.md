### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a timing change as a possibility: in the later window, the elapsed time between consecutive completions shortened to roughly half on the transitions into A29 — from A25 (median about 0.90 h to about 0.48 h) and from A30 (mean about 0.18 h to about 0.09 h) — and became more consistent, with a narrower spread in both cases. Supporting signs: both items measure this timing directly, agree in direction and approximate magnitude, share the successor activity A29, rest on 41–57 observations per window, and each difference exceeds all but about 1 of 2000 random re-splits; the screening found 2 time items where fewer than 1 in 100 were expected without a change. Limitations: TWAIT is elapsed time between consecutive completion events and cannot be decomposed into waiting, handover or processing time, so the evidence does not show which component shrank; and the evidence contains no structural or participation items, so the composition of traces reaching A29 cannot be examined. An important competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that the shorter gaps reflect a changed mix of cases around A29 rather than a change in the timing itself.

Other differences: none — both qualifying items describe the same shortening of the gaps into A29. [TWAIT001, TWAIT002]
