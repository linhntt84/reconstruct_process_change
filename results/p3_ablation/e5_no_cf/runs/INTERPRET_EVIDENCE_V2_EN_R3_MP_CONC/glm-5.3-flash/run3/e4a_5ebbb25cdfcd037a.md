### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a change in timing: the elapsed time between consecutive completion events shifted on three specific transitions. Intervals ending at A34 became shorter — from A28 they fell from about 0.9 to about 0.45 hours, and from A35 from about 0.20 to about 0.08 hours — while the interval from A38 to A14 grew from about 2.0 to about 3.0 hours. Supporting signs are that the indicator measures these intervals directly, each pair's quantiles move consistently in one direction, each difference exceeds essentially all reference re-splits, and the item count (3 against roughly 0.01 expected) is far beyond what an unchanged process typically produces. Limitations: this indicator is elapsed time between consecutive completions, so a longer or shorter interval could also reflect a different event pattern between the two activities (for example other completions falling in between) rather than a change in the durations themselves, and the evidence contains no structural items to distinguish these. The drop in A38→A14 pairs (from all 200 traces to about 105 of 200) suggests this adjacency became less common, but its nature cannot be determined from timing evidence alone. The two shortened intervals share A34 as the later activity, so they may reflect one underlying difference rather than two independent ones. [TWAIT001, TWAIT002, TWAIT003]
