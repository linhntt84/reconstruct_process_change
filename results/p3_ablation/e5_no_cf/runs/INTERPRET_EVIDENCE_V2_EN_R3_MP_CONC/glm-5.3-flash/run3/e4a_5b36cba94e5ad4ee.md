### Observed differences

- Both time items concern gaps that end at the completion of A38. In the earlier window, the elapsed time between the completion of A31 and the completion of A38 averaged about 0.9 hours (median about 0.9, middle half between about 0.8 and 1.0 hours); in the later window it averaged about 1.8 hours (median about 1.8, middle half between about 1.6 and 2.0 hours) — roughly a doubling, about +0.9 hours, with the whole distribution shifted upward rather than only the mean. - The elapsed time between the completion of A37 and the completion of A38 averaged about 0.19 hours in the earlier window (median about 0.15) and about 0.36 hours in the later window (median about 0.34) — also roughly a doubling, about +0.18 hours, with quartiles and spread increasing together. ### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a timing change around A38: in the later window, the elapsed time between the completion of the preceding activity and the completion of A38 is about twice as long for both observed predecessors — after A31 it rises from about 0.9 to about 1.8 hours, and after A37 from about 0.19 to about 0.36 hours. The change is consistent in direction and relative size across both predecessors, each difference is unusual for random re-splits, and two qualifying items far exceed the chance expectation for the time family, so sampling variation alone is unlikely to explain the pattern. [TWAIT001, TWAIT002]

The evidence cannot show what drives the lengthening. TWAIT measures elapsed time between consecutive completion events, not pure waiting or processing time, and the evidence contains no structural or positional items; a changed sequence of completions around A38 (for example another activity now completing in between) could produce the same pattern, and the opposite movements in how often the pairs occur consecutively keep this competing explanation open. [TWAIT001, TWAIT002]

Other differences: the frequency with which the pairs occur as consecutive completions moved in opposite directions — A31–A38 fell from 61 to 41 observations (about 30 to 20 per 100 traces) while A37–A38 rose from 51 to 64 (about 26 to 32 per 100 traces); with time-only evidence this shift cannot be attributed to routing or placement.
