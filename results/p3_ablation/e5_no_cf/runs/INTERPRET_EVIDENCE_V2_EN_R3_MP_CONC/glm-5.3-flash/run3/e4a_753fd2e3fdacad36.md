### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a timing change centred on activity A09: in the later window, the elapsed time between the completion of each of A03, A04, A05, A06, A07, A08 and the following completion of A09 is consistently about half of what it was in the earlier window (from typically about 1 hour to typically about 0.5 hour), and the intervals are less variable. [TWAIT001, TWAIT002, TWAIT003, TWAIT004, TWAIT005, TWAIT006]

The supporting signs are that all six activity pairs show the same direction and a similar magnitude of change, that every difference exceeds essentially all random re-splits, that six time items qualified where fewer than 0.01 were expected by chance among 17 tested units (so the set as a whole cannot be explained by sampling noise), and that each pair rests on a moderate number of observations (roughly 22–51 intervals per window). [TWAIT001, TWAIT002, TWAIT003, TWAIT004, TWAIT005, TWAIT006]

Limitations: all six pairs share A09, so the items likely describe one underlying timing change rather than six independent ones. TWAIT measures elapsed time between consecutive completion events, not pure waiting or service time, and with no control-flow or resource evidence the analysis cannot determine whether the shorter intervals reflect faster execution around A09, a different mix of paths, or a changed event sequence; a structural or routing change producing the same timing pattern cannot be ruled out. [TWAIT001, TWAIT002, TWAIT003, TWAIT004, TWAIT005, TWAIT006]

Other differences: the count of observed intervals per pair shifted slightly in both directions (for example 51 to 36 for A04–A09 and 22 to 31 for A08–A09), which on its own is consistent with sampling variation between the windows. [TWAIT002, TWAIT006]
