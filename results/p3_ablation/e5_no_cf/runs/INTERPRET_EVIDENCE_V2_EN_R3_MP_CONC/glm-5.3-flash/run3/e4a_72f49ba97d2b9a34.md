### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

Main hypothesis: a timing change on the approach to A40 — in the later window, the elapsed time between the completion of each of the predecessor activities A30, A41 and A43 and the completion of A40 dropped from about one hour to about half an hour, and the intervals became markedly more uniform. The support is direct and consistent: all three interval measures move in the same direction with similar magnitude (about 0.47–0.52 hours), each difference is larger than in about 999 of 1,000 random re-splits, and three qualifying time items is far above the roughly 0.015 expected by chance, so chance alone cannot account for the set. The pairs still occur at similar rates (200 of 200 traces for A30→A40; 68 versus 70 for A41→A40; 80 versus 66 for A43→A40), so the timing shift is not an artefact of the pairs disappearing. [TWAIT001, TWAIT002, TWAIT003]

Limitations: all three intervals end at A40, so the items describe one shared behaviour rather than three independent confirmations, and the evidence cannot localise the reduction — it cannot distinguish whether the predecessors were completed sooner or A40 was completed sooner after them. The indicator measures elapsed time between consecutive completion events, so activity occurring between the two events could also contribute in a way the evidence does not separate. [TWAIT001, TWAIT002, TWAIT003]

Other differences: the A43→A40 pair appeared in somewhat fewer traces in the later window (66 of 200 versus 80 of 200, about 33 versus 40 in 100), while A41→A40 was nearly unchanged (70 versus 68); both shifts are small and consistent with sampling variation between two 200-trace windows.
