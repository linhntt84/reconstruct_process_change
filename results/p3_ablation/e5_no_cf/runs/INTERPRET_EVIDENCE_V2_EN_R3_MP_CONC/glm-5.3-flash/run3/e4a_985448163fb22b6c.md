### Observed differences

- The elapsed time between the completions of A09 and A10 roughly doubled: in the earlier window it averaged about 1.0 hours (median about 0.98 hours, middle half between about 0.85 and 1.10 hours), while in the later window it averaged about 2.0 hours (median about 1.99 hours, middle half between about 1.77 and 2.18 hours). The whole distribution shifted upward, not just the tail, and the spread widened (standard deviation from about 0.18 to about 0.33 hours). This interval was observed in essentially all traces in both windows (200 of 200). [TWAIT001]
- The interval from the completion of A11 to the completion of A10 shows the same shift: about 1.0 hours on average in the earlier window (111 intervals) versus about 2.0 hours in the later window (120 intervals), with the quartiles rising by roughly 0.9–1.1 hours. [TWAIT002]
- Both differences are larger than those seen in about 9995 of 10000 random re-splits of the pooled data, and the screening flagged 2 time items where only about 0.011 would be expected if nothing had changed. [TWAIT001, TWAIT002]
- The A11→A10 pair occurs within five steps in slightly more traces in the later window (about 60 in 100 versus about 56 in 100), a small difference that sampling variation could produce. [TWAIT002]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a timing change: in the later window, the elapsed time between consecutive completion events on the paths into A10 is about one hour longer than before, both for the interval following A09 and for the interval following A11 — each roughly doubling from about 1 hour to about 2 hours. This rests on two agreeing time items with the same direction and nearly the same magnitude, distributions shifted across their full range rather than only in one tail, differences exceeding virtually all random re-splits, and a screening yield (2 items) far above the chance expectation of about 0.011 for the time family. [TWAIT001, TWAIT002]

Limitations: both intervals end at A10, so the evidence localises the delay to the steps ending at A10 but cannot show whether the extra hour accrues before the A09/A11 completions or between them and A10; TWAIT measures elapsed time between consecutive completions, not pure waiting or service time; and the evidence contains no structural or resource items, so the mechanism behind the longer intervals is unknown. The main competing explanation — that both items reflect a single coincidental sampling fluctuation — is made unlikely by the agreement in magnitude across two pairs and by the screening comparison, but the evidence covers only these two intervals and cannot exclude a broader timing shift elsewhere in the process. [TWAIT001, TWAIT002]

Other differences: apart from the slightly more frequent observation of the A11→A10 pair noted above, no further behavioural differences appear at the configured thresholds. [TWAIT002]
