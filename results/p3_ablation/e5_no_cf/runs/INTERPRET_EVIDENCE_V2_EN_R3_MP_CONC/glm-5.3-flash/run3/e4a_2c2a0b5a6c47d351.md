### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a change in timing: in the later window, the elapsed time between consecutive completions leading into A31 became shorter and more consistent. The interval from A24 to A31 fell from about 0.90 to about 0.46 hours, and the interval from A32 to A31 fell from about 0.18 to about 0.10 hours. Supporting signs: both items measure this timing directly and point in the same direction; the entire distributions moved, not just extreme values; the two qualifying items far exceed the chance expectation for the time family (2 items from 21 units tested, where about 0.01 would qualify if nothing had changed); and the frequency of both consecutive pairs stayed stable at roughly 100 observations per window, which argues against the intervals changing simply because the pairs became rarer. Limitations: this indicator is elapsed time between completion events, so the evidence cannot show whether the reduction lies in waiting, in the execution of A31, or in both. The two items share A31 and may reflect one underlying timing difference rather than two independent ones. A structural rearrangement that changes which completions are adjacent (for example, an activity inserted between these pairs) could produce similar interval shifts; no structural evidence is present to test this, though the stable pair counts make it less likely.
