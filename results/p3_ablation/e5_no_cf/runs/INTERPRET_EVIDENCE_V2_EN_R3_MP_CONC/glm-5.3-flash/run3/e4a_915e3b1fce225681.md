### Observed differences

- The elapsed time between the consecutive completions of A09 and A10 was about 1.0 hours in the earlier window (median about 1.0 h, middle half roughly 0.9–1.1 h) and about 0.5 hours in the later window (median about 0.5 h, middle half roughly 0.44–0.55 h) — roughly a halving, observed in all 200 traces of both windows [TWAIT001].
- The elapsed time between A11 and A10 fell by the same amount, from about 1.0 hours on average (125 consecutive pairs, about 63 in 100 traces) to about 0.5 hours (110 consecutive pairs, about 55 in 100 traces) [TWAIT002].
- The elapsed time between A24 and A28 dropped from about 1.0 hours (median about 1.0 h) in the earlier window to essentially zero in the later window: the median, lower and upper quartiles were all 0, so at least three in four of these gaps were effectively zero, and the mean was about 0.006 hours (roughly 20 seconds); observed in all 200 traces of both windows [TWAIT003].
- Each of the three differences was larger than in all but about 1 in 2000 random re-splits of the pooled data, and three time items qualified in screening where fewer than 0.02 would be expected if nothing had changed [TWAIT001, TWAIT002, TWAIT003].

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility of a change in timing: in the later window, the elapsed time between consecutive activity completions is roughly halved for the pairs A09–A10 and A11–A10 (from about 1 hour to about half an hour) and nearly eliminated for A24–A28 (from about 1 hour to essentially zero). This rests on direct timing measurements for exactly these pairs, consistent in direction (all decreases from a similar 1-hour level in the earlier window), backed by substantial support (all 200 traces for two pairs; 125 and 110 observations for A11–A10), and far exceeding the chance expectation of the screening (3 items versus about 0.02) [TWAIT001, TWAIT002, TWAIT003].

Limitations: the indicator measures elapsed time between consecutive completion events and cannot separate waiting from processing, so the shortening may reflect either. Because only time evidence is available, a structural explanation — for example the activities now completing back-to-back after a reordering — can be neither confirmed nor ruled out, and the three pairs may share a single underlying cause rather than being independent changes [TWAIT001, TWAIT002, TWAIT003].

Other differences: the number of observed consecutive A11→A10 completion pairs dipped from 125 to 110 of 200 traces (about 63 to about 55 in 100); this is small enough to be ordinary sampling variation between two 200-trace windows and is not part of the main hypothesis [TWAIT002].
