### Observed differences

- Time from A28 to A34 (consecutive completions): in the earlier window about 0.9 hours (median 0.89 h, middle half 0.83–0.97 h), seen in 47 of 200 traces; in the later window about 0.45 hours (median 0.46 h, middle half 0.41–0.50 h), seen in 63 of 200 traces. The gap roughly halved while the pair became somewhat more frequent. [TWAIT001]
- Time from A35 to A34: about 0.20 hours in the earlier window (median 0.16 h, 49 of 200 traces) versus about 0.08 hours in the later window (median 0.08 h, 48 of 200 traces) — a reduction of roughly 60% at an essentially unchanged frequency. [TWAIT002]
- Time from A38 to A14: about 2.0 hours in the earlier window (median 2.0 h, in all 200 traces) versus about 3.0 hours in the later window (median 3.0 h, in 105 of 200 traces) — about one hour longer, and the pair occurred in only about half as many traces. [TWAIT003]
- Each shift moves the whole distribution (lower quarter, median and upper quarter move together), and each difference exceeds 1999 of 2000 random re-splits of the pooled data. [TWAIT001, TWAIT002, TWAIT003]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a change in timing as a possibility: in the later window, A34 is completed sooner after both A28 (about 0.45 h versus 0.9 h before) and A35 (about 0.08 h versus 0.2 h before), while A14 is completed about one hour later after A38 (about 3.0 h versus 2.0 h before). This rests on direct elapsed-time measurements for three activity pairs in a family where about 0.01 items were expected by chance across 20 tested units and three were found; each shift is consistent across the quartiles, is larger than all but about 1 in 2000 reference comparisons, and is backed by reasonably large samples (47–63, 48–49 and 105–200 observations). [TWAIT001, TWAIT002, TWAIT003]

Limitations: TWAIT measures elapsed time between consecutive completion events, not pure waiting or service time, so the shortening of the gaps into A34 and the lengthening of the gap before A14 could partly reflect changes in the pace of neighbouring work rather than in these transitions alone. A competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that the A38–A14 result combines two effects — a structural change (the pair no longer adjacent in many traces) with a timing change on the remaining pairs — since the evidence contains no structural indicators to check this. [TWAIT003]

Other differences: The frequency of the A38→A14 pair fell from essentially every trace (200 of 200) to about half of the traces (105 of 200), a participation or ordering change not covered by the timing hypothesis; conversely, the A28→A34 pair rose from 47 to 63 traces. [TWAIT003, TWAIT001]
