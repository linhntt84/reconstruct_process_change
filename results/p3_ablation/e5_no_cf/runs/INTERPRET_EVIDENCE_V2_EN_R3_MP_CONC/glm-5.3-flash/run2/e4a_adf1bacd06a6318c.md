### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, the elapsed time between consecutive completions of A10 and A11 was about 1 hour (median ~1.0 h; the middle half of intervals fell between ~0.9 and ~1.1 h). In the later window it was about 2 hours (median ~2.0 h; middle half between ~1.8 and ~2.2 h) — an increase of about 1 hour, roughly a doubling. [TWAIT001]
- The whole distribution moved, not just the average: the 25th and 75th percentiles and the spread (std ~0.16 h → ~0.32 h) all about doubled, and the per-interval differences are similar across the distribution (~0.9–1.1 h), so the shift is not driven by a few unusually long intervals. [TWAIT001]
- The step occurred at a similar rate in both windows — 344 observed A10→A11 intervals across 200 traces before vs 365 across 200 traces after — so the change concerns duration, not how often the step runs. [TWAIT001]
- The difference is larger than in 1999 of 2000 random re-splits of the pooled data, and it is the only qualifying item: one timing item qualified where about 0.005 were expected by chance if nothing had changed (10 units tested). [TWAIT001]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a change in timing: the elapsed time between consecutive completions of A10 and A11 roughly doubled, from about 1 hour per step in the earlier window to about 2 hours in the later window. The signs supporting this are that the measurement bears directly on the hypothesis; the shift is uniform across the distribution (all quantiles and the spread rose together) rather than outlier-driven; the step's frequency was essentially unchanged (344 vs 365 intervals over 200 traces each), so a change in how often the step runs cannot explain it; the difference exceeds 1999 of 2000 random re-splits; and the number of qualifying items (1) is far above the chance expectation (about 0.005). [TWAIT001]

Limitations: TWAIT measures elapsed time between consecutive completion events, so the evidence cannot show whether the extra hour is waiting, processing or handover time, nor where between A10 and A11 it arises. A competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is a changed case mix in the later window whose intervals are intrinsically longer. [TWAIT001]
