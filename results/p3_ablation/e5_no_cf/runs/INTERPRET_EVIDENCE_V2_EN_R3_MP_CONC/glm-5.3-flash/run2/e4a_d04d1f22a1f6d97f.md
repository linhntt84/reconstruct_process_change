### Observed differences

- The elapsed time between consecutive completion events from A25 to A29 roughly halved: in the earlier window these gaps averaged about 0.9 hours (median 0.90, middle half between about 0.86 and 0.98), while in the later window they averaged about 0.5 hours (median 0.48, middle half between about 0.44 and 0.52) — a difference of about 0.44 hours over a similar number of observed gaps (56 vs 57). A difference this size was exceeded in almost none of 2000 random re-splits of the pooled data (larger than in about 1999 of 2000). [TWAIT001]
- The elapsed time from A30 to A29 also roughly halved: from a mean of about 0.18 hours in the earlier window (median 0.15, widely spread, middle half about 0.09–0.27) to about 0.09 hours in the later window (median 0.08, middle half about 0.04–0.12), with the spread narrowing (standard deviation about 0.12 to 0.07 hours) over 41 vs 48 observed gaps; this difference was also larger than in about 1999 of 2000 random re-splits. [TWAIT002]
- Both qualifying items concern gaps ending at A29, while the number of observed gaps per pair stayed similar or rose only slightly, so the change lies in the durations of these gaps rather than in how often the pairs occur. [TWAIT001, TWAIT002]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a change in timing around activity A29: in the later window, the elapsed time between consecutive completion events leading into A29 is roughly half of what it was in the earlier window, both from A25 (about 0.9 down to about 0.5 hours) and from A30 (about 0.18 down to about 0.09 hours), with the variability of these times also shrinking. Supporting signs: both time items agree in direction and relative size, each difference is larger than in about 1999 of 2000 random re-splits, and the time family produced 2 qualifying items where about 0.008 were expected if nothing had changed (roughly 250 times the chance expectation, with 16 units tested). [TWAIT001, TWAIT002]

Limitations: the two items share the activity A29 and may reflect one underlying change rather than two independent ones, and TWAIT measures elapsed time between consecutive completions, so it cannot separate shorter execution from a change in how events interleave; no structural or positional evidence is present to locate the change more precisely. An important competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that completions of other activities now fall between these pairs at different rates, shortening the measured gaps without the durations of A25, A29 or A30 themselves having changed. [TWAIT001, TWAIT002]
