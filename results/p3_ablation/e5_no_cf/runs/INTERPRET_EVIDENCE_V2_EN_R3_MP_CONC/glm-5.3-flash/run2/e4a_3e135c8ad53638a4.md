### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a change in timing as the main hypothesis: the elapsed time between the completion of A23 and the completion of A24 increased by about 0.9 hours per case, from about 1.0 hour to about 1.9 hours, nearly doubling. The change is shown directly by the timing indicator itself, it is consistent — all reported quantiles rose by a similar amount, so the distribution shifted as a whole rather than only a subset of cases slowing down — and it rests on 200 paired observations in each window, that is, every trace. It is also unlikely to be a sampling artefact: of the 24 time units screened, about 0.01 qualifying items would be expected if nothing had changed, and only this item qualified, with a difference exceeding about 1999 of 2000 random re-splits. [TWAIT001]

Limitations: the evidence covers timing only and contains no structural, ordering or participation items, so it cannot show how the longer interval arises. A competing explanation it cannot rule out is that the two completions are produced differently rather than simply taking longer — for example that other work now occurs between them, or that the activities themselves run longer. TWAIT measures elapsed time between consecutive completions, so the increase cannot be attributed specifically to waiting or to processing time. [TWAIT001]
