### Observed differences

- The elapsed time between consecutive completions from A26 to A32 roughly doubled: in the earlier window it averaged about 0.9 hours (median ~0.9 h, typical range ~0.8–1.0 h), while in the later window it averaged about 1.8 hours (median ~1.8 h, typical range ~1.6–2.0 h). The spread also grew (standard deviation from ~0.14 to ~0.29 h). This rests on 42 observations before and 46 after, and the difference was larger than in all but about 1 in 2,000 random re-splits of the pooled data. [TWAIT001]
- The elapsed time between consecutive completions from A33 to A32 also roughly doubled: about 0.17 hours on average earlier (median ~0.15 h) versus about 0.35 hours later (median ~0.28 h), with the spread roughly doubling (standard deviation ~0.13 to ~0.26 h). This rests on 48 observations before and 46 after, and again the difference exceeded all but about 1 in 2,000 random re-splits. [TWAIT002]
- For context: 2 time items qualified in screening, where far fewer than one (0.01) would be expected if nothing had changed. [TWAIT001, TWAIT002]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a timing change centred on A32: in the later window, the elapsed time between consecutive completions leading into A32 increased roughly twofold regardless of predecessor — from A26 it rose from about 0.9 to about 1.8 hours, and from A33 from about 0.17 to about 0.35 hours, with greater variability in both cases. The supporting signs are that both items measure this elapsed time directly, point in the same direction, rest on substantial observation counts (42–48 per window), and the number of qualifying items is many times the chance expectation. A consistent change in these inter-completion times is a timing hypothesis in its own right and needs no structural counterpart.

Limitations: both items share A32, so they may reflect a single underlying difference rather than two independent confirmations. The indicator measures elapsed time between consecutive completions, so it cannot show whether the added time belongs to A32 itself, to its predecessors A26 and A33, or to the interval between events. As this evidence set contains only time items, a competing explanation — for example a shift in the mix of paths or cases entering A32 rather than a pure slowdown — cannot be ruled out.
