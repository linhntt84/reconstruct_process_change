### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a timing change: in the later window, the elapsed time between consecutive activity completions shortened. The gaps from A09 to A10 and from A11 to A10 halved from about 1 hour to about half an hour, and the completions of A24 and A28 moved from about 1 hour apart to effectively simultaneous. [TWAIT001, TWAIT002, TWAIT003]

The screening comparison supports this: 3 time items qualified against roughly 0.02 expected by chance (36 units tested in the time family), far beyond what a stable process typically produces. The three items agree in direction and magnitude (0.5–1.0 hours), each difference is larger than in all but about 1 in 2,000 random re-splits, and the underlying observations are substantial (200/200, 125/110 and 200/200 traces). A consistent change in these elapsed times is a hypothesis in its own right and needs no structural counterpart. [TWAIT001, TWAIT002, TWAIT003]

Limitations: TWAIT measures elapsed time between consecutive completion events, so the evidence cannot show whether the shorter gaps come from reduced waiting or from faster processing. Only time items qualified, so any accompanying structural or participation change would go undetected, and the change is established for these three pairs rather than necessarily for the whole process. A competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is a change in how completion timestamps are recorded in the later window (for example batched logging), which would produce the same apparent shortening.
