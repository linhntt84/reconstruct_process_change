### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility of a change in timing: the elapsed time from the completion of A44 to the completion of A45 became consistently shorter, from about 1 hour per case in the earlier window to about half an hour in the later window, with a correspondingly tighter spread. The change is measured directly on the timing indicator itself, rests on many observations in both windows (157 and 148 intervals), and its size exceeds the differences seen in all but roughly 1 of 2000 random re-splits of the pooled data; the screening also produced this single item from 19 tested time units where only about 0.01 items would be expected if nothing had changed, so chance alone is an unlikely explanation. Limitations: TWAIT measures elapsed time between consecutive completion events, so the evidence shows only that this elapsed time shrank, not which portion of the interval accounts for the reduction; and the evidence contains no structural or positional items, so any accompanying change in the order or placement of A44 and A45 cannot be assessed. A competing explanation the evidence cannot fully rule out is that the slightly smaller set of later intervals (148 versus 157) reflects a somewhat different mix of cases containing the pair, although the downward shift is consistent across the entire distribution.
