### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility of a timing change between A39 and A41: in the later window, the elapsed time between the completion of A39 and the completion of A41 is about one hour longer than in the earlier window, roughly doubling from about 1 hour to about 2 hours. Supporting signs: the indicator measures exactly this gap; the shift is consistent across the entire distribution (mean, median and both quartiles each rose by roughly 0.9–1.1 hours, with non-overlapping interquartile ranges); the behaviour is well observed in both windows; and the screening produced one qualifying item against roughly 0.01 expected by chance among the 20 tested time indicators, so chance qualification of the result as a whole is not a plausible reading. [TWAIT001]

Limitations: this rests on a single indicator for a single activity pair, and the indicator captures total elapsed time between the two completions, so it cannot separate waiting, processing or intervening time, nor show whether A39 completed earlier, A41 completed later, or the time in between grew. An important competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that behaviour between the two completions (for example additional steps lying between them) lengthens the gap, rather than the timing of A39 or A41 itself having changed; the evidence contains no structural items that could confirm or exclude this. [TWAIT001]

Other differences: the consecutive A39-to-A41 completions were measured somewhat more often in the later window (173 gaps versus 157); this is minor next to the timing shift and may reflect the same underlying behaviour. [TWAIT001]
