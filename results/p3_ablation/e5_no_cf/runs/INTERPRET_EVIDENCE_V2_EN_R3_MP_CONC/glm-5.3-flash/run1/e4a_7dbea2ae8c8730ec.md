### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, the elapsed time between consecutive completion events from each of A16, A17, A18 and A19 to A28 averaged about 1.0 hour (medians about 1.0–1.06 hours). In the later window, all four intervals averaged about 2.1 hours (medians about 2.08–2.09 hours) — an increase of roughly 1.0–1.1 hours, about a doubling, in every pair [TWAIT001, TWAIT002, TWAIT003, TWAIT004].
- How often these consecutive-completion pairs occurred barely moved: about 46–53 observations per 200 traces in the earlier window and about 46–53 in the later one (roughly a quarter of traces in both), so the shift concerns timing rather than how often the pairs occur [TWAIT001, TWAIT002, TWAIT003, TWAIT004].
- The intervals also became more spread out: standard deviations of about 0.13–0.15 hours before versus about 0.24–0.28 hours in the later window [TWAIT001, TWAIT002, TWAIT003, TWAIT004].
- Each of the four differences was larger than in about 999.5 of 1000 random re-splits of the pooled traces, and screening qualified 4 time items where only about 0.01 would be expected by chance across the 21 tested time units [TWAIT001, TWAIT002, TWAIT003, TWAIT004].

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a timing change centred on A28: in the later window, the elapsed time between the completion of A16, A17, A18 or A19 and the subsequent completion of A28 is about one hour longer than before, roughly doubling from about 1 hour to about 2 hours, while the frequency of these consecutive-completion relations stayed essentially unchanged. The hypothesis rests on direct evidence (the intervals themselves are measured), is consistent (all four pairs shift by a similar ~1.0–1.1 hours in the same direction, each with empirical strength about 0.9995), and the number of qualifying items (4 against about 0.01 expected without change) is far beyond what an unchanged process typically produces [TWAIT001, TWAIT002, TWAIT003, TWAIT004].

Limitations: all four items share A28, so they most likely describe one underlying shift rather than four independent confirmations. TWAIT measures only elapsed time between consecutive completions, so the evidence cannot show whether the extra hour accrues because A28 is completed later or because A16–A19 are completed earlier relative to it; no structural evidence is present in this sample to check the surrounding order [TWAIT001, TWAIT002, TWAIT003, TWAIT004].
