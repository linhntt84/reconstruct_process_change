### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a timing change centred on A27: in the later window, the elapsed time between the preceding completion and A27 is about twice as long both after A20 (roughly 0.9 h to 1.8 h) and after A29 (roughly 0.2 h to 0.4 h), while both consecutive pairs continue to occur at similar frequencies. Supporting signs: both time items move in the same direction, each rests on a substantial sample (about 94–106 observations per window), each difference is larger than in essentially all reference comparisons, and two qualifying items is many times the chance expectation of about 0.013 for the 27 tested units. [TWAIT001, TWAIT002]

Limitations: both items share A27, so they may describe one underlying shift rather than two independent changes, and TWAIT measures only elapsed time between consecutive completions — it cannot show whether the extra time is waiting, processing or queueing. An important competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is a structural change that alters which completions are consecutive (for example an activity added between these pairs), which could also lengthen these elapsed times; the evidence contains no control-flow items to check this.
