### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a change in timing as the main hypothesis: in the later window, the elapsed time between the completions of A10 and A11 is about twice as long as in the earlier window (about 2.0 hours versus about 1.0 hours per interval). The change is direct — the indicator measures exactly this elapsed time — and consistent, since the mean, median, both quartiles and the spread all moved up together by a similar factor. It rests on many observations (344 and 365 intervals) and is hard to attribute to sampling variation: the difference is larger than in about 1,999 of 2,000 random re-splits, and one qualifying item against a chance expectation of 0.005 is many times what an unchanged process typically produces [TWAIT001].

Limitation: TWAIT is elapsed time between consecutive completions, so it bundles waiting with any work in between. The evidence cannot separate a genuine slowdown around A10 and A11 from an important competing explanation — a shift in the mix of traces toward cases with naturally longer intervals, or a change in what happens between the two completions — because no structural or resource evidence is available [TWAIT001].
