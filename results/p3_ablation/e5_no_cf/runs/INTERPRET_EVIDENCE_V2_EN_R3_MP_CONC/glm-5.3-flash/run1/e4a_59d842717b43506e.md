### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a change in timing as a possibility: in the later window, the elapsed time between consecutive completion events of A10 and A11 is about half of what it was in the earlier window — from about 1.0 hour to about 0.5 hours on average, with the median and all quartiles dropping by roughly 0.4–0.6 hours and the spread halving. [TWAIT001]

Supporting signs:
- The change is captured directly by the indicator that measures exactly this quantity, and it is consistent across the whole distribution (mean, median and quartiles all moved down together), pointing to shorter gaps across the board rather than in a subset of cases. [TWAIT001]
- It rests on many observations in both windows (319 vs 336 gaps over 200 traces each), so a rare variant appearing in only one window cannot explain it. [TWAIT001]
- Chance is an unlikely explanation for the item's presence: 19 time-related units were tested, fewer than 1 in 100 items (0.009) would be expected to qualify without any change, and 1 qualified — more than a hundred times that expectation; the difference is also larger than in all but about 1 of 2000 random re-splits of the pooled data. Limitations and competing explanation:
- The evidence shows only that the measured elapsed time shrank; it cannot show what drove the reduction. A change in the mix of situations in which A10 and A11 completions occur back-to-back could contribute, although the halved spread argues against a simple change in mixture. [TWAIT001]
- Only this timing indicator is available; there is no control-flow or positional evidence, so the hypothesis is confined to the timing between these two activities and cannot be corroborated or extended. [TWAIT001]
