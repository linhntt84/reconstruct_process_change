### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a specific timing hypothesis centred on A40: in the later window, the elapsed time between the completion of A30, A41 or A43 and the subsequent completion of A40 is consistently about half an hour shorter than before, dropping from about 1 hour to about 0.5 hours on each of the three transitions, with the variability of these gaps halving as well. The indicator measures these gaps directly, the direction and size of the change agree across all three transitions, each difference exceeds more than 999 in 1000 random re-splits of the pooled data, and the screening flagged 3 time items where roughly 0.015 would be expected if nothing had changed, with the qualifying items confined to transitions ending at A40 among the 30 units tested [TWAIT001, TWAIT002, TWAIT003].

Limitations: all three items share A40, so they may reflect one underlying difference rather than three independent confirmations; the indicator is the gap between consecutive completion events, so it cannot show whether the reduction sits in the gap before A40 or in when the predecessor activities complete relative to A40; and a localised recording or timestamp artefact affecting these transitions cannot be ruled out [TWAIT001, TWAIT002, TWAIT003].

Other differences: The A43 to A40 sequence appeared somewhat less often in the later window (about 33 in 100 traces versus about 40 in 100 before), a modest shift that finite samples can produce without any change and not part of the timing hypothesis [TWAIT003].
