### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a change in timing around A13: in the later window, the elapsed time from the completion of A11 to the completion of A13 roughly doubled (about 0.9 to 1.8 hours), and the elapsed time from the completion of A12 to A13 grew about two-and-a-half times (about 0.16 to 0.40 hours), with more A12→A13 intervals observed (41 to 62). This is supported directly, because the elapsed time between consecutive completions is exactly what these items measure; both predecessor pairs moved in the same direction with individually extreme re-split strength; and two qualifying time items against a chance expectation of about 0.006 cannot be explained by sampling alone. [TWAIT001, TWAIT002]

Limitations: the measure is elapsed time between consecutive completion events, so it cannot separate longer waiting from longer processing or batching, and both items share A13, so they may reflect one underlying slowdown rather than two independent changes. [TWAIT001, TWAIT002]

Other differences: the number of observed consecutive intervals moved in opposite directions — fewer A11→A13 intervals (50 to 45) but more A12→A13 intervals (41 to 62). The evidence contains no activity, routing or ordering items, so it cannot show whether these count shifts reflect changed frequencies of these successions or only a changed mix of cases.
