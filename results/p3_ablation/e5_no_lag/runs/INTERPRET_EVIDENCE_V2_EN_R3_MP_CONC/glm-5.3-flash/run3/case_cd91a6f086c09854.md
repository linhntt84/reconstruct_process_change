### Observed differences

- The relative order of two groups of activities reversed. In the earlier window, A30, A31 and A36 each ran before A21 and before A22 in essentially every trace (about 200 in 200); in the later window, A21 and A22 each ran before A30, A31 and A36 in about 99 in 100 traces. All six pairs still co-occur in 100% of traces in both windows, so none of these activities became optional or disappeared. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006]

- The routing into and out of the A30/A31/A36 block changed accordingly. In the earlier window, A20 spread its outgoing flow over A30 (about 40 in 100), A31 (about 33) and A36 (about 28); in the later window, A20 leads to A22 in about 99 in 100 cases. A21, which previously always led to A25, now leads to A30 (about 36 in 100), A31 (about 33) and A36 (about 31). The three block activities, which previously each led on to A22 in roughly 28–38 in 100 cases, now each lead to A25 in roughly 31–36 in 100 cases. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005]

- Event positions moved consistently with this reorder: the mean position of A30, A31 and A36 shifted from about 0.41 to about 0.59 of the trace, while A21 moved from about 0.62 to about 0.53 and A22 from about 0.47 to about 0.38. [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005]

- The screening produced 16 qualifying control-flow items against about 0.35 expected if nothing had changed (70 units tested), and every item's difference exceeds about 995 in 1,000 random re-splits of the pooled data. ### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis of a change in the placement and ordering of the block A30–A31–A36 relative to A22 and A21: in the earlier window the block was entered from A20, exited to A22, and ran before A22 and A21 (with A21 then leading to A25); in the later window the block runs after A21 — entered from A21 and exiting to A25 — while A20 leads directly to A22 and A21 follows it. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005]

The supporting signs are direct and consistent: the order reversal is observed relationally for all six activity pairs, changing from a fixed order (block first in about 200 in 200 traces) to the near-fixed reverse order (about 99 in 100); the successor changes at A20, A21 and all three block members match this exactly; the position shifts point the same way; and co-occurrence remains at 100% in both windows, so this is a reorder of activities that all still execute in every case, not a replacement of one path by another or an activity becoming optional. With 16 items against about 0.35 expected without change, chance sampling cannot account for the pattern as a whole. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005]

Limitations: the evidence shows only the relative order change, so it cannot distinguish whether the A30/A31/A36 block was placed later in the case or whether A22 and A21 were placed earlier (with A20 rewired to A22) — both readings fit the same evidence. Positions are event positions rather than clock times, so no conclusion about timing is supported. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005]
