### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the block of activities A14, A15 and A20, which in the earlier window ran between A40 and A21 near the start of each case, is executed much later in the later window — after A21 and A22 — and now feeds into A23 and A27 instead of into A21, while A40 leads directly to A21. The support is direct and consistent across three views of the same behaviour: the block's position moves from about one third to about 60% of the trace, its order against A21 and A22 flips completely with the old order disappearing entirely rather than coexisting with the new one, and the slight earlier shift of A21, A22 and A23 fits the block no longer sitting in front of A21. Participation of the block is essentially unchanged (about 31–38 of 100 traces before, 31–37 after), so this is a change in the placement of existing activities, not activities appearing or disappearing. [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006, REL001, REL002, REL004, REL005, REL006, REL007, DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR006]

The rewiring is observed on all sides of the block, and screening supports that the pattern as a whole is not a sampling artefact: twenty control-flow items qualified where fewer than one would be expected by chance for the whole family, and every item's difference exceeded 995 of 1,000 random re-splits of unchanged data. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006]

Limitations: the evidence shows observed edges and pairwise orders, not a complete model. In the later window the block and A23 occur in both orders, so the evidence cannot distinguish whether the block now alternates with A23 in a loop, runs in no fixed order relative to it, or whether the later window contains two coexisting variants with different placements around A23 — window-level profiles cannot separate these. Positions are event positions rather than clock times, so no conclusion about clock timing is drawn. [REL003, REL008]
