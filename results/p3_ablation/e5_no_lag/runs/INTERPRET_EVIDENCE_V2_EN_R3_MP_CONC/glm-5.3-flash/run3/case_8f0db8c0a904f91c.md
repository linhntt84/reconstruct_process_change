### Observed differences

- The activity group A19, A20 and A25 ran in every trace in the earlier window (participation 100 in 100, about one occurrence per trace); in the later window each appears in only about 53 in 100 traces (roughly 106 of 200) and is missing from the rest. [OCC004, OCC005, OCC006]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the consecutive group A19 → A25 → A20, which was executed in every case in the earlier window, became optional: in the later window it runs in about 53 in 100 cases in its previous internal order and is skipped in the remaining ~47 in 100, while A26, A41, A46 and A47 continue to run in every case, and in skipped cases the flow passes from A41 directly on to A26 (about 44 in 100 of A41's outgoing transitions, versus about 4 earlier). Supporting signs: the participation drop from 100 to about 53 in 100 is consistent across all three activities and all their pairwise relations, the rerouting at A41 is observed directly on about 200 outgoing transitions per window, and 38 qualifying control-flow items against fewer than 1 (0.6) expected by chance cannot be explained by random re-splits of stable behaviour. Limitations: the evidence shows that the group is skipped but not what triggers skipping, and an important competing explanation remains — that skipped cases reach A26 by more than one routing, or that a rare skip variant (below roughly 1–2 in 100 traces) already existed earlier but was too rare to appear in the 200 earlier traces. [OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, REL008, REL014, DIR005, REL010, screening]

Other differences: A02 receded independently of the main hypothesis — participation fell from 29 to about 9 in 100 traces, its feed from A46 nearly disappeared, and it now occurs earlier in the trace with its outgoing transitions dispersed, though these later profiles rest on about 17 traces only. A04 and A06 rose modestly to about 12–13 in 100 traces on small counts. [OCC001, DIR006, POS001, DIR001, OCC002, OCC003]
