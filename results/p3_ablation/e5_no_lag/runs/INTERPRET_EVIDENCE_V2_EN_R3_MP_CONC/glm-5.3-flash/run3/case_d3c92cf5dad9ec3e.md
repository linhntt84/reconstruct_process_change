### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that two blocks of activities exchanged their places in the sequence around a fixed middle segment. Before, the sequence ran approximately A40 → {A19, A21, A24} → A25 → A29 → {A20, A22, A23} → A30; in the later window, {A20, A22, A23} run early and feed A25, while {A19, A21, A24} run late after A29 and now exit to A30. Each trio kept its internal routing style (A20/A22/A23 still cycle among themselves before exiting; A19/A21/A24 still funnel almost entirely into one successor) and participation shares barely moved, so this is a reordering of the same activities, not activities appearing, disappearing or changing how often they run. [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006, DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006, DIR007, DIR008, REL008, REL010]

Supporting signs: all 21 qualified cross-group pairs reversed in near-complete unison (each flip in about 99 in 100 co-occurring cases), the position shifts match the exchange in direction and magnitude (about a quarter to a third of the trace length), and the successor changes are exactly those the exchange predicts. The volume of evidence also exceeds what an unchanged process produces: 37 control-flow items qualified against roughly 0.6 expected by chance for the 120 tested units, and every item's difference exceeded about 995 of 1,000 random re-splits of the pooled data. [REL001, REL008, POS002, POS003, DIR001, DIR007, screening]

Limitations: the evidence shows pairwise order, positions and direct successors, not the complete model; it cannot fully distinguish one coordinated exchange of the two blocks from independent re-placements of each block, and order relations inside each trio were not among the qualified items. POS is based on event positions, so no statement about clock time is implied.

Other differences: A21 now appears in slightly fewer traces (about 35 in 100 before, 30 after) and A24 in slightly more (about 36 before, 40 after); several activities also gained or lost low-share tail transitions of roughly 1–2 in 100 of their outgoing transitions (for example A19's small second successor changed from A18 to A02).
