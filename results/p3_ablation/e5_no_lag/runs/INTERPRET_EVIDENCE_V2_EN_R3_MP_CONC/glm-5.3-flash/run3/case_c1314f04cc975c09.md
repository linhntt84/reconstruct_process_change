### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, A25, A26 and A31 each occurred exactly once in every trace. In the later window, each occurs about 3.5 times per trace on average, and in about 68 in 100 traces it occurs more than once; participation stays at 100% of traces in both windows. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that, in the later window, the block of activities A25, A26 and A31 became repeatable within a trace. Previously the block ran exactly once per trace in a fixed order (A25 before A31, with A31 always followed by A20); now the three activities occur about 3.5 times per trace, repeat in about 68 in 100 traces, A31 returns to A25 in about 71% of its outgoing transitions instead of always proceeding to A20, and the A25–A31 order is interleaved or repeated in about 68 in 100 traces. The screening context makes chance an unlikely explanation: 7 control-flow items qualified against roughly 0.29 expected without a change, many times the chance expectation. The relational and directional items (REL001, DIR001) show the repetition structure directly, and the occurrence items agree in magnitude (recurrence share 0.68 matches the interleaved/repeated share 0.68), consistent with a loop back from A31 to A25 over the block, with the exit to A20 still available in a minority of transitions.

Limitations: the occurrence, directional and relational items partly reflect the same trace behaviour, so they are not independent confirmations. The evidence cannot show what determines when the block repeats versus exits to A20, and A26's role inside the loop is inferred only from its occurrence pattern, as it has no relational or directional evidence of its own. A competing explanation the evidence cannot fully rule out is that the interleaving reflects re-entrant or overlapping execution of the activities rather than a strictly sequential loop, since the relational category combines interleaving with repetition.
