### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that two previously alternative groups of activities came to be executed together in the same cases: in the earlier window a case ran either the group A33/A35/A36 or the group A37/A38/A39 (each about half of the cases, never both), while in the later window all six activities run in one case in about 98–100 of 100 cases, with the first group preceding the second (A33 before A37/A38/A39 in about 97–98 of 100), A30 preceding the block and A31 following it. This is shown directly by relational comparisons with full support in both windows (200 of 200 traces behind each pair), consistently across all nine cross-group and six within-group pairs, and corroborated by the participation rise of all six activities and by A30's transitions now leading only into the first group. Screening produced 81 qualifying items against about 0.76 expected without change, so the pattern as a whole is not what an unchanged process typically produces. The evidence cannot show where the decision to run both groups now resides — an upstream re-routing (for instance at A30 or A04) versus a change at the block itself cannot be separated, and event positions say nothing about clock-time duration. [REL039, REL046, REL048, OCC004, OCC007, DIR006, REL025, REL031]

Other differences: A04 now appears in fewer cases (about 31→14 of 100) and almost only alongside the block, with its outgoing transitions redistributed; A06 and A10 appear slightly more often, and the outgoing transitions of A02 and A08 changed, each of these resting on few observations.
