### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, A30, A31 and A36 each occurred almost exactly once per trace (means about 1.0) and virtually never repeated within a trace (repeats in at most about 1–2 in 100 traces). In the later window, each occurs about 3.5 times per trace and repeats in about 68 in 100 traces, while still appearing in every trace in both windows (participation 100 in 100 throughout). [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003]
- The strict sequence among A14, A30 and A36 broke down. These three still co-occur in every trace in both windows, but "A14 before A30", "A14 before A36" and "A30 before A36" each held in about 98–99 in 100 earlier traces and in only about 32 in 100 later traces; in about 68 in 100 later traces each pair is interleaved or repeated with no fixed order. [REL003, REL004, REL005]
- A05 co-occurs with A30 and A36 in about half of the traces in both windows. Earlier, A05 came first in nearly all co-occurring traces (about 99 in 100); later it does so in only about 31 in 100, with about 69 in 100 interleaved or repeated. [REL001, REL002]
- A36's dominant successor changed: earlier, about 97 in 100 of its observed outgoing transitions went to A25; later, about 70 in 100 go to A30 and about 28 in 100 to A25 (the later profile rests on about 700 outgoing transitions). [DIR001]
- The relative positions of A30 and A36 became much more dispersed (standard deviation roughly 0.07 to 0.12) with slightly later medians, consistent with several occurrences per trace; these are event-position measures, not clock times. [POS002, POS003]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that, in the later window, a repetition cycle emerged around A30, A31 and A36: three activities previously executed exactly once per trace now repeat about 3.5 times per trace in roughly 68 in 100 traces, and the previously fixed chain A14 → A30 → A36 lost its fixed order, with A36 now typically flowing back to A30 (about 70 in 100 of its transitions) instead of onward to A25. The repetition is shown directly by occurrence counts and within-trace recurrence; the order change is shown directly by the relation items; the rerouting is shown directly by the successor profile. Because participation stays at 100 in 100 traces for all three activities, this is repetition of existing activities rather than activities being added, removed or made optional. Twelve control-flow items qualified, about 25 times the roughly 0.48 expected for an unchanged process, and every item's difference exceeds those seen in about 995 of 1,000 random re-splits of the pooled data. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, DIR001, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL001, REL002, POS002, POS003]

Limitations: the exact shape of the cycle is not observed — the evidence cannot show whether A30, A31 and A36 repeat in one fixed cycle or re-execute independently, and A31's placement is covered only by its occurrence count, with no relation or successor evidence for it. The interleaving pattern is also consistent with about 68 in 100 traces running the loop while the remaining 32 in 100 keep the earlier single pass, rather than a uniform change to every trace. The agreeing items largely reflect the same underlying trace behaviour and are not independent confirmations. [OCC002, REL003, REL005]

Other differences: A14's relative position shifted slightly earlier (median about 0.138 to about 0.114) with somewhat more spread, and A36 gained a few very low-frequency outgoing edges (each under about 1 in 100 of its transitions) while several similarly tiny edges from the earlier window disappeared; neither forms part of the main hypothesis. [POS001, DIR001]
