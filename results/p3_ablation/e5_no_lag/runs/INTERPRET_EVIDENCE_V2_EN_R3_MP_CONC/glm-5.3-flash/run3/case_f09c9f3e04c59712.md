### Observed differences

- The relative order of two groups of activities flipped. In the earlier window, A15, A17 and A18 — each present in essentially every case — consistently came before A14, A16 and A19, and also before A20 and A24; in the later window, A14, A16, A19, A20 and A24 consistently come before A15, A17 and A18. For example, A15 preceded A20 in essentially 100 in 100 earlier cases but follows it in essentially 100 in 100 later cases, and A24 moved from following A15/A17/A18 to preceding them. [REL001, REL006, REL008, REL009, REL015, REL019]
- Event positions shifted accordingly: A15, A17 and A18 now sit at about 64–66% of the trace (median), previously around 39%; A14, A16 and A19 now occur around 33–35% of the trace, previously around 68–70%. A20 (about 46% to 38%) and A24 (about 65% to 57%) also occur earlier in the later window. [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006, POS007, POS008]
- Outgoing transitions were rewired consistently with this reversal: A14, A16 and A19 each led exclusively to A25 and now lead exclusively to A20; A15, A17 and A18 lost their exits to A20 (previously about 31–39 in 100 of their outgoing transitions) and now exit to A25 in about 32–35 in 100; A24 now leads to A15/A17/A18 (about 32–35 in 100 each) where it previously led to A16/A14/A19; A35 now leads into A19/A16/A14 where it previously led into A17/A18/A15. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006, DIR007, DIR008]
- Participation within the A14/A16/A19 group also shifted: A14 appears in about 44 in 100 later cases (about 31 earlier), A16 in about 29 (about 38) and A19 in about 28 (about 32). [OCC001, POS003, POS006, DIR003, DIR006]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the two activity groups exchanged their relative order in the case sequence: A14, A16 and A19, previously executed in the later part of each case after A20 and A24, now run in the early part, while A15, A17 and A18, previously early, now run late; A20 and A24 each stayed between the two groups (flipping their order against both), A35 still leads into whichever group runs first, and the group exits reconnected so that the early group leaves to A20 and the late group to A25 — the reverse of before. [REL001, REL004, REL005, REL008, REL009, DIR001, DIR002, DIR006, DIR008]

The pattern is direct and internally consistent: every measured pair across the two groups flipped from one fixed order to the other, the position shifts match the order flips, and 38 qualifying items against roughly 0.42 expected if nothing had changed makes sampling noise an unlikely account. The evidence cannot, however, distinguish both groups moving from one group crossing the other, and no qualified evidence covers the activities between A20 and A24, so the exact span of the reordered segment remains open; a competing explanation that cannot be fully ruled out is a different restructuring (for example relocating the three activities of one group individually rather than together) that produces the same pairwise orders. [REL001, REL006, REL008, REL010, REL015, REL020, POS001, POS002]

Other differences: The choice among A14, A16 and A19 — whose shares together cover essentially every case in both windows — shifted toward A14 (about 31 to 44 in 100 cases), with A16 (about 38 to 29) and A19 (about 32 to 28) taken less often; all three remain well observed in both windows, so this is a shift in how often existing alternatives are taken, not a replaced path. [OCC001, REL001, REL006, REL007]
