### Observed differences

- The activities A30, A31 and A36 stopped repeating. In the earlier window each occurred about 3.5 times per trace on average and recurred within a trace in about 75 in 100 traces; in the later window each occurs about once per trace, with recurrence in only about 1 in 100 traces. All three still appear in every trace in both windows (participation 100% throughout). [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003]
- In the later window, A36's dominant successor changed. Earlier, about 70 in 100 of A36's outgoing transitions led back to A30 and about 28 in 100 led to A25; in the later window about 92 in 100 lead to A25 and only about 4 in 100 to A30. In absolute terms the exit A36→A25 occurred a similar number of times in both windows (roughly 190 transitions), while the return edge A36→A30 collapsed from roughly 480 to about 8 transitions. [DIR002]
- The relationship between A30 and A36 changed from interleaved repetition to a fixed order. The two activities co-occur in every trace in both windows, but earlier they were interleaved or repeated in about 76 in 100 traces (with A30 first in only about 25 in 100), whereas in the later window A30 precedes A36 in about 98 in 100 traces. [REL001]
- A30's own routing is essentially stable: the edge A30→A31 carried about 99 in 100 of A30's outgoing transitions in the earlier window and about 97 in 100 in the later window; only single rare edges differ. [DIR001]
- The relative positions of A30 and A36 are essentially unchanged (mean positions about 0.42–0.44 and about 0.49 of the trace), but their position spread narrowed (standard deviation from about 0.12 to about 0.07), consistent with one occurrence per trace instead of several spread across the trace. [POS001, POS002]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the repeated execution of the A30/A31/A36 segment largely stopped: in the earlier window each of these activities ran about three to four times per trace, A36 returned to A30 in about 70 in 100 of its transitions, and the A30/A36 pair interleaved in about 76 in 100 traces; in the later window each activity runs once per trace, A36 exits to A25 in about 92 in 100 of its transitions, and the pair executes in the fixed order A30 before A36 in about 98 in 100 traces. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, DIR002, REL001]

The hypothesis is shown directly by the repetition measures (mean occurrences falling from about 3.5 to about 1 with participation unchanged at 100%), by the collapse of the A36→A30 return edge, and by the relational change from interleaved to fixed ordering; the screening produced 8 qualifying control-flow items where only about 0.45 would be expected without a change, so the set of findings is unlikely for a stable process. [OCC001, DIR002, REL001]

Limitations: several items describe the same underlying trace behaviour and are therefore not independent confirmations, and the residual A36→A30 transitions rest on about 210 outgoing transitions, so the evidence cannot distinguish whether the return path was removed from routing or is now only rarely taken. A competing explanation the two windows cannot rule out is a shift in the mix of trace variants (loop-heavy versus single-pass) between the samples rather than a change in a single behaviour, although the unchanged full participation and near-zero recurrence in the later window make a pure mix shift less likely. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003]

Other differences: only minor churn among the rare successor edges of A30 and A36 (each about 1–5 transitions out of roughly 690 earlier and 210 later), which is at noise level; no further behavioural difference appears at the configured thresholds. [DIR001, DIR002]
