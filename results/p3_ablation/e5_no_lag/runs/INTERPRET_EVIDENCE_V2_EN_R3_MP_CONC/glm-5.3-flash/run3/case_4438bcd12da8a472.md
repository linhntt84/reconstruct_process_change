### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, A14, A15 and A20 each occurred in about 47 in 100 traces (94 of 200), always together and at most once per trace; in the later window each occurred in about 199 of 200 traces, still at most once. Each of these differences is larger than those seen in about 199 of 200 random re-splits of the pooled data. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003]
- The flow out of A36 was redistributed: earlier, of the outgoing transitions observed at A36 (which ran in every trace in both windows), about 53 in 100 went directly to A21 and about 47 in 100 to A14; in the later window about 199 in 200 go to A14 and only about 1 in 200 still goes directly to A21. [DIR001]
- The block's internal order is unchanged in both windows — A14 before A20 before A15 — and it always sits after A36 and before A21. What changed is its frequency: earlier the three activities either all occurred together (47% of traces) or were all absent (53%); in the later window they co-occur in about 199 of 200 traces, with A21 following the block in essentially all of them. [REL001, REL002, REL007, REL003, REL008, REL012, REL004, REL009, REL013]
- A36 and A21 themselves ran in every trace in both windows and shifted only slightly in relative event position (A36 mean about 0.33 → 0.30, A21 about 0.41 → 0.43 of the trace), consistent with the block now always lying between them; note this indicator measures event order, not clock time. [POS001, POS002]
- A40 kept roughly the same overall frequency (about 60 in 100 traces in both windows), but earlier it also ran without A14 (about 33 in 100 traces), whereas in the later window it never occurs without A14. A37 was present throughout; its co-occurrence with A14 rose from 47 to about 199 in 200 traces, with A14 first in roughly 55–65 of 100 co-occurring traces and interleaved execution in most of the rest, in both windows. ### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

Screening tested 78 control-flow units and 21 evidence items qualified, against about 0.39 expected if nothing had changed; a yield many times that expectation cannot be explained by chance as a whole, even though the items overlap heavily and largely reflect one underlying behaviour. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, DIR001, POS001, POS002, REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, REL007, REL008, REL009, REL010, REL011, REL012, REL013, REL014, REL015]

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the activity group A14, A15, A20 changed from an optional block to an essentially mandatory one: earlier it was executed as A14 → A20 → A15 in about 47 in 100 cases, inserted between A36 and A21, and skipped entirely in the rest; in the later window it runs in about 199 of 200 cases, in the same order and the same placement. As a result, the direct route from A36 to A21 that bypassed the group — previously taken in about 53 in 100 cases — is now taken in only about 1 in 200 cases. The signs are direct and mutually consistent across participation, routing and pair relations, and the small opposite position shifts of A36 and A21 point the same way. A competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is a shift in case mix between the windows (for example, a case type that always includes the block becoming dominant) rather than a routing change; the roughly 1 in 200 later traces still without the block show the skip path has not disappeared entirely. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, DIR001, REL001, REL002, REL007, REL003, REL008, REL012, REL004, REL009, REL013, POS001, POS002]

Other differences: A40 became tied to the block — earlier about 33 in 100 traces contained A40 without A14, while in the later window none of the 200 traces do, even though A40's overall frequency stayed at about 60–62 in 100; the evidence does not show where A40 attaches to the rest of the flow, so this dependency change is reported separately from the main hypothesis. [REL006, REL011, REL015]
