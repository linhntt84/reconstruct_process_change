### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that, in the later window, the execution order of a recurring segment reversed. Earlier, A19, A21 and A24 typically ran before the A25 → A29 step, which in turn ran before A20, A22 and A23; in the later window A20, A22 and A23 run first, A25 and A29 remain in the middle, and A19, A21 and A24 run at the end. This is shown directly by 21 relation items that all flip in the same direction — for the five always-present activities the dominant order reverses almost completely (about 97–99 in 100 on both sides) — and by the matching position shifts and successor changes (A19/A21/A24 now continue to A30 instead of A25; A20/A22/A23 now continue to A25 instead of A30; A40 now feeds A22/A20/A23 instead of A21/A19/A24). With 64 qualifying control-flow items against roughly 0.7 expected for an unchanged process, sampling alone cannot account for the pattern as a whole. [REL016, REL021, REL023, REL024, REL030, REL033, POS003, POS005, POS006, POS002, POS004, POS007, DIR006, DIR008, DIR011, DIR013, DIR007]

The evidence cannot fully rule out two competing structural readings: a reversal of the entire segment versus an exchange of the two outer groups around a fixed A25 → A29 middle, and a change in the ordering constraint versus a shift in the mix of two order variants (a minority order of about 2–3 in 100 exists in both windows). [REL023, REL016]

Other differences: A08 is largely bypassed in the later window — it appears in about 29 in 100 traces versus about 64 in 100 earlier, and A15 now leads directly to A16 (about 88 in 100 of its outgoing transitions) instead of routing through A08 (about 7 in 100 versus 39 in 100 earlier); this looks like A08 became skippable rather than an existing path being taken with different frequency. Separately, A04, A06 and A10 occur in roughly two to three times as many traces, with new outgoing edges towards A46 and A15; the direction profiles for A04 and A10 rest on only about 13–16 outgoing transitions in the earlier window, so this part is weakly supported.
