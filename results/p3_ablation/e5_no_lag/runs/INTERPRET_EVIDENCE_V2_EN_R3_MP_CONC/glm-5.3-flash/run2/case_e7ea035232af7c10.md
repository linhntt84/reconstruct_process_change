- Four activities became near-universal: in the earlier window A27 ran in about 54 in 100 traces and A36, A37 and A42 in about 46 in 100 each, roughly once per trace and never repeated; in the later window each of the four ran in 199–200 of 200 traces, still about once per trace. ### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a specific restructuring as a possibility: in the earlier window the process chose between two branches after A25, A28 and A26 — either A27 alone (about 54 in 100 cases) or the unordered group A36/A37/A42 (about 46 in 100), both followed by A31 — whereas in the later window that choice is gone and every case runs both parts in sequence: A25 now routes directly into the A36/A37/A42 group, the group runs before A28 and A26, and A26 leads almost exclusively to A27, which again runs before A31. The supporting signs are direct and consistent: the exclusivity between A27 and the group became near-universal co-occurrence with the group first, the order of the group against A26 and A28 reversed (from about 46 to about 99 in 100), the successor profiles at A25, A26 and the group members changed coherently, and participation rose from about 46–54 to essentially 100 in 100 traces; screening found 39 qualifying items against about 0.62 expected without a change, so the ensemble is not what an unchanged process typically produces. Limitations: all items are control-flow measures that partly reflect the same traces and are therefore not independent confirmations, and the evidence cannot rule out that the true change point lies away from the window boundary or that the combined behaviour existed at very low frequency beforehand — it establishes that the two windows differ, not when or why the change occurred.
