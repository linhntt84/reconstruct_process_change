### Observed differences

- Two groups of activities exchanged their places in the flow. In the earlier window, A19, A21 and A24 (each present in roughly 30–37 of 100 traces) sat at about one-third of the trace (mean relative positions 0.34–0.36), while A20, A22 and A23 — present in every trace — sat at about two-thirds (means 0.63–0.64). In the later window the picture is reversed: A20, A22 and A23 sit at about 0.38, and A19, A21 and A24 at about 0.66–0.68, while each activity's participation share stayed almost the same. [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006]

- Every one of the six tested pairs that co-occur in essentially every trace (each of A20, A22, A23 paired with A25 and with A29) reversed its execution order: the dominant order went from A25 or A29 first in about 99 of 100 co-occurrences to the A20/A22/A23 member first in about 99 of 100. [REL008, REL009, REL015, REL016, REL018, REL019]

- The same reversal appears in all fifteen tested pairs involving the rarer A19, A21 or A24; for example, A19 preceded A20 in about 30 of 100 traces in the earlier window, whereas in the later window A20 precedes A19 in about 33 of 100. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, REL007, REL010, REL011, REL012, REL013, REL014, REL017, REL020, REL021]

- Successors were rewired consistently with this: A19, A21 and A24, which previously flowed to A25 in about 97–98 of 100 outgoing transitions, now flow to A30 in about 95–98 of 100; A20, A22 and A23 moved their exit from A30 (about 29–37 of 100 transitions) to A25 (about 27–37 of 100) while keeping similar shares among themselves; A29 now leads to A24, A21 and A19 (about 29–38 in 100 each) where it previously led to A20, A23 and A22; and A40 now leads to A22, A20 and A23 where it previously led to A21, A24 and A19. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006, DIR007, DIR008]

- A25 and A29 kept their place between the two groups, shifting only slightly later (A25 mean position 0.38 to 0.45; A29 0.57 to 0.64). [POS007, POS008]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that two groups of activities exchanged their order while the surrounding frame stayed in place: in the earlier window the flow ran A40 → A19/A21/A24 → A25 → … → A29 → A20/A22/A23 → A30, and in the later window it runs A40 → A20/A22/A23 → A25 → … → A29 → A19/A21/A24 → A30. The order change is shown directly by the relation items, the position shifts match it, and the successor rewiring (the exits into A25 and A30 exchanging between the groups, with A29 and A40 now feeding the other group) fits an exchange of placement; participation shares barely moved, so this is not activities becoming optional, repeated or being added or removed. [REL001, REL006, REL008, REL015, POS001, POS002, POS007, DIR001, DIR002, DIR007, DIR008]

The volume of qualifying evidence also exceeds what an unchanged process would produce: 37 control-flow items qualified where roughly 0.6 would be expected by chance, each with a difference larger than about 199 of 200 random re-splits, and all items describe this one coherent reordering rather than isolated anomalies. [DIR001, POS002, REL008]

A competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is a shift in the mix of two pre-existing variants, one with each ordering, rather than a reordering within a single process model; the two window samples look the same under either reading. [REL008, POS002]

Other differences: a few rare outgoing transitions (each about 1–2 in 100 of the source's transitions) appear or disappear between the windows — A24 gains rare edges to A36, A40, A32 and A04, A22 gains one to A06, and the rare edges from A20 to A07, A31, A08 and A05 are no longer observed; at these frequencies such single-edge differences can also arise from sampling noise. [DIR002, DIR004, DIR006]
