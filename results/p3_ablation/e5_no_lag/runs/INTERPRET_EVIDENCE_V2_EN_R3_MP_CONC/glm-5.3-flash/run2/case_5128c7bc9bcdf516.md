### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, the activities A15, A17, A18, A19, A20 and A21 followed one another in a variable order: each activity's observed continuations split across three or four successors (for example, A15 was followed by A18 in about 62 in 100 of its outgoing transitions and by A19, A20 or A21 in about 12–14 in 100 each; A18 led to A27 in about 53 in 100). - Pairwise order moved from mixed or reversed to fixed along one chain. ### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the ordering constraints among A15, A17, A18, A19, A20 and A21 changed from a flexible, variable order to a single fixed sequence: in the later window the flow runs A26 → A17 → A15 → A18 → A19 → A21 → A20, and the transition to A27 now leaves from A20 (about 99.5 in 100) whereas earlier it left mostly from A18 (about 53 in 100) or A20 (about 47 in 100). [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006, DIR007]

The relational evidence shows this change directly: reversed pairs (A15/A19, A18/A19, A18/A21) and previously unordered pairs (A15/A21, A17/A19) all became fixed in exactly this chain order, and the successor profiles collapsed from three–four alternatives to one, with the position shifts matching the new sequence. All six activities co-occur in 100 in 100 traces in both windows, so this is a reordering rather than a change in participation. The volume of qualifying items (21, against roughly 0.33 expected by chance in a stable process, all with differences larger than in about 99.5 in 100 random re-splits) makes chance sampling implausible as a whole. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006, DIR007, POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006, REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, REL007, REL008]

Limitations: all items come from the control-flow family and several indicators describe the same traces, so they are not independent confirmations; the positional items use event positions rather than clock time, so no claim about timing is made. A competing explanation the evidence cannot fully rule out is a shift in the mix of pre-existing variants (for example, one interleaving variant disappearing while another takes over) rather than a tightening of the ordering rules themselves; behaviourally the two look alike here.
