- The activities A20, A22, A23, A24, A25 and A26 ran in a flexible order in the earlier window and in a nearly fixed order in the later window. ### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the ordering constraints among A20, A22, A23, A24, A25 and A26 were tightened: a block that was previously executed in varying orders, with each activity branching to several common successors, is now executed as one fixed sequence entered from A31 via A22, running A22 → A20 → A23 → A24 → A26 → A25 and on to A32. This rests on direct relational evidence — eight activity pairs observed in all 200 traces of both windows moved from mixed or reversed orders to near-universal one-way order — and on seven successor profiles of about 200 outgoing transitions each that collapsed to a single dominant successor, with the individual position shifts agreeing in direction. Forty-four control-flow items qualified in screening against fewer than one expected for an unchanged process, so the set of differences as a whole is not what random re-splits of an unchanged process produce. A competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that both windows contain the same process variants in different proportions — a shift in the mix of existing case types rather than in the ordering rules themselves; two aggregate windows cannot separate these. [REL014, REL015, REL016, REL017, REL018, REL019, REL020, REL021, DIR007, DIR008, DIR009, DIR010, DIR011, DIR012, DIR013, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006]

Other differences: A08 is now skipped in most cases (72 of 100 traces earlier, 29 of 100 in the later window) and, when it runs, appears later in the trace and routes less exclusively to A16; A10 runs about three times as often and now leads mainly to A48. These participation changes stand beside the ordering hypothesis rather than within it, and the routing shifts at A02 and A04 are too thinly supported to carry weight.
