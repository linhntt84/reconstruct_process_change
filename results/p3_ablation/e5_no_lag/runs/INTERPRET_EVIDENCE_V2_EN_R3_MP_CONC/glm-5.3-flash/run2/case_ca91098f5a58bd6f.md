### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, A14, A15 and A20 each appeared in a minority of traces — about 35 in 100 for A14 and A20 and about 30 in 100 for A15, never more than once per trace — while in the later window none of the three appears in any of the 200 traces. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003]
- The three behaved as mutually exclusive alternatives: in the earlier window each pair of them never occurred in the same trace (for example, A14 and A20 each appeared alone in about 35 in 100 traces and together in none). [REL001, REL003, REL008]
- A36 occurs in every trace in both windows. In the earlier window its observed outgoing transitions split roughly evenly among the three alternatives (about 35 in 100 to A20, 30 to A15, 35 to A14); in the later window all of its observed outgoing transitions go directly to A21. [DIR004, DIR001, DIR002, DIR003]
- In the earlier window, whenever one of the three occurred it sat between A36 and the always-present activities A21 and A26 (it always followed A36 and co-occurred with and preceded A21/A26 in exactly its participation share); in the later window A21 and A26 still occur in every trace, but never together with any of the three. [REL004, REL005, REL006, REL009, REL010, REL011, REL013, REL014, REL015]
- A19 occurred in about 49 in 100 traces earlier and about 56 in 100 later; earlier it was always preceded by one of the three alternatives, whereas in the later window it occurs without any of them. [REL002, REL007, REL012]
- Minor shifts: A21's mean relative position moved slightly earlier (about 0.39 to 0.37 of the trace) and A36's slightly later (about 0.32 to 0.33); these are event positions, not clock times. [POS004, POS005]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that, in the later window, the optional block of three mutually exclusive activities A14, A15 and A20 — which previously ran between A36 and A21/A26 — is no longer executed, so that affected traces proceed from A36 directly to A21. Supporting signs: participation of all three fell from about 30–35 in 100 traces to 0 in 200; A36's observed outgoing transitions changed from a roughly even split across the three to entirely A21; and the relational items consistently show the three losing all co-occurrence with A21, A26 and each other. The screening comparison also shows 27 qualifying items against roughly 0.4 expected if nothing had changed, so the set as a whole is not plausibly explained by sampling variation. A direct consequence is that A19, which previously always followed one of the three, now occurs in about 56 in 100 traces without them. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, DIR004, REL002, REL007, REL012]

Limitations: zero participation only means the activities were not observed in the later window, so the evidence cannot distinguish complete removal from the three having become very rare, and it cannot show what, if anything, now precedes A19. The small position shifts of A21 and A36 are consistent with the affected traces becoming shorter but do not independently locate any activity. [OCC001, POS004, POS005]

Other differences: none beyond the minor position shifts of A21 and A36 reported above, which are best read as side effects of the same underlying difference rather than separate changes. [POS004, POS005]
