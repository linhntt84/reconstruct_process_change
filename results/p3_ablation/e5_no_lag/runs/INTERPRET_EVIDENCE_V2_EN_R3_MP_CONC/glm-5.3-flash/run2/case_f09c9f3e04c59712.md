### Observed differences

- The two windows show two three-activity blocks exchanging places along the case sequence. In the earlier window, A15, A17 and A18 ran early (mean event position about 0.39 of the trace) while A14, A16 and A19 ran late (about 0.68–0.69); in the later window this is reversed, with A14, A16 and A19 at about 0.35 and A15, A17 and A18 at about 0.64. [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006]
- All 21 tested pairs across the two blocks reversed their order, several completely: A15 preceded A20 in every earlier-window trace and followed it in every later-window trace; A14 followed A15, A17, A18, A20 and A24 whenever they co-occurred (about 31 in 100 traces) and now precedes all of them (about 44 in 100). [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL008, REL009, REL015, REL016, REL018, REL019]
- Successors were rewired consistently with this reversal: A14, A16 and A19 led exclusively to A25 in the earlier window and exclusively to A20 in the later one; A15, A17 and A18 lost their edges to A20 and gained A25 (about 35 in 100 of A15's outgoing transitions); A24's outgoing transitions moved from A16/A14/A19 to A15/A18/A17, and A35's from A17/A18/A15 to A14/A16/A19. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006, DIR007, DIR008]
- Participation also shifted: A14 appeared in about 31 in 100 traces earlier (62 of 200) versus about 44 in 100 later (87 of 200), while A16 fell from about 38 in 100 to about 29 in 100 and A19 from about 32 to about 28. [OCC001, POS003, POS006]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that two activity blocks swapped their places in the sequence: earlier, A35 led into the block {A15, A17, A18}, which exited to A20, and A24 led into the block {A14, A16, A19}, which exited to A25; in the later window the blocks are exchanged, so A35 feeds {A14, A16, A19} into A20 and A24 feeds {A15, A17, A18} into A25, with A20 and A24 keeping their pivot roles (shifting slightly earlier, A20 from about 0.46 to 0.38 and A24 from about 0.65 to 0.57 of the trace). [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006, DIR007, DIR008, POS007, POS008]

The support is direct and consistent: every one of the 21 cross-block pairs reversed its order, several flipping from 100% one way to 100% the other, the position distributions moved by roughly a quarter to a third of the trace in the predicted directions, and all eight successor profiles rewired exactly as the swap predicts; overall, 38 control-flow items qualified against roughly 0.42 expected by chance in an unchanged process, so the pattern as a whole is far beyond what random re-splits produce. [REL008, REL015, REL018, POS002, POS003, DIR001, DIR007, DIR008, OCC001]

Limitations: the position indicators use event positions rather than clock time, but the swap is shown directly by the order reversals and successor changes, not by positions alone. A competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is a complete switch between two process variants (each window sampling a single variant) rather than a swap within one process; both would look identical here. [REL008, REL015, POS002, DIR001]

Other differences: A14 became more frequent, appearing in about 44 in 100 later-window traces versus about 31 in 100 earlier (87 vs 62 of 200), still at most once per trace, while A16 and A19 became slightly less frequent (about 38 to 29 in 100 and 32 to 28 in 100); these participation changes are not explained by the swap itself and could partly reflect sampling variation. [OCC001, POS003, POS006]
