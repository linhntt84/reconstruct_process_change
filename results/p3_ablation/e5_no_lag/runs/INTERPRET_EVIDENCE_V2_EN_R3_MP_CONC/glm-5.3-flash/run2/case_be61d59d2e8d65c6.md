### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, A19, A20 and A25 each ran in a minority of cases — about 33, 29 and 40 in 100 traces respectively (65, 57 and 81 of 200 traces), never more than once per trace — while in the later window each appears in only about 1 in 200 traces. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003]
- The three were mutually exclusive alternatives: before, any pair of them co-occurred in only about 0.5 in 100 traces, so taken together they appeared in virtually every trace; in the later window, about 99 in 100 traces contain none of them. [REL001, REL002, REL006]
- The block sat between A27 and A29: before, A27's outgoing transitions split between A28 (about 52 in 100) and the three block activities (about 44 in 100 combined), and A28's transitions split between A29 (about 43 in 100) and the block activities (about 53 in 100); A27 came before the block activity in essentially all of its appearances, and the block activity led on to A29 in nearly all of its traces (A19 in about 32, A20 about 27, A25 about 40 in 100 traces). [DIR005, DIR006, REL003, REL005, REL009, REL012]
- In the later window, A27 and A28 still occur in essentially every trace, but their transitions into the block have disappeared: A27 now leads to A28 in about 98 in 100 of its transitions, and A28 leads to A29 in about 97 in 100. [DIR005, DIR006]
- The apparent new targets of A19, A20 and A25 in the later window each rest on a single observed trace and carry little weight. [DIR002, DIR003, DIR004]
- A10, still observed in both windows, shows a redistributed successor mix: before it led mainly to A15 (about 20 in 100), A47 and A16 (about 17 each) and A45 (about 14); in the later window mainly to A26 (about 17), A45 (about 15), A16 and A41 (about 9 each), with many targets seen only once or twice. [DIR001]
- A27 and A29 occur in every trace in both windows, and their average event positions shifted only slightly (A27 from 0.46 to 0.48 of the trace, A29 from 0.57 to 0.55). [POS004, POS005]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the three-way alternative block formed by A19, A20 and A25 is no longer executed. In the earlier window virtually every case ran exactly one of these three activities after A27, on the way towards A28/A29; in the later window the three have practically vanished (about 1 in 200 traces each) and cases run directly from A27 to A28 and from A28 to A29. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, REL001, REL002, REL006, DIR005, DIR006]

The signs are direct and consistent: the participation collapse is measured on 65, 57 and 81 traces before versus 1 each after; the transitions from A27 and A28 into the block, together roughly half of their outgoing transitions before, are gone while A27→A28 and A28→A29 absorb nearly all transitions; and the block activities went from always co-occurring with A27/A28/A29 to essentially never. Because occurrences are counted per trace, the drop indicates the activities stopped being executed rather than moved elsewhere. With 26 qualifying items against roughly 0.5 expected by chance among the 108 tested units, the set of differences is far beyond what an unchanged process produces. [DIR005, DIR006, REL003, REL005, REL010, OCC001]

Limitations: the later outgoing profiles of A19, A20 and A25 rest on a single trace each, and the evidence cannot rule out that the later window simply contains a different mix of cases that never used this branch, rather than the branch no longer being available. [DIR002, DIR003, DIR004]

Other differences:
- A10's successor redistribution is not covered by the main hypothesis; the evidence cannot show whether it is an independent rerouting or a downstream effect of the changed case mix. [DIR001]
- A27 and A29 show only small shifts in average event position while remaining in every trace; on its own such a position shift does not show that an activity was moved. [POS004, POS005]
