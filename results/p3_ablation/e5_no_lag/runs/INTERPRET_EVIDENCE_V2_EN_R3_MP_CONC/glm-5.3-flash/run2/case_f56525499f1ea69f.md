### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The 81 qualifying control-flow items are roughly 100 times the number expected without a change (0.76), so the set of differences cannot be explained by sampling noise in a stable process. The evidence supports the possibility that the late stage was restructured from two mutually exclusive alternative groups into one combined, near-mandatory sequence: in the later window A33, A35 and A36 run in varying order before A37, A38 and A39 in almost every case, with A30 preceding the whole block and A31 following it, whereas earlier each case ran only one of the two groups (each in about half of the cases) and A31 directly followed that group. Supporting signs: the relational items show the change directly (group-to-group co-occurrence from 0 to about 98 in 100, A33-group first in about 97 in 100), participation of all six activities rises from about half to nearly all cases, the successor profiles of A30 and A33/A35/A36 shift consistently (edges into A37–A39 appear, the direct handover to A31 disappears), and event positions move coherently (A33/A35/A36 earlier, A37–A39 slightly later). Limitations: the evidence cannot distinguish a modification of the routing itself from a shift in which process variants the two windows sampled, and the order within A33/A35/A36 remains unfixed, so the combined sequence is only partially ordered.
