### Observed differences

- Before, the six activities A33, A35, A36, A37, A38 and A39 each ran in every trace (200 of 200, with no repetitions); in the later window each runs in only about 47–57 in 100 traces (A33 and A35 about 46, A36 about 47, A37 and A38 about 56–57, A39 about 56). [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006]
- The six split into two trios that move as units. Within trios, members still appear together whenever the trio runs (A33–A35 co-occur in about 46 in 100 traces, matching A33's participation; A37–A38 in about 55–56 in 100). Across trios, co-occurrence collapsed from 100 in 100 traces before (e.g., A33 with A37) to about 3 in 100 in the later window (A33–A37 about 3, A36–A39 about 3). [REL013, REL015, REL022, REL024, REL025, OCC001, OCC004]
- In the later window about 44 in 100 traces contain only the first trio (A33, A35, A36), about 53 in 100 only the second trio (A37, A38, A39), about 3 in 100 contain both, and essentially none contain neither; before, all six activities ran together in every trace. [REL015, REL019, REL022, REL024]
- Ordering around the block is unchanged: A30 and A31 still occur in every trace, A30 still precedes the block and A31 still follows it whenever block members run, the order within each trio stays mixed (no fixed order) in both windows, and in the roughly 3 in 100 traces containing both trios the first trio still precedes the second. [REL001, REL004, REL007, REL013, REL015, POS001, POS002]
- Successor profiles match this picture: before, A30's outgoing transitions went almost only to the first trio (A33 about 34%, A35 about 36%, A36 about 28%); in the later window they spread over all six block members (roughly 12–19% each). A33, A35 and A36, which never led to A31 before, now lead directly to A31 in about 32%, 34% and 26% of their outgoing transitions respectively. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a structural change in the segment between A30 and A31: the six activities A33, A35, A36, A37, A38 and A39, which previously always executed together in sequence (first trio, then second trio), behave in the later window as two mutually exclusive alternative sub-blocks — roughly 44 in 100 traces run only A33/A35/A36, roughly 53 in 100 run only A37/A38/A39, about 3 in 100 run both, and virtually none run neither. [OCC001, OCC004, REL015, REL019, REL022, REL025]

The signs are direct and consistent: participation fell from 100% to about 47–57% for all six activities; cross-trio co-occurrence fell from 100% to about 3% while within-trio co-occurrence stayed at the trio's full participation; the A30 → block → A31 ordering and the mixed orders within each trio are unchanged; and the new direct transitions from A33/A35/A36 to A31 are consistent with the second trio being skipped. Each item's difference exceeds about 99 of 100 random re-splits, and 43 items qualified against fewer than 1 expected without change, so chance alone cannot explain the pattern. [OCC001, OCC006, REL015, REL025, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004]

Limitations: the evidence cannot show where the choice between the two sub-blocks is made, since no deciding activity is identified; and the roughly 3 in 100 traces containing both trios may be a retained full path or sampling noise rather than a stable path. [REL015, REL022]

Other differences: relative event positions shifted — A30's mean position moved later (about 0.62 to 0.67 of the trace) and A31's slightly earlier (about 0.82 to 0.80), with block members shifting similarly (A33 later, A39 earlier); these are consistent with the block simply being shorter when only one trio runs, and are based on event positions rather than clock time. [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS006]
