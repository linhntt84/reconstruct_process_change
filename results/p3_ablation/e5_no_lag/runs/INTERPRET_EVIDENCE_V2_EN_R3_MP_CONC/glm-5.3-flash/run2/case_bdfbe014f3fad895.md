### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, A27 ran exactly once in every trace, and A26 led to it in about 95 in 100 of A26's outgoing transitions. In the later window, A27 appears in only about 52 in 100 traces, and A26's outgoing transitions split roughly evenly between A27 (about 51 in 100) and the three activities A32, A33 and A38 (together about 49 in 100). - A06 nearly disappeared, from about 20 in 100 traces to about 4 in 100; its after-window routing profile rests on only 8 traces and is weak. [OCC002, DIR002]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that, in the later window, the point after A26 offers two alternative routes: the original route through A27 and a new route through A32, A33 and A38, which run together in varying order and then lead into A28; as a result, A27 changed from an activity performed in every trace to one performed in about half of the traces. The redirection is observed directly at A26, and the new activities' presence, mid-trace position, mutual relationships and precedence before A28 are consistently documented; their near-zero co-occurrence with A27 rules out parallel execution. With 52 qualifying items against roughly 0.65 expected if nothing had changed, chance sampling cannot explain the set as a whole, and each new activity appears in about 100 of 200 traces, far beyond a rare variant. A competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that A32, A33 and A38 existed before at a frequency too low to observe, so "newly added" cannot be distinguished from "previously very rare and now frequent"; the evidence also cannot show what determines which route a case takes.
