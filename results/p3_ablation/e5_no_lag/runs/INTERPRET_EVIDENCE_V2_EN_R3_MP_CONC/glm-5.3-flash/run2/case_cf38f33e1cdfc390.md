### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a shift in how often two existing alternative paths are taken: after A15, the branch in which A16, A17 and A18 run together (in no fixed order, accompanied by A25) was executed in about 75 in 100 cases in the earlier window and about 25 in 100 in the later, while the alternative branch in which A19, A20 and A21 run together rose from about 25 to about 76 in 100 cases. [DIR001, OCC001, OCC004, REL009, REL022]

The routing change is observed directly at A15, whose transition shares to A16/A17/A18 fell from about 75 in 100 combined to about 25 in 100 while the shares to A19/A20/A21 rose correspondingly, with all six transitions still present. Participation and relation profiles mirror this swap consistently, and the screening produced 34 qualifying items where about 0.4 would be expected without a change, so chance cannot account for the pattern as a whole; every item's difference also exceeds about 995 of 1,000 random re-splits. The internal shape of each branch — three unordered activities preceded by A15 and accompanied by A25 — is the same in both windows, and both branches remain observed in substantial shares (about 25–76 in 100), which fits a change in how often existing paths are taken rather than activities being added, removed or moved; a swap of the two blocks' positions is not supported because the within-branch order stays mixed and A15's precedence holds in both windows. [DIR001, OCC001, OCC006, REL001, REL004, REL022, REL024]

Limitations: the evidence shows aggregate branch shares, not the routing rule itself, and cannot rule out that the two windows mix case types in different proportions (more cases of a kind that uses the A19–A21 branch) rather than a modified choice; with 200 traces per window, the exact shares are estimates. [OCC001, OCC004]

Other differences: none observed — all qualifying items describe the same branch-frequency change. [DIR001, OCC001, REL001]
