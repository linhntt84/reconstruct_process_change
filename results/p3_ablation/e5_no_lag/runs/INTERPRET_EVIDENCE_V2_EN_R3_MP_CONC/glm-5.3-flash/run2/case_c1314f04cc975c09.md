### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, the activities A25, A26 and A31 each occurred exactly once per trace (present in 100% of traces, never repeated). In the later window, each of them occurs about 3.5 times per trace on average, and in about 68 in 100 traces the activity repeats within the same trace [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003].
- The outgoing transitions of A31 changed: in the earlier window every one of them led to A20, while in the later window about 71 in 100 lead to A25 and about 29 in 100 still lead to A20 (roughly one exit per trace) [DIR001].
- The pair A25–A31 still occurs together in every trace, but their fixed order largely dissolved: earlier, A25 came before A31 in 100% of traces; in the later window only about 32 in 100 traces keep that single fixed order, while in about 68 in 100 the two activities are interleaved or repeated [REL001].
- A25 and A31 now appear at slightly later mean event positions with about twice the spread (standard deviation roughly 0.06 to 0.12 in both windows' comparison), a pattern consistent with several occurrences scattered through the trace; these are event positions, not clock times [POS001, POS002].

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

One plausible reading of the evidence is that in the later window the process contains a repeating cycle over A25, A26 and A31: A31, which previously always proceeded to A20, now returns to A25 in most executions, so the three activities — previously executed exactly once each — repeat roughly three to four times per trace in about two thirds of traces, with the exit to A20 still terminating the sequence. This rests on direct observations: the successor change at A31 is seen in the transition shares [DIR001], the fixed A25-before-A31 order gave way to interleaved/repeated execution in about 68 in 100 traces [REL001], and all three activities show the same rise from one to about 3.5 occurrences with recurrence in about 68 in 100 traces [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003]. Seven control-flow items qualified against roughly 0.29 expected for an unchanged process, so this set of differences is unlikely to be a sampling artefact [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, DIR001, POS001, POS002, REL001].

Limitations: the items largely describe one underlying behaviour and are not independent confirmations of each other. A26's place inside the cycle is inferred only from its identical repetition profile, since no directional or relational item covers it. The evidence also cannot show whether the repetition is a conditional rework loop or occurs in every trace, nor whether a very rare loop variant already existed in the earlier window (it would have to be very rare, as no repeats appear among the 200 earlier traces).
