- In the earlier window, the activities A31, A22, A20, A23, A24, A26 and A25 formed a fixed chain (A31→A22→A20→A23→A24→A26→A25, then A25→A32): each named successor followed its source in about 99–100 in 100 observed transitions. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006, DIR007]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the strict sequential ordering among A20, A22, A23, A24, A25 and A26 was loosened in the later window: the six activities still occur together in every trace, but the fixed chain A22→A20→A23→A24→A26→A25 gave way to a flexible order in which some pairs typically run in the opposite direction (A24 before A20 or A23; A26 before A23) and others show no fixed order at all, while the deterministic hand-offs were replaced by several alternative successions within the same group. This rests on direct relational evidence: eight activity pairs all moved from a single fixed order (at least 98 in 100) to mixed or reversed orders, with co-occurrence unchanged at 100 in 100. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, REL007, REL008]

The change is consistent across the evidence: 21 items qualified against roughly 0.5 expected for an unchanged process, so the set as a whole is far beyond what random splitting produces; the successor profiles at six sources show the deterministic chain edges weakening while new edges inside the group appear; and the position shifts (A24/A25/A26 earlier, A20/A22/A23 later) agree with the reordered pairs. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006, POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006]

An important competing explanation remains: the later shares (for example, A20 still precedes A24 in about 25 in 100 later traces) cannot distinguish a genuinely flexible order from a mixture in which some cases keep the old fixed sequence and others follow a new one; and for single pairs such as A23/A24 a swap of the two activities would look similar, although the pairs with near-even mixed orders rule out a pure swap as the overall explanation. The exact new ordering structure cannot be determined from this evidence. [REL001, REL003, REL004, REL006, REL007]

Other differences: A31 now routes about 47 in 100 of its outgoing transitions directly to A24 instead of always to A22, bypassing the front of the chain, and a few previously unseen weak transitions (about 0.5–1 in 100) appear from A23 and A25. [DIR007, DIR003, DIR005]
