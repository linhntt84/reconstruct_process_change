### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that, in the later window, the block A14 → A15 → A20 between A36 and A21 is bypassed in roughly 47 in 100 cases — A36 then leads directly to A21 — while the original chain A36 → A14 → A15 → A20 → A21 still runs in the remaining about 53 in 100 cases. The routing change at A36 is observed directly, the participation of A14, A15 and A20 falls from 100% to about 53% while A36 and A21 stay at about 100%, and the relational items show A21-only and A36-only traces in exactly the share in which the block is absent, with all orderings inside the block unchanged. Since the A36 → A21 transition was not observed at all in the earlier window, this looks like the block becoming optional rather than a re-weighting of previously seen paths. [DIR001, OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, REL003, REL004, REL008]

The scale of the signal backs this: 14 control-flow items qualified where about 0.35 would be expected by chance among the 70 tested units, and every item's difference exceeds about 99.5 in 100 random re-splits; all 14 items describe this one underlying difference, so chance sampling or a rare variant cannot explain the pattern — a bypass taken in 47% of cases could not have hidden in 200 earlier traces that all show the full chain. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, DIR001, POS001, REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, REL007, REL008, REL009]

The evidence cannot show what determines whether a case takes the bypass, and because only screened activities were tested and positions are step-based, it cannot rule out that untested substitute work covers the block in bypass cases. [DIR001, REL003]

Other differences: none beyond the above; the slight forward shift of A21's average position is consistent with the bypass rather than a separate change. [POS001]
