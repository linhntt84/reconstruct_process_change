### Observed differences

- Six activities became far more frequent: before, A28, A30 and A31 each appeared in about 49 in 100 traces and A32, A33 and A34 in about 52 in 100; in the later window each of the six appears in about 99–100 in 100 traces, still at most once per trace. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006]
- Before, these six formed two mutually exclusive trios: A28/A30/A31 never co-occurred with A32/A33/A34 (co-occurrence 0 in 200 traces), and each trace contained exactly one trio (about 49 vs 52 in 100). In the later window, activities from both trios co-occur in about 98 in 100 traces. [REL013, REL015, REL018, REL019, REL022, REL025]
- Ordering around the segment is stable but the segment's internal structure changed: A25 precedes all six activities and all six precede A26 in both windows (in about 98–100 in 100 later traces). Within each trio the order remains unfixed (e.g., A28 before A30 in about 49 in 100 later traces, the reverse in about 50 in 100), while in the later window every activity of the first trio precedes every activity of the second trio in about 98 in 100 traces. [REL001, REL004, REL007, REL010, REL013, REL015, REL018, REL025]
- Direct successors shifted accordingly: A25 now leads almost only into the first trio (A32 as direct successor fell from about 16 to under 1 in 100 of its outgoing transitions, A34 from about 19 to 1 in 100), and the direct transitions from A28/A30/A31 to A26 nearly vanished (A28→A26: about 36 to under 1 in 100), replaced by transitions into the second trio. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004]
- Relative positions moved consistently with a longer segment: A25 shifted slightly earlier (mean relative position 0.67 → 0.62) and A26 slightly later (0.80 → 0.83); first-trio activities moved earlier (A28: 0.74 → 0.68) and second-trio activities later (A32: 0.73 → 0.77). [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS006, POS008]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the segment between A25 and A26 changed from a choice between two alternative groups — either {A28, A30, A31} or {A32, A33, A34}, executed once per case in mixed internal order — to both groups being executed in every case, first A28/A30/A31 in any order, then A32/A33/A34 in any order. This is shown directly by the relation between the groups: cross-trio co-occurrence rose from 0 to about 98 in 100 traces with a fixed first-trio-before-second-trio order, participation of all six activities rose from about half of traces to nearly all, and the successor profile of A25, A28, A30 and A31 redistributed exactly as this structure implies. The screening comparison supports that the pattern is not a sampling artefact: 45 control-flow items qualified where roughly 0.47 would be expected if nothing had changed, and the individual differences (participation gaps of about 48–50 in 100 with 97–200 supporting traces per window) are far too large and consistent for rare-variant noise. [OCC001, OCC004, REL001, REL013, REL015, REL019, REL022, REL025, DIR001, DIR002]

Limitations: the two windows are aggregates, so the evidence cannot distinguish a modification of one process from a shift in the mix of case types (for example, cases that always ran both groups becoming dominant); a small residue of about 1–2 in 100 later traces still shows the old either/or behaviour, so the previous alternative paths have not entirely disappeared. [REL015, REL019, REL022]
