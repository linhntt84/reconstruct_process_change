### Observed differences

- The dominant difference is a flip between two alternative activity groups. In the earlier window, A24, A25 and A26 each ran in about 82 in 100 traces (and always together as a group), while A21, A22 and A23 each ran in only about 19 in 100 traces. In the later window this is reversed: A21–A23 each run in about 79 in 100 traces (together as a group), A24–A26 in about 21 in 100. [OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, OCC007]

- A20 occurs in every trace in both windows. In the earlier window about 82% of its outgoing transitions led to A24/A25/A26 and about 18% to A21/A22/A23; in the later window about 79% lead to A21/A22/A23 and about 21% to A24/A25/A26. [DIR003, REL009, REL012]

- The two groups remain mutually exclusive alternatives in both windows: for example, A21 and A24 hardly ever co-occur; earlier A24 ran without A21 in about 81 in 100 traces, later A21 runs without A24 in about 79 in 100. Within each group the activities co-occur and appear in both orders, so neither group has a fixed internal order in either window. [REL017, REL024, REL030, REL015, REL016, REL023, REL036]

- Companion activities switched branches along with the flip: A30 (present in every trace in both windows) co-occurred with the A24–A26 group in about 82 in 100 earlier traces but now co-occurs with the A21–A23 group in about 79 in 100; A32 and A33 moved from co-occurring mainly with A24–A26 (about 52 in 100 earlier) to mainly with A21–A23 (about 55 in 100 later, from about 9 in 100 before). [REL020, REL027, REL033, REL038, REL042, REL045, REL021, REL022, REL028, REL029, REL034, REL035, REL039, REL043, REL046]

- A10 became rarer and behaves differently: present in about 17 in 100 earlier traces but about 7 in 100 later; its median event position moved from about 0.86 to about 0.36 of the trace with much larger spread (event positions, not clock times); its outgoing transitions shifted from almost exclusively A33 (about 97%) to several targets (A15 about 21%; A27, A34, A16, A33 about 7–14% each); and transitions from A32 to A10 nearly vanished (about 20% to about 1% of A32's outgoing). These rest on few observations (14–33 traces). [POS001, DIR002, DIR004, REL007, REL008]

- A02 rose from about 2 to about 9 in 100 traces with a redistributed successor profile; based on only 4 versus 18 observations. [OCC001, DIR001]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the routing choice after A20 changed: in the later window the branch in which A21, A22 and A23 run together is taken in about 79 in 100 cases (previously about 19 in 100), while the branch in which A24, A25 and A26 run together dropped to about 21 in 100 (previously about 82 in 100). This is shown directly by A20's successor shares and consistently by the participation and relation items; both branches are observed in substantial shares in both windows, stay mutually exclusive, and repetition counts are unchanged, so this is a change in how often existing paths are taken rather than a replacement or removal of activities. A30, A32 and A33 consistently accompany whichever branch executes and switched allegiance with it. The screening context supports this reading: 59 qualifying items against roughly 0.6 expected by chance for the control-flow family. Limitations: most items re-describe the same underlying flip rather than independent confirmations, and the evidence cannot rule out that the two windows simply contain different mixes of case types (shifting variant proportions) rather than a changed routing rule.
