### Observed differences

- The clearest difference is repetition. Before, A30, A31 and A36 each occurred about once per trace (means 1.0, 1.0 and 1.0), and repeats within a trace were almost absent (at most ~2 in 100 traces). In the later window, each of the three occurs about 3.5 times per trace on average, and in about 68 in 100 traces the activity appears more than once; every trace still contains each of the three activities in both windows. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003]
- Routing out of A36 changed accordingly: before, about 97 in 100 observed outgoing transitions of A36 led to A25; in the later window, about 70 in 100 lead to A30 and about 28 in 100 to A25. The number of observed outgoing transitions of A36 also rose from about 200 to about 700, matching its higher frequency. [DIR001]
- The ordering between A14, A30 and A36 loosened: the three still co-occur in every trace in both windows, but the fixed sequence A14 before A30 before A36, seen in ~98–99.5 in 100 traces before, holds in only about 32 in 100 later traces, while interleaved or repeated ordering rose from ~0–2 in 100 to about 68 in 100. [REL003, REL004, REL005]
- A30 and A36 now occur at slightly later mean relative positions (0.41 to 0.44, and 0.48 to 0.49) with clearly larger spread (standard deviations about 0.07 to 0.12), and A14's mean relative position moved slightly earlier (0.14 to 0.12) with more spread; these are position-based measures and corroborate, but alone would not prove, a change in placement. [POS001, POS002, POS003]
- A05 still co-occurs with A30 and A36 in about half the traces in both windows, but where they co-occur, A05 came first in ~53–54 in 100 traces before and in only ~16 in 100 after, with interleaved or repeated ordering rising to ~35 in 100. [REL001, REL002]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the sequence containing A14, A30, A31 and A36 became repeatable: before, these activities each ran once per trace in a fixed order (A14, then A30, then A36, which then typically led to A25), whereas in the later window the block runs about three to four times per trace in roughly two thirds of traces, with A36 now usually followed by A30 — a loop back into the block — instead of A25. The repetition is shown directly by occurrence counts and recurrence, the new A36 → A30 succession is shown directly in the routing profile, and the change from fixed to interleaved/repeated ordering is shown directly for three activity pairs; all items rest on full participation (100% of traces) in both windows with 200 or more observations each, all twelve items exceed ~99.5 of 100 random re-splits in difference size, and 12 items qualified against fewer than 1 expected by chance for the tested units. Limitations: the twelve items largely describe one underlying behaviour rather than independent confirmations, and while the A36 → A30 loop-back is directly observed, the evidence cannot show whether A31 and A14 lie inside the same repeated block or repeat independently, since their own routing is not in the evidence. An important competing explanation that cannot be ruled out is that A30, A31 and A36 each repeat independently rather than one block looping as a unit. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, DIR001, REL003, REL004, REL005, POS002, POS003]

Other differences:
- The ordering relation between A05 and the repeated activities loosened: A05 now precedes A30/A36 far less often where they co-occur (~54 in 100 before, ~16 in 100 after), without any change in how often they co-occur. [REL001, REL002]
