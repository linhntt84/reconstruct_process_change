### Observed differences

- The execution order between two groups of activities reversed. In the earlier window, A26 ran before A35, A36 and A41 in about 98–99 in 100 traces, and A27 ran before them in about 99–100 in 100 traces. In the later window the direction is inverted: A35, A36 and A41 ran before A26 in about 97–98 in 100 traces and before A27 in about 97 in 100. Both groups still occurred together in every trace in both windows (co-occurrence 100 in 100). [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006]

- Positions within the trace moved consistently with this reversal: A26's mean relative position rose from about 0.53 to about 0.62 and A27's from about 0.38 to about 0.47, while A35, A36 and A41 each fell from about 0.58 to about 0.42 (0 = start of trace, 1 = end). - The direct edges around these groups were rewired to match. A25's outgoing transitions went from about 98 in 100 towards A27 to an even three-way split towards A36, A41 and A35 (about 31–33 in 100 each). A26's transitions went from a split across A41, A35 and A36 (about 38, 33 and 28 in 100) to about 98 in 100 towards A30. A35, A36 and A41 previously sent about 28–38 in 100 of their transitions to A30; in the later window about 30–33 in 100 go to A27, and transitions to A30 have nearly disappeared (about 0.5 in 100). Each profile rests on roughly 200 outgoing transitions per window. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the execution order of two segments was exchanged: the group A35, A36 and A41, which in the earlier window ran after A26 and A27, ran before them in the later window. The edge changes fit this reversal exactly — A25, which previously led into A27, now leads into the A35/A36/A41 group; the group, which previously led to A30, now leads to A27; and A26, which previously led into the group, now leads to A30. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005]

Supporting signs: the order flip is observed directly for all six activity pairs, each moving from a near-fixed order (about 98–100 in 100) to the opposite near-fixed order (about 97–98 in 100), while the activities still co-occur in every trace — so the same activities were reordered rather than added, removed or made optional. The position shifts and the successor rewiring agree with the same reversal, each flagged difference exceeds about 995 of 1,000 random re-splits of the pooled data, and 16 items qualified against roughly 0.5 expected by chance across 106 tested units, so the pattern as a whole is not what an unchanged process typically produces. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005]

Limitations: positions are event positions within traces, not clock times, so the evidence shows order of execution rather than timing; the mutual order of A27 and A26 is not directly covered by a relation item (only suggested by their positions), and the evidence cannot rule out that the reversal came from a wider restructuring in which further activities between or around these seven also changed place.
