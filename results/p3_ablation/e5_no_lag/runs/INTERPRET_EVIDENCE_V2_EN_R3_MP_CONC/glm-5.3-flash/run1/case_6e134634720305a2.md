### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, A30, A31 and A36 each occurred about 3.5 times per trace, appeared in every trace, and repeated within a trace in roughly 75 in 100 traces. In the later window, all three still appear in every trace but occur only about once per trace, with repeats in only about 1–2 in 100 traces. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003]
- Routing out of A36 changed sharply: before, about 70 in 100 of A36's outgoing transitions led back to A30 and about 28 in 100 led to A25; in the later window, about 92 in 100 lead to A25 and only about 4 in 100 to A30. [DIR002]
- The relationship between A30 and A36 moved from repetition to a fixed order: the two co-occur in every trace in both windows, but before, they were interleaved or repeated in about 76 in 100 traces (A30 before A36 in 25); in the later window, A30 comes before A36 in 98 in 100 traces. [REL001]
- A30's outgoing routing is essentially unchanged — about 99 in 100 transitions went to A31 before and about 97 in 100 in the later window — so the forward step A30→A31 remains; the flagged item stems mainly from the drop in A30's outgoing-transition volume (690 to about 210) that follows from its reduced repetition. [DIR001]
- A30 and A36 keep about the same relative position in the trace (means roughly 0.42–0.49 in both windows), but their positional spread narrows from a standard deviation of about 0.12 to about 0.07, consistent with a single pass replacing a variable-length cycle. [POS001, POS002]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the cycle through A30, A31 and A36 stopped repeating: in the earlier window these three activities ran roughly three to four passes per trace in an interleaved cycle in which A36 typically returned to A30, while in the later window each executes about once per trace in a fixed A30 → A31 → … → A36 sequence, and A36 continues to A25 instead of looping back. The repetition drop is measured directly on all three activities while their participation stays at 100 in 100 traces, the loop-back edge A36→A30 collapses from about 70% to about 4% of A36's outgoing transitions, and the pairwise relation shifts from mostly interleaved to a fixed order. The eight qualified items far exceed the 0.45 expected by chance among the 90 tested units, though the occurrence, routing and relation items describe one underlying cycle rather than independent confirmations. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, DIR002, REL001, DIR001]

Limitations: the evidence cannot show whether the cycle was removed entirely or only capped at a single pass, since about 1–2 in 100 later-window traces still show repeats, and it cannot establish whether A25's own behaviour changed beyond receiving the flow that previously returned to A30. A competing explanation is a pure routing shift at A36 towards A25; it would fit the routing evidence alone but not the simultaneous occurrence drop across all three activities, so it is disfavoured but not fully excluded. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, DIR002]

Other differences: only a minor redistribution among rare outgoing edges of A30 and A36 (each affected successor below about 1 in 100 transitions), which does not alter the main picture. [DIR001, DIR002]
