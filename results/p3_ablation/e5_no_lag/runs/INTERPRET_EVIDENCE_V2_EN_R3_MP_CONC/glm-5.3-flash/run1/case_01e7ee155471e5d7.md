### Observed differences

- The relative order of two groups of activities reversed. In the earlier window, A21 and A22 came before A30, A31 and A36 in 200 of 200 traces for every one of the six pairings between the groups; in the later window, A30, A31 and A36 came before A21 and A22 in 199 of 200 traces for every pairing. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006]
- Participation did not change: all six activities still occur together in every trace in both windows, with no interleaving or repetition observed for any pair. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006]
- Event positions shifted consistently with the reversal: A22 moved later, from about 38% to about 47% of the trace length, and A21 from about 53% to about 62%, while A30, A31 and A36 each moved earlier, from about 59% to about 41%. [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005]
- Successors changed in line with this. A20, previously followed only by A22, now leads to A30, A31 or A36 in roughly one third of its outgoing transitions each, with A22 following in only about 1 in 200. [DIR001]
- A21, which previously led to A30, A31 or A36 (about 28–38 in 100 of its outgoing transitions each), now leads almost exclusively to A25 (about 199 in 200). [DIR002]
- A30, A31 and A36, which previously each led on to A25 in about 29–37 in 100 of their outgoing transitions, now lead to A22 in about 31–34 in 100 instead; the exit to A25 is no longer observed for A31 and A36 and is about 1 in 200 for A30. [DIR003, DIR004, DIR005]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the block of activities A30, A31 and A36, which previously ran after A21 and A22, is now executed before them, with the surrounding flow rewired accordingly: A20 now feeds directly into the block, the block exits to A22 rather than to A25, and A21 now exits to A25. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005]

The support is direct and consistent: 16 items qualified where only about 0.34 would be expected by chance among the 69 tested units, each item's difference exceeds about 995 in 1000 random re-splits of the pooled traces, the order reversal is shown directly by the relational items for all six pairings while co-occurrence stays at 100 in 100, and the position shifts and successor changes all point the same way. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005]

Limitations: the evidence shows that the relative order of the two groups flipped but cannot tell whether the block moved earlier or the pair moved later, positions are event positions rather than clock times, and the exact new wiring outside the observed pairs (for example what precedes A21, or where A25 sits) is inferred from successor shares rather than observed directly. A competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that the two windows sample different variants of a process containing both orders, rather than a modified process; the near-complete reversal in both 200-trace samples makes plain sampling noise unlikely but does not exclude a shift in the variant mix.
