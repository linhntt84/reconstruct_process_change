### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

Screening tested 104 control-flow units and qualified 24 items, against roughly 0.5 expected by chance in an unchanged process; a qualifying count this many times larger cannot be explained by random re-splits, and every item's difference also exceeds about 995 of 1,000 random re-splits. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, REL007, REL008, REL009, REL010, REL011, REL012]

The evidence supports the hypothesis that, in the later window, the alternative block of activities A19, A20 and A25 — three mutually exclusive, once-per-case activities that earlier sat between A27/A28 and A29 and was executed in roughly 3 to 4 of 10 cases — is bypassed: the block activities are almost never executed, and control runs directly from A27 through A28 to A29. The participation collapse is measured across all 200 traces per window (64, 56 and 80 traces down to 1 each) [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003]; the rerouting is shown directly by the outgoing-transition profiles of A27 and A28, which are well supported in both windows [DIR004, DIR005]; and all twelve relational items consistently show the block losing co-occurrence with its former neighbours while A27, A28 and A29 continue to appear in essentially every trace [REL003, REL005, REL008, REL010, REL012, POS004]. An important competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that the branch still exists but is simply no longer taken — for example because the later window is dominated by cases in which this path does not occur; the residual occurrence of each block activity in about 1 in 200 traces shows they have not strictly disappeared.
