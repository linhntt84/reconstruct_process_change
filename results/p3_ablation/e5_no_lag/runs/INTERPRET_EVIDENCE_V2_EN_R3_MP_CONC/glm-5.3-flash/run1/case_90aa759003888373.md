### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the consecutive block A14 → A15 → A20, which previously always executed between A36 and A21, became skippable: in the later window about 53 in 100 traces still run the full sequence A36 → A14 → A15 → A20 → A21, while in about 47 in 100 traces the whole block is skipped and A36 leads directly to A21. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, DIR001, REL003, REL004, REL006, REL007, REL008, REL009]

Supporting signs: the participation of all three block activities fell from 100% to about 53% with no change in repetition; a new direct A36 → A21 transition appeared alongside the original A36 → A14; and the relational items show A36 and A21 occurring without the block in about 47 in 100 traces, while the three block activities always appear or disappear together and keep their internal order when present. Because the block is wholly absent in the skipping traces, this is a bypass of a group of activities rather than a shift of frequency between existing alternative paths. Screening also found 14 qualifying control-flow items where roughly 0.35 would be expected by chance without a change, and every item's difference exceeds about 995 in 1000 random re-splits of the pooled data. [DIR001, REL001, REL002, REL005, OCC001, OCC002]

Limitations: all 14 items describe one trace-level difference, so they are not independent confirmations, and the evidence cannot distinguish a single runtime skip decision covering the whole block from two coexisting process variants (one with the block, one without); it also shows nothing about what precedes A36 or follows A21. [OCC001, DIR001, REL001]
