### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, six activities each ran in about half of the cases and formed two mutually exclusive groups: A33, A35 and A36 appeared in about 97 of 200 cases, A37, A38 and A39 in about 103 of 200, and no case contained activities from both groups. In the later window, all six run in essentially every case (197–199 of 200), about once per case. [OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, OCC007, OCC008, OCC009]

- The two groups changed from alternatives to jointly executed work: cross-group pairs such as A33–A37 or A36–A39 had zero cooccurrence in 200 cases before, and occur together in about 98 of 100 cases afterwards, with the first group (A33/A35/A36) before the second (A37/A38/A39) in about 97 of 100 cases; typical positions shifted accordingly (first group somewhat earlier, second group slightly later). [REL039, REL041, REL043, REL046, REL048, POS006, POS009]

- Order within each group stays unfixed in both windows — for example, A33 and A35 run in each order in roughly half of the jointly occurring cases before and after, and A37/A38/A39 likewise split about half/half — so the new fixed constraint lies between the groups, not within them. [REL037, REL042, REL049, REL050, REL051]

- The block is now bracketed by A30 and A31: A30, present in every case in both windows, preceded the first group in about 48–50 of 100 cases before and about 98 in 100 after; A31 followed the first group in about 48 of 100 before and about 98 in 100 after, but the direct hand-over to A31 largely disappeared — A31's share of A33's outgoing transitions fell from about 36% to under 1% (similarly from A35 and A36) — while new transitions from A33/A35/A36 into A37, A38 and A39 (about 10–12% each) appeared, and A30 now leads almost only into the first group instead of into either group. [REL025, REL031, REL033, DIR006, DIR007, DIR008, DIR009]

- A14, A15 and A16, present in every case in both windows, now co-occur with the block in about 98–99 of 100 cases (about 49–52 before); A14 precedes it in about 98 of 100, while A15 and A16 precede it in about 59–60 of 100 and interleave with it in about 40 of 100, up from about 15–18. [REL007, REL013, REL019]

- Outside this block, A04 became rarer — about 61 of 200 cases before, 27 after — and its outgoing transitions spread out: A16 took about 81% of them before and about 36% after, with A15 (about 18%) and A48 (about 14%) appearing; A06 and A10, rare before (about 6 and 5 of 200 cases), now appear in about 22 and 24 of 200. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, DIR002]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that, in the later window, the two formerly exclusive blocks are executed together in virtually every case as a fixed two-stage sequence — one pass of A33/A35/A36 in any internal order, then one pass of A37/A38/A39 — whereas the earlier window executed exactly one of the two groups per case (about half of cases each) and never both. A30 now always opens this sequence, and A31, which previously received the flow directly from the first group, now sits behind it, with direct transitions from A33/A35/A36 to A31 replaced by transitions into A37–A39. The pattern is direct and consistent across participation, cooccurrence/order and successor evidence, and its scale (81 qualifying items against fewer than 1 expected if nothing had changed) cannot be explained by random re-splitting of the windows. Limitations: the exact position of A31 relative to the second group is not directly observed, and window-level aggregates cannot exclude that a small share of later cases still follows the old either-or behaviour. [OCC004, OCC007, REL039, REL046, REL025, REL031, DIR006, DIR007]

Other differences: A04 participation fell from about 30 in 100 cases to about 13 in 100, with its follow-up work rerouted from mostly A16 towards a mix including A15 and A48; A06 and A10 roughly quadrupled in frequency but remain rare (about 11–12 in 100 cases). These sit outside the main hypothesis. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, DIR002]
