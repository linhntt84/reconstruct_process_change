### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that in the later window the two three-activity groups reversed their relative order: the segment that ran A20/A22/A23 → A25 → A29 → A19/A21/A24 now runs A19/A21/A24 → A25 → A29 → A20/A22/A23, with A25 and A29 keeping their relative order (medians ~0.45/0.65 before, ~0.39/0.58 after). The reordering is shown directly by the relational items, holds consistently across all 21 observed cross-group pairs with near-complete flips, and is corroborated by the opposite position shifts and by the successor changes, including each triple taking over the other's continuation. All activities involved occur in a substantial share of traces in both windows (A20, A22, A23, A25 and A29 in all traces; A19, A21 and A24 in about 28–44 of 100), so this is a change in the order of existing paths rather than a replacement of one path by another. With 61 qualifying items against roughly 0.7 expected without a change, and every item's difference exceeding about 995 of 1,000 random re-splits, the ensemble is far beyond what a stable process typically produces. [REL015, REL022, REL029, REL032, DIR005, DIR006, DIR010, POS002, POS003, POS008]

A competing explanation the evidence cannot fully rule out is that the reordering is entangled with changes in how often the activities occur at all: participation rose for A19 (about 32 → 44 of 100 traces) and fell for A21 and A24 (to about 29 and 28), so the reversal is not perfectly clean, and the evidence does not reconstruct the complete control flow beyond the observed pairs.
