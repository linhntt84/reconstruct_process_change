- Participation is nearly unchanged between the windows: A20, A22, A23, A25 and A29 occur in essentially every case in both windows, while A19, A21 and A24 each occur in roughly 30–40 in 100 cases (A19 about 30 before vs 33 after; A21 about 35 vs 30; A24 about 37 vs 40). - All 21 measured cross-group activity pairs reversed their order; for example, A19 preceded A20 in 30 in 100 cases before but followed it in 33 in 100 after, A25 preceded A20 in about 995 of 1000 co-occurring cases before and followed it after, and A24 moved from preceding A25 (37 in 100) to following it (38 in 100). - A25 and A29 kept their mutual order but shifted later (mean position about 0.38 to 0.45, and 0.57 to 0.64), consistent with a larger group now running ahead of them. [POS007, POS008]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that, in the later window, two segments of the case execute in the opposite order: the segment in which one of A19, A21 or A24 runs moved from before the fixed A25 → A29 sequence to after it, and the segment containing A20, A22 and A23 — all three of which run in every case, in varying order — moved from after A29 to before A25. The surrounding flow (A40 into the first segment, A25 → A29 between them, A30 after the second) and the participation shares of all involved activities are essentially unchanged. [DIR001-DIR008, POS001-POS008, REL001-REL021]

The hypothesis is shown directly and consistently: every one of the 21 cross-group pairs reversed its order with large shares (e.g., A25 before A20 in about 995 of 1000 co-occurring cases before, A20 before A25 in about 995 of 1000 after) [REL001-REL021]; the two groups' mean positions exchanged (about 0.34–0.36 vs 0.63–0.64 before, about 0.38 vs 0.66–0.68 after) [POS001-POS006]; and the successor changes agree, with A19/A21/A24 now leading to A30 instead of A25 and A20/A22/A23 now ending at A25 instead of A30 [DIR001-DIR006]. Stable participation counts against the competing reading that existing paths are merely taken with different frequencies or that activities were added or removed [POS001-POS008]. Chance is also implausible as a whole: 37 items qualified against roughly 0.6 expected for an unchanged process, and every item's difference exceeds about 995 of 1000 random re-splits. [screening]

Limitations: the two windows are snapshots, so a single wholesale exchange of the two segments cannot be distinguished from a piecewise re-wiring of the same activities that produces identical pairwise orders; and because positions are event positions rather than clock time, no claim about timing is implied. [REL001-REL021, POS001-POS008]

Other differences: A24's infrequent transition to A14 (about 3 in 100 of its outgoing transitions in the earlier window) is not observed in the later window, where A24 instead shows small shares (about 1 in 100 each) to A36, A40, A32 and A04; A20, A22 and A23 likewise had a handful of rare outgoing transitions (each about 0.5–2 in 100) in the earlier window that are not observed later. These concern rare edges only and do not affect the main hypothesis. [DIR002, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006, DIR008]
