### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, every one of the 200 traces contained A25, A28, A30, A31, A32, A33, A34 and A26 exactly once (participation 1.0 for all). In the later window, A25 and A26 still appear in every trace, but A28, A30 and A31 each appear in only about 47 in 100 traces, and A32, A33 and A34 in about 56 in 100; no activity repeats within a trace in either window. Earlier, a first-group activity (A28, A30 or A31) co-occurred with a second-group activity (A32, A33 or A34) in 100 in 100 traces, always with the first-group activity executed first. In the later window, such cross-group pairs co-occur in only about 2 in 100 traces, while the first-group activity appears without the second-group activity in about 45 in 100 traces and the second-group activity appears alone in about 54 in 100. ### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the two three-activity blocks — A28 with A30 and A31, and A32 with A33 and A34 — changed from being executed together in every trace, in fixed sequence between A25 and A26, to being mutually exclusive alternatives: in the later window a trace typically executes A25, then exactly one of the two blocks in mixed internal order, then A26, taking the first block in about 47 in 100 traces and the second in about 55 in 100, with both blocks in only about 2 in 100. In effect, each block became optional, so that in a typical later trace one of the two groups is skipped entirely. The support is direct and consistent: participation of all six activities fell from every trace to roughly half, cross-block co-occurrence collapsed from 100 to about 2 in 100 while within-group co-occurrence remained complete, and the successor changes show the new routing directly (A25 now also feeds the second group; A28, A30 and A31 now lead to A26 instead of to A32–A34). Chance is an unlikely explanation at the screening level: 45 control-flow items qualified where only about 0.5 in 100 would be expected without a change across the 94 tested units. Limitations: the evidence cannot show what determines which block a trace takes, the exclusivity is not absolute (about 2 in 100 later traces still contain both blocks), and the position shifts are event-based corroboration rather than proof that activities were relocated.
