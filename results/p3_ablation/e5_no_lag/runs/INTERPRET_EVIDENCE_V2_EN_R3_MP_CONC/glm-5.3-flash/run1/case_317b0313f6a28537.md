### Observed differences

- The activities A19, A20 and A25 — each present in roughly a third of cases in both windows (A19 about 39 in 100 traces earlier vs 36 later; A20 about 32 vs 35; A25 about 31 vs 32) — now run much earlier in the case: their mean relative position fell from about 0.60 of the trace in the earlier window to about 0.32–0.34 in the later window. [POS001, POS002, POS003]
- Their order relative to A26 and A27 reversed. Earlier, in the 31–39 in 100 traces containing both, A26 or A27 came before A19/A20/A25 in nearly every instance (e.g. A26 before A19 in 38 of 100 traces, the reverse in none); in the later window A19/A20/A25 come first in nearly every such trace (A19 before A26 in 36 of 100, the reverse in none), and the same flip holds for A20 and A25 against both A26 and A27. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006]
- Routing around this group changed accordingly. Earlier, A19, A20 and A25 led almost only to A28 and A32 (about 95–98% of their observed outgoing transitions), A27 sent about half of its outgoing transitions to A19/A20/A25 and A28 about 47%, while A45 led to A26 in about 85 in 100 transitions. In the later window, A19/A20/A25 lead to A26 in about 88–94 in 100 transitions, A27 leads almost only to A28 (98%) and A28 almost only to A32 (96%), while A45 now leads to A19/A20/A25 (about 85 in 100 combined). [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006]
- A26, A27 and A28 stayed in place: all occur in every trace in both windows, with mean positions shifting only slightly later (A26 about 0.33 vs 0.36, A27 about 0.56 vs 0.59, A28 about 0.61 vs 0.62). [POS004, POS005, POS006]
- A15 still occurs in every trace but repeats less often: mean occurrences per trace fell from about 1.9 to about 1.6, and recurrence within a trace from about 47 to 38 in 100 traces. [OCC001]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the group A19, A20 and A25 was placed earlier in the process. In the earlier window, in the roughly one-third of cases containing them, these activities ran late (mean position about 0.6), were entered from A27 or A28, led back to A28/A32, and came after A26; in the later window they run just before A26 (mean position about 0.32–0.34), are entered from A45 and lead directly to A26, while A27 and A28 no longer connect to them and pass on via A28 to A32. [POS001, POS002, POS003, REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006]

The change is shown directly and consistently: six activity-pair relations flip from "A26/A27 first" to "group first", three position distributions drop by about 0.28 of the trace, and six direction profiles show the matching rerouting; the 19 qualifying items also far exceed the roughly 0.54 expected by chance among the 109 tested units, so the set as a whole is not a chance artefact. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, POS001, POS002, POS003, DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006]

Limitations: positions are event positions rather than clock times, direction shares are over observed outgoing transitions rather than cases, and the evidence cannot fully distinguish a relocation of the group within one flow from a replacement of a "group late" trace variant by a "group early" one; the internal order among A19, A20 and A25 is not covered. [POS001, POS002, POS003, DIR001, DIR002, DIR003]

Other differences: A15 became less repetitive — it still occurs in every trace, but its mean occurrences per trace fell from about 1.9 to about 1.6 and within-trace recurrence from about 47 to 38 in 100 traces; this repetition change stands apart from the relocation hypothesis. [OCC001]
