### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, six activities each ran in only about half of the traces: A28, A30 and A31 in about 49 in 100 traces, and A32, A33 and A34 in about 52 in 100. In the later window, all six run in about 99–100 in 100 traces, still at most once per trace in both windows. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006]
- In the earlier window the two groups were mutually exclusive: cross-group pairs such as A28–A32 or A30–A34 never shared a trace (co-occurrence 0 in 100), so every trace contained exactly one of the two groups. In the later window, such pairs co-occur in about 98 in 100 traces, always with the A28/A30/A31 member before the A32/A33/A34 member. [REL015, REL017, REL019, REL022, REL024]
- Within each group the order was and remains unfixed: A28 preceded A30 in about 24 in 100 traces before and about 49 in 100 after, with the reverse order about equally frequent in both windows; the other within-group pairs show the same mixed pattern. [REL013, REL014, REL018, REL025, REL026, REL027]
- Successor profiles moved in step: A25 previously split its outgoing transitions roughly equally between the two groups (about 49 vs 52 in 100) and now leads almost exclusively to A28/A30/A31 (about 99 in 100); A28, A30 and A31 previously led to A26 in about 30–36 in 100 of their outgoing transitions, now almost never (under 1 in 100), instead gaining transitions to A32, A33 and A34 (about 10–13 in 100 each). [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004]
- Relative event positions shifted consistently with both groups now running in one trace: A25 moved earlier (mean 0.67 to 0.62) and A26 later (0.80 to 0.83); A28/A30/A31 moved earlier (about 0.74 to 0.68) and A32/A33/A34 later (about 0.73 to 0.77). [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006, POS007, POS008]
- For scale: 45 control-flow items qualified in total, against roughly 0.47 expected by chance alone in an unchanged process. [screening]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the two three-activity blocks between A25 and A26 — A28/A30/A31 and A32/A33/A34 — changed from mutually exclusive alternatives, each executing in about half of the cases with mixed internal order, to both executing in virtually every case, first the A28/A30/A31 block and then the A32/A33/A34 block, each still in mixed internal order; the six activities thus appear to have changed from conditionally executed to mandatory, with the exclusive choice between the blocks replaced by a fixed sequence of the two. [OCC001, OCC004, REL015, REL019, REL022, DIR002]

Supporting signs: participation of all six activities rose from about half of traces to nearly all; cross-group co-occurrence rose from never to about 98 in 100 with a fixed inter-block order; the routing redistribution at A25, A28, A30 and A31 matches this structure; and the qualified-item count (45 versus about 0.47 expected) is far beyond what random re-splitting produces. Because both groups now appear together within single traces, a mere shift in the mix of two unchanged variants is ruled out. [OCC001, REL015, DIR001, screening]

Limitations: the evidence cannot show whether the inter-block order is a hard constraint or only dominant, since about 1–2 in 100 later-window traces still contain only one of the groups, and it cannot indicate what governed the earlier exclusive choice; positions are event indices, so no statement about clock timing is implied. [OCC001, OCC004, REL015, POS003]

Other differences: none beyond the above; A25 and A26 themselves occur in every trace in both windows and only shift slightly in relative position as a consequence of the change. [POS001, POS002, REL001, REL004]
