### Observed differences

- The branch choice at A15 flipped between the windows. Earlier, about 82 in 100 of A15's outgoing transitions went to A19 (about 29%), A21 (about 29%) or A20 (about 24%), and only about 18 in 100 to A16, A17 or A18 (about 7%, 9% and 3%). ### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the two existing branches after A15 swapped in how often they are taken: in the later window, traces leaving A15 usually continue into the block A16–A18, which executes as a group in varying order together with A25 (about 79 in 100 traces, up from about 19), while the alternative block A19–A21 with A25 dropped from about 82 in 100 traces to about 21. Both blocks, all six transitions out of A15 and A25 are observed in substantial shares in both windows, and only the shares between the alternatives moved, so this reads as a change in the frequencies of existing paths rather than a replacement, addition or removal of a path. The change is shown directly by the redistribution of A15's outgoing transitions and is consistent across the participation and co-occurrence indicators; 34 control-flow items qualified against roughly 0.39 expected if nothing had changed, so chance sampling cannot explain the pattern as a whole. [DIR001, OCC001, OCC004, REL001, REL004, REL007, REL012, REL022, REL024]

An important competing explanation remains: the evidence cannot show what selects between the two branches at A15, so a shifted mix of the two pre-existing case types entering A15 (upstream of the observed activities) cannot be ruled out as the driver of the swapped shares.

Other differences: none beyond this routing shift; in particular, no activity among A15–A21 repeats within a trace in either window. [OCC001, OCC004]
