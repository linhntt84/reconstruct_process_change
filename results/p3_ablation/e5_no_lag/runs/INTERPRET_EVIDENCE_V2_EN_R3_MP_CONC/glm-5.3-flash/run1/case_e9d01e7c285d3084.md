### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that in the later window A30, A31 and A36 — previously executed exactly once per trace in a fixed sequence (A14 before A30 before A36, with A36 typically followed by A25) — became repeated: each now runs about 3.5 times per trace, with repetitions in about two-thirds of traces, and A36 leads back to A30 in about 70 in 100 of its executions while the transition to A25 remains as the exit in about 28 in 100. Supporting signs: the direct occurrence and recurrence counts for all three activities [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003]; the new dominant A36-to-A30 transition [DIR001]; and the breakdown of the previously near-absolute orders A14→A30, A14→A36 and A30→A36 into interleaved-or-repeated patterns in about 68 in 100 traces, which is what repetitions after a single A14 would produce [REL003, REL004, REL005]. All twelve qualifying items are control-flow items, against roughly 0.48 expected by chance alone, and each difference is larger than in about 995 of 1000 random re-splits of the pooled data, so the volume and size of the differences far exceed what a stable process typically produces.

Limitations and competing explanations: the interleaved-or-repeated category does not by itself separate interleaving from repetition — the repetition claim rests on the occurrence counts, and a mode where the three activities are re-executed in varying orders rather than as one repeating block cannot be ruled out. A31's repetition is supported only by its occurrence counts, with no relational or successor evidence, so its placement relative to the A30–A36 repetition is unknown; nor can the evidence show whether A14 itself repeats, as its occurrence count is not reported. [OCC002, DIR001, REL003, POS001]

Other differences: A14's mean event position moved slightly earlier (about 0.136 to about 0.115) with increased spread, and A05's fixed ordering before A30 and A36 weakened in the roughly half of traces where they co-occur; both are consistent with the repetition above rather than independent changes.
