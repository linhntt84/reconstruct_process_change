### Observed differences

- Repetition increased sharply for three activities: in the earlier window, A30, A31 and A36 each occurred almost exactly once per case (mean about 1.0, with repeats in at most about 1 in 100 cases), while in the later window each occurs about 3.5 times per case on average and about 68–69 in 100 cases contain at least one repeat; every case still contains all three activities in both windows. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003]

- The routing out of A36 changed accordingly: earlier, about 98 in 100 of A36's observed outgoing transitions went to A25 and well under 1 in 100 went back to A30; later, about 71 in 100 go back to A30 and about 28 in 100 to A25, while A30→A31 and A31→A36 remain the dominant transitions in both windows (about 97–99 in 100). These shares are over outgoing transitions of the source activity, not over cases. [DIR003, DIR004, DIR005]

- The fixed order among A14, A30 and A36 loosened: all three pairs co-occur in 100 in 100 cases in both windows, but the plain A-before-B order (about 98–99 in 100 cases earlier) drops to about 32 in 100 later, with interleaved or repeated orderings rising to about 68 in 100; between A05 and A30/A36 the same shift appears in weaker form (A-before-B about 54–55 in 100 earlier, about 17 in 100 later). [REL003, REL004, REL005, REL001, REL002]

- A30 and A36 now occur across a much wider band of event positions (the standard deviation of relative position roughly doubles, from about 0.06 to about 0.12) while their mean positions shift only slightly later — consistent with repeats spread through the case rather than a relocation. [POS003, POS004]

- Separately, A05's outgoing transitions redistributed (A07 up from about 19 to about 39 in 100, A14 down from about 35 to about 18 in 100, with A09 still largest at about 39–42 in 100); A05 also occupies slightly earlier positions (median relative position about 0.07 to 0.05) and its participation slipped from about 55 to about 52 in 100 cases, while A14 occurs slightly earlier and more variably. [DIR001, POS001, POS002]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that, in the later window, the cycle A30 → A31 → A36 is repeated within cases — about 3.5 iterations per case instead of one — because A36 now returns to A30 in about 71 in 100 of its outgoing transitions instead of proceeding to A25. The supporting signs are the matching occurrence profiles of the three activities (means about 1.0 to about 3.5, repeat share from near 0 to about 68–69 in 100 cases), the loop-back edge A36→A30 becoming the majority successor while A30→A31 and A31→A36 stay dominant, and the shift from a fixed to an interleaved/repeated ordering in about two-thirds of cases with co-occurrence unchanged; the screening context fits, with 17 qualifying control-flow items against fewer than 1 expected without a change. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, REL003, REL004, REL005]

This is a possibility supported by the evidence, not a confirmed modification: the loop-back edge already existed rarely in the earlier window, so the change lies in how often the existing cycle repeats rather than in new activities, and the evidence cannot fully rule out that the repeats have a finer structure than whole-cycle repetition, since the interleaved/repeated category aggregates all repeat patterns; with two 200-case windows, the iteration count is also only a sample estimate. [DIR005]

Other differences: A05's routing shift toward A07 and away from A14, its slightly earlier position and slightly lower participation, and A14's slightly earlier and more variable position are not explained by the repetition of the cycle and may be independent changes. [DIR001, POS001, POS002]
