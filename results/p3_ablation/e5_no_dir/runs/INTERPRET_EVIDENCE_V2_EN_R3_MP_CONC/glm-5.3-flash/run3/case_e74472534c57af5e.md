### Observed differences

- The three activities A30, A31 and A36 changed from single executions to repeated ones. In the earlier window each occurred about once per trace, with repeats in at most about 1 in 100 traces; in the later window each occurs about 3.5 times per trace, with repeats in about 69 in 100 traces. All three still appear in every trace in both windows, so they did not become optional — their repetition changed. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003]
- The previously strict sequence around A14 dissolved into interleaving. A14 still co-occurs with A30 and with A36 in every trace in both windows, but "A14 before A30/A36" falls from about 99 in 100 traces to about 32 in 100, while interleaved-or-repeated execution rises from about 1 in 100 to about 68–69 in 100; likewise A30 preceding A36 falls from about 98–99 in 100 to about 32 in 100. [REL003, REL004, REL005]
- The tight succession A30 → A36 largely disappeared: before, it occurred within five steps in essentially every trace, almost always at a gap of two events (about 97 in 100); in the later window the gap-two share is about 12 in 100 and a gap-five share of about 5 in 100 appears. Conversely, the reverse pair A36 → A30 within five steps, seen in only 1 of 200 earlier traces, appears in about 136 of 200 later traces. [LAG001, LAG002]
- Relative positions spread rather than shifted: A30 and A36 keep similar median positions (about 0.40–0.43 and about 0.48–0.49) but their spread roughly doubles (standard deviation about 0.06 to about 0.12); A14's median position moves slightly earlier with its spread rising from about 0.02 to about 0.05. [POS002, POS003, POS004]
- A05, present in about 55 in 100 traces before and about 51 in 100 after, no longer strictly precedes A30/A36 when they co-occur: A05-first falls from about 54–55 in 100 to about 17 in 100, with interleaving rising from none to about 34 in 100, and A05's relative position moves slightly earlier (mean about 0.074 to about 0.052). [REL001, REL002, POS001]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the block A14 → A30 → A36, which in the earlier window ran once per trace in a fixed order, is in the later window repeated about three to four times per trace in roughly 69 in 100 traces: A30, A31 and A36 each went from about one occurrence to about 3.5 occurrences per trace, and the repetition dissolved the strict ordering — A14 precedes A30/A36 in only about 32 in 100 later traces, and the tight two-step succession A30 → A36 is largely gone. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, REL003, REL004, REL005, LAG001]

Supporting signs: the repetition increase is measured directly on all three activities with full participation (200 of 200 traces) in both windows; the ordering change is shown directly by the relation profiles while co-occurrence stays at 100 in 100, indicating a change in ordering constraints and repetition rather than activities being skipped; and 14 qualifying items against roughly 0.27 expected by chance is far beyond what an unchanged process typically produces, with every item's difference exceeding about 995 of 1,000 random re-splits. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, REL003, REL004, REL005, LAG001]

Limitations: the occurrence, relation and lag items describe one underlying trace behaviour, so they are not independent confirmations, and the earlier profile of the reverse pair A36 → A30 rests on a single observation. A competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that only a large subset of traces (about 69 in 100) switched to the repeating pattern while the rest kept the single-pass sequence — about 31 in 100 later traces still show the strict order — rather than every trace changing uniformly. [REL003, LAG002]

Other differences: A05's strict precedence over A30/A36 loosened in the same direction (A05-first from about 54–55 in 100 to about 17 in 100, interleaving up to about 34 in 100) and its relative position moved slightly earlier, though A05 itself does not repeat and appears in only about half of the traces. [REL001, REL002, POS001]
