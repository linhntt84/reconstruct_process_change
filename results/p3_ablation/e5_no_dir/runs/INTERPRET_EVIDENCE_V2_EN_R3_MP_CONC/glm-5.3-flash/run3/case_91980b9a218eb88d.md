The evidence supports the possibility that the block of activities A19, A20 and A25 was relocated. The hypothesis is shown directly and consistently: the position shift of all three activities, the flipped ordering with A26 and A27, and the matching appearance and disappearance of close successions all describe the same movement. The screening context also fits: 25 control-flow items qualified against fewer than one (0.36) expected by chance for the 73 tested units, so the ensemble is not what random re-splits of an unchanged process typically produce. Note, however, that the many items are not independent confirmations — they largely re-describe one underlying relocation [POS001, POS002, POS003, REL001, REL003, REL006, LAG001, LAG004, LAG010, LAG011].

Limitations: positions are event indices, not clock times, so "later" means later in the sequence of events. The insertion point relative to A28 varies (mixed orders), and A25's new neighbours are bounded only by order relations, not by observed close successions. Some lag profiles rest on only 1–2 observations in one window, so their distance details are weak even though the appearance or disappearance of the pair is informative. A competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that the three activities were attached to a different, later branch rather than moved within one path, and it cannot establish whether they moved as one block or were re-placed individually.

Other differences: A24 now participates in about 58 in 100 traces instead of about 43, and A26 and A27 each occur slightly earlier in the sequence; the evidence does not link these to the relocation [OCC001, POS004, POS005].
