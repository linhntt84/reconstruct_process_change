### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the ordering constraints among A20, A22, A23, A24, A25 and A26 were tightened: in the earlier window these always-present activities appeared in case-dependent, partly mixed orders with varying step gaps, while in the later window they follow one fixed sequence (A22 → A20 → A23 → A24 → A26 → A25, with A31 immediately before A22 and A32 after A25), each observed pair taking that order in about 96–99+ in 100 traces and most pairwise distances fixed to a single step count. This is shown directly by the relation items, where co-occurrence stays at 100 in 100 traces while order shares flip from mixed to one-sided, and corroborated by the collapse of distance distributions and the consistent position shifts [REL014, REL016, REL019, LAG006, LAG007, LAG012, LAG014, LAG021, LAG031, LAG032, POS002, POS005]. Chance is unlikely to explain the pattern as a whole: 67 control-flow items qualified against fewer than 1 expected for the 153 tested units in an unchanged process [screening]. Limitations: positions are event positions rather than clock time, the exact chain is reconstructed from pairwise distances because no direct successor evidence qualified, and a few distance items rest on only 1–13 observations and were not relied upon [LAG002, LAG019, LAG024, LAG025, LAG027, LAG035, LAG037]. A competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that activities were inserted or individually repositioned between these steps rather than the ordering constraint alone being tightened; the data show a fixed order replacing a mixed one, not the mechanism that produced the new gaps.

Other differences: A08 became a rare, late activity — executed in about 72 in 100 traces before versus about 29 in 100 after, and when executed appearing later in the trace (mean relative position about 0.24 vs 0.41); the many relation items naming A08 mirror this single participation drop rather than confirming it independently [OCC001, POS001, REL001, REL004]. A10 participation rose from about 8 to about 22 in 100 traces, though the earlier share is based on only 15 traces [OCC002].
