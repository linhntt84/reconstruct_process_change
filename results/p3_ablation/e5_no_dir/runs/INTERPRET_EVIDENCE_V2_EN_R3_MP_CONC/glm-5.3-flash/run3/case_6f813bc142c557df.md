### Observed differences

- The order among a block of activities became fixed. In the earlier window several pairs appeared in both directions: A24 preceded A20 in about 75 in 100 traces (now A20 precedes A24 in about 99 in 100), A24 preceded A23 in about 88 in 100 (now A23 precedes A24 in about 98 in 100), and A20/A26, A22/A24 and A23/A25 were in each order roughly 50 in 100 (now each pair follows one order in about 98–99 in 100). [REL002, REL004, REL005, REL007, REL008, REL009]
- Spacing became deterministic. Before, distances between these activities spread over one to five event positions (for example A23 followed A20 one step later in about 62 in 100 traces and 2–4 steps later in the rest; A32 followed A20 spread over lags 2–5). In the later window each pair sits at one fixed distance in essentially every trace, forming one contiguous run A31 → A22 → A20 → A23 → A24 → A26 → A25 → A32 with consecutive activities one step apart; the reverse pairs (e.g. A24 then A20) now occur within five steps in only about 1 in 200 traces. [LAG003, LAG004, LAG005, LAG007, LAG008, LAG016, LAG019, LAG024, LAG026, LAG027, LAG029]
- Event positions shifted consistently with this: A20, A22 and A23 now occur earlier in the trace (mean relative position about 0.64→0.59, 0.59→0.56, 0.68→0.62), while A24, A25 and A26 occur later (about 0.59→0.65, 0.68→0.70, 0.64→0.68), with all three pairs observed in every trace in both windows. [POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006, POS007]
- A08 occurs in far fewer traces: about 32 in 100 before versus about 13 in 100 after; when it occurs it sits later and more variably (mean relative position about 0.18→0.34), and it co-occurs with A32 in fewer traces (about 32→13 in 100), with A08-before-A32 dropping from about 31 to 11 in 100. [OCC001, POS001, REL001, LAG001]
- A10 appears in more traces (about 3→12 in 100), but this rests on only 6 versus 25 traces. [OCC002]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

One possible change supported by the evidence is a tightening of ordering constraints: the activities A20, A22, A23, A24, A25 and A26 — anchored by A31 upstream and A32 downstream — changed from a flexible, case-dependent order with variable spacing to a single fixed sequence, A31 → A22 → A20 → A23 → A24 → A26 → A25 → A32, executed back-to-back in nearly every trace of the later window. This is shown directly by the relational items (order flips and fixations in about 98–99 in 100 traces per pair) and corroborated by the fixed one-step lags and the consistent position shifts of all six activities, which all move in the directions the new sequence implies. The volume of qualifying items (49 versus roughly 0.5 expected by chance in the control-flow family) makes sampling noise implausible as an explanation of the pattern as a whole. Limitations: lags and positions are event positions, not clock time; the REL, LAG and POS items largely describe the same traces, so they are not independent confirmations; and the evidence cannot distinguish whether a new fixed sequencing was introduced or whether the earlier window mixed several orderings that later collapsed into one dominant variant. [REL002, REL004, REL005, REL007, REL009, LAG003, LAG004, LAG019, LAG024, LAG027, POS002, POS005]

Other differences: A08 now occurs in far fewer traces, later and more variably when it occurs, and its tie to A32 has weakened; A10 occurs somewhat more often. Both fall outside the ordering hypothesis, and the A10 change rests on few observations. [OCC001, POS001, REL001, LAG001, OCC002]
