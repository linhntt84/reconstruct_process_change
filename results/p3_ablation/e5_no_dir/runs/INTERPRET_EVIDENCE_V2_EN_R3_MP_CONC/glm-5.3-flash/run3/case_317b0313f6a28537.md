### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

In the later window, the block of alternative activities A19, A20 and A25 is executed earlier in the case — before A26 and A27, in the region between A45 and A26 — whereas in the earlier window it ran after A26 and A27, around A28. This is a change in the placement and ordering of activities that already participated at the same rates, not a change in participation. The order flip is shown directly by six relational items that all move the same way while co-occurrence stays stable; the positional items agree (a drop of about 0.28 in relative position for A19/A20/A25 against shifts of only about 0.02–0.03 for A26/A27/A28, which argues against A26/A27 being the ones that moved); and the gap items corroborate the new placement (A45→A26 from one to two steps, A27→A32 from three to two, and the former neighbours A28/A32 now out of five-step reach of A19/A25). With 20 qualifying control-flow items against roughly 0.35 expected if nothing had changed, the set of items as a whole is far beyond what chance produces, and every item's difference exceeds about 199 of 200 random re-splits of the pooled data. Limitations: the after-profiles for the pairs involving A32 rest on only 1–3 observations, positions are measured in event order rather than clock time, and the exact insertion point between A45 and A26 is inferred from gap counts rather than observed directly, so the evidence cannot fully rule out that other unobserved shifts contribute to the new arrangement.
