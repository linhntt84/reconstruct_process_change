### Observed differences

- The three activities A30, A31 and A36 were repeated work before and single-step work after: in the earlier window each occurred about 3.4–3.5 times per trace and repeated in about 75 in 100 traces, while in the later window each occurs about once per trace (about 1.05) and repeats in only 1–2 in 100 traces. All three still appear in every trace in both windows (participation 100 in 100 throughout). [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003]
- The relationship between A30 and A36 changed accordingly: before, they were interleaved or repeated together in 75 in 100 traces (fixed A30-before-A36 order in only 25); in the later window the order is fixed, with A30 before A36 in 98 in 100 traces and interleaving in only 2. They still co-occur in every trace in both windows. [REL001]
- The distance between the two also tightened: in the later window A36 follows A30 two positions later in about 89 in 100 traces, whereas before, a gap of 2 occurred in only about 12 in 100 and the distances were spread out (e.g. gap 5 in about 5). [LAG002]
- The mean relative position of A30 (about 0.44 → 0.42) and A36 (about 0.49 → 0.49) barely moved, but the spread around it halved (standard deviation about 0.12 → 0.07 and 0.12 → 0.06), consistent with single occurrences at stable spots. [POS001, POS002]
- Self-distance profiles of A30 and A36 shifted in the same direction, but the after-window profiles rest on only 1–2 observations and are weak on their own. [LAG001, LAG003, LAG004]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that A30, A31 and A36 stopped repeating: in the earlier window each of them typically recurred within a trace (about 3.4–3.5 occurrences, repeats in 75 in 100 traces), while in the later window each executes essentially once per trace (about 1.05 occurrences, repeats in 1–2 in 100), with participation unchanged at 100 in 100 traces — so the activities did not become optional or disappear. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003] Together with this, A30 and A36 moved from mostly interleaved, repeated execution to a fixed sequence in which A36 follows A30 two positions later in about 89 in 100 traces. [REL001, LAG002]

Supporting signs: the screening produced 10 control-flow items where roughly 0.24 would be expected if nothing had changed (about 40 times as many), so the pattern as a whole is unlikely to be a sampling artefact; the key items have full support (200 traces per window) and agree with one another; and the unchanged mean positions argue that this is a repetition change rather than a relocation of the activities. Each item's difference also exceeds about 995 of 1000 random re-splits of the pooled data. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, REL001, LAG002, POS001, POS002]

Limitations: no relational or distance item covers A31, so the evidence cannot show whether A31 belonged to the same repeated block as A30 and A36 or repeated independently of them; and the after-change self-distance profiles rest on 1–2 observations. [OCC002, LAG001, LAG003, LAG004] A competing explanation the evidence cannot fully rule out is a restructuring of the repeated fragment that preserves each activity's position and single occurrence, since no direct successor or routing evidence is present; the collapse of repetition itself, however, is directly observed. [OCC001, REL001, POS001]

Other differences: none that stand apart from the repetition change; the self-distance items echo it but are only weakly supported in the later window. [LAG001, LAG003, LAG004]
