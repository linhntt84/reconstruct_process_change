### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a specific hypothesis about the segment between A25 and A31, involving A25, A26, A27, A28, A31 and the block A36/A37/A42. In the earlier window every trace ran A25, then the block A36–A37–A42, then A28, A26, A27 and A31. In the later window, A25, A28, A26 and A31 still run unconditionally, but the block runs in only about 47 in 100 traces and sits after A26 instead of before A28 (mean position about 0.40 → 0.57 of the trace), while A27 is skipped in about 46 in 100 traces; block and A27 are now mutually exclusive, so a trace executes either the block or A27 between A26 and A31. The placement change is shown directly by order reversals, by the collapse of A27's co-occurrence with each block member from 100 to about 1 in 100 traces, and by A25→A28 becoming adjacent, with participation and position indicators agreeing; 43 qualifying items against roughly 0.5 expected without change cannot be explained by chance sampling as a whole. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, REL006, REL011, REL014, REL017, POS006, POS007, POS008, LAG001, LAG005]

Limitations: a few LAG items rest on only 1–2 later-window observations and were not relied upon; the evidence contains no timing or resource items, so nothing can be said about durations or allocation. The main competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that the two windows sample differently from two pre-existing variants (one always executing A27, one the block); the earlier window shows no trace of the later pattern — all 200 earlier traces contain every activity — which makes a pure sampling explanation hard to maintain but not impossible. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004]

Other differences: small position shifts of the always-present activities — A26 earlier (mean about 0.60 → 0.54), A28 earlier (about 0.46 → 0.39), A25 slightly later and A31 slightly earlier — consistent with knock-on effects of the block's relocation rather than independent changes.
