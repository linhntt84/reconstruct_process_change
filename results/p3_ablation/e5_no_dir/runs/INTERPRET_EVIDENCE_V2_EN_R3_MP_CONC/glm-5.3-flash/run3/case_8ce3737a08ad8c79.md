### Observed differences

- The dominant difference is an exchange between two groups of three activities. In the earlier window, A24, A25 and A26 each ran in about 82 in 100 traces and almost always together, while A21, A22 and A23 each ran in only about 19 in 100 traces. In the later window, A21, A22 and A23 each run in about 79 in 100 traces (all three together in about 79 in 100), while A24, A25 and A26 each run in only about 21 in 100. - The two groups behave as mutually exclusive alternatives in both windows: an activity from one group and one from the other share a trace in at most about 1 in 100 cases, before and after. [REL017, REL024, REL030]
- A20 appears in every trace in both windows. In the earlier window it typically preceded A24–A26 (co-occurrence about 82 in 100, A20 first in about 81 in 100); in the later window it typically precedes A21–A23 (co-occurrence about 79 in 100, A20 first in about 77–79 in 100). A32 and A33 (present in roughly 60–70 in 100 traces) likewise shifted from running with A24–A26 (about 52 in 100) to running with A21–A23 (about 56 in 100). - A10 became rarer and its placement spread: it appeared in about 17 in 100 traces in the earlier window, typically late (median relative position about 0.86), and in about 7 in 100 in the later window at earlier and much more variable positions (median about 0.36); these are event positions, not clock times. The apparent change in the gap from A32 to A10 rests on only 4 later observations. [POS001, LAG001, REL001, REL004]
- A02 rose from about 2 to about 9 in 100 traces (4 vs 18 traces) — a small, weakly supported increase. [OCC001]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that two existing alternative blocks of activities exchanged their selection frequencies: the block A21–A23, which earlier ran in about 19 in 100 traces, ran in about 79 in 100 in the later window, while the block A24–A26 dropped from about 82 in 100 to about 21 in 100. Both blocks are well observed in both windows (37–164 traces per activity per window), they practically never occur in the same trace in either window, and neither shows within-trace repetition, so the pattern fits a change in how often two existing alternative paths are taken rather than activities being added, removed or repeated. The blocks sit between A20, which is present in every trace and typically precedes whichever block runs, and A30, which is present in every trace and runs in the same traces as the chosen block; the internal order within each block stays mixed in both windows. The screening comparison makes a chance explanation of the overall pattern implausible: 56 control-flow items qualified against about 0.7 expected if nothing had changed, and each item's difference exceeds what was seen in about 199 of 200 random re-splits. A competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that the two windows contain different mixes of case types, so the shift could reflect which cases entered each window rather than a changed choice within the process; the evidence also cannot show that both blocks hang on the same decision point, since they are never observed together. Other differences: A10 declined from about 17 to about 7 in 100 traces with an earlier, more variable position, and A02 rose slightly from about 2 to about 9 in 100 traces; neither is part of the block exchange above. [POS001, LAG001, OCC001, REL001, REL004]
