### Observed differences

- The order between two groups of activities reversed. In the earlier window, A30, A31 and A36 ran before A21, and before A22, in all 200 traces; in the later window, A21 runs before each of A30, A31 and A36 in about 99 in 100 traces, and the same holds for A22. Both activities still occur together with the others in every trace of both windows, so participation did not change — only the order. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006]
- Relative positions shifted in the same direction: A21's mean relative position fell from about 0.62 to about 0.53 and A22's from about 0.47 to about 0.38, while A30, A31 and A36 rose from about 0.41 to roughly 0.58–0.59. All five activities appear in 200 of 200 traces in both windows. [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005]
- Step distances changed consistently with this reordering: the gap from A20 to A22 shrank from 4 steps (in all earlier traces) to 1 step (in about 99 in 100 later traces), and the gap from A21 to A25 grew from 1 step to 4 steps. [LAG001, LAG004]
- Four further gap items (A20–A31, A20–A36, A31–A22, A36–A22) show mixed gaps of 1–3 steps in the earlier window; their later profiles rest on only 2 traces each and are too weak to characterise. [LAG002, LAG003, LAG005, LAG006]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the execution order between A21 and A22 on one side and A30, A31 and A36 on the other reversed: earlier, A30, A31 and A36 always ran before A21 and A22, whereas in the later window A21 and A22 run before them in about 99 in 100 traces, with both groups still executed in every case. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006]

The support is direct and consistent: all six activity-pair relations flip with full support (200 traces per window), the five position items move in matching directions (A21 and A22 earlier, A30/A31/A36 later), and the gap changes for A20–A22 and A21–A25 fit A21 and A22 now running earlier in the trace. Seventeen control-flow items qualified in total, far above the fewer than one (about 0.27) expected for an unchanged process, so the ensemble is unlikely for a stable process. [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, LAG001, LAG004, REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006]

Limitations: positions and gaps are measured in event positions, not clock time, so no claim about timing is made; the evidence cannot distinguish whether A21 and A22 moved earlier, the A30/A31/A36 group moved later, or both shifted (the position changes suggest both moved); about 1 in 100 later traces still follow the earlier order; and the four low-support gap items add no weight. [LAG002, LAG003, LAG005, LAG006]
