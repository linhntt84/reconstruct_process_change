### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, A19, A20 and A25 each ran at most once per case and appeared in about 33, 29 and 40 in 100 cases (65, 57 and 81 of 200 traces); in the later window each appears in only about 1 in 200 cases. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003]
- The three were mutually exclusive alternatives that together covered essentially every earlier case: no pair of them co-occurs in more than about 0.5 in 100 cases, and their participation shares sum to about 100 in 100. In the later window, both members of each pair are absent in about 99 in 100 cases. [REL001, REL002, REL006]
- Earlier, A27 (present in every case) came before each of A19, A20 and A25, and each of the three came before A29 in virtually every case where they co-occurred; their order relative to A28 (also in every case) was mixed. In the later window, A27, A28 and A29 still occur in every case, while A19, A20 and A25 are absent from about 99.5 in 100 cases. [REL003, REL005, REL007, REL009, REL010, REL012, REL004, REL008, REL011]
- Gaps between the remaining activities shrank by about one event: A27→A29 went from a gap of 3 events in about 90 in 100 cases to a gap of 2 in about 96 in 100; A27→A28 from gaps of 1–2 events (about 52 vs 45 in 100) to a gap of 1 in about 99 in 100; A28→A29 from gaps of 1–2 events (about 43 vs 52 in 100) to a gap of 1 in about 96 in 100. [LAG001, LAG002, LAG004]
- A27 and A29 appear in all cases in both windows, with only small shifts in normalized position (A27 mean about 0.46 to 0.48, A29 about 0.57 to 0.55); such position shifts alone do not show that an activity was relocated. [POS004, POS005]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that in the later window the block of three mutually exclusive activities A19, A20 and A25 — which earlier ran between A27 and A29 in essentially every case, one of the three per case, with varying order relative to A28 — is no longer executed, so that A27, A28 and A29 now follow one another directly, each adjacent pair's gap shrinking by about one event. Because these activities are nearly absent in the later window, this is a disappearance of a block of alternatives, not a shift in how often existing paths are taken. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, REL003, REL005, REL009, REL010, REL012, LAG001, LAG002, LAG004]

This is unlikely to be a sampling artefact: 24 items qualified where about 0.33 would be expected without a change, and the participation evidence is direct, resting on 57–81 earlier cases per activity against 1 later case. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003]

Limitations: non-observation in the later window does not prove the activities are gone — each was still seen in about 1 in 200 cases — so removal and always-skipping cannot be distinguished; the after-window picture for the A28→A25 pair rests on a single trace; and items describing the same traces are not independent confirmations. [LAG003, OCC001]

A competing explanation the evidence cannot fully rule out is an extreme sampling fluctuation in an unchanged process, since the windows are finite samples, though three activities each falling from 57–81 to 1 of 200 cases makes this implausible. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003]

Other differences: none beyond this change; the small normalized-position shifts of A27 and A29 fit the same shortening of traces rather than a separate difference. [POS004, POS005]
