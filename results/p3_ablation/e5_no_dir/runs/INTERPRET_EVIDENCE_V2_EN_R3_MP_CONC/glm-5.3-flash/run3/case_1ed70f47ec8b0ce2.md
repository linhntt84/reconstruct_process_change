### Observed differences

- The clearest difference is a reversal of execution order between two groups of activities. In the earlier window, A20 and A24 always ran before A15, A17 and A18 (for example, A20 before A15 in 200 of 200 traces); in the later window this is fully reversed, with A15, A17 and A18 always running before A20 and A24. [REL008, REL009, REL015, REL016, REL018, REL019]
- The same reversal holds against the optional activities A14, A16 and A19, which appear in roughly 30–36 in 100 traces. In the earlier window they preceded the A15/A17/A18 group and A20/A24 whenever they occurred (e.g., A16 before A15 in about 35 in 100 traces, A19 before A20 in about 36 in 100); in the later window they follow them (A15 before A16 in about 29 in 100, A20 before A19 in about 39 in 100). [REL001, REL004, REL006, REL007, REL012, REL020]
- Positions shifted accordingly: A15, A17 and A18 moved from a mean relative position of about 0.64 (roughly two-thirds into the trace) to about 0.38 (roughly one-third), while A14, A16 and A19 moved from about 0.34 to about 0.67. A20 (about 0.38 to 0.44) and A24 (about 0.57 to 0.64) shifted only slightly later. [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006, POS007, POS008]
- Participation and co-occurrence barely changed: A15, A17, A18, A20 and A24 occur in all 200 traces in both windows; A14, A16 and A19 occur in 60, 69 and 71 of 200 traces before versus 65, 58 and 77 after; A15 and A20 still co-occur in every trace. The difference lies in ordering, not in how often activities run. [POS002, POS004, POS005, POS007, POS008, REL008]
- Event distances changed consistently with a group of activities relocating across the A20–A24 region: A25 now follows A24 two steps later instead of four, while A20 now follows A35 four steps later instead of two, in each case in essentially all traces. [LAG001, LAG002]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the always-executed activities A15, A17 and A18 changed placement: they previously ran late in the trace, after A20 and A24 (mean relative position about 0.64), and in the later window they run early, before A20 and A24 (about 0.38). At the same time, the optional activities A14, A16 and A19, which previously ran before A20 and A24, now run after A24. Participation is essentially unchanged — the trio and A20/A24 still occur in every trace, and the optional activities occur at similar rates — so this reads as a reordering of existing behaviour, not as activities becoming optional or repeated, or one path replacing another. [REL008, REL020, POS002, POS004, POS006, POS007, POS008]

The hypothesis is shown directly and consistently: all 21 observed activity pairs spanning the two groups reversed their order (six of them from 100% one order to 100% the other), the position shifts are large (about 0.26–0.33 on the position scale) and point the same way, and the observed distance changes match a block of activities moving across the A20–A24 region. The screening context also supports a real change: 31 control-flow items qualified, versus roughly 0.32 expected by chance for an unchanged process (for example POS002, REL008, LAG001). [REL008, REL015, REL020, POS002, POS004, LAG001, LAG002]

Limitations: positions and lags are measured in event steps, not clock time, so no statement about calendar timing is implied. The exact relocated set cannot be pinned down — each observed distance changes by two steps, which does not exactly match a three-activity move — so the possibility that additional, unobserved activities also moved, or that the surrounding segments rather than the A15/A17/A18 group changed placement, cannot be ruled out. The many agreeing items largely describe the same underlying reversal and are not independent confirmations. [LAG001, LAG002, REL008]

Other differences: minor participation fluctuations (A14: 60 to 65, A16: 69 to 58, A19: 71 to 77 of 200 traces) are within ordinary window-to-window variation, and no evidence item ties them to the reordering. [POS001, POS003, POS006]
