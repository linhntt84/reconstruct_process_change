### Observed differences

- The always-present group stayed the same, but gained members: A14, A15, A16, A26 and A41 occur in every trace in both windows, while A19, A20 and A25 rose from about 47–48 in 100 traces (roughly half an occurrence per trace) to about 995 in 1,000 traces with almost exactly one occurrence each. [OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, REL012, REL020]
- In the later window these activities form a fixed, evenly spaced chain: A41 is followed by A19 after 1 event step in about 98 in 100 cases, by A25 after 2 steps in about 96, by A20 after 3 in about 95, and by A26 after 4 in about 93; the pairwise orders A41→A19, A19→A25, A25→A20 and A20→A26 hold in 96–99 in 100 traces. Before, the same relative order was already taken whenever the activities met, but they co-occurred in only about 47–48 in 100 traces. [REL019, REL021, REL022, REL023, REL025, LAG018, LAG019, LAG020, LAG021]
- A14 precedes this chain in the later window (before A19/A20/A25 in 96–98 in 100 traces), whereas before it co-occurred with them in only about 47–48 in 100 traces. [REL009, REL010, REL011]
- A06 became more frequent and earlier: participation rose from about 24 to 73 in 100 traces (mean occurrences 0.25 to 0.76) and its mean relative position moved from mid-trace to about a quarter in; it now typically follows A14 by 2 steps (about 67 in 100), immediately precedes A16 (about 97 in 100 of observed pairs) and precedes A41 by about 3 steps (about 82 in 100). [OCC001, POS001, LAG002, LAG005, LAG006]
- A08 receded: from about 20 in 100 traces to under 8 in 100. [OCC002]
- A15 and A16 are placed less rigidly relative to the chain than before: in the later window they precede A19/A20/A25 in about 57–58 in 100 traces and are interleaved with them in about 41–43 in 100, against roughly 30 and 18 in 100 before. [REL012, REL015, REL016]
- Smaller shifts: A26's mean position moved slightly later (0.41 to 0.44); a few lag results (e.g. A06–A15, A15–A26, A16–A26) rest on only 1–6 later-window observations and carry little weight. [POS002, LAG001, LAG012, LAG017]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the sequence A41 → A19 → A25 → A20 → A26, preceded by A14, became a near-mandatory backbone in the later window: A19, A20 and A25, which before were executed in about 47–48 in 100 cases (already in that relative order whenever they ran), are now executed in virtually every case, once per case, at fixed one-step spacing. [OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, REL018, REL019, REL021, REL022, LAG018, LAG020, LAG021]

Supporting signs: the participation and co-occurrence shifts are about 50 percentage points, each backed by 95–199 traces per window; relational and lag items show the fixed chain directly rather than only through frequencies; and the 54 qualifying control-flow items are roughly ninety times the ~0.6 expected if nothing had changed, so the pattern as a whole is not a sampling artefact. [OCC003, REL018, LAG018, LAG021]

Limitations: the many relation items summarise overlapping behaviour on the same traces and are not independent confirmations, and POS/LAG reflect event positions rather than clock time. An important competing explanation remains: the evidence cannot distinguish whether A19/A20/A25 became mandatory steps of a single path or whether an existing optional path containing them is now chosen in nearly every case — both fit the data. [OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, REL018]

Other differences: A06 is executed in about 73 in 100 traces (up from about 24) and earlier within the trace; A08 appears in under 8 in 100 traces (down from about 20); A15 and A16 are interleaved with the chain more often than before; A26's position shifted slightly later. These are not folded into the main hypothesis. [OCC001, POS001, OCC002, REL012, POS002]
