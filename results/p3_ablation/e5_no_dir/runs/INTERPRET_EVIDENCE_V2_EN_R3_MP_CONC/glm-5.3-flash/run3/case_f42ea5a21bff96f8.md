### Observed differences

- The dominant difference is a reversal between two activity groups. In the earlier window, A21, A22 and A23 each ran in about 75 in 100 traces and typically together (co-occurrence about 75 in 100), while A24, A25 and A26 each appeared in only about 25 in 100 traces. In the later window the shares flipped: A24–A26 each run in about 76 in 100 traces and typically together, while A21–A23 dropped to about 25 in 100. [OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, OCC007, OCC008, OCC009, REL025, REL034, REL049, REL055]

- The two groups behave as alternatives: pairs such as A21–A24 or A22–A25 almost never share a trace in either window (co-occurrence about 1 in 100); before, A21 appeared without A24 in about 75 in 100 traces, after, A24 appeared without A21 in about 75 in 100. Within each group the three activities run in no fixed order (both orders occur in similar shares). [REL027, REL035, REL042, REL025, REL049]

- The surroundings kept their roles: A19 and A20 (present in essentially all traces in both windows) preceded the active group (A19 before A21 in about 74 in 100 before; A19 before A24 in about 75 in 100 after), A29 likewise preceded it (about 73 in 100 in both windows, of the respective group), and the active group preceded A34/A35 (A21 before A34 about 73 in 100 before; A24 before A34 about 74 in 100 after). [REL001, REL013, REL016, REL030, REL051, REL032, REL053]

- A06 became much more frequent and moved to the end of the trace: participation rose from about 17 to about 52 in 100 traces, its median relative position moved from about 0.48 to about 0.96, and it now immediately precedes A35 in about 97 in 100 of the ordered-pair occurrences; accordingly the direct A34→A35 step at distance 1 fell from about 90 to about 49 in 100, with distance 2 (consistent with A34→A06→A35) appearing in about 51 in 100. [OCC002, POS001, LAG001, LAG013, REL011, REL012]

- Smaller shifts: A02 and A08 became rarer (participation about 17 → 8 and about 14 → 5 in 100), A34's position moved slightly earlier with more spread, and some step distances between surrounding activities stretched slightly (e.g., A30→A35 now typically 4–5 steps instead of 3–4). [OCC001, OCC003, POS002, LAG012]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the process chooses between two alternative activity groups and that their frequencies reversed: before, about three in four cases executed A21, A22 and A23 together (with A24, A25 and A26 as the minority path in about one in four), while in the later window about three in four cases execute A24–A26 together, with the A21–A23 path persisting at about one in four. This is a shift in how often existing alternative paths are taken, not an addition, removal or displacement of activities: both groups are well observed in both windows (e.g., A21 in 150 vs 51 traces; A24 in 51 vs 153), the groups remain mutually exclusive, and the surrounding sequence (A19/A20 and A29 before, A34/A35 after) is preserved. [OCC004, OCC007, REL027, REL035, REL013, REL016, REL030, REL051, REL032, REL053]

Chance is not a plausible explanation for the pattern as a whole: 87 control-flow items qualified, against roughly 1 expected if nothing had changed, and the individual differences are large, well-supported and mutually consistent across dozens of activity pairs. [OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, OCC007, OCC008, OCC009, REL013, REL016, REL019, REL022]

Limitation: the evidence cannot show why the choice flipped — a changed routing decision and a changed mix of cases reaching this choice point look identical here — and it cannot rule out that other unobserved paths connect the same surrounding activities. [OCC004, OCC007]

Other differences: A06 now occurs in about half of later traces (up from about 17 in 100) and sits late in the trace, typically between A34 and A35; A02 and A08 became rarer; A34's position shifted slightly. These are separate from the block swap above. [OCC002, POS001, LAG001, LAG013, OCC001, OCC003, POS002]
