### Observed differences

- The main difference is a swap in how often two alternative activity groups run. A16, A17 and A18 each appeared in about 19 in 100 traces in the earlier window (37 of 200) and in about 79 in 100 in the later window (158 of 200), while A19, A20 and A21 each fell from about 82 in 100 (163 of 200) to about 21 in 100 (42 of 200); each activity occurs at most once per trace in both windows. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006]
- The two groups behave as strict alternatives in both windows: cross-group pairs such as A16 with A19 or A18 with A21 never occur in the same trace (cooccurrence 0 before and after). Earlier traces typically contained A19–A21 without A16–A18; in the later window this is reversed. [REL009, REL010, REL011, REL014, REL015, REL016, REL018, REL019, REL020]
- Within each group the activities occur together without a fixed order in both windows; for example, in the later window A16 precedes A17 in about 42 in 100 traces and follows it in about 38 in 100, and A19, A20 and A21 likewise appear in both orders. The ordering inside the groups did not change. [REL007, REL008, REL013, REL022, REL023, REL025]
- A15 occurs in every trace in both windows and always precedes the group activities when they run (it is never observed after them), consistent with a shared point upstream of both groups. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004]
- A25 also occurs in every trace in both windows; before, it co-occurred with A19–A21 in about 82 in 100 traces, after, with A16–A18 in about 79 in 100, tracking whichever group executed. [REL012, REL017, REL021, REL024, REL026, REL027]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a change in how often two existing alternative paths after A15 are taken: the path on which A16, A17 and A18 run together in varying order rose from about 19 in 100 cases to about 79 in 100, while the alternative path with A19, A20 and A21 fell from about 82 in 100 to about 21 in 100. All six activities and both paths are observed in substantial shares in both windows, only the shares between the alternatives shifted, and no activity was added, removed, bypassed or repeated differently, which distinguishes this from a replacement of one path by another. A25's switched co-occurrence partner follows from the same shift, since A25 runs in every case. [OCC001, OCC004, REL001, REL004, REL009, REL012, REL024]

Chance is an unlikely explanation for this pattern: 33 control-flow items qualified where roughly 0.41 would be expected without a change (about 80 times the expectation, out of 82 units tested), every item shows the same 0.605 difference on its own scale, the participation and relational indicators agree on the same activity groups, and each difference exceeds what was seen in about 995 of 1000 random re-splits of the pooled data. [OCC001, REL009, screening]

Limitations: the branch point itself is inferred from A15's precedence rather than from observed outgoing edges, and the evidence cannot distinguish a change in the routing from a shift in the mix of cases entering the process, nor completely rule out two coinciding independent participation changes — although the strictly complementary, mutually exclusive shares favour the single branch-selection reading. [REL001, REL009, OCC001]
