### Observed differences

- The three activities A30, A31 and A36 stopped repeating. In the earlier window each occurred in every trace, about 3.5 times per trace on average, and appeared more than once in a trace in about 75 in 100 cases. In the later window they still occur in every trace, but only about once per trace (mean about 1.05), with more than one occurrence in only about 1 in 100 traces. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003]
- The order between A30 and A36 became fixed. Earlier, about 76 in 100 traces showed the two interleaved or repeated with no fixed order (A30 first in about 25 in 100); in the later window A30 precedes A36 in about 98 in 100 traces, with the mixed pattern remaining in about 2 in 100. The two activities still co-occur in every trace in both windows. [REL001]
- A36 now follows A30 closely: about 89 in 100 later-window observations of the pair place A36 two steps after A30, compared with about 12 in 100 earlier, when the pair also appeared at longer distances (about 5 in 100 at five steps). [LAG001]
- The reverse order and self-repetition all but vanished: A36 followed by A30 within five steps appears in about 150 of 200 earlier traces but in only 1 later trace, and A36 followed by itself in about 150 earlier traces but in 2 later traces; these later counts rest on very few observations. [LAG002, LAG003]
- The position distributions of A30 and A36 within the trace tightened (spread roughly halved, e.g. standard deviation from about 0.12 to about 0.07) while their mean positions stayed around 0.42–0.49 of the trace, consistent with single rather than multiple occurrences rather than a relocation. [POS001, POS002]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the repeated execution of A30, A31 and A36 was stopped: in the earlier window each of the three activities ran about 3.5 times per trace in every trace, while in the later window each runs about once per trace, still in every trace, with recurrence in only about 1 in 100 traces [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003]. As part of the same difference, the relation between A30 and A36 changed from mostly interleaved or repeated (about 76 in 100 traces) to a fixed order with A30 before A36 (about 98 in 100 traces), and A36 now follows A30 within about two steps in most traces [REL001, LAG001]. The nine qualifying items far exceed the fewer than 0.3 expected by chance among the 49 tested control-flow units, and the occurrence, relation and lag indicators all point to the same behaviour with full two-window support, so chance sampling cannot explain the set of differences [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, REL001, LAG001].

Limitations: the evidence cannot show how the repetition was stopped — for example whether a repeated block is no longer entered or its occurrences were consolidated — because no successor-level (direction) evidence is included, and A31's involvement rests only on its occurrence pattern; the near-disappearance of the reverse pair and of A36 self-repetition is backed by only 1 and 2 later-window observations [LAG002, LAG003]. A competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that the fixed A30-before-A36 order is a byproduct of the activities now occurring only once, rather than a newly enforced ordering constraint.

Other differences: the tightening of the position distributions of A30 and A36, with essentially unchanged mean positions, is consistent with the repetition stop rather than a separate relocation of these activities. [POS001, POS002]
