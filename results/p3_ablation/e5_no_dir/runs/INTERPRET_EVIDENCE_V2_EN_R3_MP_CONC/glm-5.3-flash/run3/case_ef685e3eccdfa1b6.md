### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, six activities — A28, A30 and A31 in one group, A32, A33 and A34 in the other — each ran exactly once in every one of the 200 traces. In the later window, A28, A30 and A31 each appear in about 47 in 100 traces (93 of 200) and A32, A33 and A34 in about 56 in 100 (111 of 200), still once per trace when present [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006].
- Before, the two groups always ran together and in sequence: each of A28, A30, A31 preceded each of A32, A33, A34 in 100% of traces. In the later window the groups have become almost mutually exclusive: about 2 in 100 traces contain both groups, about 45 in 100 contain only A28/A30/A31, about 54 in 100 only A32/A33/A34, and no trace contains neither group [REL015, REL016, REL017, REL019, REL020, REL021, REL022, REL023, REL024].
- Each group remains internally cohesive with no fixed internal order in both windows: A28, A30 and A31 still co-occur whenever any of them appears, with the order split roughly half and half (e.g., A28 before A30 in about 52 in 100 earlier traces, the reverse in about 49); the same holds within A32/A33/A34 [REL013, REL014, REL018, REL025, REL026, REL027].
- The surrounding activities are unchanged: A25 and A26 still run in every trace in both windows, A25 still precedes whichever group runs, and the group still precedes A26 [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, REL007, REL008, REL009, REL010, REL011, REL012].
- Event distances shrank accordingly: A25 to A32/A33/A34 sat 4–5 steps apart in essentially all earlier traces but 1–3 steps apart in about 97 in 100 later traces; the same shrinkage holds from A28/A30/A31 to A26, consistent with the other group being skipped [LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG008, LAG011].
- Relative positions shifted only modestly (A25's mean position from about 0.62 to 0.67 of the trace, A26's from about 0.83 to 0.81, group activities toward mid-trace), as expected when traces get shorter because one block is missing [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006, POS007, POS008].

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the two always-executed activity blocks between A25 and A26 became optional alternatives: in the later window a case typically runs either the block A28–A30–A31 (about 47 in 100 cases) or the block A32–A33–A34 (about 56 in 100), almost never both (about 2 in 100), while A25 still runs before and A26 after whichever block executes. This is a participation and structure change — the blocks became optional and bypassable — not a re-weighting of pre-existing alternative paths, since before the change both blocks ran in every case [OCC001, OCC004, REL015, REL019, REL022, REL001, REL010, LAG001, LAG004]. The screening flagged 54 items where fewer than 1 (0.49) would be expected by chance across 98 tested units, so the volume of findings cannot be explained by random re-splitting, and every item points to the same behaviour [OCC001, REL015, LAG001]. Note that the occurrence, relation and lag items largely reflect one underlying trace behaviour, so they corroborate rather than independently confirm each other [OCC001, REL015, LAG001].

Limitations: the evidence contains no direct-successor (direction) information, so the new direct links from A25 into the chosen block and from the block into A26 are not observed; the roughly 2 in 100 traces containing both blocks, and several lag items resting on only about 4 later-window observations, are too weak to characterise [LAG005, LAG006, LAG007, LAG009, LAG010, LAG012, LAG013]. A competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is what determines which block a case runs — the data show the either-or pattern but not whether it is random or driven by case characteristics.

Other differences: the modest relative-position shifts of A25, A26 and the group activities are consistent with shorter traces after one block is skipped and do not by themselves indicate that any activity was moved [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS006].
