### Observed differences

- Three activities stopped repeating. Before, A30, A31 and A36 each occurred about 3.4 times per trace and recurred within a trace in about 75 in 100 cases; in the later window each occurs about once per trace and recurs in only about 1–2 in 100 cases. All three still appear in every trace in both windows (200 of 200). [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003]
- The order between A30 and A36 tightened. Before, in about 75 in 100 traces the two were interleaved or repeated with no fixed order (A30 first in about 25 in 100); in the later window A30 comes before A36 in about 98 in 100 traces, with interleaving in about 2 in 100. The two still co-occur in every trace in both windows. [REL001]
- A36 now follows A30 closely. Before, only about 17 in 100 A30→A36 pairs fell within five steps (mostly two apart); in the later window about 92 in 100 do, with about 89 in 100 exactly two steps apart. [LAG002]
- Self-repetitions and the reverse order largely vanished: A30→A30, A36→A36 and A36→A30 pairs within five steps drop to only 1–2 supporting observations in the later window (from about 150 before), so these items carry little weight on their own. [LAG001, LAG003, LAG004]
- The relative positions of A30 and A36 barely moved but became steadier: A30's median relative position went from about 0.43 to 0.41 and A36's stayed near 0.48, while the spread roughly halved for both. [POS001, POS002]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that a repeatedly executed group of activities stopped repeating: A30, A31 and A36, which earlier recurred about 3–4 times per trace (recurring in about 75 in 100 traces), are now executed about once per trace while still appearing in every case. Alongside this, the order between A30 and A36 changed from mostly interleaved to a fixed A30-before-A36 sequence, with A36 following A30 within about two steps in most traces. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, REL001, LAG002]

Supporting signs: the occurrence drop is observed directly for all three activities with full participation in both windows, so it is not an appearance or disappearance; the order change is shown directly by the relation and lag items rather than inferred from positions alone; and ten control-flow items qualified against roughly 0.24 expected by chance across 48 tested units, so the set of differences is unlikely to be a sampling artefact of the two windows. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, POS001, POS002, REL001, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004]

Limitations: the evidence cannot show whether the fixed A30→A36 order is a new ordering constraint or simply a byproduct of the activities now running only once; no relational item covers A31, so its position relative to A30 and A36 is unobserved; and the apparent disappearance of self-repetitions and the reverse order rests on only 1–2 later-window observations. [REL001, LAG002, OCC002, LAG001, LAG003, LAG004]

Other differences: the reduced variability of A30's and A36's relative positions, with their medians essentially unchanged, is consistent with the main hypothesis rather than a separate shift in placement. [POS001, POS002]
