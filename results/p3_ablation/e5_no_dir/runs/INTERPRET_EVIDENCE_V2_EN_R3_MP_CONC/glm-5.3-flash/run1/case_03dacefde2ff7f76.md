### Observed differences

- The three activities that occur in only about a third of cases — A19, A20 and A25 — moved much later into the case: in the earlier window they sat around 31–34% of the way through the case, in the later window around 58–60%, a shift of about a quarter of the case length. Their participation stayed at about the same level (A19: 76 of 200 cases before, 61 after; A20: 64 before, 67 after; A25: 64 before, 75 after) [POS001, POS002, POS003]
- The order between these three activities and the two activities that occur in every case, A26 and A27, reversed: earlier, A19, A20 and A25 each preceded A26 and A27 in practically every co-occurring case (for example A19 before A26 in about 37 in 100 cases, the reverse in none); in the later window A26 and A27 come first (A26 before A19 in about 31 in 100, A19 before A26 in none), and the same reversal holds for A20 and A25 against both A26 and A27 (old order about 30–32 in 100 falling to at most 1 in 100) [REL001, REL002, REL004, REL005, REL006, REL007]
- In line with this, the close succession in which A25 immediately followed A26 — seen in 63 of 200 cases earlier — is essentially gone later (one case, and then at a distance of five steps) [LAG003]
- Relative to A28, a previously fixed order became unfixed: earlier A19 and A25 each preceded A28 in about 36 and 32 of 100 cases with virtually no reverse order; in the later window both orders appear in similar shares (A19 first about 16 vs A28 first about 15; A25 first about 15 vs A28 first about 22) [REL003, REL008]
- A26 and A27 themselves shifted slightly earlier (mean position about 0.35 to 0.32 and 0.58 to 0.55) [POS004, POS005]
- The spacing in the A27 → A28 → A32 sequence loosened: A27 followed by A32 went from two steps apart in about 96 of 100 cases to three steps in about 97, while A27→A28 and A28→A32, previously almost always one step (about 96–98 in 100), are now split roughly half at one step and half at two [LAG004, LAG005, LAG007]
- A26 now immediately follows A45 (one step in about 89 of 100 cases), whereas earlier it followed A45 at two steps in about 84 of 100 (three steps in about 16) [LAG008]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

In the later window, the low-frequency activities A19, A20 and A25 are executed after the mandatory activities A26 and A27 rather than before them: their position within the case moved from roughly the first third to roughly three-fifths of the event sequence, the old order fell from about 30–38 in 100 co-occurring cases to at most 1 in 100, and the previously fixed order towards A28 became unfixed. This is shown directly by the positional shift of all three activities [POS001, POS002, POS003] and by six relation items that reverse the order against A26 and A27 [REL001, REL002, REL004, REL005, REL006, REL007], with the near-disappearance of the A25→A26 close succession as corroboration [LAG003].

Chance is an unlikely explanation for this set of findings: screening tested 75 control-flow units and qualified 21 items where fewer than one (0.37) would be expected without change, each item's difference exceeding all but about 1 of 200 random re-splits, and the reversal is near-complete within co-occurring cases rather than a rare variant appearing in one window [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, REL007, REL008, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006, LAG007, LAG008].

Limitations: positions and lags are measured in event order, not clock time, so the evidence establishes a change in execution order within the case, not when work happens in time; the items share the same traces and are not independent confirmations; three lag items rest on a single earlier observation and were not relied on; and the data cannot show whether the three activities moved as one block or reached their new position through different routings [POS001, POS002, POS003, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG006]. An important competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is a shift in the mix of case variants — for example, cases containing A19/A20/A25 switching to a variant in which these activities run late — rather than a change in the ordering constraint itself.

Other differences: the loosened spacing between A27, A28 and A32, and the change so that A26 immediately follows A45, affect nearly all cases and are therefore not explained by the relocated activities alone, which appear in only about a third of cases [LAG004, LAG005, LAG007, LAG008, POS001, POS002, POS003].
