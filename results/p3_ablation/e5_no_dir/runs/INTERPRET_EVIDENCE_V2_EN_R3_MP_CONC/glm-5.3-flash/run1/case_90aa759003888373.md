### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the three-activity block A14 → A20 → A15, which ran between A36 and A21 in every earlier case, is skipped whole in about 47 in 100 later cases — with A21 then directly following A36 — while the remaining about 53 in 100 cases still execute the full sequence A36 → A14 → A20 → A15 → A21. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, REL001, REL002, REL005, LAG001]

Supporting signs: the participation of all three activities fell in lockstep from every case to about 53 in 100, with no partial skips; A21 and A36 themselves are unaffected; the A36–A21 step distance collapsed from 4 to 1 in exactly the share of cases in which the block is absent; and A21's slight forward position shift is consistent with the block sometimes missing rather than indicating an independent move. Fourteen items qualified where about 0.22 would be expected by chance at these thresholds, so the volume of flagged behaviour cannot be explained by random splitting of the windows. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, POS001, REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, REL007, REL008, REL009, LAG001]

Limitations: the evidence cannot show what determines whether the block is skipped — a random per-case choice and a mixture of two case populations would look identical here — and there is no direct successor evidence, so the A36 → A21 step in skipped cases is inferred from the step-distance pattern rather than observed as an edge.
