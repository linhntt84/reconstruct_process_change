### Observed differences

- The activities A30, A31 and A36 stopped repeating. In the earlier window each appeared in every trace and occurred about 3.5 times per trace on average, recurring within the same trace in about 75 in 100 traces; in the later window each still appears in every trace, but occurs about once per trace, with repeats in only about 1–2 in 100 traces. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003]
- The relationship between A30 and A36 changed from overlapping repetition to a fixed sequence. The two activities co-occurred in every trace in both windows, but earlier A30 came strictly before A36 in only about 25 in 100 traces (interleaved or repeated in about 76 in 100), whereas in the later window A30 precedes A36 in about 98 in 100 traces. [REL001]
- The A30 → A36 pair became tightly spaced: in the later window A36 follows A30 within two event positions in about 89 in 100 observed pairs, against about 12 in 100 earlier, while gaps of four or five positions have nearly disappeared. [LAG001]
- Linkages that only repetitions can produce vanished: A36 followed by a later A30 within five steps was seen in 150 of 200 traces earlier but in only 1 trace later, and A36 repeating within five steps in 150 traces earlier versus 2 later; these later figures rest on only 1–2 observations and are weak on their own. [LAG002, LAG003]
- The event positions of A30 and A36 became more tightly clustered (spread shrinking from about 0.12 to about 0.07 of trace length) while their average positions stayed at about 0.44 and 0.49 — consistent with single occurrences replacing repetitions scattered through the trace, not with a move to a different place in the trace. [POS001, POS002]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the repeated execution of A30, A31 and A36 stopped: a group of activities that previously ran about three to four times within each case now runs once per case, and the previously mixed, overlapping order between A30 and A36 gave way to a fixed sequence with A30 about two event positions ahead of A36. A31 shows the same once-only shift in its occurrence profile, although no relational item covers its order relative to the other two. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, REL001, LAG001]

Supporting signs: the screening flagged 9 items where fewer than 1 (about 0.24) would be expected if nothing had changed, so the set as a whole is unlikely under random re-splits; every item's difference exceeds about 995 of 1,000 random reference splits; and the occurrence, relation and lag items all point in the same direction with substantial support (200 traces per window for the main items). The fixed A30-before-A36 order is best read as a by-product of the repetition stop rather than a separate new ordering constraint, since with one occurrence each the order is necessarily fixed. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, REL001, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003]

Limitations: the items largely describe one underlying trace behaviour and are not independent confirmations; the after-sides of the reverse-pair and self-pair items rest on 1–2 observations; and the evidence cannot show why the block now runs once — for example, whether repetitions were removed from the routing or the recorded cases simply no longer iterate through the block. [LAG002, LAG003]

Other differences: none beyond the above; the tighter clustering of A30's and A36's positions reads as a by-product of the repetition stop rather than a separate change. [POS001, POS002]
