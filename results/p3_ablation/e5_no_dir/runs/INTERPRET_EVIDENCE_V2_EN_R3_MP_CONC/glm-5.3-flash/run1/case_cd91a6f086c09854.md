### Observed differences

- The execution order of two groups of activities was reversed. In the earlier window, A30, A31 and A36 each came before A21, and before A22, in all 200 traces; in the later window, A21 and A22 came before A30, A31 and A36 in 99% of traces (about 198 in 200), with the old order remaining in about 2 in 100. Both activities of every pair still occurred together in every trace in both windows, so this is a reorder, not a change in participation. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006]
- Relative positions shifted accordingly: A21's mean relative position fell from about 0.62 to 0.53 and A22's from about 0.47 to 0.38, while A30, A31 and A36 rose from about 0.41 to about 0.58–0.59; all five activities appeared in all 200 traces of both windows. [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005]
- The spacing at the edges of the moved segment changed: A22 followed A20 after 1 step in the later window where it had followed after 4 steps in every earlier trace, and A25 followed A21 after 4 steps where it had followed after 1 step. This matches the three-activity block no longer sitting between A20 and A22 and instead sitting between A21 and A25. [LAG001, LAG004]
- Screening flagged 17 control-flow items against roughly 0.27 expected for an unchanged process (55 units tested), so the volume of flagged behaviour far exceeds what random re-splitting typically produces. [screening]
- Four further lag items (pairs A20–A31, A20–A36, A31–A22, A36–A22) also changed, but their later-window distributions rest on only 2 observations each; they describe the roughly 2 in 100 later traces that keep the old order and carry little weight. [LAG002, LAG003, LAG005, LAG006]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a reordering hypothesis: the block A30–A31–A36, which previously ran before the segment containing A21 and A22 (consistently with the block occupying the four-step gap between A20 and A22), now runs after them — in the later window A21 and A22 come first in 99% of traces, A22 immediately follows A20, and A25 follows A21 after four steps, matching the block settling between A21 and A25. The hypothesis rests on direct relational evidence (order shares flipping from 100% to 99% with full co-occurrence throughout), consistent position shifts for all five activities in the predicted directions, and matching distance changes at both boundaries of the segment, each backed by all 200 traces per window; with 17 flagged items against a chance expectation of 0.27, a sampling explanation of the overall pattern is implausible. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, LAG001, LAG004, screening]

Limitations: the evidence shows the two blocks swapped order but cannot establish which of them moved — a later placement of A30–A31–A36 and an earlier placement of A21/A22 fit the data equally well — and the internal order within each block is not observed; the roughly 2 in 100 later traces still following the old order could be residual variation or a persisting alternative path, which the evidence cannot distinguish. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, LAG001, LAG004]

Other differences: none beyond the reordering; the remaining lag items involve the same activities and only capture the rare old-order traces. [LAG002, LAG003, LAG005, LAG006]
