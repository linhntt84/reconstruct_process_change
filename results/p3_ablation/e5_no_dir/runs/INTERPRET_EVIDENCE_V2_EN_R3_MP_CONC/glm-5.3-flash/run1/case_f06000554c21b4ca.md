### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the block of activities A35, A36 and A41, which previously executed after A26 and A27 (between A26 and A30), now executes before them — shortly after A25 and before A27 — while A26 and A27 kept their mutual order (A27 before A26). As part of the same rearrangement, A30 now immediately follows A26, and A27 no longer directly follows A25. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG007]

Supporting signs: the order flip is shown directly by six relational items that each move from roughly 99-in-100 "A26/A27 first" to roughly 98–99-in-100 "A35/A36/A41 first" while co-occurrence stays at 100%; the positional shifts point the same way for all five activities; and screening produced 21 items where about 0.38 would be expected if nothing had changed, so the item set as a whole is far beyond what a stable process yields. Every item's difference is also larger than in about 99.5 of 100 random re-splits of the pooled data. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005]

Limitations and competing explanation: the evidence cannot determine whether the block moved earlier or the pair A26/A27 moved later, since both produce the same pairwise relations; there is no direct successor evidence, so the new adjacencies are inferred from position distances rather than observed edges; and several lag profiles in the later window rest on only 1–2 observations, although the corresponding before-window behaviour is well supported. The 21 items largely describe one underlying rearrangement rather than independent confirmations. [LAG002, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006, LAG007, LAG008, LAG009, LAG010]

Other differences: none — the remaining distance changes (A30 now adjacent to A26, A27 about 4–5 steps after A25) fit the same rearrangement described above. [LAG001, LAG003]
