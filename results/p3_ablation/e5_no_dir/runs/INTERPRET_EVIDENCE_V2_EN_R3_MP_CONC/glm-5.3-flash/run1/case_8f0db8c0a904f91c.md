### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, the segment A19 → A25 → A20 ran exactly once in every trace (200 of 200) in a fixed order (A19 before A25 before A20 in about 99 in 100 traces). In the later window, each of the three appears in only about 53 in 100 traces (106–107 of 200), still once per trace, still together and in the same internal order; in about 47 in 100 traces none of the three occurs. [OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, REL008, REL009, REL014]

- This segment sits between A41 and A26, and both of those still occur in every trace in both windows. Before, the step distance from A41 to A26 was 4 steps in about 93 in 100 traces; in the later window it is 1 step in about 44 in 100 and 4 steps in about 49 in 100 — so in roughly the share of traces without the segment, A26 now directly follows A41. [POS002, POS003, LAG002]

- When the segment does run in the later window, its placement is unchanged: A41 still precedes it, and it still precedes A26 and A46 in essentially all co-occurring traces (for example, A19 precedes A26 in about 53 in 100 traces, equal to their co-occurrence). A26, A41 and A46 show only small shifts in normalized position. [REL010, REL011, REL012, POS002, POS003, POS004]

- A02, previously present in about 29 in 100 traces — always together with the segment, placed late (median position about 0.97, one step before A47) — appears in only about 9 in 100 traces in the later window; about 4 in 10 of those appearances lack the segment, and its position is now spread across the whole trace (mean about 0.51). Consistently, A46 and A47 — with A02 previously sitting between them in about a quarter of earlier traces — are now one step apart in about 97 in 100 traces, versus about 75 in 100 before. The after-window figures for A02 rest on only 17 occurrences (the A02–A47 distance on 5). [OCC001, POS001, REL001, LAG001, LAG003, REL006, REL007]

- Two rare activities occur somewhat more often: A04 in about 12 in 100 traces (about 5 in 100 before) and A06 in about 13 in 100 (about 4 in 100 before), each based on only 8–9 earlier occurrences. [OCC002, OCC003]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the fixed segment A19 → A25 → A20, previously executed once in every trace between A41 and A26, became optional: in the later window it is skipped in roughly 47 in 100 traces, and in those traces A26 follows A41 directly (one step apart instead of four). When it runs, its internal order and its position between A41 and A26 are unchanged. [OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, REL008, REL009, REL014, LAG002, REL010, REL011]

Supporting signs: the screening produced 35 qualifying control-flow items against fewer than 1 expected for an unchanged process, so the pattern as a whole is not what random re-splits of a stable process produce; the participation drop from 100% to about 53% is large relative to 200 traces per window; and the skip reading is directly corroborated by the A41–A26 gap collapsing to one step in about 44 in 100 traces, matching the segment's absence rate. The preserved order relations (A41 before the segment, the segment before A26 and A46 whenever they co-occur) rule out the competing reading that the segment moved to another position in the trace. Note that the many pair relations describe the same traces, so they corroborate one underlying difference rather than many independent ones. [OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, LAG002, REL008, REL010, REL011, REL012]

Limitation: the evidence shows only that the three activities are absent from the A41–A26 span in about half of the later traces; it cannot show whether that work is dropped entirely or displaced to activities outside this analysis, and it cannot point to a cause. [OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, LAG002]

Other differences: A02 became rarer (about 29 in 100 traces before, about 9 in 100 after) and less tightly placed, now sometimes appearing without the segment — consistent with its earlier tie to the segment, but possibly an independent change, and based on thin after-window support; A04 and A06 occur somewhat more often, on small numbers. [OCC001, POS001, REL001, LAG001, OCC002, OCC003]
