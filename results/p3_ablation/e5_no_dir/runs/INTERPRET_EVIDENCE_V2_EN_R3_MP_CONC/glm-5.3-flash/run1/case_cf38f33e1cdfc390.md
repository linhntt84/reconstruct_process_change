### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, A16, A17 and A18 each ran in about 75 in 100 cases; in the later window, each appears in only about 25 in 100 (participation 0.75 → 0.245, backed by about 150 vs 49 traces per activity). [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003]
- The mirror image holds for A19, A20 and A21: about 25 in 100 cases earlier, about 76 in 100 later (0.25 → 0.755, backed by about 50 vs 151 traces). [OCC004, OCC005, OCC006]
- The two groups never occur in the same case in either window (co-occurrence 0.0 throughout), so they behave as mutually exclusive alternatives, with each activity occurring at most once per case. [REL009, REL014, REL018]
- A15 appears in essentially every case in both windows and precedes whichever group runs: A15 came before A16–A18 in about 75 in 100 earlier cases, and comes before A19–A21 in about 76 in 100 later cases. [REL001, REL004]
- Togetherness within each group followed the shares: A16–A18 ran together in about 75 in 100 earlier cases (both orders mixed, roughly 38% vs 37%) but only about 25 in 100 later; A19–A21 ran together in about 76 in 100 later cases (orders mixed, roughly 34–42% each way) versus about 25 in 100 earlier. [REL007, REL013, REL022, REL025]
- A25 switched which group it accompanies: earlier it ran together with A16–A18 in about 75 in 100 cases (A16 before A25 in 43%, interleaved/repeated in 32%); later it runs with A19–A21 in about 76 in 100 (A19 before A25 in 40%, interleaved in 36%) and without A16–A18 in about 76 in 100. [REL012, REL024, REL027]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the shares of two alternative branches after A15 flipped: earlier, roughly three in four cases executed the branch containing A16, A17 and A18 (in no fixed order among themselves) and one in four executed the branch with A19, A20 and A21; in the later window these shares are roughly one in four and three in four, with A25 accompanying whichever branch runs. [OCC001, OCC004, REL009, REL022, REL024]

Supporting signs: the participation changes are measured directly for all six activities on substantial support in both windows; the relational items show the two groups were already mutually exclusive alternatives before the change, and within-group togetherness and A25's pairing move in step with the shares — the pattern expected when only the choice between existing paths shifts, not when activities appear or disappear. Every item's difference exceeds about 995 in 1,000 random re-splits, and the 34 qualifying control-flow items are roughly 80 times the 0.42 expected by chance, so random re-splitting cannot account for the ensemble. [OCC001, OCC004, REL007, REL022]

Limitations: all items derive from the same 200-plus-200 traces, so they describe one underlying difference rather than independent confirmations; A25's own participation is not directly measured (only its relations); and the evidence cannot distinguish a change in the routing decision itself from a change in the mix of cases reaching this point in the process. [OCC001, REL012]

Other differences: among cases containing both A15 and A20, the positional gap between them shortened — a three-step gap fell from about half of cases to about a third, while one- and two-step gaps together rose to about two thirds; this concerns event positions, not clock time, and may partly reflect the changed branch composition. [LAG001]
