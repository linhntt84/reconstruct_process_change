### Observed differences

- The clearest difference is a reversal of execution order between two groups of activities. In the earlier window, A35, A36 and A41 each ran before A26 and A27 in about 99–100 in 100 traces; in the later window, A26 and A27 run before A35, A36 and A41 in about 97–99 in 100 traces. All six cross-pairs show this flip, and in both windows each pair occurs together in every single trace, so the change concerns order, not whether the activities run. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006]
- Relative positions move accordingly: A26's mean position shifts from about 0.62 to 0.53 of the trace and A27's from about 0.47 to 0.38, while A35, A36 and A41 shift from about 0.41–0.42 to about 0.58–0.59; each figure is based on all 200 traces in both windows. [POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006]
- Spacing around these activities changed consistently: A25 now immediately precedes A27 (one-step gap in about 98–99 in 100 traces, versus a four-step gap in about 93 in 100 before), and A30 now follows A26 by about four steps (about 91 in 100) instead of immediately (about 99 in 100 before). [LAG005, LAG009]
- The moved group also drifted away from its earlier neighbours: A35, A36 and A41 each followed A25 or A27 within one to three steps in essentially all 200 earlier traces, but the ordered pairs occur within five steps in only about 2 in 100 later traces (the after-profiles of these pairs rest on just 2 traces each). [LAG006, LAG007, LAG008, LAG010, LAG012]
- Two participation changes sit outside this reordering: A06 appears in about 12–13 in 100 later traces, down from about 27 in 100, and sits later within the trace (mean position about 0.25 to 0.44); A02 appears in about 11–12 in 100 later traces versus about 4–5 in 100 before, though this rests on only 9 versus 23 traces. Several other lag-profile differences rest on 1–11 observations in one window and are too weak to interpret. [OCC001, OCC002, POS001, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG011]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the relative execution order between the activities A26 and A27 on the one hand and A35, A36 and A41 on the other was reversed: in the earlier window A35, A36 and A41 ran before A26 and A27 in nearly every case, whereas in the later window A26 and A27 run first and A35, A36 and A41 follow. The sign is direct and consistent — all six cross-pairs flip from about 99–100 in 100 traces with one order to about 97–99 in 100 with the other, each backed by all 200 traces per window — and the five position shifts, the A25–A27 gap shrinking to one step and the A26–A30 gap growing to about four steps all point the same way, while co-occurrence stays at 100 in 100, so participation did not change. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006, LAG005, LAG009]

The volume of qualifying evidence also far exceeds what an unchanged process produces: 26 items qualified across 103 tested control-flow units, where about 0.5 would be expected by chance, although many of these items describe the same underlying reordering rather than independent confirmations. [OCC001, OCC002, POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006, REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006, LAG007, LAG008, LAG009, LAG010, LAG011, LAG012]

Limitations: the indicators capture event positions within cases, so the hypothesis concerns execution order rather than clock timing; the relational evidence covers only cross-group pairs, so any change in the order within each group (between A26 and A27, or among A35, A36 and A41) is not shown; and the evidence cannot distinguish a modified ordering rule from the two windows sampling two variants with opposite orders. Lag items with only 1–11 supporting observations in one window were not used for the hypothesis. [LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG011]

Other differences: A06 occurs in fewer traces in the later window (about 12–13 in 100 versus about 27 in 100) and sits later within the trace; A02 occurs in more traces (about 11–12 in 100 versus about 4–5 in 100, on few observations). These participation changes are not part of the ordering hypothesis. [OCC001, OCC002, POS001]
