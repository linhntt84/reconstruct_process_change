### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, A19, A20 and A25 each took part in a sizable share of cases — about 65, 57 and 81 of 200 traces (roughly one third each) — while the three almost never co-occurred (about 0.5% of traces) and together covered essentially every trace, so exactly one of them ran in nearly every case. In the later window, each of the three appears in only about 1 of 200 traces. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, REL001, REL002, REL006]

- In the earlier window, each of the three alternatives ran inside the span between A27 and A29: A27 preceded it and it preceded A29 in the same 28–40% of traces in which it occurred, and its order against A28 was mixed (both orders in similar proportions). In the later window, co-occurrence of A19, A20 or A25 with A27, A28 or A29 has fallen to about 0.5% of traces; A27, A28 and A29 still occur in every trace, now essentially always without any of the three. [REL003, REL004, REL005, REL007, REL008, REL009, REL010, REL011, REL012]

- The spacing between the remaining activities tightened by about one step: A29 followed A27 typically three positions later before (about 90 in 100 traces) but two positions later after (about 96 in 100); A28 immediately follows A27 in about 99 of 100 later traces (about 52 in 100 before, with a two-step gap in about 45), and A29 immediately follows A28 in about 96 of 100 (about 43 in 100 before). [LAG001, LAG002, LAG004]

- The always-present activities shifted only slightly in position: A29's mean relative position moved from about 0.57 to about 0.55, and A27's from about 0.46 to about 0.48. [POS004, POS005]

- The after-window position and lag figures for A19, A20 and A25 rest on a single trace each and cannot describe where these activities now sit. [POS001, POS002, POS003, LAG003]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the alternative step formed by A19, A20 and A25 — of which exactly one was executed between A27/A28 and A29 in nearly every case in the earlier window — is no longer executed in the later window, so that A29 now directly follows A28, which directly follows A27. This reads as a removal or bypass of that block rather than a shift in how often existing paths are taken, because the three activities are observed in only about 1 of 200 traces each afterwards and the A27→A29 distance shortened by exactly one step, matching the loss of one intervening activity. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, REL003, REL005, REL007, REL009, REL010, REL012, LAG001, LAG002, LAG004]

The change is seen directly in participation (65, 57 and 81 traces before versus 1 after), consistently across all twelve relational profiles involving the three activities, and in the lag structure; the screening qualified 24 items where only about 0.33 would be expected if nothing had changed, so this set of differences is far beyond what random re-splits of a stable process produce. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, REL003, REL012, LAG002]

Limitations: each of the three activities still appears in about 1 of 200 later traces, so the evidence cannot distinguish a complete removal from a residual execution in a small share of cases, nor tell whether the step is skipped conditionally; the after-window placement of A19, A20 and A25 cannot be characterised because it rests on single traces. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, POS001, POS002, POS003]

Other differences: none beyond the change above; the slight position shifts of A27 and A29 and the remaining lag item (A28 to A25 moving from an immediate to a five-step separation, on a single after-window observation) are consistent with the same removal. [POS004, POS005, LAG003]
