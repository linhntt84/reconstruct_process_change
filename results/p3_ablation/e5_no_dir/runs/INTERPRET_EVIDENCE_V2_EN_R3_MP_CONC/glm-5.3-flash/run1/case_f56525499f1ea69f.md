### Observed differences

- Six activities became near-universal: A33, A35, A36, A37, A38 and A39 each ran in roughly half of the cases before (about 49–52 in 100 traces) but in about 99 in 100 traces in the later window. [OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, OCC007, OCC008, OCC009]
- Before, these six formed two mutually exclusive blocks: a first-block activity such as A33 and a second-block activity such as A37 never occurred in the same case (co-occurrence 0, with exactly one of the two in every case — the first block in about 49 in 100 cases, the second in about 52 in 100). In the later window they co-occur in about 98 in 100 cases, with the first-block activity earlier in about 97 in 100 of them. [REL039, REL041, REL046, REL048]
- Within each block, the activities already appeared together whenever they appeared at all; in the later window they still co-occur in about 98–99 in 100 cases and occur in both orders in similar proportions, so the order inside each block stays unfixed. [REL037, REL042, REL049, REL051]
- Downstream spacing shifted accordingly: the positional distance from A30 to A37/A38/A39, and from A33/A35/A36 to A31, was mostly 1–3 steps before and is almost always 4–5 steps in the later window, consistent with the other block now executing in between. [LAG003, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006, LAG007, LAG008]
- A04 became rarer: about 31 in 100 cases before, about 14 in 100 in the later window; when it occurs now it always co-occurs with the first-block activities (cases with A04 alone fell from about 13–18 in 100 to 0), and its position is more spread out. [OCC001, REL001, POS001]
- A06 and A10 occur more often, from about 3 in 100 cases to about 11–12 in 100, though on small bases (6→22 and 5→24 traces). [OCC002, OCC003]
- Minor position shifts: A14's position became much more tightly concentrated (spread 0.07→0.01), A30's mean position moved slightly earlier (0.67→0.62) and A31's slightly later (0.80→0.82). [POS002, POS004, POS005]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that two previously alternative blocks became mandatory and sequential. Earlier, each case executed exactly one of two blocks — A33 together with A35 and A36 in about half of the cases, or A37 together with A38 and A39 in the other half — and activities from different blocks never shared a case. In the later window, all six activities run in about 98–99 in 100 cases, the first block preceding the second (A33 before A37 in about 97 in 100 cases), while the order within each block remains unfixed, with both orders in similar proportions. [REL039, REL046, OCC004, OCC007]

The change is shown directly by the relational evidence — cross-block co-occurrence rising from 0 to about 98 in 100 with a stable cross-block order, identically across all nine cross-block pairs — and is corroborated by the participation rise of all six activities and by the downstream lag shifts. Screening qualified 79 items where fewer than 1 would be expected if nothing had changed, and every item's difference exceeds about 99.5 in 100 random re-splits, so chance cannot account for the pattern as a whole. [REL039, REL048, LAG003, LAG006]

The evidence cannot rule out that the two windows sample a different mix of pre-existing case types — for example, a variant executing both blocks that was absent from the earlier window — rather than a modification of a single process; the within-block "no fixed order" reading also rests on one sample per window, and a few secondary items have small after-samples (e.g., the A30–A31 lag profile on 5 observations). [LAG002]

Other differences: the decline of A04 (now occurring only alongside the first-block activities, with a more variable position), the increased occurrence of A06 and A10, and the small position shifts of A14, A16, A30 and A31 are not part of the main hypothesis. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, POS001, POS002, POS004, POS005]
