### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that, in the later window, the activities A35, A36 and A41 are executed after A26 and A27, whereas in the earlier window they were executed before them — a reversal of the relative order of the two groups. This is shown directly by the relational evidence: all six activity pairs co-occur in every trace of both windows, and the dominant order flips from the A35/A36/A41 side first in about 97–100 in 100 traces to the A26/A27 side first in about 97–98 in 100 traces. [REL010, REL011, REL012, REL013, REL014, REL015]

The positional and gap evidence agrees with this reading: A35, A36 and A41 shifted later in the trace (mean position about 0.40 to 0.58) while A26 and A27 shifted earlier (0.60 to 0.53 and 0.46 to 0.39), the A26→A30 gap widened from one to about four steps, and A25 now immediately precedes A27 where it previously preceded it by about four steps. With 34 qualifying items against roughly 0.58 expected without change in the control-flow family, the volume of flagged differences cannot be explained by random re-splitting of a stable process. [POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006, LAG002, LAG005, screening]

Limitations: positions and lags are measured in event positions, not clock time, so the change is established as a change in ordering, not in timing; and the relational data pins down pairwise order but not the full surrounding sequence, so it cannot rule out the competing explanation that a broader restructuring also inserted or removed steps around the moved group — the widened A26→A30 gap is consistent with something now sitting between them, though the corresponding before-profiles of some lag pairs rest on very few observations. [LAG005, LAG006, LAG008]
