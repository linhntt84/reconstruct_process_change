### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the shares of two alternative paths flipped. In the earlier window, roughly 4 of 5 traces executed the block A24–A26 after A20 (the three activities together, in no fixed order, alongside A30), while the block A21–A23 ran in only about 1 of 5 traces; in the later window this reversed, with about 4 of 5 traces running A21–A23 after A20 and the A24–A26 block dropping to about 1 of 5. Both blocks were observed with substantial support in both windows (about 37–38 versus 42 of 200 traces on the minority side), the blocks never mix within a trace, repetitions per trace did not change, and no activity bypasses another, so this reads as a change in how often two existing alternative paths are taken rather than activities being added, removed or repeated. [OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, OCC007, REL009, REL012, REL015, REL017, REL024, REL036]

The volume of qualifying evidence is far beyond chance: 56 items qualified against roughly 0.72 expected if nothing had changed, and the pattern is consistent across occurrence and relational indicators, including the matching flips in A30 and A32/A33 co-occurrence. [OCC002, OCC005, REL009, REL012, REL020, REL027, REL021, REL039]

Limitations: the evidence contains no direct successor (edge) data, so the branch point after A20 is inferred from precedence and co-occurrence rather than observed routing; and a shift in the mix of pre-existing case types entering the process would look identical in these windows, which the evidence cannot rule out. [REL009, REL012, REL017, REL024]
