### Observed differences

- The dominant difference is a flip in how often two three-activity blocks run. In the earlier window, A21, A22 and A23 each appeared in about 75 in 100 traces (about 150 of 200), while A24, A25 and A26 each appeared in about 25 in 100 (about 50); in the later window, A21–A23 each appear in about 25 in 100 (about 49) and A24–A26 each in about 76 in 100 (about 151). [OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, OCC007, OCC008]
- The two blocks behave as alternatives. Before, a trace typically contained A21 without A24 (about 75 in 100) or A24 without A21 (about 25 in 100), with the two almost never together; in the later window these shares reversed (about 25 vs about 75 in 100). The same mutual exclusivity holds for A22 and A23 against A24, A25 and A26. [REL015, REL017, REL022, REL024, REL028, REL030]
- Each block keeps its internal structure. A21, A22 and A23 still co-occur with one another whenever they run (together in about 75 in 100 traces before, about 24 after, mirroring participation), and A24, A25 and A26 are together in about 76 in 100 traces after versus about 25 before; within each block both orders occur and no fixed internal order exists in either window, and repetition stays essentially absent throughout. [REL013, REL014, REL021, REL034, REL035, REL039]
- The blocks' partners switched with them. A20 occurs in every trace in both windows: before it preceded A21/A22/A23 in about 3 of 4 traces and A24/A25/A26 in about 1 of 4; in the later window this is reversed (about 3 of 4 followed by A24–A26). A30, also in every trace, co-occurred with A21–A23 in about 75 in 100 traces before versus about 25 after, and with A24–A26 in about 25 before versus about 76 after. [REL007, REL008, REL009, REL010, REL011, REL012, REL018, REL025, REL031, REL036, REL040, REL043]
- A32 and A33, present in about two thirds of traces in both windows, likewise moved their association: co-occurrence with A21–A23 fell from about 48 in 100 traces to about 15, while co-occurrence with A24–A26 rose from about 17 to about 52 in 100. [REL019, REL026, REL032, REL037, REL041, REL044]
- Secondary differences: A10's participation rose from about 6 in 100 traces (11 of 200) to about 21 in 100 (42), and its typical position moved later in the trace (mean relative position about 0.57 to about 0.82, with a tighter spread). A02 fell from about 9 in 100 traces (18) to about 1 in 100 (2). Where A24 follows A20, the step gap is now more often 2–3 steps (3-step share about 20 in 100 before, about 39 after), and the gaps for A25–A30 and A32–A33 also shifted slightly toward larger lags. ### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the relative frequency of two existing alternative paths changed: before, roughly three in four cases ran the block A21–A22–A23 (typically after A20 and alongside A30 and A32/A33) and roughly one in four ran A24–A26; in the later window the shares are reversed, with A24–A26 taken in about three in four cases. This is shown directly by the participation counts and by the co-occurrence and precedence relations of both blocks with A20, A30, A32 and A33, which all flipped together, while each block's internal structure (mutually unordered activities, no repetition) stayed the same. All six activities remain observed in at least about a quarter of traces in both windows and only the shares between the alternatives moved, which fits a change in how often existing paths are taken rather than a path being added, removed or bypassed; the ensemble of 58 qualifying items against roughly 0.74 expected if nothing had changed makes chance sampling implausible as an account of the whole. [OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, OCC007, OCC008, REL007, REL010, REL015, REL018, REL034, REL036]

The evidence contains no direct transition-level (DIR) measurements, so the predecessor–successor wiring is inferred from co-occurrence and precedence rather than observed edges, and with only two windows it cannot show whether the share flip was abrupt or gradual. An important competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is a changed mix of cases entering the process between the windows, which would produce the same flip in observed branch shares without any change to the routing itself. [REL007, REL010, REL018, REL036]

Other differences: A10 became more frequent (about 6 to about 21 in 100 traces) and now sits later in the trace; A02 nearly disappeared (about 9 to about 1 in 100, resting on very few cases); and the step gaps for A20–A24, A25–A30 and A32–A33 shifted toward larger lags, which is consistent with the changed branch mixture rather than a separate repositioning. [OCC002, POS001, OCC001, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003]
