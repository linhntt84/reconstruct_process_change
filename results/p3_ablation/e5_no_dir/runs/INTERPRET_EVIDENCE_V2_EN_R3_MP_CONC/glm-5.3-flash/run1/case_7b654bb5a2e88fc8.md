### Observed differences

- A14, A15 and A20 behave as three alternatives: each occurs in roughly 3 in 10 cases (about 38, 31 and 32 in 100 in the earlier window; about 31, 33 and 37 in the later), and their shares sum to 100 in 100 in both windows, so every case contains exactly one of them. A21, A22 and A23 occur in every case in both windows. [POS001, POS002, POS003, REL001, REL004, REL006]
- In the earlier window the running alternative sat about a third of the way through the case (mean relative position about 0.31–0.33, where 0 is the start and 1 the end); in the later window it sits at about 0.59–0.60, essentially at A23's position. [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS006]
- The order against A21 and A22 reversed completely: earlier, the alternative came first in every case containing both (e.g., A14 before A21 in all 38 in 100 co-occurring cases); later, A21 and A22 came first in every co-occurring case (A21 before A14 in all 31 in 100). [REL001, REL002, REL004, REL005, REL006, REL007]
- The order against A23 changed from fixed to mixed: earlier the alternative always preceded A23; later A23 precedes A14 in about 15 in 100 cases while A14 precedes A23 in about 16 (for A20: A23 first in about 23, A20 first in about 15) — no fixed order. [REL003, REL008]
- Step distances changed accordingly: A22→A27 stretched from always 2 steps to always 3; A40→A21 shortened from always 2 steps to always 1; A22→A23 went from always 1 step to 1 step in about 52 in 100 cases and 2 steps in about 48; A23→A27 from always 1 to 1 in about 48 and 2 in about 52. This fits the chosen alternative now being inserted between A22 and A27, sometimes before and sometimes after A23, and no longer sitting between A40 and A21. [LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004]
- A21, A22 and A23 themselves shifted only slightly earlier (mean relative position down by about 0.02–0.03), consistent with one event that previously preceded them no longer doing so. [POS004, POS005, POS006]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the three alternative activities A14, A15 and A20 — exactly one of which executes in every case — changed their placement relative to the A21–A23 sequence: in the earlier window each case ran the chosen alternative immediately before A21 (between A40 and A21, which was always two steps upstream of A21); in the later window the chosen alternative runs after A21 and A22 and immediately around A23, before it in about half the cases and after it in the other half, adding one step between A22 and A27 and removing the one between A40 and A21. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, REL007, REL008, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, POS001, POS002, POS003]

The support is direct and consistent: six activity pairs show complete order reversals, two show a fixed order becoming mixed, and all four gap changes fit the same single rearrangement; 18 control-flow items qualified where about 0.24 would be expected if nothing had changed, so chance can hardly account for the set as a whole. The main limitation is that the items overlap heavily — the position, relation and gap indicators largely view the same rearrangement, so they are not independent confirmations — and all evidence concerns relative event order: the data cannot tell whether the alternatives moved later or the A21–A23 block moved earlier, nor whether the alternative and A23 now run in parallel rather than in a variable sequential order. [POS001-POS006, REL001-REL008, LAG001-LAG004]

Other differences: the split of cases among the three alternatives shifted somewhat (A14 about 38→31 in 100, A20 about 32→37), a difference small enough to be within ordinary sampling variation for 200 cases per window. [POS001, POS002, POS003]
