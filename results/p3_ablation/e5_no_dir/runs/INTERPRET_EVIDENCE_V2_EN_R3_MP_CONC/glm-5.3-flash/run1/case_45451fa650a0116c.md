### Observed differences

- The dominant difference is a flip between two three-activity blocks. In the earlier window, A21, A22 and A23 each ran in about 20 in 100 traces; in the later window, each runs in about 80 in 100. A24, A25 and A26 show the mirror image, falling from about 80 in 100 traces to about 20–22 in 100. [OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, OCC007, OCC008, OCC009]
- The two blocks remain mutually exclusive alternatives in both windows — they co-occur in only about 1 in 100 traces either way — so what shifted is which alternative is chosen, not the block membership. [REL019, REL026, REL032]
- Each block still runs as one group: A21–A23 co-occurred in essentially every trace where any of them ran, both before (about 19 in 100 traces) and after (about 79 in 100); the same holds for A24–A26 (about 82 in 100 before, about 21 in 100 after). [REL017, REL025, REL038, REL043]
- The surrounding activities kept their roles. A20 appears in all traces in both windows; before it ran with the A24–A26 block (about 82 in 100 traces, A20 first in about 80 of them), after with the A21–A23 block (about 79 in 100, A20 first in about 77). A30, A34 and A35 likewise appear throughout and simply switched which block accompanies them (about 82 in 100 with A24–A26 before, about 79 in 100 with A21–A23 after, with the block typically before A34/A35). [REL011, REL014, REL022, REL023, REL041]
- A06 became much rarer: about 46 in 100 traces before, about 12 in 100 after; when it occurs it now sits earlier in the trace (mean relative position about 0.91 vs 0.52), and its almost-always one-step link to A35 largely disappeared, though only about 7 after-window observations back the lag detail. [OCC003, POS001, REL001, LAG001]
- A34 is now followed immediately by A35 in about 92 in 100 traces, versus about 56 in 100 before (previously one event often sat between them). [LAG006]
- The rare activities A02 and A04 roughly doubled (about 8 to 16 in 100 and about 5 to 19 in 100 traces) but remain uncommon. [OCC001, OCC002]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that, in the later window, the choice between two existing alternative paths flipped: the path containing A21, A22 and A23, taken in about 20 of 100 cases earlier, is taken in about 80 of 100 cases, while the alternative path with A24, A25 and A26 dropped from about 80 to about 20 of 100. This is a change in how often existing paths are taken rather than a replacement of activities: both blocks were observed in both windows, each ran as an intact group throughout, they never ran together in either window, and the backbone activities A20 (before the block) and A30, A34, A35 (after it) kept their participation and simply changed which block they accompany. The volume of flagged behaviour cannot be explained by sampling noise: 67 items qualified where roughly 0.72 would be expected for an unchanged process, and every item's difference exceeds those seen in about 995 of 1000 random re-splits. Limitations: the evidence cannot show whether the flipped choice reflects a modified routing decision or a shifted mix of cases entering the process, and within the A21–A23 block the internal order is mixed, so the now-dominant path is not a fixed sequence.
