### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, three activities each ran in roughly a third of the cases, always at most once per case: A19 in about 37 of 100 cases, A25 in about 35, and A20 in about 31. In the later window they have practically disappeared — A19 appears in 1 of 200 cases, A20 and A25 in none. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003]
- Before, these three behaved as mutually exclusive alternatives: pairings such as A19 with A20, A19 with A25 or A20 with A25 occurred in about 1 in 100 cases or fewer, while exactly one of the pair appeared in roughly two thirds of the cases. In the later window, almost no case contains any of them and none contains a pair. [REL001, REL003, REL008]
- The three activities sat between A41 and A26/A31 in the earlier window: A41 came before A19, A20 or A25 in about 31–37 of 100 cases, and each of the three came before A26 and before A31 in about 30–36 of 100 cases. A41, A26 and A31 themselves ran in every case in both windows. [REL004, REL005, REL006, REL009, REL010, REL011, REL013, REL014, REL015]
- In the later window, A26 and A31 run without any of the three in essentially every case (about 199–200 of 200), whereas before they co-occurred with one of them in about 31–37 of 100 cases. [REL004, REL005, REL009, REL010, REL013, REL014]
- The distance from A41 to A26 shortened: before, A26 typically followed A41 two event positions later (about 85 in 100 cases; three positions later in about 15 in 100); after, it typically follows directly (about 86 in 100 cases at distance one). [LAG001]
- A24 continues in about half of the cases in both windows (about 50 in 100 before, about 56 in 100 after); before, it sometimes ran after one of the dropped activities, co-occurring with each of them in about 17 of 100 cases. [REL002, REL007, REL012]
- Minor positional shifts accompanied this: A26 now sits slightly earlier within cases (median relative position about 0.38 to 0.36) and A41 slightly later (about 0.31 to 0.32). [POS004, POS005]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that an optional step between A41 and A26 — filled in the earlier window by one of three mutually exclusive activities, A19, A20 or A25 — is no longer executed in the later window, so that A26 now follows A41 directly, with A31 behaving the same way. Before, about one case in three contained one of these three activities in that slot, followed by A26/A31; after, none of the three is observed and A26 immediately follows A41 in about 86 of 100 cases. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, REL004, REL009, REL013, LAG001]

The support is direct and consistent: the participation collapse is observed for all three activities with substantial before-support (62–73 cases each, versus at most 1 case after), so a rare-variant artefact is unlikely; the relational items show the structural change itself — A26 and A31 went from always co-occurring with one of the three to running without them in every case — rather than a marginal shift alone; and the A41-to-A26 distance shortening from mostly two positions to mostly one matches removal of the intermediate step. The volume of qualified evidence (24 items against roughly 0.33 expected for an unchanged process) is far beyond what chance sampling produces. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, REL004, REL005, REL009, REL010, REL013, REL014, LAG001]

Limitations: zero participation only means the activities were not observed in the later sample, so the evidence cannot distinguish a step that is skipped or removed from one that is simply not recorded in the later window; with only two windows it also cannot show whether the change was abrupt or gradual. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003]

Other differences: A26 now sits slightly earlier and A41 slightly later within cases (median relative-position shifts of about 0.02); these small shifts are consistent with the same restructuring but are based on event positions, not clock time. [POS004, POS005]
