### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the ordering constraints among this block of activities were relaxed in the later window: A26, A17, A15, A18, A19, A21, A20 and A27, previously executed in one fixed sequence with constant spacing, are now executed in varying orders — A19 typically precedes A15 and A18 (about 74 and 85 in 100), several pairs have no dominant order, and the spacing between activities varies from case to case, while every activity still appears in every trace. This is a change in ordering constraints, not a change in participation or in how often existing paths are taken. [REL001, REL006, REL003, REL007, LAG001, LAG007, LAG023, POS001, POS004]

The support is direct and consistent: all eight relation items flip from a 100% fixed order to mixed or reversed orders with co-occurrence unchanged at 100 in 100; all 24 lag items show the same constant-gap-to-variable-gap pattern; the position shifts agree in direction with the relation changes; the directional pair A15 before A19 within five steps, present in all 200 earlier traces, appears in only about 52 of 200 later traces, matching the reversal; and 38 qualifying control-flow items against roughly 0.32 expected for an unchanged process (each item's difference exceeding about 995 in 1000 random re-splits) cannot be explained by chance sampling. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, REL007, REL008, LAG001, LAG002, LAG007, LAG023, POS001, POS004, POS005, POS006]

Limitations: the window-level relations cannot show whether each individual case now uses a genuinely flexible order or whether the later window mixes a few fixed-order variants (for example two fixed sequences each used in a subset of cases); the complete new ordering structure cannot be established from pair-level relations alone, and the position and gap indicators use event positions, not clock time.
