### Observed differences

- A30, A31 and A36 changed from single executions to repeated ones. Before, each occurred about once per trace and repeats were almost absent (at most about 2 in 100 traces); in the later window each occurs about 3.5 times per trace on average, with repeats in about 68 in 100 traces. All three still appear in every trace in both windows, so participation did not change — the number of executions per trace did. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003]
- The strict sequence among A14, A30 and A36 dissolved. The three activities still co-occur in every trace in both windows, but before, A14 preceded A30 and A30 preceded A36 in about 98–100 in 100 traces; in the later window the fixed order holds in only about 32 in 100 traces, while interleaved or repeated execution accounts for about 68 in 100. [REL003, REL004, REL005]
- The tight A30→A36 adjacency is gone: before, A36 followed A30 about two positions later in roughly 98 in 100 pair instances; in the later window that two-step gap covers only about 12 in 100, with occurrences spread over larger gaps of four to five steps. [LAG002]
- Positions became more variable: the spread of A30's and A36's relative positions grew by roughly two thirds (std about 0.07 to about 0.12), and A14 now sits slightly earlier in the trace (mean relative position about 0.14 to about 0.12). [POS001, POS002, POS003]
- A05's ties to the repeated activities loosened: A05 still co-occurs with A30 and with A36 in about half of traces in both windows, but A05 running before them fell from about 54 in 100 traces to about 16, with interleaving rising to about 36 in 100; the typical gap between A05 and A14 also moved from about one step to two or three steps (observed in roughly 110 and 100 of 200 traces respectively). [REL001, REL002, LAG001]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that in the later window A30, A31 and A36 are executed several times per trace where they previously ran once, and that the previously fixed order A14 → A30 → A36 gave way to a mixed, interleaved order. The repetition is measured directly (mean occurrences rising from about 1 to about 3.5 per trace, repeats in about 68 in 100 later traces against at most about 2 in 100 before), the relational items show the same dissolution of the fixed sequence among the same activities, the lag item shows the characteristic two-step A30→A36 gap disappearing, and the positional items show the later executions spread far more widely across the trace. The 13 qualifying control-flow items are roughly 50 times the about 0.27 expected by chance, and every item's difference exceeds about 199 of 200 random re-splits, so sampling variation alone cannot explain the pattern. Limitations: the rise in interleaved relations is expected whenever activities repeat, so the evidence demonstrates the repetition directly but cannot separate a genuinely relaxed ordering constraint from the mechanical effect of repetition; it also cannot establish that the roughly 32 in 100 later traces retaining the fixed order are exactly those without repeats, even though the two shares match. POS and LAG reflect event positions rather than clock time, so these are statements about order within traces, not about timing. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, REL003, REL004, REL005, LAG002, POS002, POS003]

Other differences: A05's ordering relative to A30 and A36 changed (A05-first from about 54 in 100 traces to about 16, interleaved up to about 36 in 100) and the A05–A14 gap shifted from about one step to two or three steps; these plausibly accompany the repeated block but are not part of the main hypothesis. [REL001, REL002, LAG001]
