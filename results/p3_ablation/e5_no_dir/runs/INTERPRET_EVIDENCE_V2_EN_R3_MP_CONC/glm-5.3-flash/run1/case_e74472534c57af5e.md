### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, A30, A31 and A36 each occurred almost exactly once per trace (means about 1.0) and repeats were nearly absent (at most about 1 in 100 traces); in the later window each occurs about 3.5 times per trace on average, and about 68 in 100 traces contain repeats. Participation stayed at 100 in 100 traces in both windows, so these activities did not become optional — they became repeated. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003]
- The strict sequence among these activities dissolved. A14 preceded A30 in about 99 in 100 traces before and about 32 in 100 in the later window, with interleaved-or-repeated orderings rising from about 1 to about 68 in 100; the same pattern holds for A14–A36 (about 99.5 in 100 down to about 32) and A30–A36 (about 98.5 down to about 31.5). [REL003, REL004, REL005]
- The tight step pattern changed accordingly: A30 was followed by A36 at a distance of two positions in about 97 in 100 traces before, but at that distance in only about 12 in 100 later; the reverse pair A36 before A30 within five steps, seen in only 1 of 200 traces before (a weak before-window basis), now appears in about 136 of 200 traces — roughly two thirds. [LAG001, LAG002]
- A05 appeared in about 55 in 100 traces before and about 51–52 in 100 later, always alongside A30/A36; when both were present, A05 came first in about 54–55 in 100 before versus about 17 in 100 later, with interleaving rising from near zero to about 34–35 in 100. A05's relative position also shifted slightly earlier (mean about 0.07 to about 0.05 of the trace). [REL001, REL002, POS001]
- The positions of A14, A30 and A36 became much more spread out (standard deviations roughly doubled to tripled) while their median positions stayed broadly similar, consistent with several occurrences scattered through each trace rather than one fixed spot. [POS002, POS003, POS004]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the stage composed of A30, A31 and A36 changed from a single fixed-order pass into a repeated, interleaved one. In the earlier window these three activities ran once per case within a strict sequence A14 → A30 → A36 (holding in about 99 in 100 traces); in the later window each runs about three to four times per case, repeats occur in about 68 in 100 traces, the strict precedence holds in only about three in ten traces, and the reverse order A36 → A30 — previously essentially unobserved — now appears in about two thirds of traces. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, REL003, REL004, REL005, LAG001, LAG002]

The repetition evidence is direct (occurrence counts and recurrence support computed over all 200 traces in both windows), the ordering change is shown by relational and lag evidence rather than inferred from position shifts alone, and screening qualified 14 items where only about 0.27 would be expected if nothing had changed, so the set of findings is not what random re-splits of an unchanged process typically produce. These items converge on one underlying trace behaviour, so they should be read as one coherent signal rather than independent confirmations. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, REL003, REL004, REL005, LAG001, LAG002]

Limitations: the relational indicator merges "interleaved" with "repeated", so the apparent loss of fixed order could be a by-product of the repetition itself rather than a separate loosening of the ordering constraint, and the evidence cannot show the exact repetition mechanism (for example a loop back versus re-execution scattered through the case). A31 has no relational or positional evidence, so its place in the block is inferred only from its occurrence profile; positions and lags use event order, not clock time. [REL003, REL004, REL005, OCC002]

Other differences: A05 itself did not become repeated — its participation stayed around 52–55 in 100 traces and only its position moved slightly earlier — but its fixed precedence over A30 and A36 loosened in the same way as A14's, which is consistent with the repeated block now interleaving around it. [REL001, REL002, POS001]
