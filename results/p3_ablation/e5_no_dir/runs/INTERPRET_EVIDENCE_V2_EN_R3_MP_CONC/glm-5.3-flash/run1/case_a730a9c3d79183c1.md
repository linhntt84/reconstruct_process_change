### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the two groups, which in the earlier window were mutually exclusive alternatives, are in the later window executed together in almost every case: a case previously ran either {A33, A35, A36} (about 49 in 100) or {A37, A38, A39} (about 51 in 100), whereas in the later window both run in sequence — first {A33, A35, A36}, then {A37, A38, A39}, followed by A47 and A48 — with the order inside each group still mixed. Supporting signs: all nine cross-group pairs show co-occurrence rising from at most about 2 in 100 to about 98 in 100 with a now fixed between-group order; each of the six activities is observed in 197–199 of 200 later traces; the LAG distances match the new sequence; and the screening produced 99 qualifying items against roughly 1 expected if nothing had changed. The evidence cannot rule out that the case population differed between the windows in a way that produces the same participation and co-occurrence shifts, and about 1–2 in 100 later cases still resemble the earlier single-group behaviour.
