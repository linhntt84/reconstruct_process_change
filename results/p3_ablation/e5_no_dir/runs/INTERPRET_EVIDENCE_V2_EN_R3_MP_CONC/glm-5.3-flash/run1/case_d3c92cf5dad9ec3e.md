### Observed differences

- The three activities that occur in every case — A20, A22 and A23 — sat late in the earlier window (mean relative position about 0.63–0.64 of the trace) and sit early in the later window (about 0.38), a shift of about a quarter of the trace length. [POS002, POS004, POS005]
- The optional activities A19, A21 and A24 — each present in roughly 30–40 in 100 cases in both windows — shifted the opposite way, from a mean relative position of about 0.34–0.36 to about 0.66–0.68. [POS001, POS003, POS006]

Earlier, A25 preceded A20 in about 99.5 in 100 co-occurrences and A29 preceded A20 in about 99 in 100; later, A20 precedes A25 in about 99.5 in 100 and A29 in about 98.5 in 100. ### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that, in the later window, the always-executed block A20–A22–A23 is placed much earlier in the case — directly after A40 and directly before A25 — whereas in the earlier window it ran after A29, between A29 and A30; in exchange, the optional activity (whichever of A19, A21 or A24 occurs, in roughly 30–40 in 100 cases) moved from directly after A40 and directly before A25 to directly after A29 and before A30. The two groups thus exchanged sides around A25 and A29, whose own positions shifted only slightly, while A40 still precedes and A30 still follows the section, at changed distances, and no activity's participation changed appreciably. [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006, REL008, REL009, REL015, REL019, LAG002, LAG004, LAG005, LAG009]

The supporting signs are that every tested pair between the groups reverses in the same direction with near-deterministic strength (about 99 in 100 or more on 200 traces per window, each difference larger than in about 99.5 in 100 random re-splits), that stable participation rules out add/remove or frequency-shift explanations, and that 38 items qualified against roughly 0.46 expected for an unchanged process — far beyond what chance re-splits produce. [REL008, REL015, REL018, POS002, POS003, POS005]

Limitations: the evidence establishes the reversal of relative order, not how it was realised; it cannot show whether the block kept a fixed internal order (its internal arrangement is unobserved in the earlier window, where the A40-to-block pairs had support of about 1 in 200, and varies in the later window), and a competing reading in which the unobserved activities between A25 and A29 also changed, rather than a clean two-block exchange, would produce the same pairwise flips. Some lag items rest on only 1–2 observations in one window and carry weight only together with the relation items. [LAG001, LAG006, LAG007, LAG008]

Other differences: small participation shifts for the optional activities (A19 in about 30 in 100 cases earlier versus 33 in 100 later; A21 about 35 to 30; A24 about 37 to 40), which are within the sampling variation expected between two 200-trace windows. [POS001, POS003, POS006]
