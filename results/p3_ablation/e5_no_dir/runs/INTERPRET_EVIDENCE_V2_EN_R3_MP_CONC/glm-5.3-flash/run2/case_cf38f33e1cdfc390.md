### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the relative take-up of two existing alternative paths after A15 flipped: before, the block A16–A17–A18 ran in about 75 in 100 cases and the block A19–A20–A21 in about 25, with A25 present alongside in both; in the later window the shares are about 25 versus about 75. All six block activities are observed in substantial shares in both windows, no activity was added or removed, no predecessor bypasses either block, and repetition behaviour is unchanged, so this fits a shift in how often existing alternative paths are taken rather than a replacement of one path by another. The internal order within each block shows no fixed sequence in either window, so no change in ordering constraints is indicated. [OCC001, OCC004, REL001, REL004, REL009, REL022, REL024]

Supporting signs: 34 control-flow items qualified against an expectation of about 0.42 items without change, many times the chance level; every item moves by the same margin (about 50 percentage points) in a mutually consistent direction, and each difference exceeds those seen in about 995 of 1000 random re-splits of the pooled data. The relational evidence shows the flip directly in the co-occurrence and precedence shares between A15 and each trio. [OCC001, OCC004, REL001, REL004, REL009]

Limitations: the evidence cannot rule out that the two windows simply sampled two pre-existing process variants in different proportions (a 75/25 mixture becoming 25/75) rather than a change in routing; the anonymous activity codes also leave the meaning of the two blocks open. The slight shortening of the A15–A20 positional gap is consistent with the main hypothesis but is not an independent confirmation, since it rests on event positions. [LAG001]
