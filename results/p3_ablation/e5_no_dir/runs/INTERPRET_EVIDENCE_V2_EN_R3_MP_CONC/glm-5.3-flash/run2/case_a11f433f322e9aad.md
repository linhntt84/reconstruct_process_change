### Observed differences

- In the earlier window every case ran the same short sequence: A26, then A27, then A28 — A27 appeared in 100% of the 200 earlier cases, and A32, A33 and A38 never appeared at all [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, REL001].
- In the later window, A27 appears in only about 51 in 100 cases, while A32, A33 and A38 — previously unseen — each run in about 49–50 in 100 cases, once per case where present [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004].
- A27 and each of the three new activities are now alternatives: they co-occur in at most about 1 in 200 later cases, with roughly half the cases containing only A27 and the other half only the respective new activity [REL006, REL007, REL008].
- In the later window, each of A32, A33 and A38 runs after A26 (about 49 in 100 cases) and before A28 (about 49 in 100 cases); the three co-occur in about 49 in 100 cases and appear in both orders in similar shares (for example A32 before A33 in 22.5% of cases versus 26.5% the other way round), so there is no fixed order among them [REL002, REL003, REL004, REL009, REL010, REL011, REL012, REL013, REL014].
- The distance from A26 to A28 was 2 steps in about 98 in 100 earlier cases; in the later window it is 2 steps in about 50 in 100 and 4 steps in about 48 in 100, consistent with three activities now sitting between them in those cases [LAG001].
- When the new activities run, they sit around the middle of the trace (mean position about 0.49–0.50, tightly concentrated) [POS001, POS002, POS003].

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a structural change in the stretch between A26 and A28 involving A27, A32, A33 and A38. In the earlier window this stretch always contained exactly A27; in the later window about half the cases still run A26 → A27 → A28, while in the other half A27 does not run and A32, A33 and A38 execute in the same stretch — after A26 and before A28 — with no fixed order among the three. In about half of the later cases A27 is thus skipped, and exactly those cases run the three activities in its place [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, REL001, REL005, REL006, REL007, REL008, REL002, REL009, REL012, LAG001, POS001].

The pattern is consistent across participation, relational, positional and distance evidence: A27's participation fell from 100% to about 51% while the three new activities rose from 0% to about 49% each; the new activities are mutually exclusive with A27; each sits between A26 and A28; and the A26–A28 distance doubles from 2 to 4 steps in about 48 in 100 later cases, matching the replacement of one activity by three. With 22 qualifying items against roughly 0.34 expected by chance for the 68 tested units, the volume of evidence is far beyond what random re-splitting of an unchanged process produces [OCC001, OCC002, LAG001, REL006, REL002, REL009, POS001].

Limitations: the evidence shows the two behaviours are mutually exclusive within a case but cannot show how the choice arises — whether the trio substitutes for A27 within the same fragment or cases are routed into a separate branch. It also cannot rule out that A32, A33 and A38 occurred at a low, unobserved rate in the earlier window, although their presence in about half of 200 later cases versus zero of 200 earlier ones is a substantial shift either way; and the unfixed order among the three could reflect parallel execution or freely ordered steps, which the evidence does not distinguish [REL006, REL012, OCC002].

Other differences: only marginal residual signals — about 1 in 200 later cases show A27 repeated or interleaved with A26, and the A26–A28 distance is 3 or 5 steps in about 2 in 100 later cases; these are negligible alongside the main pattern [OCC001, REL001, LAG001].
