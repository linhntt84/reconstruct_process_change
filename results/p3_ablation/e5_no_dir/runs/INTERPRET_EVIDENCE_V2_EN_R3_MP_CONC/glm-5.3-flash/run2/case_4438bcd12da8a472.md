### Observed differences

- The activities A14, A15 and A20 each appeared in about 47 in 100 traces in the earlier window; in the later window each appears in about 199 of 200 traces. In both windows they occur exactly once per trace when present — no repetition in either window. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003]
- These three activities form a fixed-order block, A14 before A20 before A15, and that internal order is identical in both windows. What changed is joint presence: the block ran together in about 47 in 100 traces earlier and in about 995 in 1000 traces later, with traces containing none of the three falling from about 53 in 100 to about 1 in 200. [REL001, REL002, REL007]
- The block sits between A36 and A21 in both windows: A36 precedes it and it precedes A21. Earlier, in about 53 in 100 traces the block was skipped and A21 followed A36 directly (distance 1 step), while in about 47 in 100 the block lay between them (distance 4). In the later window the distance is 4 in about 995 in 1000 traces, with a direct A36-to-A21 step remaining in only about 1 in 200. [REL003, REL004, REL008, REL009, REL012, REL013, LAG001]
- Two small positional shifts accompany this: A21's mean relative position moved slightly later (about 0.41 to 0.43) and A36's slightly earlier (about 0.33 to 0.30), each measured on all traces in both windows. [POS001, POS002]
- A40 occurs in about 60 in 100 traces in both windows, but earlier it also occurred without the block activities (about 33 in 100 traces), whereas in the later window it never does; A37 occurs in every trace in both windows and its order relative to A14 remains mixed (A14 before A37 in about 54 in 100 later traces, interleaved or repeated in about 46 in 100). [REL006, REL005]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the block A14 → A20 → A15, executed between A36 and A21, changed from optional to executed in nearly every case: in the earlier window it ran in about 47 in 100 traces and was skipped otherwise, with A21 then directly following A36; in the later window it runs in about 995 in 1000 traces, while its internal order and its placement between A36 and A21 are unchanged. The difference is therefore in how often the block executes, not in a relocation or swap of activities. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, REL001, REL002, REL007, LAG001]

Supporting signs: the screening produced 21 qualifying items where roughly 0.28 would be expected if nothing had changed, so the pattern as a whole cannot be a sampling artefact; the participation items name exactly the three block activities with identical support (94 vs 199 traces); the relational items show the block's order and its neighbours unchanged, so the change is in execution frequency rather than placement; and the lag item measures the consequence directly, with the A36-to-A21 distance moving from mixed (1 or 4 steps) to consistently 4 steps. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, REL001, REL002, REL007, LAG001]

Limitations: the evidence cannot show whether the skip option was removed from the process or only became rare — one later-window trace still skips the block — and it cannot distinguish a change in routing from a change in the mix of cases entering the process. [LAG001, OCC003]

Other differences: the slight relative-position shifts of A21 and A36 and A40's disappearance from block-free traces are consistent with the same underlying change rather than separate differences; A37's mixed ordering relative to the block is essentially unchanged apart from the block now always being present. [POS001, POS002, REL006, REL005]
