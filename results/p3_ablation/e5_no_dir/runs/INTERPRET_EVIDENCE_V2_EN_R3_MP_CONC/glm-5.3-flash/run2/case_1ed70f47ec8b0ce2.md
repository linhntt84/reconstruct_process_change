### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that two groups of activities exchanged their order around the middle segment A20–A24. In the earlier window, cases ran the conditional activities A14, A16 and A19 — each occurring only alongside A15, in roughly 30–36 in 100 cases — before A20 and A24, with the always-executed A15, A17 and A18 after A24; in the later window, A15, A17 and A18 run first, before A20 (and after A35), while A14, A16 and A19 run after A24. A20 and A24 keep their mutual order and shift only slightly later (mean position from about 0.38 to 0.44 and from about 0.57 to 0.64). The hypothesis rests on direct evidence: complete order reversals for the six fully co-occurring pairs, matching reversals for all pairs with the conditional activities, consistent position shifts for all eight activities, and lag changes around A24–A25 and A35–A20 that fit the same movement. Participation stays within a few percentage points and no activity appears or disappears, so this reads as a reordering of existing behaviour rather than activities becoming optional or repeated, or one path replacing another. [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006, POS007, POS008, REL001, REL008, REL009, REL015, REL016, REL018, REL019, LAG001, LAG002]

Limitations: the positional indicators count event positions rather than clock time, so no conclusion about timing is possible; no pair relation was measured inside either group, so the internal sequence of each group — and whether the groups moved as one coordinated exchange or as two independent relocations — remains unresolved; and the many agreeing relation items largely reflect this one underlying difference rather than independent confirmations. A competing reading the evidence cannot fully rule out is that the middle segment A20–A24 moved across the two groups rather than the groups moving around it; the small shifts of A20 and A24 (about 0.07 of case length) against the large shifts of the two groups (about 0.26–0.33) favour the exchange reading.
