### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, A14, A15 and A20 each ran exactly once in every trace (participation 100%, mean 1.0 occurrences per trace, never repeated). In the later window, each of the three appears in only about 53 of 100 traces (mean 0.53, still never repeated). [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003]
- The three activities move as a group: pairs within the block (A14–A15, A14–A20, A15–A20) went from co-occurring in every trace to co-occurring in about 53 of 100 later traces and both being absent in about 47 of 100; neither activity of a pair is ever seen alone. When the block does run, its internal order is unchanged: A14 before A20 before A15 in every trace containing them, in both windows. [REL001, REL002, REL005]
- A36 and A21 still appear in every trace in both windows. Before, A36 always preceded the block and A21 always followed it (co-occurrence with A14, A15 and A20 was 100%). In the later window, co-occurrence drops to about 53 of 100, and in about 47 of 100 traces A21 (and likewise A36) appears without the block. [REL003, REL004, REL006, REL007, REL008, REL009]
- The distance from A36 to A21 was 4 steps in every earlier trace; in the later window it is 1 step in about 47 of 100 traces and 4 steps in the remaining ~53 — that is, when the block is skipped, A21 directly follows A36. [LAG001]
- A21's relative position shifted slightly earlier (median about 0.43 to 0.41 of the trace, with somewhat more spread); positions are event ranks, not clock time. [POS001]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the fixed sequence A14 → A20 → A15, executed between A36 and A21, became skippable: in the later window the entire block is skipped in about 47 of 100 traces, whereas in the earlier window it was executed in every trace; when executed, its internal order and its placement between A36 and A21 are unchanged. Supporting signs: the participation of all three activities drops in lockstep from 100% to about 53%; the block members are never observed without each other (pairs fall to "neither", not to single activities); A36 and A21 remain in every trace; and the A36→A21 distance collapses from 4 steps to 1 step exactly in the traces where the block is missing. The 14 qualifying items far exceed the roughly 0.22 expected by chance across the 44 tested units, so the set of differences is unlikely under an unchanged process. Limitations: the LAG indicator measures proximity within five steps rather than a directly observed successor edge, the evidence cannot show whether the skip is a per-case choice or reflects a subpopulation of later cases in which the block never runs, and the occurrence and relation items describe the same underlying traces, so they are not independent confirmations. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, REL007, REL008, REL009, LAG001]

Other differences: A21's relative position moved slightly earlier (median about 0.43 to 0.41), which is consistent with the block being skipped in some traces rather than an independent relocation; because POS uses event positions, this does not show that A21 was moved in clock time. [POS001]
