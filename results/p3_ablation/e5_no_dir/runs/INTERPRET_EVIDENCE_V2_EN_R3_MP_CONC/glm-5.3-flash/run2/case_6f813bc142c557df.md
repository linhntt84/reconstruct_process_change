### Observed differences

- The order between the two activity groups {A20, A22, A23} and {A24, A25, A26} changed from mixed to fixed. In the earlier window, A24 preceded A20 in about 75 in 100 traces and preceded A23 in about 88 in 100, while A20–A26 and A22–A24 occurred in both orders roughly half of the time each; in the later window, A20, A22 and A23 each precede A24, A25 and A26 in about 98–99 in 100 traces, and the reverse orders have almost disappeared. ### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis of a change in ordering constraints among A31, A22, A20, A23, A24, A26, A25 and A32: pairs that previously executed in mixed order and at variable distances — with A24, A25 and A26 often before the A20/A22/A23 group — now execute in one fixed sequence with constant gaps in the later window. The support is direct and consistent: all eight tested pairs flipped from mixed to near-fixed order [REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, REL007, REL008, REL009], the lag distributions of the same pairs collapsed from several distances to a single distance [LAG003, LAG004, LAG005, LAG008, LAG009, LAG010, LAG011, LAG012, LAG013, LAG018, LAG019, LAG024, LAG026, LAG027, LAG028, LAG029, LAG031], and the small position shifts of the six activities point the same way [POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006, POS007]. Screening qualified 49 items where about 0.5 would be expected if nothing had changed, so the pattern as a whole is not what random re-splits of an unchanged process produce. Limitations: positions and lags are event positions, not clock time, so nothing follows about timing; the many items largely describe one underlying restructuring rather than independent confirmations; and the exact chain order is a reconstruction from pairwise relations. A competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that the earlier mixed order reflected concurrent execution recorded in arbitrary order, so the change could lie in the degree of overlap rather than in the ordering rules themselves.

Other differences: A08 is observed in far fewer traces and later within them, with weaker relations to A32 and A14, and A10 is observed more often; both are participation changes outside the ordering hypothesis, and both rest on modest observation counts (A10 on 6 versus 25 traces, the A14–A08 gap on 16 later observations).
