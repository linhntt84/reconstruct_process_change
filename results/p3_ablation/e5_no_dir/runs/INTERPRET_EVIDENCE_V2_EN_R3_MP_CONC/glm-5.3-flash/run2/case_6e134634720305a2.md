### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, A30, A31 and A36 each ran in every trace about 3.5 times on average, with repeats in roughly 75 in 100 traces; in the later window all three still appear in every trace but only about once (mean about 1.05 occurrences per trace), and repeats have nearly disappeared (about 1–2 in 100 traces). [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003]
- The relationship between A30 and A36 changed from mixed to fixed: earlier the two were interleaved or repeated in about 76 in 100 traces (with a strict A30-before-A36 order in about 25 in 100), while in the later window A30 precedes A36 in about 98 in 100 traces and interleaving remains in only about 2 in 100; the two still occur together in every trace in both windows. [REL001]
- In the later window A36 follows A30 within about two steps in roughly 89 in 100 traces, against about 12 in 100 earlier. [LAG001]
- The reverse order (A36 before A30) and A36 repeating itself within five steps were each seen in about 150 of the 200 earlier traces but in only 1 and 2 later traces respectively; these two items rest on very few later observations. [LAG002, LAG003]
- The relative position of A30 and A36 within the trace barely moved (mean relative position about 0.42 for A30 and 0.49 for A36 in both windows), but the spread tightened (standard deviation from about 0.12 to about 0.07), consistent with a single occurrence at a stable spot instead of several. [POS001, POS002]
- For scale: 9 control-flow items qualified, against roughly 0.24 expected if nothing had changed. [screening]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the repeated execution of A30, A31 and A36 stopped: in the earlier window each of the three was executed about 3.5 times per trace in every trace, whereas in the later window each is executed once per trace, and the execution of A30 and A36 changed from interleaved repetition (about 76 in 100 traces) to a fixed sequence with A30 before A36 (about 98 in 100 traces), with A36 following A30 within about two steps in most later traces. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, REL001, LAG001]

Supporting signs: the collapse of repetition is measured directly for all three activities with the same pattern and full support in both windows (200 traces each); the ordering change is shown directly by relational and lag evidence while co-occurrence stays at 100 in 100, so this is a change in ordering constraints and repetition rather than an exchange of positions; the stable relative positions of A30 and A36 argue against a relocation; and the number of qualifying items (9) is far above the roughly 0.24 expected by chance, so the set as a whole is not a sampling artefact. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, REL001, LAG001, POS001, POS002, screening]

Limitations: the items on the reverse order and on A36 self-repetition rest on only 1–2 later observations, so they corroborate but cannot on their own establish how completely the earlier interleaved behaviour disappeared; and the evidence cannot fully rule out that the later window is dominated by a previously rare variant without repetition rather than by a modified process, although the uniform disappearance of repeats across all 200 later traces makes pure sampling variation unlikely. [LAG002, LAG003]
