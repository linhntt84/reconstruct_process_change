### Observed differences

- The two windows show a reversal of execution order between two groups of activities. In the earlier window, the optional activities A19, A21 and A24 — each present in roughly 30–37 of 100 traces — sit early in the trace (mean relative position about 0.34–0.36), while A20, A22 and A23, present in every trace, sit late (about 0.63–0.64, roughly two-thirds in). In the later window this is inverted: A20, A22 and A23 sit at about 0.38, and A19/A21/A24 at about 0.66–0.68. [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006, POS008]

- Every one of the 21 measured activity pairs spanning the two groups reverses its order, mostly near-deterministically. A25 preceded A20 in 199 of 200 before-traces and follows it in 199 of 200 after-traces; A29 likewise switches from preceding A20/A22/A23 (about 197–198 of 200) to following them (about 197 of 200). Pairs with the optional activities flip on their joint occurrences, e.g. A21 preceded A20 in about 34 of 100 joint occurrences before, and A20 precedes A21 in about 29 of 100 after. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, REL007, REL008, REL009, REL010, REL011, REL012, REL013, REL014, REL015, REL016, REL017, REL018, REL019, REL020, REL021]

- The middle of the process is stable: A25 still runs before A29, both appear in all 200 traces in both windows, and their mean relative positions move later by only about 0.07 (A25 from 0.38 to 0.45; A29 from 0.57 to 0.64), consistent with three activities now preceding A25. Participation barely moves: A19 appears in 60 of 200 traces before and 66 after, A21 in 70 then 59, A24 in 73 then 79. [POS001, POS003, POS006, POS007, POS008]

- Adjacency and gap evidence matches the reversal. Before, A21 and A24 each ran immediately before A25 (lag 1 in about 69 of 70 and 71 of 71 joint occurrences); after, A29 immediately precedes A21 in 58 of 59 joint occurrences, and the ordered pair A21→A25 within five steps survives in only 1 trace. A40 is followed within five steps by A20, A22 or A23 in only 1 of 200 before-traces but in all 200 after-traces (at lags 1–3), while A40→A25 moves from lag 2 (about 95 in 100) to lag 4 (about 92 in 100). The gap from A29 to A30 shrinks from 4 steps (about 93 in 100) to 2 steps (about 99 in 100). [LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006, LAG007, LAG008, LAG009]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a specific reordering: the always-executed activities A20, A22 and A23 and the optional activity (one of A19, A21, A24) exchanged places around an unchanged middle. In the earlier window the sequence ran roughly [A19|A21|A24] → A25 → … → A29 → A20/A22/A23 (with A30 four steps after A29); in the later window it runs A40 → A20/A22/A23 (within 1–3 steps) → A25 → … → A29 → the optional activity (immediately after A29, shown directly for A21) → A30 (two steps after A29). Which activities run, and how often, is essentially unchanged, so this is a change in ordering constraints, not in participation. [POS001-POS008, REL001-REL021, LAG001-LAG009]

The judgement rests on directness and consistency rather than item count: every spanning pair reverses with near-deterministic shares, the position shifts are large (about 0.25–0.33 of trace length) and unimodal in both windows, and the lag anchors around A40, A29 and A30 move exactly as the reordering predicts. Each item's difference exceeds about 995 of 1,000 random re-splits of the pooled traces, and 38 items qualified where fewer than one (0.46 for 93 tested units) would be expected by chance, so the ensemble cannot be explained as sampling noise even though the windows are finite samples. [screening, REL008, POS002, LAG005]

Limitations: the internal order within each group is not resolved — in the later window the trio's members appear 1–3 steps after A40 in varying proportions, and no qualifying item covers their mutual order; a few lag items rest on only 1–2 observations in one window (the informative part is the support count, e.g. 1 vs 200 traces). The evidence also cannot exclude that the reordering was achieved by moving a larger segment containing unflagged activities (for example those between A25 and A29), and a change in typical trace length alone cannot explain the pairwise order reversals but could contribute to the position shifts. [LAG002, LAG004, LAG006, LAG007, LAG008]

Other differences: none beyond the small shifts in how often the optional activities appear (A19 +6, A21 −11, A24 +6 traces out of 200), which two finite windows of this size typically show without any change. [POS001, POS003, POS006]
