### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, the activities A31, A22, A20, A23, A24, A26, A25 and A32 executed as one fixed sequence in that order: checked adjacent pairs were exactly one step apart in essentially every trace (A31→A22, A22→A20, A20→A23, A24→A26, A26→A25, A25→A32), and more distant pairs sat at the corresponding fixed distance (A20 four steps before A25, A31 four steps before A24, A20 five steps before A32). [LAG001, LAG006, LAG017, LAG020, LAG022, LAG025, LAG003, LAG027, LAG005]
- In the later window, the order between the first four and the last four of these activities is no longer fixed: A24 now precedes A20 in about 73 in 100 traces (previously essentially never) and precedes A23 in about 84 in 100 (previously essentially never); A26 precedes A23 in about 68 in 100; A20–A26 and A23–A25 are now near even splits (about 49/51 and 48/52). [REL001, REL003, REL006, REL007, REL008]
- Reverse-order executions that were previously almost absent became common: A24 before A20 within five steps occurs in 148 of 200 after-traces versus 1 of 200 before, A24 before A22 in 94 versus 1, A26 before A20 in 101 versus 1, and A25 before A20 in 64 versus 1; conversely, the previously universal forward pair A20 before A24 now occurs in only 52 of 200 after-traces. [LAG014, LAG015, LAG019, LAG021, LAG002]
- Step distances that were constant became variable: A20–A23 was always adjacent and is now adjacent in about 68 in 100 traces and 2–4 steps apart otherwise; A22–A25 was always five steps apart and is now spread over one to five; A31–A24 was always four steps apart and is now adjacent in about 47 in 100; A23–A32 was four steps apart and is now adjacent in about 49 in 100. [LAG001, LAG009, LAG027, LAG013]
- Relative positions moved consistently with this: A20, A22 and A23 now occur later within the trace (mean relative position about 0.59 to 0.63, 0.56 to 0.59 and 0.62 to 0.68), while A24, A25 and A26 occur earlier (about 0.65 to 0.59, 0.70 to 0.68 and 0.67 to 0.64). [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006]
- Participation did not change: every one of these activities appears in all 200 traces in both windows, each pair still co-occurs everywhere, and the internal orders A31→A22→A20→A23 and A24→A26→A25→A32 still hold in essentially every after-trace. [REL001, LAG001, LAG006, LAG016, LAG017, LAG020, LAG022, LAG025]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the ordering constraint between the two activity groups A31–A22–A20–A23 and A24–A26–A25–A32 was relaxed in the later window: a block that previously always executed as A31→A22→A20→A23→A24→A26→A25→A32 now executes in varying, partly reversed orders — for example, A24 now precedes A20 in about 73 in 100 traces and A23 in about 84 in 100, where previously the reverse order held in about 98–99 in 100 — while each group keeps its internal sequence and no activity is skipped, added or repeated differently. The change is shown directly by relational and distance evidence rather than by a single indicator: 42 control-flow items qualified against roughly 0.42 expected for an unchanged process, every item's difference exceeds about 995 of 1,000 random re-splits, the pairwise order shares, the newly appearing reverse pairs and the position shifts all describe the same reversal, and unchanged co-occurrence rules out a participation explanation. [REL001, REL006, LAG014, LAG002, POS003, POS004]

The evidence cannot show whether the activities of the two groups now overlap in time or still run sequentially in a per-case variable order, because positions and distances use event order rather than clock time; nor can it distinguish free interleaving from a small set of fixed alternative sequences. [LAG001, REL003]

Other differences: none beyond this ordering change — occurrence and co-occurrence of all involved activities were complete in both windows. [POS001, POS006, REL001, REL008]
