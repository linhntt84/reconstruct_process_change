### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, the six activities A15, A17, A18, A19, A20 and A21 — which co-occur in every trace in both windows — were executed in varying orders: A15 came before A19 in only about 26 in 100 traces, A18 before A19 in about 12 in 100, A17 before A19 in about 50 in 100, and A18 before A20 in about 47 in 100. In the later window, each of these pairs runs in one direction in virtually every trace (about 995–1000 in 1000). [REL001, REL004, REL006, REL007, REL003]
- The remaining cross pairs show the same move to a fixed direction: A15 before A20 rose from about 69 in 100 traces to all traces, A15 before A21 from about 50 in 100 to all, A17 before A21 from about 78 in 100 to all, and A18 before A21 from about 31 in 100 to about 995 in 1000. Co-occurrence stays at 100 in 100 for every pair in both windows, so participation did not change — only the order did. [REL002, REL003, REL005, REL008]
- Spacings became fixed. Before, gaps were spread over one to five positions (for example A15 was immediately before A18 in about 62 in 100 traces and 2–4 positions away otherwise; A15 reached A20 at 1–4 positions). After, each pair has one dominant spacing: A17→A15 and A15→A18 adjacent in about 995 in 1000 traces, A15→A19 at two positions in all observed traces, A15→A20 at four positions in about 995 in 1000, and A19→A21, A21→A20 and A20→A27 adjacent. [LAG001, LAG003, LAG006, LAG002, LAG017, LAG022, LAG020]
- Orders that were common before have practically disappeared: the pairs A19→A15 (observed in about 149 of 200 traces before), A19→A18 (176), A20→A18 (106), A21→A18 (139) and A26→A20 (106) each appear in only about 1 trace in the later window. [LAG014, LAG015, LAG019, LAG021, LAG028]
- Relative positions shifted accordingly: A15, A17 and A18 now sit slightly earlier in the trace (mean relative position about 0.64→0.59, 0.59→0.56 and 0.68→0.62), while A19, A20 and A21 sit slightly later (about 0.59→0.65, 0.68→0.71 and 0.64→0.68); each activity appears in all 200 traces of both windows. [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006]
- Taken together, the later-window distances fit a single fixed sequence A26 → A17 → A15 → A18 → A19 → A21 → A20 → A27, with consecutive activities one step apart in nearly every trace (A26→A17, A17→A15, A15→A18, A19→A21, A21→A20 and A20→A27 all adjacent in about 995–1000 in 1000 traces). [LAG025, LAG006, LAG001, LAG017, LAG022, LAG020]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the ordering constraints among A15, A17, A18, A19, A20 and A21 changed: in the earlier window these six activities, always present together in each trace, were executed in an order that varied from case to case, while in the later window they follow one fixed sequence — A26, A17, A15, A18, A19, A21, A20, A27 — with fixed one-step spacings, in essentially every trace. [REL001, REL006, LAG001, LAG006, LAG014]

The support is direct and consistent: the relational items show order shares moving from mixed (for example about 26 in 100 versus 74 in 100) to one-directional in about 995–1000 in 1000 traces while co-occurrence remains complete, the lag items show gaps collapsing to single values and previously common reverse orders dropping to about 1 trace in 200, and the position items shift in the same direction for both groups. The screening comparison also rules out chance as an explanation of the ensemble: 43 control-flow items qualified against roughly 0.33 expected if nothing had changed. [REL001, REL006, LAG001, LAG014, POS001, POS004]

Limitations: the evidence cannot distinguish whether the earlier variability was a per-case variable order or a mixture of several coexisting orderings whose use has collapsed onto one dominant sequence; some before-window distance profiles rest on few traces (for example A15→A19 in only about 51 of 200 traces); and the position indicator reflects event positions rather than clock time, so the change concerns order, not timing. [LAG002, POS001]
