### Observed differences

- The two windows show opposite execution orders between two groups of activities. In the earlier window, A15, A17 and A18 (each present in all 200 traces) ran before A14, A16 and A19 whenever they co-occurred: A15 preceded A16 in all 75 shared traces (about 37 in 100 traces overall), and A15 preceded A20 in 200 of 200 traces. ### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the execution order of two groups of activities reversed: the group A15, A17, A18, which previously ran before A14, A16 and A19, now runs after them, with A20 and A24 remaining between the two groups in both windows. Supporting signs: all 21 measured cross-group pairs reverse completely, with a single order in each window (for example A15 before A20 in 100 of 100 traces before, A20 before A15 in 100 of 100 after) [REL001–REL021]; every position shift points in the same direction as the pairwise reversals [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006, POS007, POS008]; the two distance changes match the predicted shift of about two positions [LAG001, LAG002]; and 32 items qualified where roughly 0.32 would be expected if nothing had changed, far beyond what random re-splits of stable traces produce [screening]. The many relation items describe one coherent reversal rather than independent confirmations. Limitations: positions are event positions, so no statement about clock-time timing is possible, and the evidence cannot show whether the groups moved as intact blocks or how unmeasured activities such as A25 and A35 (observed only as distances) fit into the new order. A competing explanation the evidence cannot fully exclude is a changed mix of trace variants that differ in this block order; the complete absence of mixed orders within either window speaks against a simple variant-mix shift, but rare variants could hide in samples of 200 traces.

Other differences: the participation changes are not part of the ordering hypothesis — A14 occurs in about 13 more traces per 100, while A16 and A19 occur in about 4–9 fewer traces per 100; the evidence cannot tell whether these shifts affect the same cases as the reordering.
