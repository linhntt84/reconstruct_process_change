### Observed differences

- The execution order between two groups of activities reversed. In the earlier window, A21 ran before A30, A31 and A36 in 100 in 100 traces, and A22 likewise ran before all three; in the later window, A30, A31 and A36 ran before A21 (and before A22) in about 995 in 1000 traces, with only about 1 in 200 traces keeping the old order. Both groups still occurred together in every trace in both windows, so participation did not change — only the order did. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006]
- Relative positions moved in the same direction: in the later window, A21's mean position within the trace rose from about 0.53 to 0.62 and A22's from about 0.38 to 0.47, while A30, A31 and A36 each fell from about 0.59 to about 0.41. All five activities appeared in every trace in both windows. [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005]
- Step distances changed consistently with this reversal: A22 followed A20 immediately (gap of 1 step in 100 in 100 traces) in the earlier window and 4 steps later in about 995 in 1000 traces in the later window; A25 followed A21 by 4 steps before and immediately afterwards in about 995 in 1000 traces. [LAG001, LAG002]
- Several lag readings are weak: pairs such as A21–A30 and A30–A25 had 200 supporting traces in the earlier window but only 1 in the later window, so their after-window gap values rest on single traces. [LAG003, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006, LAG007, LAG008]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the order of two groups of activities was exchanged: A30, A31 and A36, which previously ran after A21 and A22, now run before them, and A25 now immediately follows A21 where it previously followed it by four steps. Nineteen control-flow items qualified where roughly 0.27 would be expected by chance in an unchanged process, so the overall volume cannot be explained by random re-splitting. The reversal itself is shown directly and consistently: all six activity pairs between the two groups flipped from 100% one order to about 99.5% the opposite order, with both activities co-occurring in every trace in both windows (200 traces per side), which rules out sampling fluctuation and changes in participation as competing explanations for these items. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, LAG001, LAG002]

The position shifts (A21 and A22 about 0.09 later in the trace, A30/A31/A36 about 0.17–0.18 earlier) and the gap changes around A20–A22 and A21–A25 agree with the same reorder. Limitations: positions and gaps are measured in event positions, not clock time, so nothing can be said about timing; the evidence does not show which other activities sit between the two groups or whether further unobserved activities moved with them; and six lag items rest on a single after-window trace each, adding little weight. A competing explanation the evidence cannot fully rule out is a re-routing that places A21 and A22 in a different surrounding structure; the unchanged 100% co-occurrence makes simple addition or removal of these activities unlikely, but the wider structure around the swap is not observed.
