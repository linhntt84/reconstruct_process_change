### Observed differences

- The relative order of two groups of activities reversed. In the earlier window, A30, A31 and A36 each ran before A21, and before A22, in all 200 traces. In the later window, A21 runs before A30, A31 and A36 in about 99 in 100 traces, and likewise A22. Both activities of every pair still occur together in every trace in both windows, so participation did not change — only the sequence. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006]
- Relative positions shifted accordingly: in the later window A21 occurs at about 53 in 100 of the trace (previously about 62) and A22 at about 38 in 100 (previously about 47), while A30, A31 and A36 each moved from about 41 in 100 to about 59 in 100. Each of these activities appears in all 200 traces of both windows. [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005]
- Two adjacency relations changed in a matching way: in the later window the gap from A20 to A22 shrank from 4 steps (all earlier traces) to 1 step (about 99 in 100 traces), while the gap from A21 to A25 grew from 1 step (all earlier traces) to 4 steps (about 99 in 100 traces). [LAG001, LAG004]
- Four further distance items (A20–A31, A20–A36, A31–A22, A36–A22) also differ between the windows, but each rests on only 2 later-window observations; in the earlier window these pairs were 1–3 steps apart in all 200 traces. [LAG002, LAG003, LAG005, LAG006]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the execution order between A21 and A22 on one side and A30, A31 and A36 on the other reversed: the three activities, which previously ran before A21 and A22 in every trace, now run after them in about 99 in 100 traces. The change is shown directly by the relational items, which flip from one fixed order to the other while co-occurrence stays at 100 in 100, and it is consistent with the positional shifts (A21 and A22 earlier, A30, A31 and A36 later) and with A20–A22 becoming consecutive while A25 now follows A21 only after four steps. Seventeen control-flow items qualified against fewer than one expected for an unchanged process, more than chance can explain, though the six relational items and the positional items most likely describe one underlying reordering rather than independent confirmations. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, LAG001, LAG004]

Limitations: the evidence shows that the order reversed but not the exact mechanism — for example, whether A30, A31 and A36 moved as one block or the intervening steps were rearranged in another way. The roughly 1 in 100 later traces that keep the old order could be residual old behaviour or a recurring alternative path, and the evidence cannot distinguish between these. [REL001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG005, LAG006]

Other differences: the four weak distance items noted above, which most plausibly reflect the same reordering (the pairs are mostly more than five steps apart in the later window) but are too thinly supported to interpret on their own. [LAG002, LAG003, LAG005, LAG006]
