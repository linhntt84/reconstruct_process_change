### Observed differences

- The three activities A19, A20 and A25 each appeared in about 47 in 100 traces in the earlier window (94–95 of 200), always together, at most once per trace; in the later window each appears in 199 of 200 traces, still once per trace (mean occurrences per trace rose from about 0.47 to about 1.0) [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003].
- Their relative order did not change — whenever the block occurred earlier, it already ran A41 → A19 → A25 → A20 → A26 (about 46–47 in 100 traces) — but in the later window this chain holds in about 96–99 in 100 traces [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL007, REL008, REL009, REL012, REL013].
- Accordingly, the direct step from A41 to A26 largely disappeared: in the earlier window A26 followed A41 immediately in about 52 in 100 traces (the block-free cases) and four steps later in about 42 in 100; in the later window immediate succession is nearly gone (about 1 in 200) and the distance is four steps in about 89 in 100 traces, five steps in about 11 in 100 [LAG001].
- A42 occurs in every trace in both windows and keeps no fixed order with the block; interleaved-or-repeated positioning with A19 appears in about 46 in 100 later traces versus about 17 in 100 earlier ones [REL005, REL010, REL014].
- A45 remains optional in both windows (about 60 in 100 traces), but in the earlier window it appeared without the block in about 33 in 100 traces, whereas in the later window it always appears alongside it, usually after A19 (about 42 in 100 traces) [REL006, REL011, REL015].
- Minor positional shifts: A26's median relative position moved slightly later (0.40 to 0.42 of trace length) and A41's slightly earlier (0.31 to 0.29), each over all 200 traces per window [POS001, POS002].

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the block A19 → A25 → A20, executed between A41 and A26, became effectively mandatory: in the earlier window these three activities were carried out together, once per case, in only about 47 in 100 cases and skipped in the rest, while in the later window each is executed exactly once in about 995 in 1000 cases, with the surrounding order (A41 before the block, the block before A26) unchanged. The participation change is measured directly for each activity with 94–95 versus 199 supporting traces, the relational items consistently show the same chain in about 96–99 in 100 later traces, and the A41-to-A26 step distance moving from mostly one step to mostly four matches three activities now always sitting between them [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, REL001, REL003, REL004, REL007, LAG001]. With 82 units tested and only about 0.41 items expected by chance if nothing had changed, the 21 qualifying control-flow items cannot be explained as chance sampling as a whole, and every item's difference exceeds those seen in about 995 of 1000 random re-splits of the pooled windows [OCC001, POS001, REL001, LAG001]. Limitations: the many pairwise relation items largely re-describe the same block-level change and are not independent confirmations [REL001, REL002, REL007, REL008, REL012], and two windows alone cannot distinguish a modification that made the block mandatory from a shift in the mix of executed process variants between the windows [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003].

Other differences:
- A42, present throughout, increasingly interleaves with the block rather than keeping a fixed order (about 17 in 100 traces earlier, about 46 in 100 later) [REL005, REL010, REL014].
- A45 stays optional (about 60 in 100 traces in both windows) but is now observed only alongside the block, whereas earlier it appeared without it in about 33 in 100 traces [REL006, REL011, REL015].
- The small relative-position shifts of A26 (later) and A41 (earlier) are consistent with the block now always sitting between them, but position is measured in event steps, not clock time [POS001, POS002].
