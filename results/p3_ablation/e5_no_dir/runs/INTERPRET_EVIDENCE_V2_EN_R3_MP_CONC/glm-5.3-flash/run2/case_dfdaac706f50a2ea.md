### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the block A36–A37–A42 became an alternative to A27 and now runs later in the order. Earlier, every case ran all eight activities: the block together (in mixed order) immediately after A25 and before A26, A28, A27 and A31, with A27 between A26/A28 and A31. In the later window, in about 53 in 100 cases A27 runs — after A25, A26 and A28, before A31 — and the block is skipped; in the remaining about 47 in 100 cases the block runs, all three activities together in mixed order, after A26 and A28 and before A31, roughly where A27 runs in the other cases, and A27 is then skipped. [REL011, REL012, REL013, REL020, REL021, REL022, REL006, REL014, REL017, REL001, REL005, REL009, REL010, POS006, POS007, POS008, POS003, OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004]

The support is direct and consistent: the alternativity is shown relationally (co-occurrence from every case down to at most about 1 in 100), the relocation by order reversals against A26 and A28 plus the position shift from about 0.40 to about 0.58, and the distance changes match it (A25→A28 from 4 steps to 1; A26→A31 from 2 steps to 2 or 4). Screening qualified 45 items where about 0.47 would be expected without a change, so the ensemble is far beyond what random re-splits of a stable process produce, and every item's difference exceeds about 995 of 1000 random re-splits; since many items describe the same trace behaviour, they corroborate one restructuring rather than acting as independent confirmations. [REL011, REL012, REL013, REL006, REL014, POS006, POS007, POS008, LAG001, LAG005]

Limitations: the evidence cannot show whether the split between "A27 runs" and "the block runs" is a single routing decision within one process or a shifting mix of two case variants; the lag profiles for the A25→block and block→A28 pairs rest on a single observation in one window and corroborate only weakly on their own; and positions are event positions, so the relocation concerns order, not clock time.
