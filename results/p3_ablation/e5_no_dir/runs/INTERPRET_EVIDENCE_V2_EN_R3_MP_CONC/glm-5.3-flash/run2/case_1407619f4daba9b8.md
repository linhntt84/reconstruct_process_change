### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the exclusive branch between A22 and the trio A31/A32/A37 was replaced by a flow in which both always execute, with the trio relocated to run between A20 and A23: in the earlier window the observed segment ran A20 → A23 → A21 → then either A22 (about 54 of 100 cases) or the trio → A26, while in the later window it runs A20 → A31/A32/A37 (in no fixed internal order) → A23 → A21 → A22 → A26. [REL005, REL006, REL009, REL010, REL011, LAG001, LAG002, POS006]

Supporting signs: the relational items show the change directly — co-occurrence of A22 with each trio member rose from zero to about 199 of 200 traces, and the pairwise orders against A21 and A23 flipped; the lag items match (A20→A23 from one to four events; A21→A26 from a two/four mix to always two); the participation changes rest on 92–200 traces per window; and 36 qualifying items against roughly 0.35 expected without change is far beyond what a stable process produces. A mere shift in how often the two existing paths are taken is ruled out, because the two blocks co-occur in the later window after being mutually exclusive earlier. [REL011, REL014, LAG001, LAG002, OCC002]

Limitations: positions and gaps are event positions, not clock times; A23's placement between the trio and A21 rests on position statistics, as no relation item directly covers A21–A23; and about 1 in 200 later traces still shows the old exclusive pattern, so the evidence cannot rule out that the later window mixes a dominant new flow with a small residue of the old one. [POS002, POS004, REL020]

Other differences: the slight opposite-direction shifts of A20, A23 and A26 and the narrowed position spread behave as side effects of the trio's new placement rather than independent changes. [POS001, POS004, POS005]
