### Observed differences

- The clearest change concerns the activity group A19, A25 and A20. In the earlier window each of the three appeared in every trace (participation 1.0, 200 of 200 traces); in the later window each appears in only about 53 in 100 traces (about 106 of 200), with no repetition when present. [OCC004, OCC005, OCC006]
- The three drop out together rather than one at a time: their pairwise co-occurrence fell from 1.0 to about 0.53, and in about 47 in 100 later traces neither activity of a pair is present (previously 0 in 100). Their mutual order (A19, then A25, then A20) is the same in both windows. [REL008, REL009, REL014]
- The group's placement is unchanged: A41 still precedes the group, and the group still precedes A26, A46 and A47, all of which still appear in every trace in both windows. [REL010, REL011, REL015, REL017, REL019, REL021]
- The skip is visible directly in the A41-to-A26 distance: before, A26 followed A41 four steps later in about 93 in 100 traces (the three group activities sitting in between); in the later window about 44 in 100 show A26 immediately after A41, while about 49 in 100 keep the four-step distance. [LAG002]
- A02 shows a separate shift: it appeared in about 29 in 100 traces before (58 of 200) and about 9 in 100 after (17 of 200); when present, it moved from the end of the trace (median position about 0.97) to mid-trace (median about 0.47), and its usual single-step distance before A47 (about 94 in 100 before) spread over two-to-three-step gaps, though only five after-window observations back that lag. [OCC001, POS001, LAG001]
- A04 and A06, previously rare (about 4 in 100 traces each), now appear in about 12–13 in 100 (24 and 25 of 200 traces); these figures rest on few observations. [OCC002, OCC003]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the sequential group A19 → A25 → A20, executed between A41 and A26, became optional: before, every trace executed the three activities in that order, while in the later window about 53 in 100 traces still do and the rest bypass the group, with A26 then following A41 directly. All three activities lost participation in lockstep (100% to about 53%), they vanish as a unit with their internal and external order intact, and the A41-to-A26 distance collapsed from a near-universal four steps to a mix of one step (about 44 in 100) and four steps (about 49 in 100) [OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, REL008, REL009, REL014, LAG002]. The volume of qualifying evidence (35 items against roughly 0.5 expected if nothing had changed) also exceeds what a stable process typically produces. A competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that a new bypassing route was introduced and taken, rather than the existing group having become skippable; behaviourally the two look the same here, and the evidence cannot show what the skipped traces do between A41 and A26 beyond reaching A26 sooner.

Other differences: A02 became rarer (about 29 in 100 traces before vs about 9 in 100 after) and, when present, now sits mid-trace (median about 0.47) instead of near the end adjacent before A47; this rests on only 17 after-window traces (5 for the lag), so it is weaker than the main finding [OCC001, POS001, LAG001]. A04 and A06 appear somewhat more often (about 4 in 100 traces before vs about 12–13 in 100 after), based on 24 and 25 after-window traces [OCC002, OCC003].
