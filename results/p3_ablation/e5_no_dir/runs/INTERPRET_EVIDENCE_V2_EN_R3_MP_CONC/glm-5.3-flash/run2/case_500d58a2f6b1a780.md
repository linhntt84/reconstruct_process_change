### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, A25, A26 and A31 each executed about 3.4 times per case on average, and each recurred (appeared more than once in a case) in about 75 in 100 cases. In the later window, each of the three executes about once per case, with recurrence in fewer than 1 in 100 cases. All three activities still appear in every single case in both windows (participation 100% throughout, 200 cases per window). [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003]
- The relation between A25 and A31 changed from interleaved repetition to a fixed order: earlier, in about 75 in 100 cases the two were interleaved or repeated within the case (A25 strictly before A31 in only about 25 in 100); in the later window, A25 comes before A31 in about 995 in 1000 cases, with interleaving almost gone. The two activities still occur together in every case in both windows. [REL001]
- The tight succession A25 → A31 at a distance of two positions accounts for about 92 in 100 observed A25→A31 pairs within five steps in the later window, compared with about 12 in 100 earlier; gap-5 pairs (about 5 in 100 earlier) have nearly vanished. [LAG001]
- The reverse succession (A31 before A25 within five steps), observed in 150 of 200 earlier cases, is essentially absent in the later window (1 case). [LAG002]
- The positional spread of A25 and A31 within the case roughly halved (standard deviation from about 0.12 to about 0.06) while their mean positions stayed at roughly 0.41–0.48 of the case length — consistent with single execution replacing repeats, not with a shift in where the activities sit. [POS001, POS002]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the repeated execution of A25, A26 and A31 was largely removed: earlier, these three activities ran about 3–4 times per case with recurrence in about 75 in 100 cases, whereas in the later window each runs once per case while still appearing in every case; at the same time, A25 and A31 changed from interleaved repetition to a fixed sequence in which A31 follows A25 two positions later in about 92 in 100 cases. The repetition drop is observed directly for all three activities with full participation in both windows; the ordering change is shown directly by the relation and lag evidence, including the near-disappearance of the reverse succession; and eight items qualified against roughly 0.18 expected for an unchanged process, far more than chance would produce. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, REL001, LAG001, LAG002, POS001, POS002]

Limitations: the eight items largely describe one underlying behaviour (the collapse of a repeated block), so they are not independent confirmations; the evidence cannot distinguish whether the repetition was removed entirely or only strongly reduced, since about 1 in 200 later cases still shows recurrence [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003], and no relational evidence shows where A26 sits relative to A25 and A31 [OCC002]. An important competing explanation the evidence cannot fully rule out is that the loop still exists but executes in only a small residual share of cases; the consistency of the occurrence, relation and lag patterns across 200 cases per window makes pure sampling noise implausible as a whole, however.

Other differences: none beyond the above; the concentration of A25 and A31 positions in the later window is consistent with the same repetition collapse rather than a separate relocation of the activities. [POS001, POS002]
