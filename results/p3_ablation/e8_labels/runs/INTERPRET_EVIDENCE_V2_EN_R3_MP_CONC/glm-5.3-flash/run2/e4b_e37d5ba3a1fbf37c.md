### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a change in resource allocation: work on the offer and application activities was reassigned from a previously dominant set of resources to a largely different set (User_5 dropping out of the leading handlers; User_49 among the gainers), and A24, previously shared by about 13 resources of which User_30 handled about a fifth, became handled exclusively by User_30. This rests on direct allocation measurements: the resource family produced 8 evidence items where about 0.004 would be expected by chance alone (control flow produced 0 against about 0.18 expected), the gainer/loser pattern is the same in all six activities, and every difference exceeds about 1999 of 2000 reference re-splits, so chance sampling of stable allocations cannot explain the pattern as a whole. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, ASSIGN007, CONC001]

Limitations: the six assignment profiles likely reflect one underlying reallocation rather than independent confirmations, and the evidence shows who handled the work, not why — it cannot distinguish a deliberate reassignment from a change in which resources were active in the period (User_30 handling 100 in 100 of validation is also consistent with only one resource being available for that task later). Resource codes are anonymous, so no roles, people or teams can be inferred, and no control-flow items qualified, so within this evidence the change appears confined to allocation. [ASSIGN007, CONC001]
