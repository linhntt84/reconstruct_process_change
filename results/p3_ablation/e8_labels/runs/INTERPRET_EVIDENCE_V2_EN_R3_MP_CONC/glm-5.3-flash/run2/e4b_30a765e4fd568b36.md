### Observed differences

- Resource allocation changed on all four activities that have assignment data. In the earlier window, assignment work was spread very widely: the resources outside the ten most used ones ("OTHER") carried about 56 in 100 assignments of A03, about 54 in 100 of A13 and A14, and about 45 in 100 of A22. In the later window these tail shares fell to about 48, 48 and 28 in 100 respectively, so work moved toward a smaller leading group, most strongly for A22. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004]
- The leading performers themselves changed. User_67, not among the ten most used resources before, became the largest performer of A13 and A14 (about 9 in 100 assignments, from below 4 in 100) and a leader for A03 (about 7 in 100). User_79's share of A22 rose from about 5 to 13 in 100. User_65, previously a top or near-top performer on every one of these activities (for example about 8 in 100 of A22), dropped out of the leading group in the later window. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004]
- The shifts are consistent across activities: five resources gained share on all four activities and four lost share on all four (in addition to the long tail), indicating one overall reallocation rather than four unrelated shifts. Each profile difference is larger than in about 1999 of 2000 random re-splits of the pooled data, with substantial observation bases (about 198–200 observations for A03, about 270–277 for the offer activities, 119 vs 100 for A22). [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004]
- The elapsed time between consecutive completions of A22 and A03 became more variable: the median stayed at about 10 milliseconds in both windows, but the upper quartile rose from about 11 to 13 ms and the spread roughly tripled (about 2.6 to 8.1 ms); about 17% fewer such consecutive pairs were observed (119 vs 99). ### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a change in resource allocation: in the later window, the work on A03, A13, A14 and A22 was handled by a partly different set of resources — a group of previously heavy performers, including User_65, reduced its share or left the leading group, while a partly new group, including User_67 and User_79, took over a larger share — and work on A22 became more concentrated, with the share outside the ten leading resources falling from about 45 to 28 in 100. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004]

Supporting signs: the resource family produced 4 qualifying items where about 0.005 would be expected if nothing had changed, a count chance cannot plausibly produce, while control flow produced none (0 against about 0.17 expected); all four profiles moved in the same direction with the same resources gaining and losing; each difference exceeds about 1999 of 2000 random re-splits; and the observation bases are substantial. Limitations: the evidence shows that the shares changed, not why — a different mix of cases reaching these activities in the later window could produce the same redistribution, and the evidence cannot separate a change in allocation rules from a change in workload or staffing. Resource codes are anonymous, so no roles can be inferred, and the two offer-creation items have identical profiles and likely mirror the same events rather than confirming each other independently. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004]

Other differences: the single timing item shows the elapsed time between consecutive completions of A22 and A03 became more variable with a longer upper tail and fewer consecutive pairs. As the only time item (against about 0.017 expected without change), it does not by itself establish a timing change, and the evidence cannot show whether it is connected to the reallocation.
