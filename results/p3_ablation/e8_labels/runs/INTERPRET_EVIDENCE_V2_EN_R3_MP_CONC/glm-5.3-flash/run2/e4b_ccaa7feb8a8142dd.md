### Observed differences

- All qualifying evidence concerns how activities are allocated across resources, and every item shows the same turnover. In the earlier window, one stable group of resources dominated each activity — for A01, User_49 alone held about 11 in 100 assignments and the ten leading resources together about 62 in 100. In the later window, that group had largely dropped out of the leading shares, and a new group took over — User_3, absent from the leading shares before, held about 9 in 100 A01 assignments [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002].
- The same pattern repeats for A03, A13, A14, A17 and A22: the earlier leading group (including User_37) loses most of its share — e.g., User_49 falls from about 10 in 100 to effectively none for A03 — while the new group (including User_3) rises to about 9 in 100 [ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006].
- Work also became less concentrated overall: the share falling outside the ten largest resources rose from about 38 to about 48 in 100 for A01, and from about 28 to about 39 in 100 for A22 [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN006].
- The reallocation is large on the indicator's own scale (cosine dissimilarity about 0.39–0.49), and each difference exceeds about 1,995 of 2,000 random re-splits of the pooled data [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006].
- The activities themselves occurred at similar rates in both windows (e.g., A13 in 268 vs 271 traces, A22 in 136 vs 134 of 200 traces), so the difference lies in which resources executed them, not in how often [ASSIGN003, ASSIGN006].
- For context: six resource items qualified where roughly 0.004 were expected by chance; no control-flow items (0 vs about 0.16 expected) and no timing items (0 vs about 0.02 expected) qualified [screening].

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the allocation of work across resources changed between the windows: for all six activities examined (A01, A03, A13, A14, A17, A22), assignments shifted from a previously dominant group of resources — including User_49 and User_37 — to a different and broader group — including User_3 — with the earlier group's shares falling to near zero and the share outside the leading resources growing by roughly 8–11 in 100 [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006].

Supporting signs: the resources family produced six items against roughly 0.004 expected without change, far beyond what an unchanged process typically yields; all six show the same "redistributed" direction with the same incoming and outgoing resource groups; each difference is larger than about 1,995 of 2,000 random re-splits; and the support bases are substantial in both windows (200 of 200 traces for A01, about 136 vs 134 for A22) [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN006, screening].

Limitations: the six items describe one underlying reallocation rather than six independent changes (A13 and A14 have identical profiles), and because the resource codes are anonymous, the evidence cannot show whether the shift arose from changed assignment rules, changed availability of resources, or a different case mix in the later window — the last of these cannot be ruled out [ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004].

Other differences: none — no control-flow or timing evidence qualified, so no structural or timing change is evidenced [screening].
