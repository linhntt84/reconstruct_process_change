### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

A broad reallocation of work across resources is supported: in the later window, assignments for A01, A03, A13/A14, A17 and A22 moved from one set of resources to a largely different set, with User_3 and User_78 among the main gainers and a previously frequent group reduced to marginal shares, and A11 is now handled exclusively by User_100 instead of about 23 resources. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, ASSIGN007, CONC001]

The support is direct and consistent: the resource family produced 8 qualifying items where about 0.006 would be expected without a change, far beyond what chance produces; all seven assignment profiles shift in the same direction, each backed by roughly 100–280 assignments per window; and per-activity volumes stayed similar across windows (for example 273 vs 281 assignments for A13), so this is a reallocation of existing work rather than a change in how often the activities run. Note that ASSIGN003 and CONC001 describe the same A11 shift and are not independent confirmations. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, ASSIGN007, CONC001]

An important competing explanation remains: the resource codes are anonymous and the profiles only separate the ten most frequent assignees from a residual bucket, so the evidence cannot distinguish a reassignment of work from a change in which resources were active at all, and shifts inside the residual bucket (still roughly 46–49 in 100 assignments) are invisible. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, ASSIGN007]

Other differences: the mix of outcomes following A10 shifted (A07 up from about 10 to about 24 in 100 of its outgoing transitions, A16 down from about 57 to about 44 in 100). This is the single control-flow item, in a family where about 0.2 items would qualify by chance alone, so on its own it does not establish a control-flow change and it is not part of the main hypothesis.
