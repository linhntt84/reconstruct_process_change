### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a change in the resource allocation of A13: in the earlier window the activity was executed by a pool of 46 resources with no dominant one (top share under 7 in 100), while in the later window a single resource, User_134, carried every recorded assignment. The change is shown directly by the assignment profile (complete dissimilarity) and the concentration statistics (index about 0.03 to 1.0; 46 resources to 1), both stronger than every random re-split; the resource family produced 3 items against roughly 0.006 expected when nothing changes — several hundred times the chance expectation — so sampling variation cannot account for the count as a whole. Participation barely moved (about 270 vs 280 assignments) and no control-flow item qualified (0 vs about 0.19 expected), which fits a reallocation of who executes the activity rather than a structural or participation change. Limitations: the two A13 items describe the same underlying behaviour and are not independent confirmations, and the evidence cannot show whether the single code reflects a genuine concentration of work or a change in how the executing resource was recorded. [ASSIGN001, CONC001]
