### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that resource allocation changed while the control flow stayed intact. Main hypothesis: the work on A01, A03, A13, A17 and A22 was reassigned from the resource pool that dominated the earlier window to a largely different pool (User_22 and its peers losing share while User_97 and its peers gain it), and A11, previously shared across about 20 resources with User_115 handling about 15 in 100, is in the later window assigned exclusively to User_115. Supporting signs: the resource family produced 8 qualifying items against an expectation of about 0.004 without a change — a gap chance cannot explain as a whole — while the control-flow and timing families produced none; the same before-leading and after-leading resource groups recur across six activities, so the redistribution is consistent rather than isolated; every item's difference exceeded about 1,999 of 2,000 random re-splits; and the A11 shift is shown directly by two complementary indicators, the assignment profile and the concentration index. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, ASSIGN007, CONC001]

Limitations: the evidence cannot show why the pools differ — in particular, it cannot distinguish a change in assignment behaviour from a different mix of cases or a different set of available resources in the later window — and the resource codes are anonymous, so no roles or groups can be inferred.
