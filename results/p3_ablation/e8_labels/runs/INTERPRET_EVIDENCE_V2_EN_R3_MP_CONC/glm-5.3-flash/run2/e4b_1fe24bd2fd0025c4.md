### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that work was reallocated from one group of resources to a largely different group across the application-handling activities — A01, A03, A13, A14, A17 and A22. In the earlier window these activities were concentrated on a stable top group (for example User_40); in the later window a nearly disjoint set (for example User_84) carries comparable shares, a few resources such as User_3 continue with reduced shares, and slightly more of the work sits with occasionally involved resources. This rests on direct allocation measurements that all point in the same direction, each beyond about 999 in 1,000 random re-splits, and on a resource-family count (6 items) far above the no-change expectation (about 0.005). Limitations: the six items most likely describe one underlying reallocation rather than independent changes, and the evidence cannot rule out that the resource codes themselves were re-labelled — the same handlers under new anonymous codes — rather than work actually changing hands. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, screening]

Other differences: the elapsed time between consecutive completions of A05 and A09 rose slightly (median about 0.8 to about 1.1 seconds), which is a timing hypothesis in its own right; it rests on a single item with sub-second intervals, and this indicator measures elapsed time between consecutive completions, not pure waiting or processing time. No structural or routing change is supported, since no control-flow item qualified. [TWAIT001, screening]
