### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that resource allocation changed in the later window. Its sharpest facet concerns A13: an activity previously spread across roughly 45 resources, none handling more than about 8 in 100, was performed exclusively by User_3 (about 257 offers) in the later window. Alongside this, allocation shifted for A01, A03, A17 and A22: User_91's share rose for all four (most strongly for A22, from about 7 to about 19 in 100), a group of former leaders lost their top shares, and a newcomer group including User_46 took on roughly 4–7 in 100 of each activity. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, CONC001]

The screening comparison supports this reading: six resource items qualified where the resource family's own chance expectation is 0.005 — far beyond what an unchanged process typically produces — while the control-flow and timing families produced no items against expectations of 0.17 and 0.014, so the change appears confined to allocation rather than to structure or timing. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, CONC001]

Limitations: ASSIGN003 and CONC001 describe the same A13 behaviour and are not independent confirmations; the profiles show only the top contributors, so movements inside the roughly 40–46 in 100 "other" tail are not visible; and the evidence cannot rule out that the shifts stem from a changed case mix or from which resources were active in the later window rather than from a change in how work is assigned.
