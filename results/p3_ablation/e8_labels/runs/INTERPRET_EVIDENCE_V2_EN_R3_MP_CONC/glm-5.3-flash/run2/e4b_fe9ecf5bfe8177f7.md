### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

Per family, screening produced 0 control-flow items against about 0.18 expected without change, 1 time item against about 0.02 expected, and 4 resource items against about 0.005 expected. The resource count is hundreds of times its chance expectation, so chance cannot explain that family as a whole, whereas a single time item is what an unchanged process typically produces. [TWAIT001, ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004]

The evidence supports a reallocation of work across resources: the execution of A01, A03, A17 and A22 shifted from a dispersed set of many resources to a smaller set of dominant ones, with User_40, User_3 and User_49 taking the largest shares and several previously frequent resources dropping out of the top ranks, while the residual tail share fell by about 10 in 100 in every profile. Supporting signs: all four activity profiles move in the same direction with largely the same gaining and losing resources; each difference exceeds essentially all 2,000 random re-splits; activity participation stayed similar, so the shift is in which resources do the work, not in how often the activities run. Limitations: the four profiles plausibly describe one underlying reallocation rather than independent confirmations, and the anonymous codes show only that shares moved, not how assignments were made. A competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is a shift in the case mix between the windows — a different mix of traces could produce different resource shares without any change in allocation behaviour. Other differences: the elapsed time between consecutive completions of A16 and A24 was much shorter in the later window (median about 93 hours versus about 45), but as the time family's single qualifying item against about 0.02 expected without change, it does not by itself establish a timing change and is therefore not part of the main hypothesis.
