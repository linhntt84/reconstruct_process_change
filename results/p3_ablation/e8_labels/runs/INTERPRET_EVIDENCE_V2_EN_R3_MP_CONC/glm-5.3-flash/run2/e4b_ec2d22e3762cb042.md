### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the allocation of work to resources changed for A01, A07 and A22: in the earlier window each activity was handled by one set of users, and in the later window a largely different set handles it, with several users gaining share in both A01 and A22. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003]

Supporting signs: the resource family produced 3 evidence items where about 0.005 would be expected if nothing had changed, so chance cannot account for the family as a whole; each assignment-profile difference was larger than in all but about 1 in 2000 random re-splits; and the profiles rest on 200 of 200, 144 of 170 and 114 of 91 traces. The three items describe one broad reassignment rather than three independent confirmations, and the user groups involved differ between A07 and the other two activities. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003]

Limitations and competing explanation: the identifiers are anonymous codes, so no roles can be inferred, and the evidence shows who was assigned, not whether the reassignment was one coordinated change or three separate ones; the share of traces containing A07 rose (144 to 170 of 200) while that of A22 fell (114 to 91), so a changed mix of cases reaching these activities is an alternative explanation the evidence cannot rule out. [ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003]

Other differences:
- Timing: the elapsed time between consecutive completion events from A04 to A01 roughly tripled at the median (about 20 hours to about 66 hours), consistently across the quartiles. This is a candidate timing change in its own right, but it is a single item in its family (1 against about 0.02 expected without change), so the evidence cannot confirm it independently. [TWAIT001]
- Control flow: the redistribution of A12's outgoing transitions described above is consistent with sampling variation: its family produced 1 item against about 0.2 expected without change, and the profile rests on about 40 transitions per window. [DIR001]
