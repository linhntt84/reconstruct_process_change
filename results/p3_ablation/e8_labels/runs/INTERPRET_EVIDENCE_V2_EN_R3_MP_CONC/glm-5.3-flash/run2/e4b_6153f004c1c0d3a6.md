### Observed differences

- Resource allocation changed markedly. In the earlier window, A04 was spread over about 29 distinct resources, with the busiest handling about 48 in 100 of its occurrences; in the later window, a single resource performs every observed A04 (top-resource share 100 in 100, concentration index about 0.24 → 1.0) [CONC001].
- For A01, A03, A13, A14, A17 and A22, the later window is led by User_98 with roughly 13–20 in 100 of each activity's assignments; in the earlier window this code was not among the leaders, work was spread more evenly (top shares about 8–10 in 100, e.g. User_69, plus a long tail holding about 41–46 in 100 in total). The previously leading codes fall to about 5–6 in 100 or drop out of the leading group, and a largely new group of resources takes the top positions [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, ASSIGN007, ASSIGN008].
- For A08 and A11, User_133 becomes the top performer in the later window (about 17 in 100, with another newcomer second at about 9 in 100), whereas in the earlier window three resources each held about 11–13 in 100 [ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004].
- A09 occurs in about 57 in 100 traces in the earlier window and about 71 in 100 in the later window (at most once per trace in both); consistently, after A05 the share of observed outgoing transitions to A09 rises from about 57% to about 71%, while the share to A04 falls from about 44% to about 29% [OCC001, DIR001].
- After A16, the later window sends about 12 in 100 transitions to A11 (down from about 29 in 100), while A07 rises from about 31 to about 40 in 100, A10 from about 11 to about 16 in 100 and A24 from about 21 to about 23 in 100; A02 appears at under 1 in 100, based on very few cases [DIR002].
- A04 sits slightly later within traces in the later window (mean relative position about 0.13 vs about 0.15; median about 0.12 vs about 0.14); this is an event-position shift, not evidence of a move in clock time [POS001].

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that resource allocation changed in the later window: work was moved to a largely different and more concentrated set of resources. A04, previously shared by about 29 resources, is now performed by a single resource; across A01, A03, A13, A14, A17 and A22, User_98 is now the largest performer (about 13–20 in 100 of assignments) in place of a previously leading group that included User_69; and for A08 and A11, User_133 now leads with about 17 in 100 [CONC001, ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, ASSIGN007, ASSIGN008].

Supporting signs: the resource family produced 9 qualifying items where about 0.006 would be expected if nothing had changed, so chance cannot account for the family as a whole; all eight assignment profiles move in the same direction (a new group of resources gains share, the previous group loses it), the concentration item independently shows the extreme case for A04, and each item's difference exceeds about 9995 in 10000 random re-splits. The agreement is consistent but not fully independent: A08/A11 and A13/A14 yield identical profiles, so some items describe the same underlying assignments [CONC001, ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, ASSIGN007, ASSIGN008].

Limitations: the evidence shows which resources perform which activities, not why; it cannot distinguish a change in assignment rules from a change in the mix of cases reaching each activity, and the anonymous codes permit no inference about roles or teams [CONC001, ASSIGN001].

Other differences: Control flow shifted independently of the allocation pattern. A09 participates in about 71 in 100 traces in the later window (about 57 in 100 before), the split after A05 moves toward A09 and away from A04, and after A16 outcomes shift from A11 toward A07 and A10; A04 also sits slightly later within traces. These four control-flow items exceed what chance would produce (4 against about 0.2 expected), but the evidence does not connect them to the resource reallocation or establish them as one underlying routing change [OCC001, DIR001, DIR002, POS001].
