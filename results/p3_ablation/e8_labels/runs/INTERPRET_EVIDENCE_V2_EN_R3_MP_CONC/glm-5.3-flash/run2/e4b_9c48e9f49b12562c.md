### Observed differences

- In the later window, the activities A01, A13/A14, A17 and A22 were executed by a largely different set of resources than in the earlier window. Before, a group of resources each held roughly 3–11 in 100 of the assignments of these activities; afterwards most of them lost the larger part of their share — several, such as User_56 (about 10 in 100 of A22 and about 6 in 100 of offer creation), no longer appear among the ten largest shares — while resources that previously held little or none, such as User_3 and User_78, rose to about 6–12 in 100 depending on the activity. ### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the allocation of work across resources changed: the execution of A01, offer creation (A13/A14), A17 and A22 was handed over from the group of resources that previously carried shares of roughly 3–11 in 100 (including User_56) to a largely disjoint group that previously did little of this work (including User_3 and User_78). All five assignment profiles move in the same direction, and the resource family produced five evidence items where about 0.005 would be expected if nothing had changed, so chance cannot account for the set of differences; the similar observation counts and the absence of control-flow items (none, against about 0.16 expected) indicate the activities themselves continued at similar rates, pointing to a change in which resources executed them rather than in which activities occur. Two of the five items (A13 and A14) carry identical profiles and likely reflect the same events, so the hypothesis rests on four distinct activity behaviours. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005]

Limitations: the resource codes are anonymous and the ten leading resources cover only about half of the assignments in each window, so the change is characterised among the resources with the largest shares only; the evidence also cannot rule out that the redistribution follows from a changed mix of cases being routed to different resources rather than from a reallocation of the same work. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005]

Other differences: the elapsed time between consecutive A04 and A01 completions fell (median about 67 to about 28 hours). This is the only time item, against roughly 0.013 expected without change, and a single item of this kind can still arise in an unchanged process, so it does not by itself support a timing hypothesis.
