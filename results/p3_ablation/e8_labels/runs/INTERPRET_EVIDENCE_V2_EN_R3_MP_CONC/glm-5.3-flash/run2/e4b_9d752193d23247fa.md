### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a change in resource allocation: across A01, A03, A13, A14, A17 and A22, work was redistributed between resources — a group including User_49 and User_5 took a clearly larger share of assignments in the later window, while a group including User_71 lost its share or dropped out, with all six profiles characterised as redistribution rather than activities appearing or disappearing. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006]

Supporting signs: the resource family yielded 6 qualifying items against about 0.005 expected for an unchanged process, a count chance cannot explain; the same gainers and losers recur across all six activities; each difference exceeds about 999 of 1000 random re-splits; and activity participation stayed stable, pointing to allocation rather than routing or frequency changes. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN006, screening]

Limitations: about half of the assignments fall in the pooled remainder of less frequent resources in both windows, so the visible shift concerns only the most frequent codes; the six items likely reflect one underlying reassignment rather than six independent changes; and the evidence cannot rule out that a different case mix between the windows, rather than a reallocation of work, produced the different resource mix. The resource codes are anonymous, so no roles or teams can be inferred. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN003]
