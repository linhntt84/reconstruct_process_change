### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the work on the offer and application activities was reallocated between the windows: A13, A14, A17, A01, A03, A07 and A22 were performed in the later window by a largely different group of resources than in the earlier window — the earlier largest shares (about 7–9 in 100, e.g. User_79) disappeared from the top shares, a new group led by User_67 took shares of about 4–8 in 100, and A07 changed from being shared across about 29 resources to being performed solely by User_100. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, ASSIGN007, CONC001]

This hypothesis is direct and consistent: the assignment profiles measure who performed each activity and all seven move in the same direction; the concentration item shows the A07 case directly (29 distinct resources down to 1); every item's difference exceeds about 999 in 1000 random re-splits of the pooled data; and the resource family produced 8 qualifying items where roughly 0.006 were expected without change, a count chance cannot account for, while control flow produced none (0 against about 0.19), so the difference lies in who performs the work rather than in process structure. Note that ASSIGN003 and CONC001 describe the same A07 behaviour and are not independent confirmations. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, ASSIGN007, CONC001]

Limitations: the profiles aggregate everything beyond the top ten resources into a remainder of roughly half of all assignments in both windows, so the evidence cannot show whether the earlier resources stopped performing these activities entirely or only fell below the top shares, and it cannot separate a reallocation of the work from routine turnover among the resources active in each period; the codes are anonymous, so no roles or teams can be inferred. [ASSIGN001, CONC001]

Other differences: the elapsed time between consecutive completion events from A09 to A23 was typically about 17 h in the later window versus about 1.5 h earlier, with fewer observed intervals (14 versus 26). This rests on a single time item built on few observations (26 and 14 intervals), which an unchanged process can still produce at these thresholds, so it remains a tentative timing observation rather than part of the main hypothesis.
