### Observed differences

- Resource allocation across the offer and application stages changed. In the earlier window, activities such as A13, A14, A17, A01, A03 and A22 were dominated by one group of resources — for example User_19 took about 7–8 in 100 assignments of A01 and of A22 — while in the later window a largely different group dominates, with User_37 at about 9 in 100 A01 assignments and User_61 rising from about 5 to about 13 in 100 of A22; the resources most frequent earlier no longer appear among the top assignments, while the long-tail share stayed similar (about 48–58 in 100 in both windows). [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, ASSIGN007]

- Validation became much less concentrated. Earlier, A10 was handled mostly by a handful of resources — the seven most frequent covered about 83 in 100 validation assignments and the remaining tail about 9 in 100; in the later window no single resource exceeds about 7 in 100 and the tail grows to about 57 in 100. [ASSIGN003]

- The positional gap between consecutive A17 events shortened. Earlier the most common gap was 5 event positions (about 43 in 100 pairs, with a 1-position gap at about 23 in 100); in the later window a 1-position gap is most common (about 39 in 100) and the 5-position gap fell to about 20 in 100. This rests on only 46 versus 32 traces. [LAG001]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

Main hypothesis: a change in resource allocation. In the later window, the offer- and application-stage activities — A13, A14, A17, A01, A03, A22 and A10 — are performed by a largely different group of resources than earlier, and A10 in particular shifted from being concentrated on a few resources to being spread across many. The resource family flagged 7 items where about 0.005 would be expected without change — a count many times larger than chance — all seven activities move in the same direction (resources such as User_37 and User_61 gain share while the earlier frequent resources lose it), and each difference is larger than all but about 1 in 2000 random re-splits. Participation itself barely moved (A01 and A03 occur in essentially every trace in both windows; the other activities are backed by 114–286 observations per window), so the shift concerns who performs the activities, not whether they occur. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, ASSIGN007]

Limitations: the seven assignment items describe one underlying reallocation and are not independent confirmations of each other; because the resource codes are anonymous, the evidence cannot separate a change in allocation behaviour from a simple change in which resources were active in each window. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, ASSIGN007]

Other differences: the positional gap between consecutive A17 events shortened (a 1-position gap rose from about 23 to about 39 in 100 pairs). This is the single control-flow item against about 0.2 expected by chance — a result an unchanged process typically produces — and although the difference is unusual for random re-splits (exceeding about 995 in 1000), it is based on only 46 and 32 traces and does not establish a structural or placement change on its own.
