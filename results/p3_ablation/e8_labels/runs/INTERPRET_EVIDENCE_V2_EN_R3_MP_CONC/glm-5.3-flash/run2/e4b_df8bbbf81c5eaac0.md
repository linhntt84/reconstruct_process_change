### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that, in the later window, work on A01, A03 and A07 was allocated to a different and wider set of resources: previously leading resources lost share or disappeared from the leading group, resources that had not led before took over, and the combined share of the ten leading resources fell in all three activities (about 55 to 46 in 100 for A01, about 55 to 47 for A03, about 82 to 69 for A07). This rests on direct allocation evidence: the resource family produced 3 qualifying items where about 0.004 would be expected if nothing had changed, so the signal is far beyond what an unchanged process typically produces, all three items point in the same direction, and each profile difference exceeded all but about 1 in 2000 random re-splits. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003]

Limitations: the profiles distinguish only the ten leading resources from an aggregated remainder, so turnover at the edge of the leading group may overstate how completely the group was replaced; the resource codes are anonymous, so no roles or teams can be inferred; and the evidence cannot rule out that a different mix of cases reaching these activities, rather than a change in allocation itself, produced the new assignee pool. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003]

Other differences: A24 was executed less often in the later window (about 0.6 versus 0.4 occurrences per trace, about 40 versus 29 in 100 traces, repetition 0.14 versus 0.06). This is the only control-flow item, against roughly 0.16 expected by chance in that family, so it falls within the range an unchanged process produces and is not incorporated into the main hypothesis.
