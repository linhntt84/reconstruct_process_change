### Observed differences

- Work on **A22** was redistributed between resources. In the earlier window, assignments were spread widely: User_67 held about 10 in 100 assignments and a long tail of many resources held about 39 in 100. In the later window, User_67 rose to about 17 in 100, User_56 — not among the top shares before — took about 9 in 100, and the long tail shrank to about 29 in 100 [ASSIGN005].
- The same redistribution pattern appears on the other offer-handling activities. On **A01**, User_56 went from unlisted to about 8 in 100 assignments; on offer creation (**A13 / A14**), User_56 reached about 7 in 100 while User_61 fell from about 6 in 100 to outside the top shares [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004]. Across all five activity profiles, a stable small set of resources gains share and another stable set loses share, with cosine dissimilarity of about 0.30–0.38 (0 would be identical profiles) and each difference larger than all but about 1 in 2,000 random re-splits; the profiles rest on roughly 143–275 observations before and 128–249 after, and A01 appears in all 200 traces in both windows [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005].
- The elapsed time between consecutive completions on the **A09 → A23** segment lengthened across its whole distribution: median about 1 hour before versus about 14 hours in the later window, mean about 4 versus about 15 hours, on 29 versus 25 observed intervals [TWAIT001].

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that, in the later window, the allocation of work over resources changed for the offer-handling activities: on A01, A03, A13/A14 and A22, a group of resources that previously held only small shares — including User_56 — took over a substantial portion of the assignments, while several previously active resources — including User_61 — dropped to negligible shares, and User_67, already the most frequent single handler, increased further [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005].

Supporting signs: the resource family produced 5 qualifying items against about 0.005 expected if nothing had changed — far beyond what chance produces — while the time family produced 1 item against about 0.016 expected, which is within what an unchanged process occasionally produces, and no control-flow item qualified (0 against 0.18) [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, TWAIT001]. The redistribution is direct (assignment-share profiles per activity) and consistent in direction across all five activities, and the activities themselves were observed at stable, substantial rates in both windows, so the shift is not the appearance or disappearance of an activity [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005].

Limitations: the five resource items plausibly reflect one underlying redistribution rather than five independent confirmations, and a shift in the mix of cases between the windows that is too small to surface in the control-flow screening could contribute to the changed assignment profiles; the resource codes are anonymous, so no roles, people or teams can be inferred, and the strength values show the differences are unusual for random re-splits rather than the probability of a change [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005].

Other differences: the A09 → A23 completion interval lengthened (median about 1 hour to about 14 hours; mean about 4 to about 15 hours; 29 vs 25 intervals). As the single time item, against about 0.016 items expected by chance in that family, the evidence cannot separate a genuine lengthening of these elapsed times from a sampling fluctuation [TWAIT001].
