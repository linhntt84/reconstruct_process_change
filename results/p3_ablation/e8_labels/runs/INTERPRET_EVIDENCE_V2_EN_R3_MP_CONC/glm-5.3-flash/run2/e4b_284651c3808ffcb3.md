### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a change in how work was allocated to resources between the two windows. Across A01, A03, A13, A14, A17 and A22, execution shifted from one group of resources to a largely different group — the resources that previously held the largest shares (for example User_42) lost share on nearly every activity, while another set (for example User_56) took over. In addition, A16 work that was previously distributed across about 15 resources became handled exclusively by one resource (User_28). [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, ASSIGN007, CONC001]

The resource family produced 8 qualifying items where roughly 0.005 would be expected if nothing had changed — far too many to attribute to chance — every item points to redistribution, each difference exceeds virtually all random re-splits of the pooled data, and each rests on roughly 100–250 traces per window; the concentration item directly corroborates the A16 case. With no control-flow or timing items, the change is confined to allocation, which stands as a hypothesis in its own right. [screening, ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, ASSIGN007, CONC001]

Limitations: the items are not independent confirmations — A13 and A14 show identical profiles, and the two A16 items describe the same behaviour — so they likely reflect one underlying reallocation; the resource codes are anonymous, so no roles, people or teams can be inferred; and the evidence cannot fully rule out that the swap of resource groups reflects a change in how resources were recorded at assignment rather than a genuine shift in who performed the work.
