### Observed differences

- **A16 assignments became concentrated on a single resource.** In the earlier window, its roughly 150 assignments were spread across 18 distinct resources, with the most frequent one (User_123) handling about 14 in 100; in the later window, all of its roughly 165 assignments went to User_123 alone (top-resource share from about 14 in 100 to 100 in 100; concentration index from 0.10 to 1.0) [ASSIGN005, CONC001]

- **The main resource group behind the other offer activities was largely replaced.** In the earlier window, A01, A03, A13, A14 and A17 each showed a set of resources carrying roughly 3–8 in 100 assignments (for example User_77) plus a long tail covering about half of all assignments; in the later window, several of those resources no longer appear among the main group, a largely new set led by User_49 (about 7–8 in 100) took over similar shares, and the long-tail share stayed at about half (47–51 in 100). The shift runs in the same direction in all five activities, with profile dissimilarity of about 0.29–0.32 each [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN006]

- **The elapsed time between consecutive completions of A04 and A01 shortened.** In the earlier window the mean gap was about 48 hours (median about 30; 75th percentile about 80); in the later window it was about 29 hours (median about 22; 75th percentile about 47), and the spread narrowed (standard deviation from about 74 to 39 hours); every summary measure moved down together [TWAIT001]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the allocation of offer-handling work across resources changed in the later window. Most directly, A16 moved from being distributed across about 18 resources — its top resource taking about 14 in 100 assignments — to being assigned to a single resource, User_123, in essentially all cases. In addition, for A01, A03, A13, A14 and A17, the group of main resources was largely replaced: resources such as User_77 that each carried roughly 3–8 in 100 assignments earlier dropped out of the main group, while a new group led by User_49 took over similar shares. The resource family produced 7 qualifying items where about 0.006 were expected without change — a count chance cannot explain — each difference was larger than in all but about 1 in 2,000 random re-splits, and the profiles rest on roughly 150–270 observations each [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, CONC001]

Limitations: the resource codes are anonymous, so the evidence shows a shift between groups of codes, not what it represents operationally; for the multi-resource activities each resource's share is small (about 3–8 in 100), so the exact make-up of the main group could partly reflect sampling — a competing explanation the data cannot fully exclude, although the identical direction across five activities and the move from 18 resources to 1 on A16 argue against pure chance. The items also overlap: A13 and A14 show identical profiles, and the A16 assignment and concentration items describe the same assignments, so the seven items are not independent confirmations [ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, CONC001]

Other differences: the elapsed time between consecutive completions of A04 and A01 was about 19 hours lower on average in the later window (mean about 48 to 29 hours; median about 30 to 22 hours), which is consistent with a timing change but rests on a single qualifying time item (1 against about 0.013 expected without change), a count an unchanged process can still produce, so it remains outside the supported hypothesis [TWAIT001]
