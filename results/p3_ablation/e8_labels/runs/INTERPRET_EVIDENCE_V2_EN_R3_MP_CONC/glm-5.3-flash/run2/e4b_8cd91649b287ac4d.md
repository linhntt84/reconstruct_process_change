### Observed differences

- The activity "A17" was performed by a broad pool of resources in the earlier window: about 45 distinct resources appear in its assignment profile, with the largest single shares about 14 in 100 (User_67) and about 12 in 100 (User_104), and roughly 43 in 100 spread across many others. In the later window, all about 237 recorded assignments of this activity went to one single resource, User_84: the top-resource share rose from about 14 in 100 to 100 in 100, distinct resources fell from 45 to 1, and the concentration index rose from about 0.05 to 1.0. The activity occurred at a similar rate in both windows (about 245 vs 237 observations), so what changed is who performed it, not how often. [ASSIGN002, CONC001]
- Assignments of "A01" were redistributed more moderately. In the earlier window two resources dominated (about 16 and about 12–13 in 100); in the later window they kept the largest shares but at lower levels (about 8 and about 11 in 100), several resources with small earlier shares fell out of the profile, and new resources entered with shares of roughly 4–8 in 100, while the share spread over many minor resources stayed near 40 in 100 in both windows. [ASSIGN001]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that, in the later window, the activity "A17" was assigned to a single resource (User_84) in every recorded case, whereas in the earlier window it was shared across a pool of about 45 resources with no resource above about 14 in 100. The assignment profile and the concentration measure show this directly and agree with each other, each difference being larger than all but about 1 in 2000 random re-splits, and both rest on substantial samples (about 245 and 237 observations). The resource family produced three qualifying items where roughly 0.006 would be expected if nothing had changed, while the control-flow and timing families produced none, so chance sampling cannot account for the pattern and the difference is confined to resource allocation. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, CONC001]

Limitations: the two items on "A17" describe the same underlying assignments and are therefore consistent rather than independent confirmations, and the evidence cannot distinguish a genuine concentration of the work on one resource from a change in how the resource was recorded for this activity. [ASSIGN002, CONC001]

Other differences: the redistribution of "A01" assignments away from its two previous main resources (User_104 from about 16 to about 8 in 100, User_67 from about 12–13 to about 11 in 100) toward a wider set of resources, one of which is the resource that now exclusively handles "A17" (about 4 in 100); the evidence does not establish whether the two activities' changes share a common origin. [ASSIGN001]
