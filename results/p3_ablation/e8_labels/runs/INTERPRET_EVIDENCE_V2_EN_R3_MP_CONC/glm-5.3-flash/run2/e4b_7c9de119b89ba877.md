### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that resource allocation changed: in the later window, the six offer- and application-handling activities (A01, A03, A13, A14, A17, A22) were executed with a markedly different distribution of work — concentrated on User_46 (about 15–19 in 100 of each activity, up from about 4–6 in 100), with User_39 no longer observed on any of them and User_61 together with several previously unseen codes taking up the released shares. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006]

Supporting signs: the resource family produced six qualifying items where about 0.006 would be expected without any change — a count far beyond what chance produces; all six activity-level profiles moved in the same direction (one code rising sharply, one disappearing, new codes entering), which reads as one underlying reallocation rather than six unrelated shifts; each profile rests on roughly 130–270 observations per window; random re-splits almost never reach dissimilarities this large (above 99.9 in 100 reference comparisons for every item); and stable activity volumes argue against a frequency change being the cause. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, screening]

Limitations: the evidence cannot rule out that the mix of cases reaching these activities shifted — if different case types are handled by different codes, a case-mix change alone could produce these profiles without any explicit reassignment. The small shares of the newly entered codes (about 3–5 in 100 each) could individually be sampling noise, though their joint appearance alongside the rise of User_46 and the exit of User_39 is consistent. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006]

Other differences: A21 was executed in about 18 in 100 traces earlier versus about 8 in 100 later, hinting that this step is triggered less often. This rests on a single control-flow item with modest support (35 vs 15 traces), so a sampling difference alone could explain it; it is not part of the main hypothesis. [OCC001]
