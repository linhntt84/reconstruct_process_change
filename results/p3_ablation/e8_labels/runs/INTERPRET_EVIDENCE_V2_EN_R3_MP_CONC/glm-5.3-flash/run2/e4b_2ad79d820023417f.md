### Observed differences

- Resource allocation was redistributed across the main offer-handling activities. In the earlier window, work on A01, A03, A13, A14 and A17 was spread widely: the largest single resource, User_3, handled about 8–9 in 100 events of each activity, and roughly 44–49 in 100 went to a long tail of others. In the later window, User_3 no longer appears among the top handlers, User_56 is the largest at about 10–12 in 100 events, and a largely different group of resources fills the top positions; the allocation profiles of all five activities differ by a cosine dissimilarity of about 0.32–0.35, where 0 would mean identical. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN006]
- A16 changed from widely shared to single-handler. In the earlier window its events were spread across about 19 distinct resources, the largest share being about 18 in 100; in the later window all 143 A16 events were handled by one resource, User_64, and the concentration index rose from about 0.09 to 1.0. The number of A16 observations is the same in both windows (143), so this is a reallocation rather than a participation change. [ASSIGN005, CONC001]
- The elapsed time between the completion of A04 and that of A01 increased: the median gap rose from about 5.6 to about 17.6 hours and the mean from about 17.5 to about 32.8 hours, with the upper quartile rising from about 18 to about 45 hours while the lower quartile stayed near zero, so the increase sits mostly in the slower half of cases. - No control-flow difference passed the screening: 0 of 31 tested units qualified, in line with the roughly 0.15 items expected by chance in that family. [screening]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that resource allocation changed while the process structure stayed intact. In the earlier window, the activities A01, A03, A13, A14 and A17 were each handled by a broad set of resources led by User_3; in the later window the same activities were handled by a largely different set of resources led by User_56, with most earlier top handlers contributing little or nothing. In the same direction, A16 moved from being shared across about 19 resources to being handled exclusively by User_64. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, CONC001]

Supporting signs: seven resource items qualified where about 0.005 would be expected if nothing had changed, so chance cannot account for that family as a whole; the redistribution runs in the same direction across five activities spanning the offer lifecycle; every item's difference exceeds all but about 1 in 2000 random re-splits of the pooled data; and no control-flow item qualified, so no structural counterpart is involved. [screening, ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN006, CONC001]

Limitations: the evidence cannot rule out that the two windows contain different mixes of cases that happen to be handled by different resources, rather than a reallocation of the same workload; ASSIGN005 and CONC001 describe the same A16 behaviour and are therefore not independent confirmations; and the anonymous resource codes allow no inference about roles or teams. [ASSIGN005, CONC001]

Other differences: the elapsed time between the completion of A04 and A01 increased (median from about 6 to about 18 hours), which would be a timing change in its own right; it rests on a single time item (1 item versus about 0.01 expected by chance in the time family), so the evidence cannot confirm it independently of sampling variation. [TWAIT001, screening]
