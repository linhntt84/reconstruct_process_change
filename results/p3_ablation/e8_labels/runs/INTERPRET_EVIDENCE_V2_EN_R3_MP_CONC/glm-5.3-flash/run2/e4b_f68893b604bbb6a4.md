### Observed differences

- Across the offer and application handling activities (A01, A03, A13/A14, A17, A22), the resources doing the work changed: in the earlier window User_37 and User_39 were the largest named contributors to every one of these activities (roughly 10–12% and 6.5–9% of each activity's assignments), while in the later window neither appears among the contributors at all [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, ASSIGN007].
- In the later window these activities are led by a largely different set of resources, none of which was a main contributor before, each handling roughly 5–9% of assignments (the largest shares, about 9%, occur for A22); the long tail of many small contributors ("other") stays at about half of all assignments in both windows (about 44–55% before, 41–53% after) [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, ASSIGN007].
- A11 shows the sharpest shift: in the earlier window its assignments were spread over about 18 resources, with the largest (User_115) at about 18 in 100; in the later window User_115 alone carries all of them (top share 100%, distinct resources 1, concentration index about 0.10 rising to 1.0) [ASSIGN003, CONC001].
- The activities themselves occurred at similar rates in both windows — the profiles rest on roughly 100–270 observations per window (for example A11: 107 before vs 101 after) — so the difference concerns who performs the work, not how often the activities run [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN007, CONC001].

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis of a change in resource allocation. In the earlier window, a group of resources led by User_37 and User_39 handled a visible share of A01, A03, A13/A14, A17 and A22, on top of a diffuse tail of many small contributors; in the later window that leading group no longer performs these activities and a largely disjoint set of resources has taken over, while A11 — previously shared across about 18 resources — is now assigned exclusively to User_115 [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, ASSIGN007, CONC001].

This qualifies because the resource family produced 8 qualifying items where about 0.005 would be expected if nothing had changed — far beyond what an unchanged process typically yields — while the control-flow and time families produced no items (0 of 35 and 0 of 37 units), so the flagged differences are confined to allocation [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, ASSIGN007, CONC001]. All items point in the same direction, each difference is larger than in about 999.5 of 1000 random re-splits of the pooled data, and the profiles rest on substantial samples in both windows [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN007].

Limitations: the eight items cover only about six distinct behaviours (A13 and A14 have identical profiles, and CONC001 restates the A11 assignment shift), so they are not independent confirmations [ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, CONC001]. The evidence also cannot distinguish a deliberate reassignment of work within a stable resource pool from a change in which resources were active at all (for example, some resources ceasing and others replacing them), since the codes are anonymous and no role or team information can be inferred; the persistence of a roughly 50% "other" tail in both windows means only the top contributors were clearly swapped [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN004, CONC001].
