### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that work on the main offer and application activities was reallocated between resources. In the earlier window, one stable group of resources dominated the assignment of A13/A14, A01, A03 and A22 events; in the later window, that group's share fell in all five profiles while another group — including the two clearest gainers named above — took over a larger share in each activity. The change is measured directly (the indicator is the resource mix per activity), is consistent in direction across all five profiles, and is unlikely as a whole by chance: five resource items against roughly 0.005 expected without change, each exceeding about 1999 of 2000 random re-splits, on about 96–259 observations per activity and window. Overall, nine items qualified — five resource (against about 0.005 expected) and four timing (against about 0.015 expected) — while no control-flow item qualified (0 against about 0.16 expected), so no structural, participation or ordering difference was detected. The resource codes are also anonymous, so the evidence shows shares moving between codes, not whether resources were replaced or work was reassigned.
