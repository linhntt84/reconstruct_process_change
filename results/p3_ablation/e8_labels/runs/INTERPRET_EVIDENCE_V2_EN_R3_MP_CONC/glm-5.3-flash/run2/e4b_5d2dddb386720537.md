### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

One possibility supported by the evidence is that, in the later window, the offer-creation step is repeated less often: A13 and A14 still appear in every trace, so they did not become optional, but traces with repeated occurrences dropped from about 33 in 100 to about 19 in 100 and mean occurrences per trace fell from 1.38 to 1.22. Supporting signs are direct occurrence measurements of the activities themselves on full samples, a difference larger than in about 995 of 1,000 random re-splits, and a control-flow screening yield of 3 items where about 0.21 would be expected without change (the remaining families produced no items against negligible expectations). [OCC001, OCC002]

An important competing explanation cannot be ruled out: the two occurrence items mirror the same traces (the activities co-occur with identical statistics), so the repetition signal rests on one underlying behaviour, and a shift in the mix of cases between the windows — rather than a change in the process — could produce the same pattern. [OCC001, OCC002]

Other differences: The spacing between A17 and A10 shifted upward, from a typical gap of 2 event positions to a typical gap of 3, with 1-position gaps falling from about 9 to about 3 in 100 observed pairs while the pair itself remained about as common (about 72 in 100 traces in both windows). This relational change is observed directly, but it does not show that an activity was moved or that a step was inserted between the two, and the evidence does not connect it to the reduced repetition of offer creation.
