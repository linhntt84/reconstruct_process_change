### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the allocation of work to resources changed. In the earlier window, the offer-handling activities A01, A03, A13, A14 and A17 were each handled by a broad set of resources with leading shares of about 7–8%; in the later window, a largely different group of resources, led by User_49, took over the leading shares on every one of these activities, and A16, previously spread over 18 resources with a top share of about 14%, was handled exclusively by User_123. Supporting signs: the resource family produced 7 qualifying items where about 0.006 were expected without change (8 items in all, against about 0.2 overall) — a family-wide count chance cannot plausibly account for, even if all items describe one underlying difference; every item's difference exceeded all but about 1 in 2000 random re-splits; the direction is consistent, with the same incoming group rising and the same previous group receding on each activity; and the profiles rest on about 150–270 observations per window. The change appears confined to allocation: no control-flow item qualified (0 against about 0.18 expected), so no structural difference was detected alongside it. Limitations: the five assignment profiles describe one underlying shift of work seen on several activities and are therefore not independent confirmations, and the individual share shifts are small (mostly about 3–8 percentage points), so the consistent pattern rather than any single shift carries the hypothesis. A competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that the pool of resources handling these activities changed as a whole rather than work being deliberately reassigned; the anonymous codes do not distinguish the two. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, CONC001]

Other differences: the elapsed time between the completions of A04 and A01 decreased by about 19 hours on average (mean about 48 to about 29 hours; median about 30 to about 22; upper quartile about 80 to about 47), with the spread roughly halving, consistently across all quantiles and on 200 traces per window. This is the only time item, where about 0.013 were expected without change, so on its own it stays within what an unchanged process can produce; it is consistent with a timing change but is not corroborated by any other evidence.
