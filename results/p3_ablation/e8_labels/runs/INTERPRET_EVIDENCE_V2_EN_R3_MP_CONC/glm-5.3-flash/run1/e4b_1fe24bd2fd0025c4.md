### Observed differences

- **Work was reassigned to a largely different set of resources on all six flagged activities.** In the earlier window, one stable group of resources dominated every stage of the flow: on A01, for example, User_40 and User_42 each carried about 8 in 100 assignments, and the same resources led A13, A14, A17, A03 and A22 with roughly 7–9 in 100 each. In the later window these resources have left the leading positions and a largely disjoint group has taken over — User_84, for instance, accounts for about 8 in 100 of A01 assignments and about 11 in 100 of A22 assignments, while not appearing among the leading resources in any earlier profile. On five of the six activities the share spread over many minor resources ("OTHER") also grew, from roughly 41–47 in 100 to about 50–54 in 100, so allocation became somewhat more dispersed. The per-activity distributional distance is 0.41–0.48 (cosine dissimilarity), each difference rarer than 999.5 in 1000 random re-splits; the profiles rest on roughly 100–275 observations per window, the smallest (A22) on 96 before and 123 after. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006]

- **The elapsed time between two consecutive completions lengthened slightly.** Between the completion of A05 and that of A09, the mean elapsed time rose from about 0.00022 to 0.00029 hours (roughly 0.8 to 1.0 seconds) and the median from about 0.00021 to 0.00031 hours, with quartiles and spread rising in step; 120 intervals were observed before and 132 after, and the difference exceeds 999.5 in 1000 random re-splits. [TWAIT001]

- **No structural differences qualified.** Screening of 34 control-flow units produced no qualifying items (about 0.17 expected by chance if nothing had changed), so participation, ordering and routing show no differences at the configured thresholds; the qualifying evidence consists of the six resource items and the one time item above. ### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that resource allocation changed across the flow: in the later window, the activities A01, A03, A13, A14, A17 and A22 are executed by a largely different set of resources than before, with work also spread more thinly over many minor resources. The earlier leading resources — including User_40 and User_42, which each handled roughly 7–9 in 100 of these activities — no longer hold leading shares, while resources such as User_84 (about 8 in 100 on most of these activities, about 11 in 100 on A22) now do. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006]

Supporting signs: all six assignment profiles moved in the same direction (earlier leaders out, a new group in, the "OTHER" share up on five of six), each with a distributional distance of 0.41–0.48 and each rarer than 999.5 in 1000 random re-splits; the resource family produced six qualifying items against about 0.005 expected if nothing had changed, a count chance cannot plausibly produce as a whole; and control flow produced no items, so the difference is confined to allocation rather than to which activities run or in what order. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006]

Limitations: the resource codes are anonymous, so the evidence shows that different resources now execute these activities but cannot separate a change in assignment behaviour from a difference in which resources were active in each window — an important competing explanation it cannot rule out; the six profiles also describe overlapping trace behaviour and are not fully independent confirmations. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006]

Other differences: the single time item shows a slightly longer elapsed time between the completions of A05 and A09 in the later window (mean up by about 0.00007 hours, roughly a quarter of a second, with intervals well under a second in both windows). As the only time item, against about 0.017 expected by chance in that family, it is what an unchanged process typically produces and does not by itself establish a timing change. [TWAIT001]
