### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a change in resource allocation: for A01, A03, A13, A14, A17 and A22, assignment shares moved from one group of resources that previously each handled roughly 5–11 in 100 assignments to a largely different group that now handles roughly 4–12 in 100 each, while the long tail of occasional resources stayed about the same. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006]

The screening context makes chance an unlikely explanation: six resource items qualified where about 0.004 would be expected without a change — a count many times the chance expectation — while no control-flow or timing item qualified (0 against roughly 0.2 expected), so the difference is specific to allocation. The six items agree in direction and in which shares rose and fell, and each difference is larger than in about 1995 of 2000 random re-splits. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006]

Limitations: the resource codes are anonymous, so the evidence cannot show whether the declining resources stopped performing these activities altogether or only reduced their share, nor whether the rising resources are new or were previously minor contributors; it shows redistributed shares, not a change in how concentrated each activity is on few resources. A competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that the two sampled periods simply contained different sets of active resources, rather than a reallocation of the same work.
