### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports one specific possibility: a change in resource allocation involving A03, A13, A14 and A22. In the earlier window each activity's work was distributed across a large pool of resources with no dominant handlers; in the later window it concentrated on fewer resources — User_67 and User_79 became leading handlers on all four activities (up to about 9–13 in 100 of an activity's assignments) while a set of previously frequent handlers, including User_65, fell to negligible shares. The resource family produced 4 qualifying items against roughly 0.005 expected if nothing had changed, each difference exceeds about 999 in 1000 random re-splits, and all four point in the same direction, so chance sampling cannot account for the pattern as a whole. No control-flow items qualified (0 against about 0.17 expected), so no structural or ordering difference accompanies the reallocation at these thresholds. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004]

Competing explanations the evidence cannot rule out: a change in which resources were available in each window (new codes appearing, others disappearing) would look the same as a change in assignment behaviour, and a shift in the case mix could also produce allocation-like differences; the codes are anonymous, so nothing about roles can be inferred. The A13 and A14 profiles are identical and likely reflect the same underlying events, so they are not independent confirmations. [ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003]

Other differences: the elapsed time between consecutive completions of A22 and A03 became more variable, with the median unchanged, the mean about 30% higher and a longer upper tail (75th percentile up about 18%), and about 20 fewer such consecutive pairs (119 vs 99). This is the only qualifying time item (against about 0.017 expected without change), it covers a single activity pair, and the evidence cannot show whether it is connected to the reallocation.
