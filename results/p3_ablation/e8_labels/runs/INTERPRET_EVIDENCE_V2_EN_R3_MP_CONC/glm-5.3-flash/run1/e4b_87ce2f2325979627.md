### Observed differences

- Resource allocation changed markedly for four activities. In the earlier window, assignments of A01, A03, A17 and A22 were spread thinly: the ten most frequent resources each held roughly 3–9 in 100 assignments, and all remaining resources together about 47–55 in 100. In the later window, work is more concentrated on a different set of resources: the unnamed remainder falls by about 10–13 points in 100 (for example from 54 to 43 in 100 for A01 and from 47 to 35 in 100 for A22), User_3 — not among the ten most frequent resources before — becomes the most frequent with about 11–13 in 100 assignments, and User_39 rises to about 7–9 in 100, while previously visible resources such as User_37 (about 5–7 in 100 before) drop to minor or unlisted shares. All four activities show the same direction of redistribution, and each difference is larger than in about 999.5 of 1,000 random re-splits of the pooled data. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004]

- Offer creation repeats less often. A13 and A14 still appear in every trace in both windows, but their mean occurrences per trace fall from 1.48 to about 1.26 (roughly a 15% relative decrease), and the share of traces in which the activity recurs falls from about 34 in 100 to about 20 in 100. The two items have identical profiles and describe the same paired behaviour, so they represent one observation rather than two independent confirmations. [OCC001, OCC002]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility of a change in resource allocation: the handling of A01, A03, A17 and A22 was reassigned from one broad group of resources to a smaller, different group. In the earlier window assignments were spread across many resources; in the later window a new group, led by User_3 and User_39, carries the largest shares, resources that previously held visible shares such as User_37 recede, and the unnamed remainder shrinks by about 10–13 points in 100. This rests on four assignment items where the resource family expected about 0.006 without change — a count many times the chance expectation — with all four showing the same redistribution direction and each difference exceeding about 999.5 in 1,000 random re-splits. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004]

Limitations: the resource codes are anonymous, so no roles, people or teams can be inferred, and the evidence shows that the assignment distribution changed but not why. It cannot rule out that an upstream shift in the mix of cases, or in which resources were active in each period, produced the same pattern without any change in allocation behaviour; the four activities may also share a single assignment mechanism, so the items likely reflect one underlying allocation change rather than four independent ones. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004]

Other differences: A13 and A14 repeat less often per trace (mean occurrences from 1.48 to about 1.26; recurrence in about 34 in 100 traces down to about 20 in 100) while participation stays at 100 in 100 in both windows. The control-flow family produced 2 items against about 0.21 expected without change — within the range an unchanged process typically produces — so this repetition decrease does not, on its own, rise above the chance threshold and is kept out of the main hypothesis.
