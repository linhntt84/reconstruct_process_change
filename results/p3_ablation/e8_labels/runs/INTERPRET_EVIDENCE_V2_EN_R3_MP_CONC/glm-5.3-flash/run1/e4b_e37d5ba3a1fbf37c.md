### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that resource allocation changed: in the later window, execution of A01, A03, A13, A14, A17 and A22 shifted from a previously dominant group of resources, led by User_5, to a largely different group led by User_49, and A24, previously distributed across 13 resources with User_30 at about 19 in 100, was carried out entirely by User_30. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, ASSIGN007, CONC001]

Supporting signs: the resource family produced 8 qualifying items where about 0.004 would be expected if nothing had changed — thousands of times the chance expectation — while the control-flow and time families produced none (0 items against 0.18 and 0.017 expected), so the differences are confined to resource allocation; every item points in the same direction (former leaders out, new leaders in, validation fully concentrated); and the underlying samples are substantial rather than a handful of traces. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, ASSIGN007, CONC001]

Limitations: the eight items are not independent — the six activity profiles likely reflect one common reallocation, and the validation concentration may be part of the same shift, so the item count should not be read as eight separate confirmations. The evidence also cannot distinguish a deliberate change in assignment from a simple turnover in which resources were active, since both would produce these profiles, and the aggregated "other resources" bucket hides any partial overlap between the old and new groups.
