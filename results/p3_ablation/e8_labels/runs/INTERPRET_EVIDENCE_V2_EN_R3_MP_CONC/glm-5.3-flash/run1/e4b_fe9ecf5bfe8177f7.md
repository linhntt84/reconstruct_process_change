### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a change in how work on the application-handling activities was allocated across resources: in the earlier window, A01, A03, A17 and A22 were each executed by a diffuse set of resources in which occasional contributors held about half of the work, whereas in the later window execution concentrated on a smaller, partly different group — resources such as User_40 and User_3 moved from the margins to roughly 8–13 in 100 assignments each, while a group that previously held prominent shares, including User_37 at about 6–7 in 100, fell below the leading group in every activity. The pattern is direct (the assignment shares themselves are what changed) and consistent, with the same resources gaining and the same losing across all four activities; each difference is larger than in all but about 1 of 2,000 random re-splits, and the resource family produced 4 qualifying items against roughly 0.005 expected for an unchanged process, so chance cannot account for the set as a whole. No control-flow item qualified, so no accompanying structural change is indicated. The evidence cannot distinguish a real reallocation of work from a change in the mix of cases or in which resources were active, and the anonymous codes allow no inference about roles or teams.
