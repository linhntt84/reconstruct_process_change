### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a change in **resource allocation** as the main difference between the windows. In the later window, A11 is performed exclusively by User_115, whereas earlier it was shared by about twenty resources with User_115 the most frequent at about 15 in 100; at the same time, the offer-handling activities A01, A03, A13, A14, A17 and A22 passed from one group of frequent performers (for example User_22) to a largely different group (for example User_97), with the unlabelled remainder rising from about 43–45 to about 47–54 in 100 of assignments. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, ASSIGN007, CONC001]

The support is direct and consistent: the allocation indicators themselves show the change; the A11 result appears in two complementary views (assignment profile and concentration index, 20 distinct resources shrinking to 1); all eight items point in the same direction; the resources family produced 8 items where 0.004 were expected by chance, so random re-splits cannot account for the set as a whole; and each profile rests on substantial observation in both windows (for example 106 vs 110 observations for A11, about 200 vs 200 for A01). [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, ASSIGN007, CONC001]

Limitations: the evidence consists of aggregate assignment shares, so it cannot show how assignments were decided case by case, nor whether a single process-wide shift in the active resource pool underlies all six activities. The identical profiles of A13 and A14 indicate those two items reflect the same underlying traces rather than independent confirmations. An important competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that the two windows simply sampled different active resources rather than a change in assignment behaviour — although the complete concentration of A11 on a single resource across 110 observations is difficult to attribute to sampling alone. No control-flow or timing item qualified, so the detected difference is confined to resource allocation. [ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, CONC001]
