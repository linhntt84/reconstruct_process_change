### Observed differences

- Work was reallocated between resources across all profiled activities. In the earlier window, one resource (User_105) held the largest share on every profile — about 11 in 100 assignments for A01 and A03, about 10 in 100 for A13, A14 and A17, and about 16 in 100 for A22. The same handover pattern repeats on every profile: a group of previously dominant resources lost share and a largely different group gained, while the unlisted remainder stayed at roughly 40–45 in 100 in both windows. ### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a change in resource allocation. Across A01, A03, A13, A14, A17 and A22, assignment work shifted from a group of previously dominant resources, led by User_105 with roughly 10–16 in 100 assignments per activity, to a largely different group of resources, led by User_72 with roughly 6–13 in 100; in addition, A16 moved from being shared across about 20 resources to being handled exclusively by User_17. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, ASSIGN007, CONC001]

Supporting signs:
- Eight resource items qualified where about 0.005 would be expected if nothing had changed, so chance cannot account for this family as a whole. [screening]
- All seven allocation profiles move in the same direction on large observation bases (roughly 120–260 assignment records per window per activity), and the A16 concentration is shown directly by two agreeing indicators. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, ASSIGN007, CONC001]
- Each difference exceeds those seen in essentially all 2,000 random re-splits of the pooled data. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN005, CONC001]

Limitations: the evidence shows that shares changed but cannot distinguish whether the previously dominant resources became unavailable or the same pool was used differently; the codes are anonymous and no roles can be inferred. The agreeing profiles are not independent confirmations and may describe one underlying reallocation — the A13 and A14 profiles are identical and likely reflect the same events. [ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004]

Other differences: The elapsed time between consecutive completion events on the A07–A13 pair fell from a mean of about 17 hours to about 3.5 hours (median about 20 to about 1.7), with more intervals observed (6 versus 17). This is the only qualifying time item (against about 0.014 expected by chance) and rests on few observations, so a timing change remains possible but is not confirmed by this evidence.
