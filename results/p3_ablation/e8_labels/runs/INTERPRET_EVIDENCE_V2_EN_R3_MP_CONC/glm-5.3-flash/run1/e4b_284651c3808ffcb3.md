### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, the six flagged activities (A01, A03, A13, A14, A17, A22) each spread their assignments across a broad mix of resources: the largest single share was about 7–10 in 100 assignments, and roughly half of all assignments came from a long tail of less-frequent resources. In the later window, a largely different group of resources holds the top shares while most previously frequent assignees recede; on A22, for example, User_56 rises from about 3 in 100 assignments to about 14 in 100. Profile dissimilarity per activity is 0.26–0.57, and every difference exceeds 99.95% of random re-splits. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN006, ASSIGN007]
- The direction is consistent across activities: an overlapping set of resources gains share on nearly all flagged activities (User_56 gains on all six), while a largely different set loses share. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN006, ASSIGN007]
- A16 changed most sharply. In the earlier window its assignments were spread over 15 distinct resources, none handling more than about 16 in 100; in the later window a single resource, User_28, handled all 137 recorded A16 assignments, moving the concentration index from 0.11 to 1.0. - The activities' own volumes barely moved (for example, 245 vs 253 observations for A13 and 96 vs 107 for A22), so the difference lies in who is assigned the work, not in how often the activities occur. [ASSIGN003, ASSIGN007]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a change in resource allocation as the main hypothesis: in the later window, work on A01, A03, A13, A14, A17 and A22 was reassigned from one group of resources to a largely different group, and A16, previously shared across about 15 resources, became assigned entirely to one resource (User_28). The support is direct (assignment shares per activity are exactly what the flagged indicators measure), consistent in direction across all flagged activities, and unlikely to be a sampling artefact: eight resource items qualified where about 0.005 would be expected by chance for that family, while no control-flow or timing items qualified (0 of 39 and 0 of 27 units tested), and each item's difference exceeds 99.95% of random re-splits. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, ASSIGN007, CONC001]

Limitations: the resource codes are anonymous, so no roles or organisational meaning can be attached to the shift, and the eight items are not independent — the A13 and A14 profiles are identical, and ASSIGN005 and CONC001 both describe A16 — so they reflect roughly two distinct behaviours (a broad reallocation, and the concentration of A16). An important competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that the mix of cases entering each window changed, bringing different resources with them, rather than the same work having been redirected to different resources.

Other differences: none — all qualifying items concern resource assignment; no control-flow or timing evidence passed the screening, so no structural or timing difference is supported at these thresholds. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, ASSIGN007, CONC001]
