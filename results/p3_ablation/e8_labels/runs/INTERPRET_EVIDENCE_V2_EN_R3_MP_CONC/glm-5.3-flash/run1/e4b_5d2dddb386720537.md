### Observed differences

- Repeats of the offer-creation step became less common. In the earlier window, "A13" and its completion event "A14" occurred about 1.4 times per trace, with repetition within the same trace in about 33 in 100 traces; in the later window the average fell to about 1.2 occurrences per trace and repetition to about 19 in 100. Participation did not move: the step appears in every trace in both windows (200 of 200), so only the repeats became rarer. Each difference is larger than in about 995 of 1000 random re-splits of the pooled data. [OCC001, OCC002]

- The number of events separating "A17" from the subsequent "A10" shifted upward. Before, a distance of 2 positions was most common (about 29% of observations) with 3 positions at about 25% and immediate adjacency at about 9%; in the later window, 3 positions became dominant (about 44%), while one-step separations fell to about 3%. The pair itself still occurs within five steps at a similar rate in both windows (about 145 vs 143 of 200 traces), and this difference also exceeds about 995 of 1000 random re-splits. [LAG001]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

A specific possibility supported by the evidence is that, in the later window, the offer-creation step — "A13" together with its completion event "A14" — is repeated less often within a case: repetition fell from about one in three traces to about one in five, and the average per trace from about 1.4 to about 1.2 occurrences, while the step itself remains present in every trace. This is a change in repetition, not in participation or in which activities appear. [OCC001, OCC002]

The sign is direct (the occurrence indicator for these very activities) and internally consistent: the mean-occurrence and recurrence components fell together while participation stayed at 100%, the differences are larger than in about 995 of 1000 random re-splits, and the control-flow family produced three qualifying items against roughly 0.2 expected if nothing had changed — more than chance would typically yield. The main limitation is that the two occurrence items carry identical values and most plausibly describe the same start-and-complete behaviour, so the hypothesis effectively rests on one behavioural signal; ordinary sampling variation between two 200-trace windows is made unlikely by the re-split comparison but cannot be fully excluded. [OCC001, OCC002]

Other differences: the positional distance from "A17" to the following "A10" redistributed toward three events (about 25% of observations before, about 44% after), with immediate adjacency nearly disappearing (about 9% to about 3%), while the rate at which the pair occurs within five steps stayed about the same. Because this indicator uses event positions rather than clock time, it shows a change in how many events lie between the two activities, not that either was moved, and the evidence does not show which intervening behaviour changed; this shift is not part of the repetition hypothesis and its relation to it remains unclear.
