### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the allocation of work across resources changed. Cancellation work (A12), previously spread over a pool of about 44 resources with the busiest handling about 40 in 100, was handled entirely by one resource (User_36) in the later window; and across the offer-handling activities (A13 / A14, A17) and the application activities (A01, A03, A22), assignment volume shifted from one set of main handlers to another, most visibly User_71 taking about a quarter of offer creation and sending (up from about 6 in 100) while User_76 dropped out of the main handlers (down from about 11–13 in 100). [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, ASSIGN007, CONC001]

Supporting signs: eight resource items qualified where roughly 0.005 would be expected by chance for that family, far beyond what an unchanged process produces; the six assignment profiles move in the same direction (the same resources gain across activities while others lose), the concentration result shows the cancellation case directly, and every item's difference exceeds about 9995 of 10000 random re-splits, with 121–343 observations per window. The agreeing items partly reflect one underlying reallocation rather than independent confirmations, but the family-level excess cannot be explained by chance as a whole. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, ASSIGN007, CONC001, screening.by_family.resources]

Limitation: the resource codes are anonymous, so the evidence shows which resources gained or lost work but cannot distinguish a change in assignment behaviour from a period in which a different set of resources was available; both would produce the same redistribution pattern. [ASSIGN003, CONC001, ASSIGN004]

Other differences: the elapsed time between the completions of A03 and A13 shortened by about 95 hours on average (mean about 167 to 72 hours; median about 168 to 66 hours) — a consistent timing change in its own right (one item against roughly 0.02 expected by chance) for which the evidence cannot localise where the time was saved, and no structural counterpart was detected.
