### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a change in how work was allocated to resources. In the earlier window, A16 was shared across a broad pool of about 19 resources (top share about 18 in 100) and the other activities were each handled by wide pools with no resource above about 9 in 100; in the later window, A16 was handled entirely by one resource (User_64), and A01, A03, A13, A14 and A17 were taken over by a largely different set of resources, with User_3 and the other earlier leaders dropping to little or no share while User_56 rose to about 10–12 in 100 on each. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, CONC001]

Supporting signs: the resource family produced 7 qualifying items against roughly 0.005 expected if nothing had changed, so random re-sampling cannot account for the pattern as a whole; each item's difference exceeded all but about 1 in 2000 random re-splits of the pooled data, the items agree in direction across six activities, and the A16 concentration is reported by two indicators with identical support (about 140 observations per window in both). Limitations: the evidence shows which resources executed which activities, not why — a change in resource availability or in the case mix could produce the same reallocation, and the anonymous codes permit no inference about roles; ASSIGN003 and ASSIGN004 describe the same traces and are not independent confirmations.
