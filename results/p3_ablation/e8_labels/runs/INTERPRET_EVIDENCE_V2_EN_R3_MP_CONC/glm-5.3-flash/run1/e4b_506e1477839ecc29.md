### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the allocation of work across resources changed for the offer-handling activities: in the earlier window, A13/A14, A01, A03 and A22 were assigned mainly by one group of resources, and in the later window a largely disjoint group — including User_56 and User_66, previously below the top assignments, alongside a larger share for the already-active User_67 — carried about 4–17 assignments in 100 per activity, while the previous holders lost most or all of theirs. The resource family produced five items where about 0.005 would be expected without a change (roughly a thousandfold), all pointing the same way, each larger than in about 999 of 1,000 random re-splits; the control-flow family produced none, so the change is in who does the work, not in what is done or in which order. Limitations: the resource codes are anonymous, the evidence shows per-activity assignment shares rather than case-level assignments, and it cannot show whether the incoming resources took over the same kind of cases or whether the case mix itself differed between the windows.
