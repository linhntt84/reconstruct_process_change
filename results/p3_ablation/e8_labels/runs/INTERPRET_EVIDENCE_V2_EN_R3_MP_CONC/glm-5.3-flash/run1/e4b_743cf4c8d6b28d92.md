### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a change in resource allocation. In the earlier window, offer creation (A13) was shared across many resources (about 45, none above roughly 8 in 100), and A01, A03, A17 and A22 were each handled by a broad mix with a stable group of frequent assignees; in the later window, all offer creation is assigned to User_3, and in the four other activities work moved away from a group of previously frequent resources (including User_37) toward another group in which User_91's share roughly doubled or tripled, to about 11–19 in 100. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, CONC001]

Supporting signs: the resource family produced 6 qualifying items where about 0.005 would be expected without any change, so this set of findings cannot be explained by chance qualification; all six point in the same direction; and the offer-creation change is categorical (about 45 distinct resources down to 1), which sampling variation between two 200-trace windows cannot plausibly produce. ASSIGN003 and CONC001 describe the same offer-creation change from two angles and should be read as one finding, not two independent confirmations. [screening, ASSIGN003, CONC001]

Limitations: the evidence cannot distinguish a genuine reallocation of work from a change in how assignments were recorded — for example, events being logged under a single default resource — which is the main competing explanation for the complete concentration on User_3. The redistribution in the four other activities is measured only on the ten most frequent assignees, so smaller shifts outside that list are not visible, and no differences outside resource allocation passed the screening thresholds.
