### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that resource allocation changed: in the later window, the work on A01, A03, A07, A13, A14, A17 and A22 was performed by a largely different group of resources than before, and A07 was handled entirely by a single resource (User_100) that previously performed about 18 in 100 of these executions. The resource family produced 8 evidence items against about 0.006 expected without change — many times the chance level — the direction is the same across all seven activities (largely the same resources gain and lose shares), the activities' participation shares stayed similar, and no control-flow item qualified (0 against about 0.19 expected), which argues against a structural explanation. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, ASSIGN007, CONC001]

The evidence shows which resources performed the work but not why: it cannot distinguish a deliberate reallocation from a change in which resources were available, and only the ten most frequent resources are itemised (an unitemised remainder covers about half of the executions in both windows), so the tail of the allocation is unobserved. [ASSIGN001, CONC001]

Other differences: the elapsed time between consecutive completions for A09 → A23 rose from a median of about 1.5 hours to about 17 hours, while the number of observed intervals fell from 26 to 14. As the single time item (about 0.013 expected without change) and based on few observations, this is consistent with a timing change, but a sampling artefact cannot be ruled out. [TWAIT001]
