### Observed differences

- Resource allocation changed across the offer-handling activities. In the earlier window, one resource, User_65, held the largest share of assignments for all six activities — A01, A03, A13, A14, A17 and A22 — at roughly 8–13 in 100 assignments each, with one other resource at about 5–7 in 100. ### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a reallocation of work: in the later window, the six offer-handling activities (A01, A03, A13, A14, A17, A22) were executed by a largely different group of resources — the earlier leading resource User_65 fell from about 8–13 in 100 assignments per activity to below the reported top shares, User_69 became the leading resource at about 10–15 in 100, and User_84 rose to about 5–7 in 100, while only two resources kept broadly similar shares. This is a change in resource allocation, not in process structure. The assignment indicator measures this allocation directly, all six activity profiles shift in the same direction with substantial observations in both windows, and the resource family produced six qualifying items where about 0.005 would be expected without change, so sampling variation cannot account for the family as a whole. The evidence cannot show the form of the reallocation — a handover between two groups of resources, a re-routing of cases, or a takeover confined to the most active resources — because about half of the assignments in both windows fall outside the ten most frequent resources; and A13 and A14 show identical profiles, so the six items likely describe fewer than six independent behaviours. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006]

Other differences: the elapsed time between the completion of A04 and that of A01 fell and became more uniform (median about 28 to about 19 hours; upper quartile about 45 to about 25 hours), a timing change that stands on its own and is based on all 200 traces per window; a second timing change, from the completion of A09 to that of A23 (median about 11 to about 1 hour), is weakly grounded with only 17 versus 27 observations, though the time family's two items also far exceed the roughly 0.016 expected without change. The evidence cannot show whether these timing changes are connected to the reallocation, and no control-flow item qualified, so no structural counterpart is evidenced. [TWAIT001, TWAIT002]
