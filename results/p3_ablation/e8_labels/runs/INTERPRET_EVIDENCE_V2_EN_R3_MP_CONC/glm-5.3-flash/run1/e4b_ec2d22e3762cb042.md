### Observed differences

- **Work allocation moved between groups of resources.** In the earlier window, A01, A07 and A22 were each spread thinly across many resource codes — no single code above roughly 6–15 in 100 assignments, with a diffuse remainder pool of about 23–47 in 100. In the later window a largely different set of codes carries this work: for A01, User_60 rose from about 6 to about 10 in 100 assignments and User_48, not observed earlier, took about 7 in 100, while User_42 (about 6 in 100 earlier) no longer appears; for A22, User_60 went from unobserved to about 12 in 100 while User_42 fell from about 7 in 100 to none; for A07, the earlier-leading codes (about 10–15 in 100) fell to about 6 in 100 or dropped out as previously minor or unobserved codes rose to about 5–13 in 100. Each of the three differences is larger than all but about 1 in 2,000 random re-splits of the pooled data. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003]

- **Elapsed time between the completion of A04 and the completion of A01 lengthened.** The median rose from about 20 to about 66 hours and the mean from about 26 to about 73 hours; the lower quartile moved from under 1 hour to about 17 hours and the upper quartile from about 24 to about 93 hours, so the whole distribution shifted upward. The comparison covers all 200 traces in each window and exceeds all but about 1 in 2,000 random re-splits. [TWAIT001]

- **Transitions out of A12 redistributed.** A12 appears in about 20 in 100 traces in both windows. Earlier, about 49 in 100 of its observed outgoing transitions led back to A12 itself and about 24 in 100 went to A13, with small shares to A17 and A20; in the later window, about 75 in 100 led back to A12, about 7–8 in 100 each went to A02 and A21, the share to A13 fell to about 5 in 100, and O_Sent and A20 no longer appear among the observed targets. The difference exceeds all but about 1 in 200 random re-splits. [DIR001]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the allocation of work to resources changed: the execution of A01, A07 and A22, previously spread over one broad set of resource codes, was taken over in the later window by a largely different set of similar overall size, with earlier-active codes (such as User_42) dropping out and others (such as User_60 and User_48) becoming the main executors. The support is direct — the assignment indicator measures exactly which resources perform each activity — and consistent, since all three activities show the same turnover while their overall dispersion stays similar (the remainder pool, for example, stays around 43–47 in 100 for A01 in both windows). The resource family produced 3 qualifying items where about 0.005 were expected if nothing had changed, a count chance cannot plausibly account for even if all three describe one underlying reallocation. Limitations: the three items share one indicator and are therefore not independent confirmations; the codes are anonymous, so the evidence shows a shift between groups of codes but not its mechanism; and a systematic difference in the case mix entering each window could produce a similar turnover, which the evidence cannot rule out. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003]

Other differences: The elapsed time between the completions of A04 and A01 increased (median about 20 to about 66 hours, seen in essentially every trace of both windows) — a candidate timing change in its own right, but the time family yielded a single item where about 0.015 were expected without any change, so this one item cannot establish a timing change here. The routing out of A12 also shifted (share returning to A12 up from about 49 to about 75 in 100 of its observed outgoing transitions; share to A13 down from about 24 to about 5 in 100), but the control-flow family likewise produced 1 item against about 0.17 expected, within the range an unchanged process produces. [TWAIT001, DIR001]
