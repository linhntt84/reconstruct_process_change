### Observed differences

- Resource allocation shifted on all seven flagged activities (A01, A03, A10, A13, A14, A17, A22). In the earlier window, each activity was handled by a stable group of resources whose top contributors held roughly 4–10% of the assignments each (User_46 the largest on most activities). In the later window, most of those contributors lost nearly all of their share (drops of about 4–10 percentage points each) and a largely different group took over at similar per-resource shares of about 3–7.5% (for example User_41, not among the top contributors before, now holds about 5–7.5% on several activities); User_39 roughly doubled its share on nearly every activity, for example from about 7 in 100 to about 13 in 100 of A22 assignments. Distributional dissimilarity is 0.20–0.37 per activity, each larger than all but about 1 in 2,000 random re-splits. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, ASSIGN007]
- Work also became more dispersed across resources: the residual "other resources" share rose on six of the seven items, most strikingly for A10, where the ten most frequent resources handled about 86 in 100 validations before and only about 48 in 100 after, with "other" rising from about 14 to about 52 in 100. - The elapsed time between consecutive completions of A09 and A04 grew: the typical (median) interval rose only slightly, from about 1.0 to about 1.2 minutes, but the mean nearly doubled, from about 1.0 to about 1.9 minutes, because the later window has a much heavier tail (spread from about 0.3 to about 7 minutes); there were also fewer such consecutive pairs (132 before vs 105 after). The overall shift is about 0.9 minutes, larger than all but about 1 in 2,000 random re-splits. [TWAIT001]
- A13 and A14 show identical resource profiles in both windows, so they reflect one underlying difference rather than two. [ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis of a change in resource allocation: in the later window, work on A01, A03, A10, A13/A14, A17 and A22 was transferred from a recurring group of previously frequent resources to a largely different group, with User_39 roughly doubling its share on nearly every activity, User_46 — previously the largest single contributor on most of these activities — falling out of the top shares, and execution becoming more dispersed across resources, especially for A10. Supporting signs: the resource family produced 7 qualifying items where about 0.007 would be expected without a change, an excess chance cannot explain as a whole; all items agree in direction and each exceeds all but about 1 in 2,000 random re-splits; and participation itself barely moved (A01 in 200 of 200 traces in both windows, A03 in 199 of 199, A22 in 133 of 133), so the difference lies in who executed the activities, not in whether they were executed. Limitations: the seven items describe one underlying reallocation (with one duplicated profile) and are not independent confirmations; because resource identifiers are anonymous codes, a re-mapping of codes onto resources cannot be distinguished from a genuine transfer of work, and the evidence cannot show what drove the shift.
