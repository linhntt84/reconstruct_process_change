- Work was handed over between two almost disjoint groups of resources. ### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a change in resource allocation as a possibility: across the offer-handling activities (A13, A14, A17, A01, A03, A10, A22), execution was reassigned from the previously dominant group of users to a largely different group, with validation shifting from a few concentrated validators to a long tail of occasional performers. The sign is direct (the profiles measure who performed each activity), consistent (the same users gain and lose in all seven profiles, each difference larger than about 999 in 1000 reference comparisons), and the resource family produced 7 qualifying items where about 0.005 — one in two hundred — would be expected if nothing had changed, a count chance cannot explain; control flow produced 1 item against about 0.2 expected and timing none. Support counts of roughly 114–286 observations per activity per window show the activities themselves remain in regular use, so the change is in who performs the work, not in whether the activities occur. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, ASSIGN007, LAG001]

Limitations: each profile shows only the ten largest shares plus a remainder, so movements inside the pool are invisible, and the evidence cannot rule out that a different case mix between the windows, rather than a reallocation of the same work, produced the new leading group. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, ASSIGN007]

Other differences: the spacing between consecutive A17 events shifted toward smaller gaps (about 39 in 100 pairs one position apart, from about 23 before, while five-position pairs fell from about 43 to about 20 in 100). As the single control-flow item, where about 0.2 would qualify by chance, and based on only 46 before and 32 after traces, it does not by itself support a structural or timing change, and it measures event positions rather than clock time; it may accompany the reallocation or be a sampling fluctuation.
