### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

Chance is an unlikely explanation for the resource picture: 8 resource items qualified where roughly 0.005 were expected for an unchanged process, and 2 time items where about 0.017 were expected, while control flow produced none (0 of 32 units against about 0.16 expected); every flagged item is also larger than all but about 1 in 2000 random re-splits of the pooled data. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, ASSIGN007, CONC001, TWAIT001, TWAIT002]

The evidence supports a reallocation of work across resources as the main reading. In the earlier window, work on A01, A03, A13/A14, A17 and A21 was assigned to one stable group of resources; in the later window a largely different group carries these activities, and A08, previously shared across 17 resources, is handled exclusively by User_121. Supporting signs: the difference is measured directly on the assignment profiles and concentration, it runs in the same direction across all these activities (largely the same resources lose share in every profile while largely the same new ones gain), and the number of qualifying resource items is far beyond what random re-splits of a stable process produce. Limitations: the profiles detail only the ten largest shares, so roughly 30–48 in 100 assignments per activity fall into an unobserved remainder where continuity or further shifts cannot be seen; absence from the top shares does not prove a resource stopped performing the activity; and the evidence cannot distinguish a genuine reallocation of the same work from a change in the mix of cases reaching these activities. Note also that A13 and A14 show identical profiles and reflect one behaviour, so the assignment items are not fully independent confirmations. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, ASSIGN007, CONC001]

Other differences: the lengthening of the A04 → A01 completion interval (mean about 26.5 → 44 hours, upper quartile about 31 → 71 hours, median nearly unchanged) is a timing difference in its own right, and the evidence cannot show whether it is connected to the reallocation. The rise in back-to-back A20 → A10 completions (7 → 17 occurrences, elapsed time negligible in both windows) rests on few observations and is at most a weak hint that this pair occurs more often. [TWAIT001, TWAIT002]
