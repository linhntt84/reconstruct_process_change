### Observed differences

- Work on the offer-handling activities was reassigned between resources. In the earlier window, assignments for A01, A03, A13, A17 and A22 were spread thinly: no resource held more than about 13 in 100 assignments, and roughly half went to a long tail of others. In the later window a new group of resources held the leading shares — User_3 rose from about 3 in 100 to about 12–15 in 100 on each of these activities, joined by previously marginal resources at about 3–9 in 100 — while a previously prominent group, including User_79, fell to about 3–6 in 100 or dropped out of the leading shares; the long-tail share stayed near half in both windows, so it is the leading resources that changed, not the tail size. Each of these redistributions exceeds all but about 1 in 2000 random re-splits of the pooled traces [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, ASSIGN007].

- A11 changed from shared to exclusive: earlier, its 111 observed assignments were spread over 23 distinct resources, with the most frequent, User_100, taking about 15 in 100; later, all 102 observed assignments went to User_100 alone (top-resource share from about 15 in 100 to 100 in 100; concentration index from 0.06 to 1.0) [ASSIGN003, CONC001].

- The outgoing transitions of A10 were redistributed: earlier, about 57 in 100 led to A16, 17 in 100 to A11 and 10 in 100 to A07; later, about 44 in 100 led to A16, 11 in 100 to A11 and 24 in 100 to A07 (220 vs 258 observed transitions; larger than in about 199 of 200 random re-splits) [DIR001].

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the allocation of work to resources changed. In the earlier window, executions of A01, A03, A13, A14, A17 and A22 were distributed across a broad pool of resources; in the later window, a different group of resources took the largest share of every one of these activities (User_3 roughly quadrupled its share, while previously prominent resources such as User_79 lost most of theirs), and A11, previously shared by about 23 resources, was assigned exclusively to User_100. Supporting signs: the resource family produced 8 qualifying items where fewer than 0.01 would be expected without any change — a count chance cannot explain as a whole; the seven assignment profiles all shift in the same direction across activities spanning offer creation, sending, acceptance and completion, and the concentration item shows the A11 change directly. Limitations: the items most likely reflect one underlying reallocation rather than independent changes, the anonymous resource codes permit no inference about roles or teams, and the evidence cannot rule out that the two sampled periods simply had different active resource pools rather than a real reallocation [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, ASSIGN007, CONC001].

Other differences: the mix of outgoing transitions from A10 shifted towards A07 and away from A16 and A11; as the only control-flow item, at about the rate an unchanged process produces (1 item against about 0.2 expected), this difference is consistent with sampling variation and is not part of the main hypothesis [DIR001].
