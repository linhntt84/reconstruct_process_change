### Observed differences

- Resource allocation changed across the main handling activities. In the earlier window, A01, A03, A13/A14, A17 and A22 each had several moderate leaders (top resource roughly 7–10% of that activity's events); in the later window a largely different set leads, and one resource (User_98) that was not among the leaders before now tops each of these activities with about 13–20% of their events, while most former leaders fall to about 3–6% or leave the top group. The unlisted remainder stayed near 40–45% in both windows, so this is a swap of leading resources, not a move to a single pool. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, ASSIGN007, ASSIGN008]
- A08 and A11, which show identical assignment profiles, also changed hands: before, three resources led with about 11–13% each; after, a new resource (User_133) leads with about 17% and another newcomer holds about 9%, while the former leaders drop to about 5–6% or below. [ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004]
- A04 became fully concentrated on one resource: earlier it was spread across 29 distinct resources with the busiest performing about 48% of its events; later a single resource performed all of them (top share 100%, concentration index about 0.24 to 1.0). [CONC001]
- Direct submission became more frequent: A09 appears in about 71 in 100 later-window traces versus about 57 in 100 earlier (at most once per trace in both). From A05, the direct transition to A09 rose from about 57% to about 71% of outgoing transitions, while the transition to A04 fell from about 44% to about 29%. [OCC001, DIR001]
- Handling after A16 shifted away from acceptance: the share of transitions to A11 fell from about 29% to about 12%, while A07 rose from about 31% to about 40% and A10 from about 11% to about 16% (A16 present in about 151 and 139 traces in the two windows). [DIR002]
- A04 occurs slightly later within traces: median relative position about 0.12 before versus about 0.14 after; this is a positional shift, not a clock-time one. [POS001]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that resource allocation changed: in the later window, the main application- and offer-handling activities (A01, A03, A08/A11, A13/A14, A17, A22) are performed by a largely different group of resources, with User_98 newly dominant at about 13–20% of each, and A04 is performed entirely by a single resource instead of about 29. This rests on direct assignment evidence for each activity, the same swap pattern repeating across activities, and a resource-family count (9 qualifying items) far above the roughly 0.006 expected without change; participation of these activities barely moved (for example, A01 appears in 200 of 200 traces in both windows), so the swap is not a by-product of the activities being performed more or less often. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, ASSIGN007, ASSIGN008, CONC001]

Limitations: the resource codes are anonymous, so the evidence shows a change in allocation between groups of resources but not its nature; some items share identical profiles (A08 with A11, A13 with A14), so the nine items describe about six distinct allocation changes. An important competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that the mix of cases itself changed in step with the routing shifts below, which could produce both the new resource profiles and the routing differences without a reallocation of the same work; no case-attribute evidence is included to separate these. [ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006]

Other differences: routing shifted without any activity disappearing — A09 now appears in about 71 in 100 traces (about 57 in 100 before), with the direct A05 → A09 share up from about 57% to 71% and the A05 → A04 share down from about 44% to 29%; after A16, the A11 share fell from about 29% to 12% while A07 (about 31% to 40%) and A10 (about 11% to 16%) rose; and A04 sits slightly later within traces (median relative position about 0.12 versus 0.14). These control-flow items (4 versus about 0.21 expected without change) are unlikely to be sampling noise, but they are kept separate from the main hypothesis, and the evidence cannot establish whether they share a cause with the reallocation; no timing items qualified. [OCC001, DIR001, DIR002, POS001]
