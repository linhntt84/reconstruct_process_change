### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a change in resource allocation: in the later window, A13, A14, A17 and A22 were carried out by a largely different set of resources than before, and allocation became less concentrated — the share of assignments spread across many minor resources rose from roughly 30–45 in 100 to roughly 37–54 in 100 depending on the activity, several previously prominent resources (each formerly around 4–16 in 100) dropped out, and new ones took their place. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004]

Supporting signs: the resource family produced 4 qualifying items where about 0.004 were expected without change, all four items point in the same direction (redistribution away from the earlier group of resources), each difference beats about 1999 of 2000 random re-splits, and both windows are well observed. The change is confined to allocation; no control-flow items qualified. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004]

Limitations: the resource codes are anonymous, so the evidence cannot show whether work was deliberately reassigned or whether the pool of active resources simply differed between the windows; a shift in the case mix behind these activities is also not ruled out. In addition, ASSIGN001 and ASSIGN002 carry identical profiles and likely reflect the same underlying assignments, so the pattern rests on three distinct activities rather than four independent confirmations. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002]

Other differences: the number of assignments behind the profiles was consistently somewhat lower in the later window (about 280 down to 250 for offer creation, about 260 down to 235 for O_Sent, about 120 down to 105 for completions); this decrease is modest and may lie within normal variation between windows.
