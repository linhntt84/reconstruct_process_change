### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the later window differs in resource allocation: the work on A01, A13, A14, A17 and A22 was shifted from one group of resources to a largely different group, with resources such as User_56 losing most of their share and resources such as User_3 and User_78 taking it up. Five resource-allocation items qualified where the screening model expects about 0.005 without change — a count chance cannot plausibly produce — all pointing the same way with largely the same gaining and losing codes, and each difference larger than in about 999 of 1,000 random re-splits. Participation in the activities was similar in both windows, and no control-flow item qualified (0 of 33 units against about 0.16 expected by chance), so neither a change in how often the activities run nor a structural change competes as an explanation. Limitations: the five items describe one underlying redistribution rather than independent confirmations — A13 and A14 show identical profiles — and the evidence cannot distinguish a reallocation within a stable resource pool from a change in the resource population (codes appearing or disappearing) or from the two windows covering different case mixes that different resources happen to handle; the anonymous codes and the absence of case attributes allow no further check. Other differences: the single time item indicates that the elapsed time between consecutive completions of A04 and A01 fell (median about 67 to about 28 hours; every distribution component decreased).
