### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility of a change in resource allocation: in the later window, work on A01, A03, A13, A14, A17 and A22 was reassigned from one group of resources to another — User_49 and User_5 took markedly larger shares (User_49 up to about 19 in 100 of A22 assignments, from about 6 in 100), while User_3 and several other previously frequent resources were assigned these activities less often. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006]

The supporting signs are directness, consistency and the screening count: O_ASSIGN measures allocation directly, all six items point the same way with the same resources gaining and losing, each difference exceeds about 1999 of 2000 random re-splits, and 6 qualifying resource items is far above the roughly 0.005 expected by chance for that family, whereas control flow and timing yielded none. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006]

Limitations: only assignment shares are observed, so the evidence cannot show whether the reallocation reflects a change in assignment practice or a shift in the case mix or workload of particular resources, and the anonymous codes allow no characterisation beyond assignment frequencies. The six items may also reflect one underlying reallocation rather than independent changes — A13 and A14 show identical profiles. [ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004]
