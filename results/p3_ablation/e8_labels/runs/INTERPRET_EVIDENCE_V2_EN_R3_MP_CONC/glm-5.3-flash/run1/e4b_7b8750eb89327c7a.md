### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a change in resource allocation rather than in structure or timing: across seven activities — A01, A03, A08, A13, A14, A17 and A22 — work was reassigned from one group of resources to a largely different group, with a set of previously prominent resources (including User_13) losing most of their shares to a mostly new set (including User_3), and A08 going from about 16 sharing resources to a single resource (User_115) in every sampled case. Supporting signs: all eight items come from the resource family, where 8 items were observed against about 0.004 expected if nothing had changed — roughly 2,000 times the chance expectation, which random sampling cannot explain; every item's difference exceeds about 1,999 of 2,000 random re-splits; the direction of the redistribution is the same in all seven activities; and the control-flow and time families produced no items, so the difference appears specific to allocation. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, ASSIGN007, CONC001, screening.by_family]

Limitations: the two A08 items rest on the same traces and do not independently confirm each other; the evidence cannot rule out that the shift reflects a different mix of cases or a different set of active resources in the later window rather than a change in assignment behaviour itself; and the resource codes are anonymous, so no roles, people or teams can be inferred.
