### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility of a change in resource allocation in the later window: across all six profiled activities, execution shifted away from a set of resources that previously each handled roughly 4–10 in 100 executions (including User_3) toward a set led by User_49 — up to about 19 in 100 for A22 — together with User_5 and other previously minor resources. The change is in who does the work, not in how often the activities run. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006]

Supporting signs: all six items point in the same direction, each difference exceeds all but about 1 in 2000 random re-splits, the profiles rest on roughly 100–310 observations per window, and the resource family produced 6 qualifying items where about 0.005 would be expected without change — a gap chance cannot plausibly explain, even though the items are compared as six and describe one underlying reallocation.
