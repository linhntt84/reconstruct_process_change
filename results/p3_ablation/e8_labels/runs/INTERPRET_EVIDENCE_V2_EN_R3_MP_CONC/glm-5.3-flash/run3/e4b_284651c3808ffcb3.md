### Observed differences

- Assignment shares for the offer-related activities (A01, A03, A13/A14, A17, A22) were redistributed between resources. In the earlier window, the largest single resource held only about 7–10 in 100 events of each activity, with roughly half of the events in a long tail of many resources; in the later window, a largely different set of resources held the top shares, with individual resources gaining or losing roughly 3–11 percentage points. For A22, for example, User_56 rose from about 3 in 100 completions to about 14 in 100, while User_78 fell from about 10 in 100 to below the ten largest shares; the same direction of shift repeats across the other activities. Each profile difference (0.26–0.57 on the dissimilarity scale) exceeds about 1999 of 2000 random re-splits of the pooled data. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN006, ASSIGN007]

- A16 changed most sharply. In the earlier window its events were spread over about 15 distinct resources, with the two largest at about 16 in 100 each (concentration index about 0.11); in the later window, every one of the roughly 137 recorded events is attributed to a single resource, User_28 (concentration index 1.0, one distinct resource). - The activities' own volumes were similar in both windows (about 145 vs 137 events for A16, 96 vs 107 for A22, and about 200–250 observations behind the other profiles), so the differences lie in which resources were assigned the work, not in how often the activities occurred. ### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the allocation of work to resources changed: across A01, A03, A13, A17 and A22, shares shifted from one group of resources to another, and A16 moved from being shared across about 15 resources to being assigned exclusively to User_28. The resource family produced 8 qualifying items against roughly 0.005 expected if nothing had changed — far beyond what chance would produce — while the control-flow and time families produced none (0 against about 0.19 and about 0.013 expected). All items point in the same direction, and the concentration measures corroborate the assignment profile for A16 directly. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, ASSIGN007, CONC001]

Limitations: the items are not fully independent — A13 and A14 show identical profiles, and A16 is described twice (assignment profile and concentration), so roughly five distinct behaviours stand behind the eight items. The evidence also cannot rule out that the redistribution reflects a different mix of cases or resource availability in the later window rather than a change in assignment rules, and it says nothing about the cause of the shift. [ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, CONC001]

Other differences: none were flagged; every qualifying item concerns resource allocation, with no control-flow or timing items at these thresholds. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, ASSIGN007, CONC001]
