- In the earlier window, each compared activity was handled by a stable leading group of resources: for A01, User_49 performed about 11 in 100 assignments, with the next resources at roughly 6–8 in 100 each. In the later window, a largely different group leads, with User_3 at about 8.5 in 100 and the following resources at about 6–7 in 100. [ASSIGN001]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a change in resource allocation: in the later window, the six compared activities (A01, A03, A13, A14, A17, A22) are assigned to a largely different set of resources than in the earlier window — a leading group that included User_49 gave way to a group led by User_3, with only a few resources such as User_51 keeping a similar share — and the work is spread across more resources, with the share outside the ten most frequent rising by roughly 7–11 in 100. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006]

The screening comparison backs this: the resource family produced 6 qualifying items against about 0.004 expected without change (roughly 1500 times the chance expectation), while the control-flow and time families produced none (0 against 0.16 and 0.017), so the qualifying differences are confined to allocation. [screening]

The hypothesis is direct (assignment distributions measure allocation itself), consistent (all six activities show the same direction with near-identical before/after profiles), and each difference exceeds about 1999 of 2000 random re-splits with 134–271 assignments behind each window's profile. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006]

Limitations: all six items come from the same indicator, so they characterise one underlying reallocation rather than independent aspects of the process; and the evidence shows only that assignment shares moved — it cannot distinguish a reallocation of the work from a change in which cases reach which resources (for example through case mix or routing). Resource codes are anonymous, so no roles can be inferred. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006]
