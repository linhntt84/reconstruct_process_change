### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the allocation of work across resources changed. In the earlier window, A01, A03, A13, A14, A17 and A22 were each dominated by a group of resources led by codes such as User_40 (about 8–9 in 100 events); in the later window, a largely disjoint group led by User_84 and User_96 holds the largest shares on all six activities, and the share of events falling outside the ten most frequent resources rose from about 43–47 to about 50–54 in 100 on most of them. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006]

The resource family produced six qualifying items against roughly 0.005 expected without change — a count far beyond what chance produces for that family — and all six point the same way, each backed by roughly 100–275 events per window and each difference larger than in about 1999 of 2000 random re-splits. No control-flow unit qualified (0 of 34, against about 0.17 expected), and the only other qualifying item is a single time item, so the qualifying differences are confined to resource allocation plus one timing measure. Limitations: the resource codes are anonymous and the named resources cover only about half of the events, so the evidence cannot distinguish a genuine redistribution of work within a stable pool from a turnover in which earlier codes stopped appearing and new codes started; the six items may also reflect one underlying re-allocation rather than independent changes.
