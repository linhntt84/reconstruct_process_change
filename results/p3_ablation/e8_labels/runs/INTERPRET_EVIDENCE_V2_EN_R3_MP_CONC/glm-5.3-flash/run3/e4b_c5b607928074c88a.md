### Observed differences

- Resource allocation for the offer-handling activities changed markedly. In the earlier window, one resource (User_65) held the largest share of assignments for all six activities — A01, A03, A13, A14, A17 and A22 — at roughly 8–13% each, with the remainder spread thinly over many other codes (about half of all assignments in a long tail). In the later window, User_69 held the largest share (roughly 10–15%), most other leading shares went to resources that had been marginal before, the earlier leading resources dropped out of the top shares, and the long tail shrank by about 3–5 percentage points. ### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a change in resource allocation: in the later window, work on the offer-handling activities (A13, A14, A17, A01, A03, A22) is assigned to a largely different set of resources than before — the earlier leading resource (User_65, about 8–13% per activity) disappears from the top shares, and a new leading resource (User_69, about 10–15%) together with previously marginal codes takes over most of the volume. This rests on six consistent per-activity profiles, each with near-maximal empirical strength and solid observation counts, and the resource family produced 6 items where about 0.005 would be expected without any change, so the pattern as a whole is not a chance artefact of the finite windows. Limitations: the resource codes are anonymous, and the evidence shows that the shares moved, not why — it cannot distinguish a change in allocation behaviour from a change in which resources were active in each period. Also, the A13 and A14 profiles are identical, so the six items describe one underlying shift rather than six independent confirmations. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006]

Other differences: an independent timing change — elapsed time between consecutive completion events shortened, most clearly for A04 → A01 (median about 28 to about 19 hours, 200 intervals per window) and, on few observations, for A09 → A23 (median about 11 to about 1.3 hours, 17 vs 27 intervals). No control-flow difference accompanies these findings (0 items against about 0.18 expected), so the timing shift stands on its own rather than reflecting a restructured process. [TWAIT001, TWAIT002]
