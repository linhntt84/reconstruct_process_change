### Observed differences

- Handling of A16 moved from widely shared work to a single resource. In the earlier window its assignments were spread across 18 distinct resources, with the busiest (User_123) taking about 14 in 100; in the later window all 165 A16 assignments went to User_123 alone, and the concentration index rose from about 0.10 to 1.0. The volume of A16 work was similar in both windows (154 vs 165 assignments), so what changed is who does the work, not how often it occurs. [ASSIGN005, CONC001]
- Across the offer-handling activities (A01, A03, A13 / A14, A17), the group of resources holding the work rotated. In the earlier window the allocation was diffuse: the ten largest holders each took roughly 3–8 in 100 assignments and about half of the work sat in a long tail. In the later window, most of those earlier holders no longer appear among the top resources and are replaced by a largely new group — the largest, User_49, takes about 8 in 100 — while a few earlier holders such as User_67 continue at roughly unchanged shares and the long tail still holds about half of the work. The same resources gain and lose across all these activities, and each profile rests on roughly 200–270 observations per window. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN006]
- The elapsed time between completing A04 and completing A01 shortened: the mean fell from about 48 hours to about 29 hours (about 19 hours, roughly 40%, lower), the median from about 30 to about 22 hours, and the upper quartile from about 80 to about 47 hours, with the standard deviation roughly halving (about 74 to about 39 hours). All distribution components moved down together. [TWAIT001]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a change in resource allocation as a real possibility: in the later window, the offer-handling activities (A01, A03, A13, A14, A17) were handled by a largely different group of resources than before, and A16 — previously shared across about 18 resources — was handled exclusively by a single resource (User_123). [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, CONC001]

Chance is an unlikely explanation for the resource family as a whole: it produced seven qualifying items where about 0.006 would be expected if nothing had changed, while control flow produced none (about 0.18 expected) and time one (about 0.013 expected). [TWAIT001, ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, CONC001] The allocation items measure the change directly and agree: the same resources gain and lose across the offer activities (dissimilarity about 0.29–0.32, each difference larger than all but about 1 of 2000 random re-splits), and the A16 concentration is shown by two indicators on the same observations (distinct resources falling from 18 to 1; top share rising from about 14 in 100 to all assignments) while A16's volume stayed stable. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN006, ASSIGN005, CONC001]

Limitations: the seven resource items are not independent — the A13 and A14 profiles are identical, and the two A16 items describe one change — so the family reflects roughly four distinct differences. Because resource identifiers are anonymous codes, the evidence cannot distinguish a change in assignment behaviour from a change in which resources were active at all, and it cannot show whether the timing change below is connected to the reallocation. [ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, CONC001]

Other differences: The elapsed time between the completions of A04 and A01 fell (mean about 48 to about 29 hours, median about 30 to about 22, upper quartile about 80 to about 47, variability roughly halved). As a single time item — about 0.013 would be expected without change — it would not on its own clear the screening bar, so it is reported as an observed timing difference rather than a supported change, and its relation to the reallocation is unknown. [TWAIT001]
