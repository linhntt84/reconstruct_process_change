### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that resource allocation changed: in the later window, work on A01, A13, A14, A17 and A22 was shifted from one group of resources to another, with resources such as User_56 losing most of their share and resources such as User_3 and User_78 taking on a markedly larger share. The resource family produced 5 items where about 0.005 were expected without a change, all five items show the same direction of redistribution with the same gaining and losing groups, and each difference exceeds about 999.5 of 1,000 random re-splits. Limitations: the codes are anonymous and the indicator shows shares only; A13 and A14 have identical profiles and describe one redistribution, so the items are not fully independent; and the A22 shares rest on only about 105–115 observations. An important competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that the pool of active resources differed between the windows (some codes stopped appearing, others started), which would produce the same redistribution without any change in assignment behaviour [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, screening].

Other differences: the elapsed time between the completion of A04 and of A01 roughly halved (median from about 67 hours to about 28 hours); this is a timing change in its own right, and the evidence cannot show whether it is connected to the reallocation. No control-flow difference qualified at the configured thresholds (0 items against about 0.16 expected) [TWAIT001, screening].
