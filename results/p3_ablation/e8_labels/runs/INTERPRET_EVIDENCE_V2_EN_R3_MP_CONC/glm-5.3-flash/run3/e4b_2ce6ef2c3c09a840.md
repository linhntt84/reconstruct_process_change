### Observed differences

- Across six activities (A01, A03, A13, A14, A17, A22), the resources executing the work changed markedly. In the earlier window, each activity's assignments were spread broadly: the ten most-used resources held roughly 4–10% each, with about 37–45% spread over many minor contributors. In the later window, the frequently used resources are largely different codes — for example, User_22, which took about 8 in 100 of the A17 assignments before, no longer holds a top share, while User_97 rises to about 5 in 100 — and the broadly spread remainder grows to about 47–54%. Profile dissimilarity per activity is 0.36–0.50, each larger than 99.9% of 2,000 random re-splits. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, ASSIGN007]

- A11 changed most sharply. Before, its events were spread over about 20 distinct resources, with the most frequent (User_115) taking about 15 in 100; in the later window, essentially every observed A11 event is assigned to User_115 alone (top-resource share 15% → 100%, distinct resources 20 → 1, concentration index 0.07 → 1.0). The activity occurs in about half the traces in both windows (106 of 200 before, 110 of 200 after), so this comparison rests on a substantial sample. [ASSIGN003, CONC001]

- In all these profiles, the losing and gaining resources form almost entirely disjoint sets while per-resource share sizes stay in similar ranges, so the behaviour changed by replacing which resources execute the work, not by shifting between two fixed alternatives. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, ASSIGN007]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility of a change in resource allocation: the execution of A01, A03, A13/A14, A17 and A22 moved from one group of resources to a largely different group, and A11, previously shared by about 20 resources, became assigned entirely to a single resource (User_115). This rests on direct allocation evidence: the resource family produced 8 qualifying items where an unchanged process would produce about 0.004, while control flow and timing produced none (0 items against about 0.2 and 0.02 expected), all items agree in direction, and the concentration indicator shows the A11 change directly rather than by inference. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, ASSIGN007, CONC001]

Limitations: the assignment items likely describe one underlying shift rather than independent confirmations (the A13 and A14 profiles are identical), and because resource identifiers are anonymous codes, the evidence cannot distinguish a reassignment of work among continuing resources from a change in which codes appear in the log, with previous codes disappearing and new ones entering. [ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, CONC001]
