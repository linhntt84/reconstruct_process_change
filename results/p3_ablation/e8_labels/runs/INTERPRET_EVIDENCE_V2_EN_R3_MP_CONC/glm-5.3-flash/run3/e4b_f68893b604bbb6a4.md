### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

Screening tested 11 resource units and 8 items qualified, against about 0.005 expected if nothing had changed — a count far above what an unchanged process produces; the control-flow and time families produced no items (0 against about 0.17 and 0.02 expected), so the qualifying differences are confined to resource allocation. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, ASSIGN007, CONC001]

Main hypothesis: resource allocation changed. Work on A01, A03, A13/A14, A17 and A22 was shifted from the previously active group of resources — including User_37 with about 10–12 in 100 assignments of each — to a largely different group, including User_48 with about 6–9 in 100; in addition, A11, previously spread over 18 resources, came to be handled entirely by User_115. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, ASSIGN007, CONC001]

Supporting signs: the same resources leave and the same new ones enter the top shares across five distinct activities; the extreme concentration of A11 is shown directly, with similar case volume in both windows (107 vs 101 observations), which rules out a drop in volume as an explanation; and every item's difference exceeds all but about 1 in 2000 random re-splits of the pooled data. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN007, CONC001]

Limitations: the resource codes are anonymous, so the evidence cannot distinguish a deliberate reallocation from a change in which resources were available in the later period — both would produce this same signature. Shifts within the aggregated remainder of resources (about 40–55 in 100 assignments in both windows) are invisible, and ASSIGN005 duplicates ASSIGN004's profile, so the eight items are not eight independent confirmations.
