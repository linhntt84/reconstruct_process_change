### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a change in resource allocation: in the later window, assignment work on A01, A03, A13, A14, A17 and A22 shifted from a group of resources led by User_104 to one led by User_67 and spread across more resources, including newcomers such as User_77, so concentration on few resources decreased; for A07 the pool of resources handling the activity was almost entirely replaced. Supporting signs: seven resource items qualified where fewer than 0.01 would be expected by chance for the resource family, a count far beyond what an unchanged process produces; all seven agree in direction; each difference exceeds 999.5 of 1000 random re-splits; and the profiles rest on substantial support (200 of 200 traces for A01, about 257 versus 280 assignments for A13). No control-flow or timing item qualified, so the qualifying differences are specific to allocation rather than structure or timing. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, ASSIGN007, screening]

Limitations: the A13 and A14 items have identical profiles and are not independent confirmations, and the six offer/application items share one turnover pattern, so they may reflect a single underlying reallocation rather than seven separate changes; the codes are anonymous, so no roles or teams can be inferred; and the evidence cannot rule out that the turnover stems from a changed mix of cases or resource availability rather than from a change in assignment behaviour itself.
