### Observed differences

- **Resource allocation moved between groups of users for three activities.** For A01, assignments in the earlier window were spread thinly (no user above about 6 in 100, with about 47 in 100 scattered across many others); in the later window a tighter leading group emerged — User_60 rose from about 5 to 10 in 100 and User_48 from under 4 to about 7 in 100 — while users such as User_42 (about 6 in 100 before) fell below 4 in 100. ### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the work on A01, A07 and A22 was reassigned between groups of resources. In the earlier window each activity was handled by a broad, flat set of users; in the later window different, partly overlapping groups held the largest shares — for A01 and A22 the incoming group includes User_60 and User_48 and the outgoing group includes User_42, while A07 moved to its own new set of handlers. Supporting signs: all three assignment profiles changed more than in over 99.9% of random re-splits; the resource family produced 3 qualifying items against roughly 0.005 expected without change, several hundred times the chance expectation; and A01 was present in all traces in both windows, so its redistribution is not a participation artefact. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003]

Limitations: the three items describe the same type of change and are not independent confirmations; the identifiers are anonymous codes, so no roles or handover mechanism can be inferred; and the evidence cannot rule out that a changed case mix entering the later window — rather than a change in assignment behaviour for the same work — produced the redistribution, particularly for A22, which itself appeared in fewer traces. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003]
