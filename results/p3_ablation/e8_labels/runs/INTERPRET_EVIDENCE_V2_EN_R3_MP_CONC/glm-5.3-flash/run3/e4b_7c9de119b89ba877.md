### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the later window differs from the earlier one in resource allocation: execution of A01, A03, A13, A14, A17 and A22 shifted from a broad spread with User_39 among the main performers (about 8–11% each) to a concentration on User_46 (about 15–19% of every one of these activities), with User_39 no longer observed on them and previously marginal resources such as User_61 taking about 5–9%. The resource family produced six qualifying items where about 0.006 were expected by chance if nothing had changed, a count far beyond what random re-splitting produces; every item moves in the same direction, and the supports are substantial (for example 273 versus 267 occurrences for A13 and 141 versus 131 for A22). [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006]

Limitations: the six items describe one underlying reallocation and are not independent confirmations — the A13 and A14 profiles are identical — and the evidence shows only that assignment shares changed, not why; it cannot distinguish a deliberate reallocation from changes in which resources were available or from a changed mix of cases. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006]

Other differences: A21 participation roughly halved, from about 18 in 100 traces to about 8 in 100. This is the only control-flow item, against about 0.17 expected by chance in that family, so it is within the range an unchanged process typically produces; it is not part of the main hypothesis and may be a sampling fluctuation of a low-participation activity.
