### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the allocation of work to resources changed for the offer-handling activities: in the later window, A13, A14, A17 and A22 were assigned to a largely different group of resources than in the earlier window, and assignment became somewhat less concentrated, with the combined minor-resource share rising from about 45 to about 53 in 100 for the offer activities. The signs are direct and consistent: all four items measure allocation directly, show the same redistribution with the same gaining and losing groups, four items qualified where about 0.004 were expected by chance, each difference exceeds about 999.5 in 1000 random re-splits, and both windows have substantial support. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, screening]

Limitations: ASSIGN001 and ASSIGN002 have identical profiles and very likely reflect the same underlying assignments, so they are not independent confirmations; and because resource codes are anonymous and only shares are observed, the evidence cannot distinguish a reallocation within a stable pool from a replacement of the active resources. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002]

Competing explanation: part of the apparent de-concentration could reflect a longer tail of occasionally used resources rather than a change in how the main workload is assigned; the evidence cannot separate these. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004]

Other differences: none — no control-flow or timing items qualified, so the detected change is confined to resource allocation. [screening]
