### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

Main hypothesis: the evidence supports that, in the later window, work on several activities was reallocated to a largely different set of resource codes. A04, previously shared across 29 codes, was performed by a single code; on A01, A03, A13/A14, A17 and A22, User_98 became the most frequent performer (about 13–20 in 100 of each activity's events) after not being a leading code; and on A08 and A11 a new leading group around User_133 (about 17 in 100) displaced the group previously led by User_100 (about 13 in 100). [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, ASSIGN007, ASSIGN008, CONC001]

The support is direct and consistent: the assignment indicator measures which code performs each activity, all nine resource items describe reassignment while the activities themselves still occur at similar or only moderately lower rates, every difference exceeds about 1,999 of 2,000 random re-splits, and the resource family produced nine qualifying items where about 0.006 would be expected if nothing had changed — a gap chance cannot explain. Limitations: several items restate one behaviour (A08 and A11 have identical profiles, as do A13 and A14), so they are not independent confirmations; the codes are anonymous, so only the movement of work between codes is shown, not its mechanism; and a different mix of cases between the windows that happens to be handled by different codes cannot be ruled out.
