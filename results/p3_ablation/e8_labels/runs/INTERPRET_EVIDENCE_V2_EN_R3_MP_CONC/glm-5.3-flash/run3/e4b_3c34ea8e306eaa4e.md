### Observed differences

- Work on the offer-handling activities moved to a largely different set of resources. In the earlier window, activities such as A01, A03, A13 / A14, A17 and A22 were each handled most often by a group of resources fronted by User_79 (roughly 7–9 in 100 events of each activity) together with several other frequently occurring codes. In the later window, none of these former leaders appears among the ten most frequent handlers; a largely new set of codes (each around 4–8 in 100 events) carries the work instead, while User_3 remains and increased its share (for example from about 8 to about 12 in 100 for A22). The assignment profiles differ by a cosine dissimilarity of about 0.30–0.38 per activity, each larger than in essentially all 2,000 random re-splits, while participation stayed stable (A01 occurs in all 200 traces in both windows; A22 in 112 vs 123 traces). - A07 became the exclusive work of a single resource. Earlier it was spread over 29 distinct resources, with the most frequent of them, User_100, performing about 18 in 100 of its events; in the later window every observed A07 event (161 traces, close to the 164 before) was performed by User_100 alone, with the concentration index rising from about 0.07 to 1.0. [CONC001, ASSIGN003]

- The elapsed time between consecutive completion events on the A09 → A23 pair lengthened: the median gap rose from about 1.5 hours to about 17 hours (mean from about 4 to about 16 hours), and the number of observed gaps fell from 26 to 14. [TWAIT001]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a change in how work was allocated to resources: across the offer-handling activities (A13, A14, A17, A01, A03, A22), execution shifted in the later window from the previously most frequent resources (including User_79) to a largely different set of codes, with User_3 taking a larger share, and A07, previously shared across about 29 resources, came to be performed only by User_100. Supporting signs: the resource family produced 8 qualifying items where about 0.006 would be expected if nothing had changed — far beyond what chance produces — the shift runs in the same direction across all seven activities, and participation in these activities stayed essentially unchanged, so the difference lies in who performed the work, not in how often the activities occurred. No control-flow item qualified (0 vs about 0.19 expected), which fits an allocation change without structural change. Limitations: the profiles show only the ten most frequent resources plus a residual share, so formerly frequent codes may still handle small amounts of work, and the codes are anonymous, so the evidence shows which codes performed the work, not whether they denote new handlers; a competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that resource identifiers were re-labelled between the windows, which would mimic a reassignment. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, ASSIGN007, CONC001]

Other differences: the elapsed time between consecutive completion events for A09 followed by A23 increased (median from about 1.5 to about 17 hours) while the number of observed gaps fell from 26 to 14; this rests on a single time item, where about 0.013 would be expected by chance — a count an unchanged process can still produce — and on few observations, so it remains a tentative timing observation rather than an established change, and it concerns elapsed time between consecutive completions, not pure waiting or processing time.
