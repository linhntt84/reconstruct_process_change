### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the allocation of work across resources changed for A01, A03, A13, A14, A17 and A21: in the earlier window these activities were handled by one group of resource codes, and in the later window a largely disjoint group took them over, led by User_78 with about 8–12 in 100 of each activity's assignments, while User_61 continued at a similar share and codes such as User_28 dropped out of the frequent set. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006]

This rests on direct allocation evidence and on the screening comparison: the resource family produced 6 qualifying items where about 0.005 would be expected without a change — many times the chance expectation — while control flow produced 0 items against about 0.17 expected and time 1 item against about 0.02. All six allocation profiles point in the same direction, so the pattern is consistent across activities rather than isolated. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006]

Limitations: the resource codes are anonymous, so the evidence shows which codes performed the work, not roles, people or teams; A13 and A14 have identical profiles and likely mirror the same underlying events, so the six items are not fully independent confirmations; and the evidence cannot distinguish work being reassigned to different codes from a different set of resources simply being active in the later window — both would look the same here. [ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004]

Other differences: the elapsed time between the completions of A04 and A01 roughly doubled (median about 22 to 45 hours; mean about 29 to 56 hours). As the only qualifying time item (1 against roughly 0.02 expected without change), it stays within what an unchanged process occasionally produces and does not by itself support a timing hypothesis.
