### Observed differences

- **A13: from widely shared to a single resource.** In the earlier window the activity was spread thinly across many resources: 46 distinct codes shared about 270 assignments, and the busiest code held only about 7 in 100 (concentration index about 0.03). In the later window, one code, User_134, performed all about 277 recorded assignments (concentration index 1.0, top share 100 in 100). [ASSIGN001, CONC001]

- **Longer gaps between A04 and A01 completions.** The elapsed time between these consecutive completions rose across most of the distribution: median from about 25 to about 42 hours, mean from about 27 to about 46 hours, upper quartile from about 38 to about 66 hours, while the fastest quarter of gaps stayed around 5–6 minutes; the gap was observed in essentially all 200 traces in each window. [TWAIT001]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a change in the resource allocation of A13: in the earlier window the activity was shared across many resource codes (46 distinct codes, top share about 7 in 100), whereas in the later window one code, User_134, carried essentially every recorded assignment (about 277 of 277). Two indicators that view this behaviour from different angles agree — the assignment mix changed completely (dissimilarity 1.0) and concentration rose from about 0.03 to 1.0 — both rest on substantial numbers of assignments in each window (about 270 versus about 280), and both exceed all but about 5 in 10000 random re-splits; the resource family produced 3 qualifying items against roughly 0.006 expected if nothing had changed, so chance cannot account for the set, while the control-flow family produced none (0 against about 0.19 expected), consistent with a reallocation without any detected structural change. [ASSIGN001, CONC001, screening]

An important competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is a change in recording rather than in work allocation: if, in the later window, this activity's events were simply attributed to a single resource code, the data would look exactly as observed; the anonymous codes also prevent any statement about roles, people or the trigger behind the shift. [ASSIGN001, CONC001]

Other differences:
- The resources performing A22 rotated — several earlier codes disappeared and new ones appeared — while the work remained spread across many codes with no concentration; this milder redistribution is not part of the main hypothesis. [ASSIGN002]
- The elapsed time between consecutive completions of A04 and A01 increased (median from about 25 to about 42 hours, a distribution shift of about 21 hours). As the only item in the time family, where about 0.014 items were expected by chance, a chance qualification cannot be ruled out, and this evidence cannot tie the timing change to the allocation change.
