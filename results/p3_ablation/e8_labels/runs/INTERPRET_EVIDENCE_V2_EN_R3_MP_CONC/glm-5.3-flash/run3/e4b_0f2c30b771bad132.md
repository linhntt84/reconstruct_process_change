### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility of a change in resource allocation. In the later window, A11 is assigned exclusively to User_123, where earlier about 20 resources shared this activity with the busiest handling about 14 in 100 of its events; at the same time, assignment shares for A03 and A21 shifted toward User_3 (roughly doubling to about 19 and about 30 in 100 of assignments respectively) and User_32, away from a broader group of previously active resources. The concentration change is measured directly by two indicators that agree, the reallocation appears consistently on both other activities, the four resource items are hundreds of times the number expected without a change (about 0.006), and each rests on substantial support (96–200 traces). Limitations: the resource codes are anonymous, so no roles or reasons can be inferred; the two A11 indicators describe the same underlying behaviour and are not independent confirmations; and the evidence cannot rule out that the concentration on a single resource is specific to the later window rather than a lasting allocation. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, CONC001]

Other differences: the shortening of the elapsed time between consecutive A04 and A01 completions (median about 70 → about 13 hours, 200 intervals per window) is a consistent change in timing in its own right, against about 0.017 time items expected by chance; the evidence cannot show whether it shares a cause with the reallocation or reflects a different mix of cases between the windows. No control-flow item qualified, so no structural change accompanies these differences. [TWAIT001, ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, CONC001]
