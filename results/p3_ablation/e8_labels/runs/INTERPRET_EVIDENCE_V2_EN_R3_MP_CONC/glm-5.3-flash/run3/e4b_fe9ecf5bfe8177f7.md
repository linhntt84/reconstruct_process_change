### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility of a change in resource allocation: the execution of A01, A03, A17 and A22 moved from a broad set of previously dominant resources to a smaller group that includes User_40, User_3 and User_49, with the share handled by the long tail dropping by about 10 percentage points on each activity. The signs are direct and consistent: all four activity-level assignment profiles show the same resources gaining and the same set losing; the resource family produced 4 qualifying items where only about 0.005 were expected without change, so chance cannot account for the family as a whole. The four profiles may reflect a single pool-level reallocation rather than four separate changes. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004]

Limitations: the resource codes are anonymous and each profile shows only the ten largest shares, so the evidence cannot show whether the earlier leaders stopped executing these activities or merely lost share, and it cannot indicate why the allocation changed. An important competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that some resources absent from the earlier top ten were already active inside the tail, which would make the individual-level turnover look larger than it is; the aggregate shift from tail to a few leaders is nonetheless well supported. This is a single time item (1 found vs about 0.02 expected by chance), so it is a plausible timing change in its own right but rests on one indicator, and the evidence does not link it to the reallocation.
