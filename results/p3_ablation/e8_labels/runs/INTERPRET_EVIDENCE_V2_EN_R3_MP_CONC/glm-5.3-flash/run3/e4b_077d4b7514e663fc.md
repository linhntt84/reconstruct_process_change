### Observed differences

- Work on the offer-handling activities was reassigned to a largely different set of resources. In the earlier window, activities such as A01, A03, A13, A17 and A21 were each spread over many resources, with leading individuals holding roughly 6–12% shares each (for example User_49 with about 12 in 100 of A21) and a long tail of many others taking about 30–47%. In the later window, most of those earlier leaders no longer appear and a largely different set holds similar shares (for example User_56 with about 11 in 100 of A21), while the tail share grew modestly (about 38 in 100 to 45 in 100 for A01). The same resources lose and the same others gain in every one of the seven tested activities. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, ASSIGN007]
- A08 shows the extreme form: in the earlier window its events (in about 54 in 100 traces) were spread over 17 distinct resources, the largest handling about 18 in 100; in the later window (about 60 in 100 traces) a single resource, User_121, handled all of them — top-resource share 100%, concentration index 0.11 to 1.0. [ASSIGN003, CONC001]
- The elapsed time between the completions of A04 and A01 lengthened in its upper tail: the median interval was about 24 hours before and about 25 hours after, but the 75th percentile rose from about 31 to about 71 hours and the mean from about 26.5 to about 44 hours (200 intervals per window; the difference exceeds all but about 1 in 2000 random re-splits). [TWAIT001]
- A minor timing difference: the practically instantaneous step from A20 to A10 appeared in 7 traces before and 17 after, with sub-second elapsed times in both windows (about 0.09 s to 0.06 s); the difference is a few hundredths of a second and rests on few observations. [TWAIT002]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a change in resource allocation: across A01, A03, A08, A13, A14, A17 and A21, execution shifted from one group of resources to a largely disjoint group, and A08 went from being shared across 17 distinct resources to being handled entirely by User_121. The resource family produced 8 qualifying items where about 0.005 were expected by chance alone, a count an unchanged process cannot plausibly produce; the evidence is direct (assignment shares and concentration measure allocation itself) and consistent (the same earlier leaders drop out and the same newcomers take over in every tested activity, and the concentration item matches the A08 assignment profile). [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, ASSIGN007, CONC001]

Limitations: the resource codes are anonymous, so no roles, teams or reasons can be read from the data, and the evidence cannot distinguish a deliberate reassignment of work from a change in which resources were active on these activities in the later period. The assignment items also overlap — A13 and A14 show identical profiles and likely reflect the same events — so they are not independent confirmations. No control-flow item qualified (0 against about 0.16 expected without change), so the evidence shows no structural change accompanying the reallocation. [ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005]

Other differences: the interval between the completions of A04 and A01 grew in its upper tail (median about 24–25 hours in both windows; 75th percentile about 31 to 71 hours; mean about 26.5 to 44 hours). This is a timing hypothesis in its own right and needs no structural counterpart; what the evidence cannot rule out is that this tail growth is connected to the same reallocation rather than being an independent timing change. A second time item concerns the A20 to A10 step (7 vs 17 traces, sub-second intervals in both windows); with so few observations it carries little weight. [TWAIT001, TWAIT002]
