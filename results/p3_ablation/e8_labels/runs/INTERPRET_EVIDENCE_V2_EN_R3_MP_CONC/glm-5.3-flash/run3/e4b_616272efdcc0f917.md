### Observed differences

- Resource allocation across the offer-handling activities was redistributed. In the earlier window, work on A01, A03, A13, A17 and A22 was spread thinly over many anonymous resources: the largest single share was about 7–13 in 100 assignments, and roughly half the work sat in a long tail. In the later window, a largely different group of resources carried this work: User_3 rose from about 3 in 100 (or less) to about 12–15 in 100 on each of these activities, several resources that had been absent or marginal took shares of roughly 3–9 in 100, and a set of previously active resources lost most of their share (User_79 fell from about 8 in 100 to about 3–6 in 100). Each profile difference is larger than those in more than 999 of 1,000 random re-splits. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, ASSIGN007]
- Handling of A11 became concentrated on a single resource. In the earlier window it was spread over 23 distinct resources with the top share about 15 in 100 (User_100); in the later window User_100 performed all of the roughly 100 A11 assignments (top share 100 in 100, distinct resources 23 down to 1, concentration index 0.06 to 1.0). [ASSIGN003, CONC001]
- The mix of outgoing transitions from A10 shifted: A07 rose from about 10 in 100 to about 24 in 100 of those transitions, while A16 fell from about 57 to about 44 in 100 and A11 from about 17 to about 11 in 100 (about 220 transitions before, 258 after; larger than in about 995 of 1,000 random re-splits). [DIR001]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a change in resource allocation: across the offer-handling activities (A01, A03, A13/A14, A17, A22), execution was reassigned from one group of anonymous resources to a largely different group — User_3 and several previously marginal resources took over work, while a set of previously active resources withdrew — and A11, previously shared across 23 resources, came to be handled exclusively by User_100. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, ASSIGN007, CONC001]

This rests on direct and consistent evidence: the resource family produced 8 qualifying items where about 0.006 would be expected if nothing had changed, all seven assignment profiles moved in the same direction with the same gaining and losing groups, and the concentration of A11 is measured directly (distinct resources and top share) rather than inferred. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, ASSIGN007, CONC001]

Limitations: ASSIGN003 and CONC001 describe the same traces and count as one finding, and the A13 and A14 profiles are identical and may reflect the same events; the evidence also cannot rule out that the shift stems from a change in how resources were recorded (for example a systematic relabelling) rather than a genuine reallocation, since the codes are anonymous and no roles can be inferred. [ASSIGN003, CONC001, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005]

Other differences: the routing mix out of A10 moved toward A07 and away from A16 and A11, but this is the only control-flow item, a count in line with the chance expectation for that family (1 item versus 0.2 expected), so on its own it does not establish a routing change and may be a sampling fluctuation. [DIR001]
