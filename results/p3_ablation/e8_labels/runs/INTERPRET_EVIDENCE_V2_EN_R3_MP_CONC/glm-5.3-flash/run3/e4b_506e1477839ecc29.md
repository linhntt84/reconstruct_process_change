### Observed differences

- Work assignment shifted between two groups of resource codes across the offer and application activities. In the earlier window, the most frequent assignees of A01, A03, A13/A14 and A22 were a set of resource codes each holding roughly 4–8 in 100 of the assignments; in the later window, most of these codes no longer appear among the ten most frequent assignees, and a largely different set of codes holds similar individual shares instead. User_67, already the most frequent assignee before, grew further (for A22 from about 10 to 17 in 100), User_56 appears only in the later window with about 7–9 in 100, and User_61 (about 6 in 100 before) is no longer among the ten most frequent for any of these activities. - For A22, assignments also became more concentrated: the combined share of all resources outside the ten most frequent fell from about 39 to about 29 in 100, while for the other activities this rest share stayed roughly stable at about 45–50 in 100 in both windows. - The elapsed time from the completion of A09 to the completion of A23, measured between consecutive completions, was longer in the later window: the median interval rose from about 1 hour to about 14 hours and the mean from about 4 to about 15 hours, based on 29 such intervals before and 25 after. ### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the allocation of work to resources changed between the windows: across A01, A03, A13, A14 and A22, a group of resource codes that had each handled roughly 4–8 in 100 of the assignments (including User_61) mostly fell out of the ten most frequent assignees, a largely new group (including User_56) took over shares of similar size, and User_67 grew to the largest single share (up to about 17 in 100 for A22). The resource family produced 5 evidence items against about 0.005 expected without change, far above what chance produces, and all five show the same direction of shift, each difference being larger than in about 999.5 of 1,000 random re-splits and backed by 128–275 assignments per activity. The control-flow family produced no items (0 against about 0.18 expected), so no structural change is detected alongside the re-allocation. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005]

The evidence shows only that shares moved between groups of anonymous resource codes; it cannot distinguish a deliberate re-allocation from, for example, a different mix of cases entering the later window or changes in which codes are available, and it does not resolve resources outside the ten most frequent. A13 and A14 show identical profiles, so the five items describe one coherent shift rather than five independent changes. [ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004]

Other differences: the elapsed time between consecutive completions of A09 and A23 increased (median from about 1 hour to about 14 hours; 29 vs 25 intervals). This is the only time item, against about 0.02 expected by chance in the time family, so on its own it is within what an unchanged process can produce; the evidence cannot establish a timing change or tie it to the re-allocation.
