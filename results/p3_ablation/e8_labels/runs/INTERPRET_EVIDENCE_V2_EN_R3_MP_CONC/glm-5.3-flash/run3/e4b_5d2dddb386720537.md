### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the offer-creation step — A13 with its completion event A14 — was repeated less often in the later window: the step remained present in every trace, but its mean occurrences per trace dropped from about 1.4 to about 1.2 and the share of traces in which it recurred dropped from about 33 in 100 to about 19 in 100. [OCC001, OCC002]

Supporting signs: the change is measured directly by the occurrence indicator on all 200 traces in each window; frequency and recurrence fell together while participation stayed at 100%, pointing to a change in repetition rather than in presence; and the control-flow family produced three qualifying items against roughly 0.2 expected if nothing had changed, so the pattern as a whole is unlikely for a random split of an unchanged process. [OCC001, OCC002, LAG001]

Limitations: the two occurrence items have identical profiles and likely reflect one underlying behaviour rather than two independent confirmations, and the evidence cannot show which traces or variants stopped repeating the step. An important competing explanation the evidence cannot fully rule out is an uneven distribution of repeats across a finite sample, although the difference exceeds about 99.5% of random re-splits. [OCC001, OCC002]

Other differences: The number of events lying between A17 and A10 increased (three-position gaps from about 25 to about 44 in 100 observed pairs; one-position gaps from about 9 to about 3). The evidence does not establish whether this is a consequence of the reduced repetition or an independent difference, since it does not show which activities now lie between the two. [LAG001]
