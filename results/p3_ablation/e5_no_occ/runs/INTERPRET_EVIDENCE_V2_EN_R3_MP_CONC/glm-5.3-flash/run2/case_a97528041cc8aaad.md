### Observed differences

- Two groups of activities exchanged their relative order. In the earlier window, A20, A22 and A23 ran about 40% into the trace (present in essentially every trace), A25 about 46%, A29 about 65%, and A19, A21 and A24 about two-thirds in (each appearing in roughly 3–4 of 10 traces). In the later window, A19, A21 and A24 ran about 36–38% in and A25 about 39%, while A20, A22 and A23 moved to about 64–65% in and A29 to about 58% [POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006, POS007, POS008, POS009].

- Pairwise order relations flipped consistently across all 21 tested pairs between these groups. A20 preceded A25 in about 98 in 100 traces before, while A25 preceded A20 in about 97 in 100 after; the same near-complete reversal appears between A20/A22/A23 and A29, and between A22/A23 and A25. A19, A21 and A24 moved from running after A20/A22/A23/A25/A29 in about 97 in 100 shared traces to running before them in about 96–97 in 100 shared traces [REL015, REL020, REL022, REL023, REL024, REL029, REL030, REL032, REL033].

It now co-occurs with A15, A20, A22, A23, A25, A29, A30, A40, A45 and A46 in about 65 in 100 traces (previously about 15 in 100) and precedes A20/A22/A23/A25/A29/A30/A40/A45/A46 in about 57–60 in 100; A14 now leads to A02 in about 60 in 100 of its outgoing transitions (previously about 1 in 100, with A15 falling from about 84 to 37 in 100), A02 leads to A15 in about 87 in 100, and A14 is followed directly by A02 in about 98 in 100 of the observed cases [POS001, REL001, REL004, REL006, REL007, REL009, REL011, REL012, REL013, REL014, DIR001, DIR004, LAG001, LAG005].

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the relative execution order of two groups of activities was reversed: A19, A21, A24 and A25, which previously ran after A20, A22 and A23, now run before them, while A29 kept a middle position and flipped its order relative to both groups; the handovers changed accordingly, with A19/A21/A24 now passing flow to A25 instead of A30, and A20/A22/A23 passing flow to A30 instead of A25 [REL015, REL022, REL023, POS002, POS003, POS005, POS008, DIR005, DIR006, DIR010].

The support is direct and consistent: relational evidence shows the order flip in every tested pair between the groups, almost completely for the tightly coupled pairs (about 98 in 100 one way before, about 97 in 100 the other way after, each based on all 200 traces per window) [REL015, REL022, REL029, REL032]; position profiles shift in the matching direction for each involved activity [POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006, POS007]; and the screening produced 84 qualifying items against fewer than 1 expected without a change, so the pattern as a whole cannot be attributed to random re-splits of stable behaviour [REL015, REL022, DIR005, POS003]. The many pair items describe one underlying reordering rather than independent confirmations.

Limitations: the indicators use event positions rather than clock time, so the evidence establishes a changed ordering constraint between the groups, not a shift in clock timing; and it cannot fully separate a reordering within each case from a changed mix of pre-existing process variants between the windows. A29's own position moves only slightly (about 0.65 to 0.58) while its relations to both groups flip, so its exact placement between the groups remains only partly resolved [POS009, REL019, REL023].

Other differences: A02 changed from a rare, mid-trace activity (about 15 in 100 traces) to a frequent, early one (about 65 in 100), typically inserted directly after A14 and before A15 and the other main activities; A14's dominant successor changed from A15 (about 84 in 100) to A02 (about 60 in 100) [POS001, REL001, DIR004, LAG005]. Scattered successor redistributions at A06 and A08 rest on few observations and are not part of the main hypothesis [DIR002, DIR003].
