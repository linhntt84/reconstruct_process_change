### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the choice between two existing alternative paths after A15 flipped: the path on which A16, A17 and A18 run together rose from about 18–19 in 100 cases to about 79 in 100, while the alternative path with A19, A20 and A21 fell from about 82 in 100 to about 21 in 100. A15 still precedes the executed group and A25 still runs with it, and the two groups never co-occur, so this reads as a shift in how often existing paths are taken rather than a path appearing, disappearing or being bypassed. [DIR001, REL001, REL004, REL007, REL009, REL012, REL022, REL024]

The flip is seen directly in A15's outgoing transitions and consistently across all 28 qualifying items, which describe the same swap with the same magnitude (about 60 in 100 traces) and matching before/after profiles; both branches are well observed in each window (37–42 vs 158–163 traces), so a rare variant appearing in one window only is not a plausible reading. Screening qualified 28 items where about 0.41 would be expected without a change, so the item set as a whole is far beyond what a stable process typically produces. [DIR001, REL007, REL022, screening]

Limitation: the evidence locates the flip at the branch choice after A15 but cannot show what determines the choice — a changed decision at A15 and a changed mix of cases reaching A15 look identical in these indicators. [DIR001]
