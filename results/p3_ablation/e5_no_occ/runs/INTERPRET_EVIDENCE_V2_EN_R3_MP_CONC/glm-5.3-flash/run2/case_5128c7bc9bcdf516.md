### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, ordering in the segment formed by A15, A17, A18, A19, A20 and A21 was variable: each activity had several successors with sizable shares (A15 led to A18 in about 62 in 100 outgoing transitions and to A19, A21 or A20 in about 12–14 in 100 each; A18 led directly to A27 in about 53 in 100; A26 split evenly between A19 and A17). In the later window each has essentially one successor: A26→A17, A17→A15, A15→A18, A18→A19, A19→A21, A21→A20 and A20→A27 each account for about 99–100 in 100 observed transitions, and the former alternatives, including the direct A18→A27 and A20→A27 links, have nearly disappeared. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006, DIR007]
- Pairwise order became fixed, and for some pairs reversed. A15 preceded A19 in only about 26 in 100 traces before (A19 came first in about 75) and in about 100 in 100 in the later window; A18 preceded A19 in about 12 in 100 before versus about 100 in 100 after; A15/A21 and A17/A19 occurred in both orders roughly half the time before and now always run A15-first and A17-first. Both activities of every measured pair still occur together in all traces in both windows, so participation did not change — only the order did. [REL001, REL003, REL004, REL006, REL007, REL008]
- Step distances collapsed onto a single pattern. In the later window the consecutive pairs of one chain are immediate in about 99–100 in 100 cases (A26→A17, A17→A15, A15→A18, A19→A21, A21→A20, A20→A27), and longer pairs sit exactly where that chain puts them (A17→A18 at 2 steps in about 99 in 100, A15→A21 at 3 steps, A15→A20 at 4 steps, A15→A27 and A26→A21 at 5 steps). Before, these distances were spread over 1–5 steps. [LAG001, LAG006, LAG007, LAG004, LAG003, LAG005, LAG029, LAG013, LAG020, LAG022, LAG025]
- Relative event positions shifted consistently with this: A15, A17 and A18 now appear earlier in the trace (mean relative position about 0.64 to 0.59, 0.59 to 0.56 and 0.68 to 0.62), while A19, A21 and A20 appear later (about 0.59 to 0.65, 0.64 to 0.68 and 0.68 to 0.71). These are positions, not clock times. [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a reordering of the segment formed by A15, A17, A18, A19, A20 and A21, with A26 upstream and A27 downstream: in the earlier window their relative order varied from case to case, while in the later window they execute in one fixed sequence, A26 → A17 → A15 → A18 → A19 → A21 → A20 → A27, so that A27 is now reached only at the end of the chain instead of directly from A18 or A20 mid-sequence. [DIR001, DIR003, DIR005, DIR007, REL001, REL006, LAG013, LAG020]

Supporting signs: the change is shown directly by relational evidence — order reversals and mixed-to-fixed transitions with co-occurrence at 100 in 100 in both windows, which rules out a participation change; the successor profiles, step distances and positions all point to the same single chain; and 50 control-flow items qualified where only about 0.36 would be expected by chance in an unchanged process, far too many to attribute to sampling variation. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, REL007, REL008, LAG001, LAG024, POS001, POS006]

Limitations and competing explanation: the evidence cannot distinguish a newly enforced single sequence from a shift in which several fixed-order variants were replaced by one surviving variant, since both produce the same profiles; POS and LAG are position-based rather than clock-based; and a few reverse-direction lag items (A19→A15, A19→A18, A20→A18, A21→A18, A26→A20) rest on a single after-window observation, so they only weakly corroborate that those orders disappeared. [LAG014, LAG015, LAG019, LAG021, LAG028]

Other differences: none — all qualifying items describe this same reordering rather than separate changes. [DIR001, DIR004, POS004, REL004, LAG016, LAG027]
