### Observed differences

- In the earlier window the segment A19 → A25 → A20 ran in every case between A41 and A26: each of these three activities co-occurred with A14, A15, A26, A41, A46 and A47 in 100 in 100 traces, in a fixed order (about 97–99 in 100 cases). In the later window the segment runs in only about 53 in 100 cases, and in about 46 in 100 cases the surrounding activities run without it; when it does run, its internal order is unchanged. [REL010, REL016, REL017, REL019, REL022, REL023]
- The segment is skipped rather than reordered: about 96 in 100 of A41's outgoing transitions went to A19 in the earlier window versus about 47 in 100 in the later window, while A41 → A26, not observed earlier, now carries about 39 in 100; A26 follows A41 within one step in about 40 in 100 later observations, where earlier it followed within four steps in about 91 in 100. [DIR008, LAG008]
- A06 is executed far less often: about 71 in 100 cases earlier versus about 26 in 100 later. Earlier it sat between A14 and A15 (A14 → A06 about 69 in 100 of A14's outgoing transitions, A06 → A15 about 92 in 100 of A06's, and A14 → A06 within one step in about 98 in 100 observations); later, A14 → A06 is about 12 in 100 and A14 → A15 within one step rises from about 17 to about 41 in 100 observations. A06 also sits later in the case (mean position about 0.18 to about 0.43) and is followed by varied activities (A15 about 37, A45 about 12, A26 about 11 in 100). The two skips are largely independent: in about 35 in 100 later cases neither A06 nor the segment appears, and each also appears without the other. [POS001, DIR003, DIR005, LAG003, LAG004, LAG005, REL001, REL003]
- A02, A04 and A08 appear in more cases in the later window (A08 in about 4 in 100 cases earlier versus about 21 in 100 later; A02 about 9 to 20; A04 about 5 to 15), with redistributed outgoing transitions. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR004]
- Smaller shifts: A14 occurs slightly later and more variably within cases, A26 slightly earlier, and the immediate A46 → A47 step weakens from about 97 to about 87 in 100 of A46's outgoing transitions. [POS002, POS003, DIR010, LAG009]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that, in the later window, two parts of the process are skipped far more often: the fixed segment A19 → A25 → A20 between A41 and A26, previously executed in every case, now executes in about half of the cases and is bypassed in the rest (A41 then leading directly to A26), and A06, previously executed in about 71 in 100 cases between A14 and A15, now executes in about 26 in 100. The participation drops are shown directly and consistently across many activity pairs whose partners' participation is unchanged (co-occurrence falling from 1.00 to about 0.53 for the segment and from 0.71 to 0.26 for A06), the order of what still runs is preserved, the bypass edge A41 → A26 is observed directly, 52 control-flow items qualified where fewer than one was expected for an unchanged process, and every item's difference exceeds about 995 of 1000 random re-splits. [REL010, REL013, REL018, REL019, REL023, DIR008, LAG008, POS001, DIR005, LAG004]

Other differences: A02, A04 and A08 occur in more cases in the later window; A14's position is slightly later and more variable and A26's slightly earlier, and A46 → A47 is slightly less immediate — none of these is part of the main hypothesis. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR004, POS002, POS003, LAG009]

Limitations: the evidence cannot show whether the skipped executions were removed, displaced to other activities (for example A02, A04 or A08), or confined to a subpopulation of cases; positions and lags use event order rather than clock time, and some lag items rest on few observations (A06 → A19 on 7 later observations, A15 → A26 on 1 earlier observation), so no timing claim is made. An important competing explanation that cannot be ruled out is that the later window mixes two kinds of cases, with and without these segments, rather than a single process in which the segments became optional. [LAG001, LAG007]
