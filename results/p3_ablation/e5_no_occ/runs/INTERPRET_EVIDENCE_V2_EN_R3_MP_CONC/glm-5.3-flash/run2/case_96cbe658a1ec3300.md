### Observed differences

- The relative order of two groups of activities reversed. In the earlier window, A26 and A27 came before A35, A36 and A41 in about 98–99 in 100 traces; in the later window, A35, A36 and A41 came before A26 and A27 in about 97–98 in 100. All five activities still occurred together in every trace in both windows, so participation itself did not change. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006]
- A25's successors changed: before, about 98 in 100 of its outgoing transitions led to A27; in the later window they split almost equally among A36 (~31 in 100), A41 (~33 in 100) and A35 (~32 in 100), with A27 down to about 0.5 in 100. [DIR001]
- A26's successors changed: before, its outgoing transitions split among A41 (~38 in 100), A35 (~33 in 100) and A36 (~28 in 100); in the later window, about 98 in 100 led directly to A30. [DIR002]
- The three activities A35, A36 and A41 now lead to A27 instead of A30: before, each sent roughly 28–34 in 100 of its outgoing transitions to A30; in the later window A30 receives at most about 0.5 in 100 from each, and each has a new edge to A27 (~31–33 in 100). Their edges among themselves kept similar shares. [DIR003, DIR004, DIR005]
- Step distances flipped accordingly: A25 was followed by A27 within one step in about 99 in 100 traces before, while in the later window A27 follows A25 about four steps later in about 90 in 100 traces (five steps in about 10 in 100); A26 reached A30 in about 4–5 steps before, but in the later window does so in a single step in about 99 in 100 traces. [LAG001, LAG003]
- Relative positions in the trace moved consistently with this: A35, A36 and A41 now sit earlier (mean relative position about 0.58 before vs about 0.42 in the later window), while A26 (about 0.53 → 0.62) and A27 (about 0.38 → 0.47) sit later. [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the group of three activities A35, A36 and A41 changed its place in the sequence. In the earlier window the behaviour is consistent with the run A25 → A27 … A26 → (A35/A36/A41 in varying internal order) → A30; in the later window it is consistent with A25 → (A35/A36/A41) → A27 … A26 → A30. The reversal is shown directly: all six activity pairs between {A26, A27} and the group flip from one fixed order to the other, while the activities still co-occur in 100 in 100 traces in both windows. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006]

Supporting signs: the successor changes at A25, A26 and the three group activities match this relocation, the step-distance flips for A25→A27 and A26→A30 agree with it, and the position shifts of all five activities point the same way. Because each activity appears in essentially all traces in both windows, this is a reordering of existing activities rather than an activity being added, removed or made optional. With 26 qualifying items against roughly 0.41 expected for the 83 tested units if nothing had changed, the set of differences is far beyond what random re-splits of a stable process produce. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, LAG001, LAG003, POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005]

Limitations: all items describe one underlying trace behaviour, so they are not independent confirmations; the internal order of the three-activity group and the segment lying between A27 and A26 are not covered by the evidence. A competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that the same pattern was produced by rewiring the connections around a stationary group rather than by moving the group; the observed traces look the same either way.

Other differences: none beyond the reordering above; the remaining distance items echo the same rewiring (pairs adjacent before but no longer within five steps after, such as A26 with A35/A36/A41 and the group with A30, and pairs adjacent after but not before, such as A25 with A35 and A35 with A27), but each rests on only 1–2 observations in one window. [LAG002, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006, LAG007, LAG008, LAG009, LAG010]
