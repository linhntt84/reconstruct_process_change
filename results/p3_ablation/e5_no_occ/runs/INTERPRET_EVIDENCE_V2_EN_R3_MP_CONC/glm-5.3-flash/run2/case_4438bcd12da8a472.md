### Observed differences

- The routing out of A36 changed sharply: in the earlier window, of A36's observed outgoing transitions about 47 in 100 went to A14 and about 53 in 100 went directly to A21; in the later window about 995 in 1000 go to A14 and almost none go directly to A21. [DIR001]
- The block A14 → A20 → A15 was optional before and is nearly always executed now: in the earlier window A14, A20 and A15 co-occurred in about 47 in 100 traces, while in about 53 in 100 traces none of them appeared; in the later window they co-occur in about 995 in 1000 traces, always in the fixed order A14, then A20, then A15. A37 shows the same pattern, co-occurring with A14 in about 47 in 100 traces before and about 995 in 1000 after. [REL001, REL002, REL007, REL005, REL010, REL014]
- A21 occurred in essentially every trace in both windows, but its placement changed: before, in about 53 in 100 traces it appeared without A14 and A36 led to it immediately (distance 1 in about 53 in 100 cases); after, it co-occurs with A14 in about 995 in 1000 traces and sits four event positions after A36 (distance 4 in about 995 in 1000). Mean event positions shifted only slightly (A36 from about 0.33 to 0.30 of the trace, A21 from about 0.41 to 0.43). [REL003, REL004, LAG001, POS001, POS002]
- A40 was present in about 60 in 100 traces in both windows, but its coupling changed: before it appeared without A14 in about 33 in 100 traces, while after it never appeared without A14 (co-occurrence about 62 in 100, with A14 alone in the remaining traces). [REL006, REL011, REL015]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

One possible reading of this evidence is that the routing at A36 changed so that the direct transition A36 → A21, which bypassed the A14–A20–A15 block, has fallen out of use. In the earlier window the process ran as two variants of roughly 47 and 53 in 100 cases: one going directly from A36 to A21 (with A14, A20, A15 and A37 absent) and one executing A36 → A14 → A20 → A15 with A21 following four positions later; in the later window about 995 in 1000 cases execute the block, A36 leads to A14 in almost all of its observed outgoing transitions, and A21 consistently follows four positions after A36. This is a change in participation and routing structure — the bypass route nearly disappeared and the A14/A20/A15/A37 block became part of almost every execution — rather than a mere rescaling of shares between equally used paths. [DIR001, REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL007, REL010, REL014, LAG001]

The supporting signs are direct and consistent: the routing change at A36 is observed directly with full support in both windows (200 outgoing-transition observations each), the co-occurrence, order and lag items all align with the same picture, and 19 items qualified against roughly 0.27 expected by chance if nothing had changed, so the ensemble is not what a stable process typically produces. Note that several REL items re-describe the same traces (for example, the A14/A15/A20 co-occurrence appears in three items), so they corroborate the picture but are not independent confirmations. [DIR001, REL001, REL002, REL007, LAG001]

A competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out: with 200 traces per window, it cannot be distinguished whether the direct A36 → A21 path was removed entirely or is still available but rarely chosen (it was observed in about 1 in 200 after-traces). [LAG001, DIR001]

Other differences: A40 remains optional (present in about 60 in 100 traces before and about 62 in 100 after) but is no longer executed without A14, whereas before it appeared without A14 in about a third of all traces; it now runs only alongside A14, usually after it. [REL006, REL011, REL015]
