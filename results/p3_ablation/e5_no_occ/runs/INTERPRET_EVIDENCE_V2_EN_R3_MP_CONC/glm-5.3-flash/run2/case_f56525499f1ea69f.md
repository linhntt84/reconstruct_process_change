- Two blocks of activities that were strict alternatives in the earlier window are executed together in the later window. Earlier, the block A33/A35/A36 ran in about 49 in 100 traces and the block A37/A38/A39 in about 52 in 100, and cross-group pairs such as A33 with A37 never co-occurred (0 of 200 traces) — every trace contained exactly one of the two blocks. ### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the segment between A30 and A31 changed from running one of two alternative activity blocks to running both in sequence: earlier, each case executed either the block A33/A35/A36 (about 49 in 100 traces) or the block A37/A38/A39 (about 52 in 100), never both, while in the later window nearly every case runs the A33/A35/A36 block first and the A37/A38/A39 block afterwards, with the order within each block still unfixed. [REL039, REL046, POS006, POS009] The change is shown directly by the co-occurrence reversal and is consistent across routing (A30 now feeds the first block; the first block now leads into the second instead of into A31) and step distances (A30 to the second block, and first block to A31, moved from 1–3 to 4–5 positions); with 80 qualifying control-flow items against roughly 0.81 expected if nothing had changed, chance sampling cannot account for the pattern as a whole. [DIR006, DIR007, LAG003, LAG006] Limitations: all measures use event positions rather than clock time, so nothing can be said about timing; the evidence cannot show what now occupies the few positions between the two blocks, nor whether the second block runs in literally every case or in about 98 in 100. [LAG003, REL046] An important competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is a shift in the mix of case types between the two sampled windows rather than a modification of the process itself, although the near-universal presence of the new pattern across 200 later traces makes pure sampling variation unlikely. [POS009]
