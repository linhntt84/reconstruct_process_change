### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, all 200 traces contained the same chain: A20, then the three activities A31, A32 and A37 in a mixed order (each taking roughly a third of A20's outgoing transitions), then A23, A21, A22 and A26. Every one of these activities appeared in 200 of 200 traces, every pair of them co-occurred in every trace, and A21 was always followed by A22. [DIR001, DIR002, LAG001, LAG005, REL001, REL005, REL009, REL020]
- In the later window, A20 is followed directly by A23 in 199 of 200 traces; the A20→A23 distance fell from four steps to one, and A23's mean position moved from about 0.45 to about 0.39 of the trace. [DIR001, LAG001, POS004]
- In the later window, the group A31/A32/A37 runs in only about 47 in 100 traces (94 of 200), always with all three members together and still in a mixed internal order, and its mean position moved from about 0.40 to about 0.58 of the trace: it now executes after A21, whereas before it always preceded A21 and A23 — the old order survives in only about 1 in 200 traces. Ordered pairs linking A20 or A23 to the group within five steps, seen in every trace before, now appear in about 1 in 200 traces. [POS006, POS007, POS008, REL006, REL014, REL020, LAG002, LAG006]
- In the later window, the group and A22 have become alternatives: A22 appears in about 54 in 100 traces (previously all 200), A21 is followed by A22 in about 54% of its outgoing transitions and by the group in about 46%, and A22 co-occurs with any group member in only about 1 in 200 traces, where before they always shared a trace. [POS003, REL001, REL005, REL011, REL012, REL013, DIR002]
- Both branches reconverge on A26: A21 reaches A26 two steps later on the A22 path (about 53% of traces) and four steps later via the group (about 47%); each group member leads to A26 in roughly three out of ten of its outgoing transitions. [LAG005, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, REL010, REL017]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the three-activity group A31, A32, A37 was relocated and made an alternative to A22. Before, the group always ran between A20 and A23, and A22 always ran between A21 and A26; in the later window A20 leads straight to A23, and after A21 each trace takes one of two paths — A22 then A26 in about 53 in 100 traces, or the group A31/A32/A37 (in its unchanged mixed internal order) then A26 in about 47 in 100, with A22 and the group practically never in the same trace. [REL006, REL011, REL014, REL020, DIR001, DIR002, LAG005]

The reordering is shown directly rather than inferred: relational items flip the group from preceding A21 and A23 to following them; A20's successor profile changes to almost only A23; the A20→A23 lag drops from four steps to one; participation of the group and of A22 falls from every trace to about half; and the position shifts of the group (about 0.40 → 0.58) and A23 (about 0.45 → 0.39) match the same account. The 43 qualifying control-flow items compare with about 0.36 expected if nothing had changed, and the earlier window shows a single behaviour across all 200 traces, so sampling noise or a rare one-window variant does not plausibly explain the pattern. [DIR001, LAG001, REL014, REL011, POS003, POS004, POS006, POS007, POS008, screening]

Limitations: the indicators cannot distinguish whether the group became skippable or A22 became conditionally replaced by the group — both readings fit the same data — and the exact 53/47 split rests on one window of 200 traces. [REL011, DIR002]

Other differences: only sparse residual transitions — A20→A31 in about 1 in 200 of A20's outgoing transitions and A32→A23 in about 1 in 100 of that activity's outgoing transitions — too rare in this sample to characterise. [DIR001, DIR004]
