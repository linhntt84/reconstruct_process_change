- In the earlier window the fragment ran as a single fixed sequence: A21 was always followed by A22 (100% of A21's observed outgoing transitions), A21 and A22 co-occurred in every trace with A21 first, A22 and A23 co-occurred in every trace with A22 first, and the distance from A21 to A23 was always two event positions. ### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a structural change around the A21–A23 fragment: in the later window an alternative branch exists in which A22 is not executed and the three activities A27, A28 and A33 run between A21 and A23 in no fixed internal order, while the original sequence A21 → A22 → A23 continues in about half the traces. Participation changed accordingly — A27, A28 and A33 went from unobserved to about 49 in 100 traces, and A22 from essentially all traces to about 51 in 100 — so this is an added branch with a new activity block, not a re-weighting of previously observed paths. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, POS001, POS002, POS003, REL001, REL005, REL006, REL009, REL012, LAG001]

The judgement rests on 22 qualifying control-flow items against fewer than 0.25 expected for an unchanged process, on direct routing evidence at A21, and on mutually consistent relational, positional and lag evidence; the many relation items describe one underlying difference rather than independent confirmations, but the indicator families (routing, position, relation, lag) all point the same way. A competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out: zero participation of A27/A28/A33 in the earlier window only means they were not observed there, so a very rare pre-existing variant that later became frequent cannot be distinguished from a newly added branch; the evidence also cannot show what determines which route a trace takes.
