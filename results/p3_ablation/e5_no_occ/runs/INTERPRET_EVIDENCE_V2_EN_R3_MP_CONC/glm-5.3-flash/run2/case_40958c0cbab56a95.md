### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, A26, A27 and A28 formed a fixed block: each ran in all 200 traces, the three always occurred together, with no fixed order among themselves (e.g., A26 before A27 in about 52 of 100 cases, the reverse in about 45). In the later window the block has nearly disappeared — each of the three appears in only about 3 of 200 traces, and they almost never co-occur. [POS004, POS005, POS006, REL036, REL037, REL042]
- In the later window, A19, A20 and A21 — previously marginal, at roughly 1–2 in 100 traces each — appear in about 30, 33 and 38 in 100 traces respectively, and exactly one of them per case: pairwise co-occurrence among the three is near zero and their shares sum to essentially every case. - A44's outgoing behaviour flipped: earlier about 82% of its observed outgoing transitions led into the block (A26/A27/A28); later about 79% lead to A19/A20/A21, on roughly 200 outgoing transitions in each window. [DIR007]
- The slot between A44 and A29 was preserved but re-populated. Earlier, the block co-occurred with A44 in 100 of 100 cases (A44 first in about 97–99) and with A29 in 100 of 100 cases (block first in about 97–99). Later, A44 and A29 each occur without the block in about 98–99 of 100 cases, while every occurrence of A19/A20/A21 sits between them: co-occurring with A44 in about 30–37 of 100 cases (A44 first) and with A29 in about 30–37 (A29 after). - The distance from A44 to A29 shortened by about two events: earlier 78% at lag 4 and 22% at lag 5; later 79% at lag 2 and 19% at lag 3 (about 200 observations per window). [LAG009]
- In the later window, about 95% of the observed outgoing transitions of A19, A20 and A21 lead directly to A29; their earlier outgoing profiles rest on only 2–4 observations and are weak. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003]
- A29's mean event position moved slightly earlier (0.44 to 0.40 of trace length) and A44's slightly later (0.31 to 0.33), consistent with a shorter middle section. [POS007, POS008]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the section between A44 and A29 was restructured: the block of A26, A27 and A28, which executed in full in every earlier case, was replaced in the later window by exactly one activity chosen among A19, A20 and A21 (about 30, 33 and 38 in 100 cases respectively), occupying the same slot — after A44, before A29. Screening makes the pattern unlikely to be a sampling artefact: 74 control-flow items qualified where roughly 0.65 would be expected without change, and the reading rests on items with strong support in both windows — the near-total participation swap (200/200 traces to 3/200, and 2–4/200 to 60–75/200, with zero co-occurrence among the new activities) [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006, REL013, REL014, REL021], A44's successor profile flipping from the block to the new activities [DIR007], the block's relations to A44 and A29 transferring to the new activities [REL038, REL039, REL043, REL044, REL047, REL048, REL018, REL019, REL025, REL026, REL032, REL033], and the A44-to-A29 distance shortening by about two events [LAG009]. Limitations: positions and lags are event positions, not clock times; several flagged items around the old block (its internal lags and outgoing edges) carry only 1–3 later-window observations and add little weight [DIR004, DIR005, DIR006, LAG001, LAG006]. An important competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that the two windows sampled two pre-existing process variants in drastically different proportions rather than a restructured single flow; either way, the executed behaviour changed from almost always the three-activity block to almost always a single one of A19/A20/A21.
