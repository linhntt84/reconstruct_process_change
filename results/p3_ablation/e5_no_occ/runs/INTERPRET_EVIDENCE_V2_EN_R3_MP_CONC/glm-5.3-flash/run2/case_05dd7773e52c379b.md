### Observed differences

- Participation: in the earlier window, A28, A30 and A31 each appeared in about 49 of 100 traces and A32, A33 and A34 each in about 52 of 100; in the later window, all six activities appear in about 98–100 of 100 traces. A25 and A26 are present in every trace in both windows. [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006, POS007, POS008]
- Alternatives became joint execution: earlier, an activity of the first group (A28/A30/A31) and one of the second group (A32/A33/A34) never shared a trace (co-occurrence 0 in 100 for all nine cross-group pairs); in the later window, each such pair co-occurs in about 98 of 100 traces, with the first-group activity before the second-group activity in essentially all of those cases. [REL015, REL016, REL017, REL019, REL020, REL021, REL022, REL023, REL024]
- Routing out of A25: earlier, its outgoing flow was spread over all six activities (about 15–19% each); in the later window, about 31–38% goes to each of A28, A30 and A31, while direct transitions to A32, A33 and A34 are about 1% or unobserved. [DIR001]
- Routing into A26: earlier, A28, A30 and A31 each led directly to A26 in about 30–36% of their observed outgoing transitions; in the later window this is about 0.5% or unobserved, and transitions from these activities to A32/A33/A34 appear (about 8–13% each). [DIR002, DIR003, DIR004]
- Event distances: A32, A33 and A34 moved from 1–3 positions after A25 (all observations) to 4–5 positions after A25 (about 98 of 100); A26 moved from 1–3 positions after A28/A30/A31 to 4–5 positions (about 99 of 100). [LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006]
- Order within each group of three remains unfixed in both windows (both directions occur in similar shares), and A25 still precedes all six activities while all six still precede A26. [REL001, REL007, REL013, REL014, REL018, REL025, REL026, REL027]
- Mean relative positions shifted accordingly: A25 slightly earlier (0.67 to 0.62), A26 slightly later (0.80 to 0.83), first-group activities earlier (about 0.74 to 0.68) and second-group activities later (about 0.73 to 0.77). [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006, POS007, POS008]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that, in the later window, the two blocks that were previously alternatives between A25 and A26 are executed one after the other: a trace runs A25, then A28, A30 and A31 in varying order, then A32, A33 and A34 in varying order, then A26. In the earlier window, each trace contained exactly one of the two blocks — A28/A30/A31 in about 49 of 100 traces, otherwise A32/A33/A34 — and the blocks never appeared together. [REL015, REL019, REL022, POS003, POS006]

Supporting signs: the relational items show the change directly, with cross-group co-occurrence rising from 0 to about 98 in 100 and a fixed first-block-before-second-block order [REL015, REL016, REL017, REL019, REL020, REL021, REL022, REL023, REL024]; the successor changes match, as A25 no longer leads directly into the second block and the first block no longer leads directly to A26 [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004]; the lag shifts from 1–3 to 4–5 positions are what placing the other block in between would produce [LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006]; and participation of all six activities rises from about half of traces to nearly all [POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006, POS007, POS008]. The 45 qualifying items are roughly 100 times the 0.44 expected by chance in an unchanged process, so sampling variability cannot explain the pattern as a whole. [screening]

Limitations: the sequential behaviour is near-universal but not complete — about 1–3 in 200 later traces still contain only one block [POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006, POS007, POS008]. The evidence covers only relations among these eight activities and uses event positions rather than clock time, so other, unobserved behaviour could also have changed. An important competing explanation that cannot be ruled out is that the later window mixes traces of two coexisting variants (one with a single block, one with both); the near-universal co-occurrence makes a single changed behaviour the reading most consistent with the data, but this evidence alone cannot separate the two. [REL015, POS006]
