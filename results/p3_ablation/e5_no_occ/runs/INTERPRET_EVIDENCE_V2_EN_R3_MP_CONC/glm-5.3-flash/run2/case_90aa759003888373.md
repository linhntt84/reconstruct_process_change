### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports that in the later window roughly half of the executions take a shortcut from A36 directly to A21, bypassing the block A14 → A20 → A15, while the other half still execute the full sequence A36 → A14 → A20 → A15 → A21. The bypass is shown directly and consistently: A36 now leads to A21 in about 47 in 100 of its observed outgoing transitions [DIR001], A36 and A21 are consecutive in the same share of traces [LAG001], and in exactly those traces A14, A20 and A15 are absent while A36 and A21 remain present [REL001, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL008, REL009]. Twelve control-flow items qualified against roughly 0.21 expected without change, so chance alone cannot account for the set, and all items describe one coherent difference rather than unrelated shifts [DIR001, POS001, REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, REL007, REL008, REL009, LAG001].

Limitations: the roughly 47/53 split rests on finite samples of 200 traces per window, so the exact share is approximate, and a rare pre-existing shortcut variant that did not appear in the earlier window cannot be ruled out. The evidence also cannot distinguish a new routing option at A36 from the block A14/A20/A15 simply becoming skippable as a whole, as the two are behaviourally equivalent here [DIR001, LAG001, REL003].

Other differences: none beyond the above; the small forward shift of A21's position within traces is consistent with the same bypass [POS001].
