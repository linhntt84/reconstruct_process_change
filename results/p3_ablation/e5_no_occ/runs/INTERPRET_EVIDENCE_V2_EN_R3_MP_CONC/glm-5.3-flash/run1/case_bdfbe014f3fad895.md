### Observed differences

- Three activities that do not occur in any of the 200 earlier traces — A32, A33 and A38 — each appear in about 49–50 in 100 later traces, around the middle of the trace (mean relative position about 0.5). [POS003, POS004, POS005, DIR007, DIR008, DIR009]
- These three run as a group: each pair co-occurs in about 49 in 100 later traces and is jointly absent in about 50 in 100, and within each pair both orders occur in similar proportions (for example A32 before A33 in about 23 in 100 versus the reverse in about 26 in 100), so their internal order is not fixed. [REL028, REL029, REL030]
- Earlier, A26 led to A27 in about 95 in 100 of its observed outgoing transitions, A27 led to A28 in about 98 in 100, and A26 and A27 co-occurred in every trace. In the later window, A26 leads to A27 in about half of its outgoing transitions and to A32, A33 or A38 in the other half (about 15–18 in 100 each), and A26 and A27 co-occur in only about 52 in 100 traces. [DIR006, REL017]
- A27 and each group member now almost never share a trace (about 1 in 100 or fewer) while each is present in roughly half of the traces, so they became alternatives; each group member precedes A28 in about 48 in 100 traces, and A28 now occurs without A27 in about 49 in 100 traces, which essentially never happened earlier. [REL021, REL022, REL023, REL024, REL025, REL026, REL027]
- The gap from A26 to A28 widened: earlier A28 followed A26 within 2 steps in about 90 in 100 cases; later the gap is either 2 steps (about 50 in 100) or 4 steps (about 46 in 100), consistent with the three-activity group running between them. [LAG003]
- A04 changed role: earlier it appeared in 19 in 100 traces, late in the trace (mean relative position about 0.54), with its outgoing transitions spread over many activities; later it appears in 60 in 100 traces, early (about 0.21), and about 87 in 100 of its outgoing transitions go to A15. A14 now sends about 54 in 100 of its outgoing transitions to A04 (earlier about 4 in 100, with about 87 in 100 to A15), and A14 is immediately followed by A04 in about 96 in 100 of the observed pairs (112 pairs; the 14 earlier pairs were spread over larger distances). [POS001, DIR001, DIR004, LAG001, REL001, REL002]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

Screening tested 101 control-flow units and 49 items qualified, against fewer than 1 expected by chance without a change, so the overall volume of flagged behaviour is far beyond what random re-splits of an unchanged process produce. [screening]

Main hypothesis: in the later window, about half of the executions no longer run A26 → A27 → A28; instead A26 is followed by the previously unobserved activities A32, A33 and A38, which run together in varying order and then lead into A28, while the other half of executions still use A26 → A27 → A28 — A27 changed from appearing in every trace to about half of them, and A28 continues to occur in every trace. The evidence shows this directly and consistently: the group is absent earlier and present in about half of the later traces at mid-position [POS003, POS004, POS005]; A26's outgoing transitions split about evenly between A27 and the group [DIR006, REL017]; A27 and the group members are mutually almost exclusive [REL022, REL023, REL024]; the group members precede A28 [REL025, REL026, REL027]; and the A26-to-A28 distance moved from consistently 2 steps to 2 or 4 steps [LAG003]. Limitations: zero participation in the earlier window strictly means "not observed", so a pre-existing but unsampled rare variant (or a shift in case mix between the windows) cannot be excluded with certainty, although 0 occurrences in 200 traces makes a behaviour now seen in about half of the cases unlikely to have been missed; the internal order of the group remains unordered in the data.

Other differences: A04 changed from a rare, late activity (19 in 100 traces) to a frequent, early one (60 in 100) that in about half of the later executions runs between A14 and A15 — A14 leads directly to A04, and A04 leads mainly to A15 — whereas earlier A14 led almost exclusively to A15 [POS001, DIR001, DIR004, LAG001, REL001, REL002]. The successor profiles of A06 and A10 also differ markedly between the windows, but with only 8 and 22 observed outgoing transitions in the later window this is weakly supported [DIR002, DIR003].
