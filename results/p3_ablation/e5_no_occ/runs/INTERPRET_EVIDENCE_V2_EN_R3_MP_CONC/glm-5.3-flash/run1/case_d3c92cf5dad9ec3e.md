### Observed differences

- The two windows run the same activities in a different order. In the earlier window, A25 preceded A20, A22 and A23 in about 99 in 100 traces, and A29 likewise preceded them; in the later window, A20, A22 and A23 precede A25 and A29 in about 98–99 in 100 traces. [REL008, REL009, REL015, REL016, REL018, REL019]
- The same reversal holds between the two groups of activities: earlier, A19, A21 and A24 came before A20, A22 and A23 (for example A21 before A20 in about 34 in 100 traces, A24 before A22 in about 36 in 100); in the later window the direction is reversed (A20 before A21 in about 29 in 100, A22 before A24 in about 39 in 100). [REL001, REL004, REL006, REL007, REL010, REL013, REL014, REL017, REL020, REL021]
- Relative positions moved accordingly: A20, A22 and A23 shifted from a mean relative position of about 0.63–0.64 to about 0.38, while A19, A21 and A24 shifted from about 0.34–0.36 to about 0.66–0.68; A25 (0.38 to 0.45) and A29 (0.57 to 0.64) moved only slightly and kept their relative order. [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS006, POS007, POS008]
- Successors changed at the boundaries of the two groups. A40 now leads mainly to A22, A20 and A23 (about 28–35 in 100 transitions each) where it previously led to A21, A19 and A24; A29 now leads to A24, A19 and A21 (about 29–38 in 100) where it previously led to A20, A23 and A22. The trio's exit moved from A30 (about 29–37 in 100) to A25 (about 27–37 in 100), and A19, A21 and A24 now lead to A30 (about 95–98 in 100) instead of A25 (about 97–98 in 100). [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006, DIR007, DIR008]
- Step distances match the relocation: A40 reaches A25 about four steps after itself in the later window (92 in 100 at distance 4) versus about two steps earlier (95 in 100 at distance 2); A29 reaches A30 about two steps after itself versus about four before; A29 is immediately followed by A21 in 58 traces (previously the pair appeared within five steps in only 1 trace); and A40 is followed by A20, A22 or A23 within one to three steps (previously 1 trace each). [LAG002, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006, LAG007, LAG008, LAG009]
- Participation itself did not change: A20, A22 and A23 still appear in essentially every trace and A19, A21 and A24 each in about 30–40 in 100 traces in both windows, and the trio's internal order remains flexible in both windows. ### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a reordering in which the single step chosen among A19, A21 and A24 and the three-activity group A20, A22 and A23 exchanged the sides of the middle part (A25, then A29): in the earlier window a case ran A40, then one of A19/A21/A24, then A25, then A29, then A20/A22/A23 in varying order into A30, whereas in the later window it runs A40, then A20/A22/A23, then A25, then A29, then one of A19/A21/A24 into A30 [REL008, REL009, REL015, REL016, REL018, REL019, DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006, DIR007, DIR008, POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS006, POS007, POS008, LAG005, LAG009]. The support is direct and consistent: all 21 pair-order items reverse in the same direction, the position and distance shifts match the relocation, the successor changes match the new entry and exit points, and 46 qualifying items against roughly 0.48 expected without change make chance sampling implausible as a whole [REL001, REL006, REL010, POS002, LAG004, LAG006]. The evidence shows the resulting order, not how it was produced: it cannot distinguish one coordinated exchange of the two groups from two independent re-routings, and a few distance statements about A21 or A24 together with A25 in the later window rest on only 1–2 observations [LAG001, LAG003].

Other differences: only low-frequency variation in rare outgoing transitions (each about 1–2 in 100 or less), without a consistent direction [DIR002, DIR004, DIR005, DIR008].
