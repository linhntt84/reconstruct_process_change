### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that, in the later window, about half of the cases skip A22 and instead run a group of three activities — A27, A28 and A33 — between A21 and A23, while the other half keep the original A21 → A22 → A23 sequence; within the group the three activities run in no fixed order. This is shown directly by the relational evidence: A21 precedes each group member in about 49 in 100 later traces, each group member precedes A23 in about 49 in 100, A22 co-occurs with none of them, and the group members co-occur with each other in both orders for every pair. The positional and distance evidence agrees (mid-trace positions near 0.49; the A21-to-A23 distance stretching from 2 to 4 positions), and the outgoing edges of A21 and of each group member match a hand-over chain that ends in A23. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL009, REL010, REL011, REL012, REL013, REL014, POS001, POS002, POS003, LAG001]

Screening qualified 22 control-flow items where an unchanged process would produce about 0.24, and each item's difference exceeds about 995 in 1000 random re-splits of the pooled data, so sampling noise cannot explain the pattern as a whole; all qualified items describe this one coherent difference rather than scattered effects. [DIR001, POS001, REL001, LAG001]

Limitations: the evidence cannot distinguish whether the group replaced A22 one-for-one or was added while A22 was skipped, since the two look identical here, and only the tested units were screened, so nothing can be said about activities outside this fragment. An important competing explanation that remains is that this behaviour existed before as a rare variant that happened not to appear in the earlier 200 traces; the 0-versus-49-in-100 contrast makes plain sampling noise unlikely, but the windows are finite samples.
