### Observed differences

- Participation changed sharply for six activities. In the earlier window, A28, A30, A31, A32, A33 and A34 each appeared in all 200 traces; in the later window A28, A30 and A31 each appear in about 93 of 200 traces (about 47 in 100) and A32, A33 and A34 in about 111 of 200 (about 56 in 100), while A25 and A26 still appear in every trace in both windows [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006, POS007, POS008].
- The six activities split into two mutually exclusive groups. Cross-group pairs such as A28 with A32, A30 with A33 or A31 with A34 co-occurred in all earlier traces but in only about 2 in 100 later traces; in about 45 in 100 later traces only the first-group activity ran, and in about 54 in 100 only the second-group activity ran [REL015, REL017, REL019, REL020, REL022, REL024]. Within each group the activities still run together whenever the group runs: A28 with A30 co-occurrence fell from 100% to about 47 in 100 (with "neither in the trace" covering the rest), and A32 with A33 from 100% to about 56 in 100 [REL013, REL018, REL025, REL027].
- Ordering is preserved where activities still meet: A25 still precedes both groups, the first-group members still precede A26, and the second-group members still follow A25 and precede A26; within-group order remains mixed (no fixed order) in both windows [REL001, REL004, REL007, REL010, REL013, REL015, REL025].
- Successors changed at the routing points. A25's outgoing transitions, previously only to A28, A30 and A31 (about 34%, 37% and 30%), now also go to A32, A33 and A34 (about 14–20% each) [DIR001]. A28, A30 and A31 gained direct transitions to A26 (about 34%, 35% and 26% of their outgoing) while their transitions into the second group nearly vanished, e.g. A28 to A32 fell from about 10% to about 1% [DIR002, DIR003, DIR004].
- Distances shrank across the skipped block. The gap from A25 to A32, A33 or A34 was 4–5 positions in essentially all earlier traces and is now 1–3 positions in about 97 in 100 cases; the gap from A28, A30 or A31 to A26 likewise moved from 4–5 positions to 1–3 positions [LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG008, LAG011].
- Relative positions shifted consistently with shorter traces: the first-group members' mean position moved later (about 0.68 to 0.74), the second-group members' slightly earlier (about 0.77 to 0.74), A25's rose from about 0.62 to 0.67 and A26's fell from about 0.83 to 0.81 [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006, POS007, POS008].
- For scale: 52 control-flow items qualified against roughly 0.5 expected by chance alone, so this ensemble of differences is far beyond what an unchanged process typically produces [screening].

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility of a structural change in routing: in the later window, each case appears to run only one of two alternative blocks between A25 and A26 — either {A28, A30, A31} or {A32, A33, A34} — whereas in the earlier window every case executed both blocks in sequence (A25, then the first block, then the second block, then A26). About 47 in 100 later cases take the first block and about 56 in 100 the second, with about 2 in 100 containing both; the block activities became conditional rather than always executed [REL015, REL019, REL022, REL024, POS003, POS006].

The support is direct and consistent: the collapse of cross-group co-occurrence from 100% to about 2%, with single-group traces filling the remainder, shows the exclusivity relation directly [REL015, REL021, REL024]; the participation drops match the two group sizes almost exactly (93 and 111 of 200) [POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006, POS007, POS008]; the new direct transitions from A28, A30 and A31 to A26 and the shortened A25-to-second-group and first-group-to-A26 gaps show the skipped block is bypassed, not reordered [DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, LAG001, LAG004]; and the qualified-item count (52 versus about 0.5 expected) rules out chance as an overall explanation [screening]. Limitations: the evidence cannot show what determines which block a case takes, and the roughly 2% of later traces containing both groups could be a retained residual path or noise. A shift in the mix of two pre-existing case types is an unlikely competing explanation because cross-group co-occurrence was exactly 100% across all 200 earlier traces, but the indicators alone cannot fully rule out a population-composition effect.

Other differences: the position shifts of A25 (mean relative position about 0.62 to 0.67) and A26 (about 0.83 to 0.81) are consistent with shorter traces once one block is skipped and are not treated as an independent change [POS001, POS002]. Several lag items concerning pairs that rarely co-occur in the later window (e.g. A28 with A33, A30 with A34) rest on only about 4 later observations and were not used as support [LAG006, LAG010].
