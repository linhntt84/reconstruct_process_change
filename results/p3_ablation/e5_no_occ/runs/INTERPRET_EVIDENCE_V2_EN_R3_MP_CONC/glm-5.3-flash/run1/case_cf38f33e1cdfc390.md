### Observed differences

- The routing after A15 flipped: in the earlier window about 75 in 100 of A15's outgoing transitions went to A16 (27%), A17 (23%) or A18 (26%), while A19 (14%), A20 (4%) and A21 (8%) were rarely chosen; in the later window about 76 in 100 went to A19 (25%), A20 (22%) or A21 (29%), and A16, A17, A18 each fell to about 8–9 in 100. [DIR001]
- The six activities act as two alternative groups that never mix: pairs such as A16–A19, A17–A20 and A18–A21 never co-occur in either window, with exactly one group present per trace. The group {A16, A17, A18} ran in about 75 in 100 traces earlier and about 25 in 100 later; {A19, A20, A21} did the reverse (about 25 earlier, about 76 later). [REL009, REL015, REL020]
- Within the first group, A16, A17 and A18 used to occur together in about 75 in 100 traces, in no fixed order between them (A16 before A17 in 38%, A17 before A16 in 37%); in the later window they co-occur in only about 25 in 100, with neither present in about 76. [REL007, REL008, REL013]
- Within the second group, pairs such as A19–A20 moved from co-occurring in about 25 in 100 traces (neither present in 75) to co-occurring in about 76 in 100, again in either order. [REL022, REL023, REL025]
- A25 occurs in every trace in both windows but switched company: earlier it co-occurred with A16, A17 or A18 in about 75 in 100 traces; later it co-occurs with A19, A20 or A21 in about 76 in 100. [REL012, REL017, REL024, REL027]
- Where A20 does follow A15, the event-position gap shortened: lag 3 fell from about 52 in 100 to about 34, while lags 1–2 rose to about 65 combined (positions, not clock time). [LAG001]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that two alternative paths after A15 swapped frequencies: earlier, about three in four cases executed the group A16, A17, A18 (in any internal order) together with A25, and about one in four executed A19, A20, A21 with A25; in the later window, about three in four execute A19, A20, A21 with A25 and only about one in four still runs A16, A17, A18. Both groups and every involved activity are observed in substantial shares in both windows (roughly 25% versus 75%), the A15 prefix and the ever-present A25 are preserved, and there is no sign of bypassing or of changed repetition counts — only the shares between the alternatives shifted. The change is shown directly by A15's redistributed outgoing transitions and by the co-occurrence and alternative relations, and the 29 qualifying control-flow items are roughly 70 times the number expected by chance (about 0.41), so sampling variation alone cannot explain the pattern; each item's difference also exceeds about 995 of 1,000 random re-splits. [DIR001, REL001, REL004, REL009, REL022, REL024, LAG001]

Limitations: the many agreeing items largely describe one underlying trace behaviour and are not independent confirmations, and the evidence cannot distinguish a modified routing decision from a shifted mix of case types entering the process, which would look identical at this level. [REL001, REL009]

Other differences: A25's ordering relative to the group activities also shifted somewhat — interleaved or repeated arrangements with A16, A17 or A18 fell from about 32 to about 7 in 100 traces, while A19, A20 or A21 preceding A25 rose from about 17 to about 40 in 100. [REL012, REL021, REL024]
