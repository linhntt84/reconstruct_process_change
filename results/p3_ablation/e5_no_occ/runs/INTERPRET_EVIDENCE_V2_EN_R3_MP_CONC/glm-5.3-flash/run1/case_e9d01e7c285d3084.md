### Observed differences

- The three activities A14, A30 and A36 still occur together in every trace in both windows, but their relative order changed: in the earlier window they ran in a fixed sequence (A14 before A30, A14 before A36, and A30 before A36 in about 98–100 in 100 traces), while in the later window that fixed order survives in only about 32 in 100 traces and about 68 in 100 show each pair as interleaved or repeated. [REL003, REL004, REL005]
- A36 is executed much more often: about 203 outgoing transitions across the 200 earlier traces (roughly one per trace) versus about 697 across the 200 later traces (roughly three to four per trace). [DIR001]
- A36's dominant successor changed: before, about 97 in 100 of its outgoing transitions went to A25; in the later window about 70 in 100 go to A30 and only about 28 in 100 to A25. [DIR001]
- A05's participation is roughly stable (it co-occurs with A30/A36 in about 55 in 100 traces before and about 52 in 100 later), but its strict precedence largely dissolved: "A05 first" falls from about 53–54 in 100 to about 16 in 100, while interleaved-or-repeated rises from at most about 2 in 100 to about 35 in 100. - Spacing changed: A05 and A14 sit one position apart in about 36 in 100 pairs before versus about 17 in 100 later, and three positions apart in about 28 versus about 45 in 100; the tight two-step gap between A30 and A36 largely disappeared, from about 97 in 100 pairs to about 12 in 100, with the remaining pairs spread over longer gaps. [LAG001, LAG002]
- Event positions became more variable: A14 sits slightly earlier (mean relative position about 0.14 to 0.12), while A30 and A36 sit slightly later and their position spread roughly doubles (standard deviation about 0.07 to 0.12 for both). [POS001, POS002, POS003]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the previously fixed sequential block A14 → A30 → A36 is, in the later window, executed as a largely interleaved or repeated pattern: A36 now repeats several times per trace and typically leads back to A30 instead of moving on to A25, the fixed order among A14, A30 and A36 survives in only about a third of traces, and A05 — still present in about half of the traces — has lost its strict position before A30/A36 and is now interleaved with them in about a third of its co-occurrences. This is a change in ordering constraints and repetition, not a swap of positions or a change in participation: B-before-A stays at zero in every relation, and A14, A30 and A36 occur in all traces in both windows. [REL003, REL004, REL005, REL001, REL002, DIR001]

Supporting signs: the relational items show the order change directly and consistently for all three pairs of the block, the successor profile of A36 shows the new routing and the higher repetition directly, and the lag and position items agree in direction (longer, more variable spacing). Eleven items qualified against roughly 0.28 expected for an unchanged process in the control-flow family (57 units tested), so the collection as a whole is not what a stable process typically produces, and every item's difference exceeds about 199 of 200 random re-splits of the pooled traces. [REL003, REL004, REL005, DIR001, LAG001, LAG002, POS001, POS002, POS003]

Limitations: the relational indicator merges interleaving with repetition, so the evidence cannot distinguish loose, unordered execution of the three activities from a repeat loop over A30 and A36; and because the direction shares are taken over A36's outgoing transitions only, no conclusion about A25's overall participation is possible. [REL003, REL004, REL005, DIR001]
