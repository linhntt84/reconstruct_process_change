### Observed differences

- In both windows A30 and A36 appear in every trace (200 of 200) and always occur together, but their interplay changed: earlier, A30 came before A36 in only about 25 in 100 traces, while the two were interleaved or repeated in about 76 in 100; in the later window, A30 comes before A36 in about 98 in 100 traces, with interleaving or repetition remaining in only about 2 in 100. [REL001]
- The pair became tightly and consistently spaced: A30 is followed by A36 exactly two positions later in about 89 in 100 later-window observations, against about 12 in 100 earlier. [LAG001]
- The reverse and self-repeating links largely disappeared: A36 followed by A30 within five steps occurs in 150 of 200 earlier traces but only 1 of 200 later traces, and A36 followed by itself in 150 of 200 earlier traces but only 2 of 200 later traces. [LAG002, LAG003]
- A36's outgoing transitions were redirected: earlier, about 70 in 100 went back to A30 and about 28 in 100 to A25; in the later window, about 92 in 100 go to A25 and only about 4 in 100 to A30. [DIR002]
- Repetition volume dropped sharply while placement stayed the same: both activities still appear in all 200 traces per window at roughly the same mid-trace position (A30 mean about 0.44 earlier vs about 0.42 later; A36 about 0.49 in both), but their outgoing transitions per window fell from about 690 to about 210 each — roughly from 3.5 occurrences per trace to about 1 — and the position spread narrowed (standard deviation about 0.12 down to about 0.07). [DIR001, DIR002, POS001, POS002]
- A30's own immediate routing is essentially unchanged: about 99 in 100 of its outgoing transitions went to A31 earlier and about 97 in 100 later. [DIR001]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the repeated, interleaved execution of A30 and A36 gave way to a single fixed pass: earlier, both activities recurred several times per trace and alternated with one another, with A36 frequently returning to A30 or to itself; in the later window each runs about once, A30 first and A36 two positions later, after which A36 proceeds to A25 instead of back to A30. The relation change is observed directly, the lag concentration and the disappearance of the reverse and self-repeats match it, A36's redirected successors show the same behaviour, and the unchanged mean positions rule out a mere relocation of either activity. [REL001, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, DIR002, POS001, POS002]

The volume of qualifying items also exceeds what an unchanged process typically produces: 8 items against roughly 0.26 expected by chance, all in control flow, and the individual shifts are far larger than typical random re-splits (for example, 150 of 200 traces falling to 1 of 200). [DIR001, DIR002, POS001, POS002, REL001, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003]

Limitations: the relational, lag and direction items all describe one underlying trace behaviour, so they are not independent confirmations, and the per-trace repetition count is inferred from transition counts rather than observed directly. A competing explanation the evidence cannot fully rule out is a shift in the mix of trace variants — a variant without the A30–A36 repetition dominating the later sample while a few repetitive traces persist (1–2 later traces still show A36 followed by A30 or by itself). [LAG002, LAG003]

Other differences: only negligible new edges appear in the later window (A30 gains successors such as A02, A06, A08, A25, A33 and A42, and A36 gains A10 and others, each under about 1 in 100 of that activity's transitions, with A30's share to A31 dipping by about 2 in 100); these do not alter the main hypothesis. [DIR001, DIR002]
