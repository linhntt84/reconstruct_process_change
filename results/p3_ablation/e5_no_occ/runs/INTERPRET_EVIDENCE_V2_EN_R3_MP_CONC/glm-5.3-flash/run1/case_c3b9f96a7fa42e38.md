### Observed differences

- The starkest difference is a reversal of execution order between two groups of activities. In the earlier window, A35, A36 and A41 each ran before A26 in about 99–100 in 100 traces and before A27 in about 98–100 in 100; in the later window this flipped, with A26 preceding each of the three in about 97–98 in 100 and A27 preceding each of them in about 98–99 in 100, while co-occurrence stayed at essentially every trace in both windows [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006].
- The wiring around these activities was rewired accordingly. In the earlier window, A25's observed outgoing transitions went to A35 (about 39 in 100), A36 (about 32) and A41 (about 27), and A26 led directly to A30 in about 99 in 100 of its transitions; in the later window, A25 leads to A27 in about 98 in 100, A26's transitions spread across A35 (about 34), A36 (about 32) and A41 (about 30), and A26→A30 fell to about 2 in 100 [DIR003, DIR004].
- The three activities now lead elsewhere: before, A35, A36 and A41 each passed to A27 in roughly 28–37 in 100 of their transitions; after, those shares dropped to about 0.5 in 100, while transitions from each of them to A30 appeared at about 30–35 in 100 [DIR005, DIR006, DIR007].
- Step distances match this: A25 was followed by A27 about four steps later before (about 93 in 100) but immediately after (about 98 in 100); A26 was followed by A30 immediately before (about 99 in 100) but about four steps later after (about 91 in 100); A36 followed by A30 within five steps occurred in only 1 trace before but about 198 in 200 after; direct links from A25 to the three activities and from them to A27, present in all 200 traces before, were seen in only about 2 traces after [LAG005, LAG009, LAG011, LAG006, LAG007, LAG008, LAG010, LAG012].
- Average positions shifted consistently: A27 moved earlier (mean position about 0.47 to 0.38) and A26 earlier as well (about 0.62 to 0.53), while A35, A36 and A41 moved later (about 0.41–0.42 to about 0.58–0.59); each of these activities appeared in every trace in both windows [POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006].
- Separately, A06 became much rarer: it appeared in about 27 in 100 traces before but about 13 in 100 after, and later within traces when present (mean position about 0.25 to 0.44); its outgoing transitions, about 86 in 100 of which went to A16 before, spread over many targets (A16 about 39 in 100 after), and A15's transitions to A06 fell from about 15 in 100 to about 3 in 100 [POS001, DIR001, DIR002, LAG001, LAG002].

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the group of activities A35, A36 and A41 changed its place in the process sequence: in the earlier window it ran between A25 and A27 (and before A26), whereas in the later window it runs between A26 and A30 — A27 now immediately follows A25, and A30 is reached through the group rather than directly from A26 [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006, DIR007, LAG005, LAG009, LAG011, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006].

Supporting signs: 31 items qualified against about 0.53 expected without a change, so the volume of findings is many times what an unchanged process typically produces. The order reversal is shown directly by relational evidence with full support (200 traces per window) and near-complete shares in both directions, and the routing, distance and position items all describe the same reordering; because these indicators partly reflect the same trace behaviour, they are best read as one coherent signal rather than independent confirmations [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, DIR003, DIR004, LAG005, LAG009, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006].

Limitations: the internal order among A35, A36 and A41 is mixed in both windows, so only the group's placement, not its internal sequencing, is supported; positions and distances are measured in event positions, not clock time, so this is a change in sequence order rather than a demonstrated timing change. An important competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that the two windows are dominated by different pre-existing process variants rather than a single resequencing, although the near-deterministic reversal within each window makes pure sampling variation unlikely [DIR005, DIR006, DIR007, POS004, POS005, POS006].

Other differences: A06 occurs in far fewer traces in the later window (about 13 vs 27 in 100), appears later within traces, and its transitions are much more dispersed, with the A15→A06 link becoming rare; this participation change around A06, A15 and A16 (including the pair A16 then A27 within five steps rising from 1 to about 149 traces, though the before-side rests on 1 observation) is separate from the main hypothesis [POS001, DIR001, DIR002, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003].
