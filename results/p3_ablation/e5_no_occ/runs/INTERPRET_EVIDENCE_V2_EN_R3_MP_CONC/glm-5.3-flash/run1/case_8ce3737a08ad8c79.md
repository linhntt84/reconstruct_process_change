### Observed differences

- The dominant difference is a swap between two alternative activity blocks after A20. In the earlier window, about 82 in 100 traces continued from A20 into the trio A24/A25/A26 (the three always occurred together), while about 19 in 100 continued into the trio A21/A22/A23; in the later window this reversed — about 79 in 100 traces ran A21/A22/A23 after A20 and only about 21 in 100 ran A24/A25/A26. A20 itself occurs in every trace in both windows, and its outgoing transitions flipped accordingly: A21/A22/A23 together rose from about 19% to about 79% of A20's outgoing transitions, while A24/A25/A26 fell from about 81% to about 22%. [DIR003, REL009, REL010, REL011, REL012, REL013, REL014]
- The two trios are mutually exclusive alternatives in both windows (they essentially never share a trace), and their internal order did not change: A21/A22/A23 co-occur in no fixed order before and after (A21 before A22 in about 9 vs about 40 in 100 traces; A22 before A21 about 10 vs about 38), and likewise A24/A25/A26 (about 47 vs 34 in 100 before; about 11 vs 10 after). [REL015, REL016, REL023, REL017, REL036, REL037, REL041]
- A30, present in all traces in both windows, switched partners: before it co-occurred with A24/A25/A26 in about 82 in 100 traces and with A21/A22/A23 in about 19 in 100; in the later window these are about 21 and about 79 in 100. [REL020, REL027, REL033, REL038, REL042, REL045]
- A32 and A33 show the same flip: co-occurrence with A21/A22/A23 rose from about 9 in 100 traces to about 55 in 100, while co-occurrence with A24/A25/A26 fell from about 52 in 100 to about 15 in 100. [REL021, REL022, REL028, REL029, REL034, REL035, REL039, REL040, REL043, REL044, REL046, REL047]
- A10 became rarer and earlier: it appears in about 17 in 100 traces before but about 7 in 100 in the later window, and its median event position moved from about 0.86 to about 0.36 of the case with far more spread (std about 0.03 vs 0.28). Its outgoing transitions shifted from almost always A33 (about 97 in 100) to a spread over eight successors (A33 about 14 in 100), and direct A32→A10 succession nearly disappeared (A10's share of A32's outgoing fell from about 20 in 100 to about 1 in 100; A32→A10 pairs within five steps fell from 32 to 4). These items rest on modest counts (about 33 vs 14 observations). [POS001, DIR002, DIR004, LAG001, REL007, REL008]
- A02's outgoing profile changed from concentrated on A11, A20 and A01 (about 50/25/25) to spread over ten successors (largest A19 about 22 in 100); only 4 vs 18 outgoing transitions were observed, so this is weakly grounded. [DIR001]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the two alternative paths out of A20 swapped prevalence: the path through A24, A25 and A26 — previously executed in about 82 in 100 traces as a jointly occurring trio in mixed order, together with A30 — was taken in only about 21 in 100 traces in the later window, while the path through A21, A22 and A23 rose from about 19 to about 79 in 100 traces. This reads as a change in how often existing paths are taken rather than an addition or removal of activities: both trios are observed in substantial shares in both windows, both keep their internal mixed order, A20 still precedes whichever trio runs, and the trios remain mutually exclusive. [DIR003, REL009, REL010, REL011, REL012, REL013, REL014, REL015, REL016, REL023, REL036, REL037, REL041]

Supporting signs: the participation and routing flips are shown directly by relational and directional items with full support (200 of 200 traces in both windows); the partner switches of A30, A32 and A33 follow the same pattern, though they largely mirror the same trace behaviour rather than confirming it independently; and 53 items qualified against roughly 0.73 expected by chance for the 147 tested units, so chance sampling cannot account for the set of differences as a whole. [DIR003, REL009, REL012, REL020, REL038, REL021, REL039]

Limitations: the evidence cannot rule out that the later window mixes two coexisting process variants, so the swap could reflect a changed case mix rather than a rewiring of one process, and it does not explain why about 21 in 100 traces still run the old A24/A25/A26 path. [REL012, REL036]

Other differences: A10 occurs in fewer traces (about 17 vs 7 in 100), sits earlier in the case, and its succession changed from almost always A33 to a spread of successors — a separate participation and placement change resting on about 33 vs 14 observations [POS001, DIR002, DIR004, LAG001, REL007, REL008]; A02's outgoing transitions spread from three successors to ten, but with only 4 vs 18 observed transitions this is weakly supported [DIR001].
