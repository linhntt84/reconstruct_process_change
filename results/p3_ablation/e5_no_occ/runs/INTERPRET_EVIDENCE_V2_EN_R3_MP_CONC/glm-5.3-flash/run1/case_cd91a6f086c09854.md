### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the three-activity block A30, A31, A36 was relocated relative to A22 and A21: in the earlier window the flow ran A20 → the block (three consecutive events) → A22, with A21 later and A25 immediately after A21; in the later window, A20 connects directly to A22, A21 feeds the block, and the block ends in A25 — the block moved from between A20 and A22 to between A21 and A25. This is shown directly by the pair relations (a fixed order with the block before A21/A22 in 100 in 100 earlier traces versus the block after them in about 99 in 100 later traces, with co-occurrence unchanged), by the successor changes at A20, A21 and the block members, and by the mirrored gap changes. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, LAG001, LAG004]

The pattern is consistent across all 22 qualified control-flow items, against roughly 0.28 expected by chance alone, so random re-splits cannot explain the pattern as a whole. Because every involved activity appears in all traces in both windows and the block's internal routing is essentially unchanged, this reads as a reordering of existing activities rather than activities being added, removed or repeated differently. Limitations: the indicators cover only these activities, so further changes elsewhere in the trace (for example between A22 and A21) cannot be excluded, and the four weak gap items rest on about 2 later-window observations each. A competing description the evidence cannot separate is two independent rewirings (A20 rerouted to A22, and A21 rerouted into the block with the exit moved to A25) rather than one block relocation; both fit the data equally.
