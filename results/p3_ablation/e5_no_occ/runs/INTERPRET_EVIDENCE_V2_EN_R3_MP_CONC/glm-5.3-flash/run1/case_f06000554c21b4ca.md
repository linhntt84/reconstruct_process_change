### Observed differences

- The order between two groups of activities reversed. Before, A26 ran before A35, A36 and A41 in about 99–100 in 100 of their joint traces, and A27 likewise preceded the trio in about 98–100 in 100; in the later window the trio ran before A26 and before A27 in about 98–99.5 in 100, while all six pairs still co-occurred in essentially every trace in both windows. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006]
- The connections around the trio were rewired. Before, A25's outgoing transitions led to A27 in about 99 in 100 cases; in the later window they split roughly evenly over A35, A36 and A41 (about one third each). A26's outgoing transitions, previously spread over A41 (about 37 in 100), A35 (about 34) and A36 (about 28), now lead to A30 in about 98 in 100 cases. The trio's transitions into A30 (about 28–37 in 100 before) disappeared; in the later window each of A35, A36 and A41 leads to A27 in roughly 31–34 in 100 of its outgoing transitions. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005]
- Step distances swapped accordingly: A25 reached A27 one step later in about 99 in 100 traces before but four to five steps later in about 99 in 100 after, while A26 reached A30 four to five steps later in about 99 in 100 before but a single step later in about 98 in 100 after. [LAG001, LAG003]
- Positions moved consistently: A35, A36 and A41 each shifted from a mean relative position of about 0.58–0.59 to about 0.41 of the trace, A27 from about 0.38 to about 0.47, and A26 from about 0.53 to about 0.62. [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005]
- The remaining lag items (pairs such as A25→A35, A26→A35, A35→A30) rest on only 1–2 observations in one window; they hint that a small minority of traces keeps the earlier arrangement but carry little weight alone. [LAG002, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006, LAG007, LAG008, LAG009, LAG010]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the three-activity block A35, A36 and A41 — executed in no fixed internal order — was relocated: before, it ran between A26 and A30 (A26 fed into it, and it exited to A30); in the later window it runs between A25 and A27 (A25 enters it, it exits to A27), and A26 now leads directly to A30. Participation did not change — every involved activity occurred in essentially all traces in both windows — so this is a relocation of an existing block, not activities becoming optional, repeated, or existing paths being taken with different frequencies. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, LAG001, LAG003, POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005]

The reading is direct and consistent across all indicator families: the pairwise orders flip for all six pairs, the successor profiles change exactly as the relocation implies, the A25→A27 and A26→A30 step distances swap (one step ↔ four steps), and all five position distributions shift in the matching direction. Screening context supports this: 26 control-flow items qualified where about 0.41 would be expected without a change, and every item's difference exceeded all 200 random re-splits. The items are not independent confirmations, however — the six relation items and the successor profiles largely describe the same traces and corroborate one underlying shift. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, LAG001, LAG003, POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005]

Limitations: the indicators use event positions rather than clock time, and relative-order data cannot fully exclude an equivalent restructuring that produces the same orders without a literal relocation of the block (for example, the two groups exchanging places through different routing); the few residual traces with the earlier arrangement are each observed only once or twice. [LAG004, LAG005, LAG006, LAG008, LAG009, LAG010]

Other differences: none beyond the main shift; the remaining qualified items describe the same relocation from pairs seen only 1–2 times in one window and add little independent weight. [LAG002, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006, LAG007, LAG008, LAG009, LAG010]
