### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, A15, A17 and A18 each ran in every case, clustered in the first half of the trace (mean position about 0.39 of the trace length), and always before both A20 and A24 — for example, A15 preceded A20 in 100 in 100 traces. In the later window, the same three activities still run in every case but in the second half (mean position about 0.64), and always after A20 and A24 — A20 now precedes A15 in 100 in 100 traces. [POS002, POS004, POS005, REL008, REL009, REL015, REL016, REL018, REL019]

- The three activities A14, A16 and A19 moved the opposite way. Earlier, each appeared in roughly 3–4 in 10 cases (A14 about 31 in 100, A16 38, A19 32) late in the trace (mean position about 0.68), always after A20 and A24. In the later window they appear at similar rates (A14 about 44 in 100, A16 29, A19 28) but early (mean position about 0.34), always before A20 and A24 — A24 preceded A14 in every joint occurrence earlier, while A14 now precedes A24 in every joint occurrence. [POS001, POS003, POS006, REL004, REL005, REL012, REL013, REL020, REL021]

- Successors changed at the boundaries of these blocks. A14, A16 and A19 led exclusively to A25 in the earlier window and exclusively to A20 in the later one; A15, A17 and A18, which earlier continued to A20 in about 31–39 of 100 outgoing transitions, now continue to A25 in about 33–35 of 100; A35 now continues to A14/A16/A19 (about 28–44 of 100) instead of to A15/A17/A18, and A24 now continues to A15/A17/A18 instead of to A14/A16/A19. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006, DIR007, DIR008]

- Step distances (in event positions) moved accordingly: the gap from A24 to A25 grew from 2 steps in all traces to 4 steps, and the gap from A35 to A20 shrank from 4 steps to 2. [LAG001, LAG002]

- A20 and A24 themselves shifted slightly earlier (mean position about 0.46 to 0.38 for A20; about 0.65 to 0.57 for A24). [POS007, POS008]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that two blocks of activities exchanged their places around the A20–A24 segment. In the earlier window the case ran A35, then the always-executed trio A15/A17/A18, then A20, then A24, then one of the alternatives A14/A16/A19, then A25; in the later window the order is A35, one of A14/A16/A19, A20, A24, the trio A15/A17/A18, then A25. In other words, the trio A15, A17, A18 moved from before A20 to after A24, and the alternative block A14/A16/A19 moved from after A24 to before A20. [REL004, REL005, REL008, REL009, REL012, REL013, REL015, REL016, REL018, REL019, REL020, REL021, DIR001, DIR003, DIR006, DIR007, DIR008, LAG001, LAG002]

The support is direct and consistent: 39 items qualified from 68 tested units, versus fewer than 1 expected by chance in an unchanged process, and they span routing, position, pairwise order and step distance while describing one and the same exchange; the pairwise order reversals are complete (100 in 100 one way earlier, 100 in 100 the other way later), and each item's difference exceeds about 995 of 1000 random re-splits of the pooled data. [DIR001, DIR002, POS001, POS002, REL005, REL008, LAG001, LAG002]

Limitations: the indicators record event positions, not clock times, so nothing can be said about timing; and a complete reversal of order between the two windows is also what a switch between two fixed-order ways of running the case would look like, which the traces cannot distinguish from a reordering within one structure. [REL008, POS002]

Other differences: within the alternative block, the selection shifted toward A14 — it was the executed alternative in about 31 in 100 cases earlier and about 44 in 100 later, while A16 fell from about 38 to 29 and A19 from about 32 to 28; this shift in choice shares is observed but is not part of the exchange hypothesis. [POS001, POS003, POS006, DIR007, DIR008]
