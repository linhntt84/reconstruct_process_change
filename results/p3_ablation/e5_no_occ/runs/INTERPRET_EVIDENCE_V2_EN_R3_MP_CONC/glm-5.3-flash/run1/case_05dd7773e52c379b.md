### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the process changed from running either one of two three-activity blocks or the other — never both — to running both blocks in sequence in almost every case: before, a trace executed either A28, A30 and A31 or A32, A33 and A34 (each triad in unfixed internal order) between A25 and A26; in the later window, about 98–99 in 100 traces execute A25, then the A28/A30/A31 triad, then the A32/A33/A34 triad, then A26, with the internal order of each triad unchanged. [REL015, REL016, REL017, REL019, REL020, REL021, REL022, REL023, REL024, REL013, REL025, REL001, REL007, LAG001, LAG004]

The change is shown directly by the relational evidence (cross-group co-occurrence rising from 0 to about 98 in 100 with a fixed cross-group order) and is consistent across participation, positional and routing indicators; the volume of qualifying evidence (45 items against about 0.44 expected for an unchanged process, roughly a hundred times the chance expectation) and the strict exclusivity in the earlier window (zero cross-group co-occurrence in 200 traces) make a sampling or rare-variant explanation implausible. [REL015, REL019, REL022, POS003, POS006, LAG001, LAG004, DIR001, DIR002]

Limitations: the evidence covers only control flow for these eight activities, so it cannot show what governs the new always-both behaviour (for example, whether the earlier exclusivity was removed at a decision point or the two blocks became separately mandatory), and it cannot rule out that the later window draws on a case mix in which both blocks always applied rather than a change in routing itself — the executed behaviour differs between the windows either way. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, REL015, REL022]

Other differences: none — all 45 qualifying items describe aspects of this single structural difference. [DIR001-DIR004, POS001-POS008, REL001-REL027, LAG001-LAG006]
