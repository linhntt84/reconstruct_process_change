### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the block of activities A30, A31 and A36 swapped places with the segment containing A22 and A21: in the earlier window the flow ran from A20 to A22, then A21, with A30/A31/A36 executing after A21 and A25 reached about four steps later, while in the later window A20 feeds the group, the group executes before A22 and A21, and A21 connects directly to A25. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, LAG001, LAG002]

Supporting signs: the order change is shown directly by the relational items and is near-total (from 100% A21/A22-first to about 99.5% group-first, with co-occurrence unchanged at 100%, which rules out activities being added, removed or made optional); the position shifts move the same activities in the same directions; the successor changes at A20, A21, A30, A31 and A36 and both gap changes match the same rearrangement. With 24 qualifying items against roughly 0.28 expected if nothing had changed, chance sampling cannot explain the pattern as a whole, and every item's difference exceeds about 99.5% of random re-splits. Limitations and competing explanation: the evidence establishes the reversal of order and the changed connections but not the complete resulting sequence — the relative placement of A22 and A21 and any activities between them are not covered by the tested pairs, so a single block swap cannot be distinguished from coordinated individual relocations. The internal routing among A30, A31 and A36 in the later window is visible only through the direction shares, and the lag profiles inside the group rest on one later-window observation each.
