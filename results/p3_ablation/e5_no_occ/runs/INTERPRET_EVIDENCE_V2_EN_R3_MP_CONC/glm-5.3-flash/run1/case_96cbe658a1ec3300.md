### Observed differences

- The relative order of two groups of activities reversed. Before, A26 preceded A35, A36 and A41 in about 98–99 in 100 traces, and A27 preceded them in about 99 in 100 or more; in the later window, the trio A35/A36/A41 preceded A26 in about 97–98 in 100 and preceded A27 in about 97 in 100. All six pairs still occurred together in every trace in both windows, so participation itself did not change. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006]

- Direct successors changed accordingly. Before, about 98% of A25's outgoing transitions went to A27; in the later window they went to A35, A36 and A41 in roughly equal shares (about 31–33% each) and to A27 in under 1%. Before, A26's transitions split across A41 (about 38%), A35 (about 33%) and A36 (about 28%); in the later window about 98% went to A30. Each of A35, A36 and A41 sent about 28–38% of its transitions to A30 before and under 1% after, while transitions to A27 rose from under 1% to about 31–33%. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005]

- Step distances moved as this reversal implies. A25→A27 went from immediately consecutive (lag 1 in about 99 in 100) to 4–5 steps apart (about 99.5 in 100); A26→A30 went from 4–5 steps apart (100 in 100) to immediately consecutive (about 98.5 in 100). The pairs A25→A35 and A35→A27, seen in about 1 in 200 traces before, appeared in about 199 in 200 traces in the later window; the pairs A26→A35/A36/A41 and A35/A36/A41→A30, seen in about 200 in 200 before, dropped to about 1–2 in 200. [LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006, LAG007, LAG008, LAG009, LAG010]

- Event positions shifted consistently: A27's mean position moved from about 0.38 to about 0.47 of the trace and A26's from about 0.53 to about 0.62, while A35, A36 and A41 moved earlier, from about 0.58–0.59 to about 0.42. [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the execution order of two segments was reversed. Before, the flow ran A25 → A27 → A26 → A35/A36/A41 (the three executed in a varying internal order) → A30; in the later window it runs A25 → A35/A36/A41 → A27 … A26 → A30, with A30 now immediately following A26. In effect, the block containing A27 and A26 and the block containing A35, A36 and A41 exchanged places, and A30 moved from closing the trio to directly following A26. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG007]

Supporting signs: all six order relations flip almost completely (from about 98–99.5 in 100 one way to about 97–98 in 100 the other) while co-occurrence stays at 100 in 100; the step-distance changes match the reversal exactly; the successor profiles of all five source activities shift consistently; and 26 qualifying items against roughly 0.4 expected by chance in a stable process is far beyond what random re-splits of these windows produce. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006, LAG007, LAG008, LAG009, LAG010, DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005]

Limitations: the relative order of A27 and A26 in the later window is not directly observed and rests only on position statistics (mean about 0.47 versus 0.62), and many items describe the same underlying reversal rather than independent confirmations. A competing explanation the two windows cannot rule out is that each window is dominated by a different pre-existing, near-deterministic variant (for example different case types), so the flip could reflect a shift in the case mix rather than a reordering within one process. [POS001, POS002, REL001, REL004]
