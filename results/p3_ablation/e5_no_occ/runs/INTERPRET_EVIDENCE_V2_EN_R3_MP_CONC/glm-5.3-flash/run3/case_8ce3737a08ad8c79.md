### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the choice between two existing alternative paths after A20 shifted: the path in which A21, A22 and A23 are executed together, taken in about 19 in 100 cases in the earlier window, is taken in about 79 in 100 cases in the later window, while the alternative path executing A24, A25 and A26 together fell from about 82 to about 21 in 100. The change is seen directly in the successor shares of A20 (observed in all 200 traces in both windows) and consistently across both blocks' relations with A20, A30, A32 and A33, whose co-occurrence and order patterns scale proportionally while the blocks remain mutually exclusive; every involved activity stays well observed in both windows, so this is a shift in how often existing paths are taken rather than a replacement of one path by another. Screening found 53 qualifying items where fewer than 1 would be expected without a change, so sampling variation cannot account for the ensemble, though many items describe the same underlying swap rather than independent differences. A competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that the mix of cases reaching the A20 decision changed, making an existing branch more common, rather than the routing itself having changed. [DIR003, REL009, REL012, REL015, REL020, REL024, REL036, REL038, REL039]

Other differences: A10 became less frequent (about 16 to about 7 in 100 traces) and moved from a stable late position to a variable mid-trace position, with its outgoing transitions changing from almost always A33 to a spread over several activities and A32's transitions to A10 nearly disappearing; these observations rest on 33 and 14 traces and are not part of the main hypothesis. A02's successor profile also differs, but on very few observations. [POS001, DIR002, DIR004, DIR001]
