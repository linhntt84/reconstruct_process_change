### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that, in the later window, two three-activity groups exchanged the slots they occupy between stable surrounding activities: A19, A21 and A24, which earlier formed a block between A40 and A25, now form a block between A29 and A30, while A20, A22 and A23, which earlier ran between A29 and A30, now run between A40 and A25; A25 and A29 kept their places between the two blocks (both shifted slightly later), and the internal cycling of A20/A22/A23 persisted. The sign is direct and consistent across indicator types: near-deterministic order flips between the groups (A25 before A20 in 98 of 100 traces earlier, A20 before A25 in 98 of 100 later), position shifts of all six activities in the predicted directions, complete successor replacements at A19/A21/A24, A29 and A40, and adjacency counts flipping between about 200 and about 1 of 200 traces. Screening produced 90 qualifying control-flow items where roughly 0.84 would be expected without a change, so chance cannot account for the evidence as a whole, and each item's difference exceeds about 99.5 of 200 random re-splits; note, however, that many items describe the same underlying behaviour and are not independent confirmations. A competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that the blocks were not relocated as wholes but that entry and exit routing changed so as to produce the same sequences; the stable anchor positions and the position shifts of all six activities make plain rewiring less plausible, but the window-level aggregates cannot fully separate the two.
