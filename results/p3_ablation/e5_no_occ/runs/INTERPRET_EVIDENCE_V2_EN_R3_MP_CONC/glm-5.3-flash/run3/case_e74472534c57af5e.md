### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that, in the later window, the chain A30 → A31 → A36 is executed several times per case instead of once: A36 typically returns to A30 between repetitions (about 71 in 100 of its outgoing transitions, versus under 1 in 100 before), A30, A31 and A36 occur about three to four times per case versus about once, and the block's fixed position after A14 gave way to interleaved-or-repeated orderings, while the chain's internal order itself stayed intact (A30 → A31 and A31 → A36 at about 97–99 in 100 in both windows). [DIR005, LAG002, DIR003, DIR004, REL003, REL004, REL005]

Sixteen control-flow items qualified against roughly 0.29 expected if nothing had changed, far beyond what an unchanged process typically produces. The signs converge on one underlying difference: the occurrence counts rise only for A30, A31 and A36 while A14 and A05 stay flat, the return edge is visible directly in A36's successor profile, and the doubled position spread of A30 and A36 fits repeated execution; the reverse order never appears, so this is not an exchange of positions. [DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, POS003, POS004, REL003, REL004, REL005]

Limitations: the relational indicator merges interleaving with repetition, so a strict sequential cycle cannot be separated from repeated execution interleaved with other activities — the main competing reading the evidence cannot rule out; the before-window evidence for the return edge rests on a single lag observation, though the successor profile shows the same relation with large support; and both windows are finite samples, so exact shares carry sampling noise. [REL003, REL004, REL005, LAG002, DIR005]

Other differences: A05's routing shifted toward A07 and away from A14 as successors, it occurs slightly less often and slightly earlier, and it precedes A30/A36 in far fewer co-occurring traces; the evidence cannot show whether these A05 changes belong to the same redesign or are an independent shift.
