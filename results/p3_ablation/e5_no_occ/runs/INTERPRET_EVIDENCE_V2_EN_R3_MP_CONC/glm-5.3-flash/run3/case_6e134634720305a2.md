### Observed differences

- Both A30 and A36 still appear in every trace in both windows, but their repetition dropped sharply: in the earlier window each had about 690 outgoing transitions over 200 traces (roughly 3.5 occurrences per trace), while in the later window each has about 210 (roughly one occurrence per trace). [DIR001, DIR002]
- The order between A30 and A36 changed from mixed to fixed: earlier, the two were interleaved or repeated in about 75 in 100 traces, with A30 strictly before A36 in only about 25 in 100; in the later window A30 comes before A36 in about 98 in 100 traces, with interleaving in about 2 in 100. [REL001]
- The return path from A36 back to A30 nearly vanished: A36 followed by A30 within five steps was seen in about 150 traces earlier but in only 1 trace later, and A36 repeating itself within five steps fell from about 150 traces to 2. [LAG002, LAG003]
- A36's main successor switched: earlier, about 70 in 100 of its outgoing transitions led to A30 and about 28 to A25; later, about 92 in 100 lead to A25 and only about 4 to A30. [DIR002]
- The gap from A30 to A36 tightened: A36 followed A30 within two positions in about 12 in 100 observed pairs earlier, versus about 89 in 100 later. [LAG001]
- A30's own routing is essentially unchanged, with about 99 in 100 outgoing transitions to A31 earlier and about 97 in 100 later; both activities' event positions also became less spread out (standard deviation roughly 0.12 earlier versus 0.07 later) at nearly unchanged median positions. [DIR001, POS001, POS002]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the repeated alternation between A30 and A36 stopped and gave way to a single ordered pass: in the earlier window both activities ran roughly 3.5 times per trace in a largely interleaved pattern, with A36 frequently returning to A30; in the later window each executes about once, in the fixed order A30 then A36 about two positions apart (A30 still leading mainly to A31), after which A36 continues to A25 instead of returning to A30. This is a change in repetition and in the ordering constraint between the two activities, not an exchange of their positions. [REL001, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, DIR001, DIR002]

The support rests on direct relational evidence rather than item count: the fixed A30-before-A36 order, the disappearance of the reverse A36-to-A30 link and of A36 self-repetition, and the successor shift from A30 to A25 all describe one coherent behavioural difference, and every item's difference exceeds 99.5 in 100 random re-splits of the same traces. Eight control-flow items qualified overall, about thirty times the roughly 0.26 expected for an unchanged process, so chance sampling cannot account for the set as a whole. [DIR001, DIR002, POS001, POS002, REL001, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003]

Limitations: all items reflect the same underlying trace behaviour, so they are not independent confirmations, and the evidence cannot rule out that shorter traces in the later window contribute to the tighter position spreads; the "interleaved or repeated" category also mixes the two, although the occurrence counts and the A36 self-repetition result separate them. [POS001, POS002, LAG003, REL001]

Other differences: a few minor new targets of A30 appear in the later window, but each represents about a single outgoing transition and is consistent with sampling noise rather than a routing change. [DIR001]
