### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a structural hypothesis: in the later window, exactly one of the three previously unobserved activities A14, A15 or A20 is executed in almost every case (about 995 in 1000 traces, never more than one per trace) as a new step inserted into the formerly fixed sequence A22 → A23 → A24 — directly after A22 in about 56 in 100 traces and between A23 and A24 in about 43 in 100 — with A15 used somewhat more often (about 41 in 100 traces) than A14 or A20 (about 29–30 in 100 each). This is well beyond what chance would produce: 24 control-flow items qualified against an expectation of about 0.26 items for an unchanged process, and every item's difference exceeded roughly 995 of 1000 random re-splits. The participation change is direct (absent in all 200 earlier traces versus present in 58–82 later traces per activity, so the shares rest on substantial counts), and the direction indicators, the pairwise relations and the step-distance shifts all describe the same insertion coherently. The evidence cannot show what determines which of the three activities is executed or where it is placed, and it cannot rule out that these activities were already executed earlier but not recorded in the earlier window's logs, although 0 of 200 versus about 199 of 200 traces makes a pure sampling explanation implausible.
