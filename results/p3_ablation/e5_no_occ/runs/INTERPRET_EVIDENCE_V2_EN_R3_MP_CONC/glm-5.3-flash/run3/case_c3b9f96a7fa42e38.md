### Observed differences

- The dominant difference is a reversal of execution order between two groups of activities. In the earlier window, A35, A36 and A41 each ran before A26 and before A27 in roughly 99 in 100 traces (98–100 across the six pairs); in the later window, A26 and A27 each ran before A35, A36 and A41 in about 97–99 in 100 traces. All six pairs still co-occurred in every trace in both windows, so participation did not change. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006]
- The connections at the group boundaries moved with the reversal. In the earlier window, A25's outgoing transitions went to A35 (about 39%), A36 (about 32%) and A41 (about 27%), and A26 led almost always directly to A30 (about 99 in 100); in the later window, A25 leads almost always to A27 (about 98 in 100), and A26's transitions go to A35, A36 or A41 in about 97 in 100 cases (roughly a third each). [DIR003, DIR004]
- The trio's exit changed: earlier, A35, A36 and A41 each passed on about 26–39% of their outgoing transitions to A27; later, A27 is almost gone as their successor (about 0.5%) and A30 has taken its place (about 30–35%). [DIR005, DIR006, DIR007]
- Distances confirm the same picture. A25 reached A27 four steps later in about 94 in 100 earlier traces but directly (one step) in about 99 in 100 later traces; A26 reached A30 directly in about 99 in 100 earlier traces but four to five steps later in about 98 in 100 later traces; an A36-then-A30 pair one to three steps apart rose from 1 earlier trace to 198 later traces. [LAG005, LAG009, LAG011]
- Event positions shifted consistently: A26's median position fell from about 0.62 to 0.53 and A27's from about 0.47 to 0.37, while A35, A36 and A41 rose from about 0.41–0.42 to about 0.58–0.59; each of the five activities still appeared in essentially every trace in both windows. [POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006]
- The reversal is visible further upstream too: five steps after A16, traces previously reached A35 (58 traces) and now reach A27 (149 traces), while A16 followed by A35 within five steps nearly disappeared (4 traces). [LAG003, LAG004]
- A separate difference concerns A06 and A15: A06 appeared in about 27 in 100 traces earlier but about 13 in 100 later, at later and more variable positions (median about 0.20 to 0.28); of A06's observed outgoing transitions, the share to A16 fell from about 86% to about 39%, and A15's transitions to A06 fell from about 15% to about 3% while those to A16 rose from about 85% to about 95%. [POS001, DIR001, DIR002, LAG001]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the group A35/A36/A41 and the pair A26/A27 exchanged their order in the later window: the run previously read A25 → A35/A36/A41 (in varying order) → A27 → A26 → A30, whereas now it reads A25 → A27 → A26 → A35/A36/A41 → A30. The reversal is shown directly by the six pairwise relations, each based on all 200 traces of both windows and flipping almost completely; the successor changes at the group boundaries, the distance changes (A25→A27 from four steps to one, A26→A30 from one step to four–five) and the position shifts of all five activities all match one and the same rearrangement. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006, DIR007, LAG005, LAG009, LAG011, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006]

Chance is implausible for the ensemble: 31 control-flow items qualified against roughly 0.53 expected in an unchanged process (about 60 times as many), and because all five activities still ran in essentially every trace in both windows, the reversal cannot be a rare-variant or participation effect. Limitations: the indicators use event positions rather than clock time, and the internal order within each group is not directly observed, so the evidence establishes the exchange of the two groups but not that each moved as a fixed block; a broader re-routing that reuses the same activities in a different sub-sequence would look identical on these indicators. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006, DIR007, POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006, REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006, LAG007, LAG008, LAG009, LAG010, LAG011, LAG012]

Other differences: A06 became less frequent (about 27 in 100 traces earlier, about 13 in 100 later) and later in the trace, and routing around A15 shifted so that A15 now usually bypasses A06 and goes directly to A16; the later-window samples behind the A06 distance and position items are small (11–25 traces), so this upstream change is less firmly characterised than the group reversal. [POS001, DIR001, DIR002, LAG001, LAG002]
