### Observed differences

- The order between two groups of activities reversed. In the earlier window, A26 came before each of A35, A36 and A41 in about 98 in 100 traces, and A27 came before each of them in about 99 in 100. In the later window this is inverted: each of A35, A36 and A41 comes before A26 in about 97–98 in 100 traces and before A27 in about 97 in 100. All six pairs still occur together in every trace in both windows, so participation itself did not change. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006]
- Step distances moved with the reversal. Earlier, A27 followed A25 immediately (about 99 in 100 within one step) and A30 followed A26 only after 4–5 steps (100 in 100). In the later window, A27 follows A25 only after 4–5 steps (about 99 in 100), while A30 immediately follows A26 (about 99 in 100). [LAG001, LAG003]
- Successors were rewired consistently. Earlier, A25 led to A27 in about 98 in 100 of its observed outgoing transitions; in the later window it leads roughly equally to A36, A41 and A35 (about 31–33 in 100 each). Earlier, A26's outgoing transitions spread over A41, A35 and A36 (about 28–38 in 100 each); in the later window A26 leads to A30 in about 98 in 100. Each of A35, A36 and A41 earlier led to A30 in about 28–34 in 100 cases; in the later window A30 is practically no longer observed as their successor, with A27 appearing there instead (about 31–33 in 100). [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005]
- Relative event positions shifted in the same direction: A35, A36 and A41 each moved earlier (mean relative position from about 0.58 to about 0.42), while A27 (about 0.38 to 0.47) and A26 (about 0.53 to 0.62) moved later. [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005]
- Several pair-distance items rest on only 1–2 observations in one window (for example, A26 and A35 appeared within five steps in about 200 traces earlier but in only 1 trace later); they are consistent with the above but carry little weight alone. [LAG002, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006, LAG007, LAG008, LAG009, LAG010]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the relative execution order of two blocks of activities was reversed. In the earlier window the sequence ran A25 → A27 → … → A26 → {A35, A36, A41, in no fixed internal order} → A30; in the later window it runs A25 → {A35, A36, A41} → A27 → … → A26 → A30. A35, A36 and A41, which previously ran after A26 and A27, now run between A25 and A27, and A30, which previously followed the trio, now directly follows A26. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, LAG001, LAG003, POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005]

Supporting signs: all six order relations reverse completely and in the same direction, each backed by about 200 traces per window with co-occurrence unchanged at 100 in 100, so no activity became optional or was replaced; the successor changes at A25, A26 and the trio members match the same rearrangement; the two step-distance items with full support (A25–A27 and A26–A30, about 200 observations per window each) quantify the block shift; every item's difference exceeds about 99.5 in 100 random re-splits of the pooled data; and screening produced 26 qualifying items where about 0.41 would be expected without a change. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, LAG001, LAG003]

Limitations: all items describe one underlying rearrangement, so they corroborate rather than independently confirm it; the activities filling the gap between A27 and A26 are not in the evidence, so the full sequence around them is unknown; and the evidence cannot distinguish whether the trio moved earlier or the A27–A26 segment moved later, nor rule out that unobserved activities between these points also changed position. [LAG004, LAG005, LAG006, LAG007, LAG008, LAG009, LAG010]

Other differences: only minor shifts within the trio's internal, already unordered transitions (for example, A36 led to A41 in about 29 in 100 cases before and about 35 in 100 after, while A41 led to A36 in about 32 in 100 before and about 27 in 100 after), and a slightly wider spread of relative positions in the later window; both are small next to the main reversal.
