- The outgoing transitions of A15 shifted from one group of successors to the other. ### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the choice between two existing, mutually exclusive branches after A15 flipped in frequency: the branch in which A16, A17 and A18 run together — preceded by A15 and accompanied by A25 — was taken in about 3 of 4 cases in the earlier window and about 1 in 4 in the later window, while the branch in which A19, A20 and A21 run together rose from about 1 in 4 to about 3 in 4. This is a shift in how often existing paths are taken, not a replacement, bypass or reordering: every branch activity is observed in a substantial share of traces in both windows, A15's successor set is unchanged, only the shares moved, and within each branch the activities keep a mixed order in both windows. The signs are direct and consistent: A15's transition profile redistributed in step with the co-occurrence flips, all 27 relation items move by the same 50-percentage-point margin in the same direction, and the lag item agrees. The volume of flagged evidence also exceeds what chance produces as a whole: 29 control-flow items qualified where about 0.41 would be expected for an unchanged process (roughly 70 times as many), and every item's difference is larger than in about 199 of 200 random re-splits of the pooled traces. Limitations: the relation items overlap heavily — most of them re-describe the same branch-level flip and are not independent confirmations of one another. The transition shares at A15 are measured over its observed outgoing transitions rather than over cases. The evidence also cannot show whether the flip reflects a change in the routing itself or a change in the mix of cases reaching A15; both would produce the same pattern. [DIR001, REL007, REL022]
