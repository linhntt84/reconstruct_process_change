### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The number of qualified items alone exceeds what an unchanged process produces: 67 control-flow items qualified where about 0.6 would be expected by chance across 118 tested units, so the ensemble cannot be a sampling artefact. Beyond that count, the evidence supports one specific structural hypothesis: the segment between A44 and A29 changed its content — in the earlier window, traces executed exactly one of the three alternatives A19, A20 or A21 between A44 and A29, while in the later window A19/A20/A21 are observed in only about 1 of 200 traces each and the trio A26, A27 and A28 runs together in every trace in varying order, with A44 leading into it and A29 (and A45) following it. The support is direct and consistent: the successor change at A44 and the predecessor changes at A29 and A45 were observed relationally, the participation swap is near-complete (0 to 200 of 200 traces for the newcomers; about 30–36 down to about 0.5 in 100 for the old alternatives), and the A44→A29 distance growing from about 2 to about 4 steps matches one activity being replaced by three. Limitations: only two finite windows are compared, and zero participation means A19/A20/A21 were not observed later rather than provably removed; an important competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that they persist at a very low rate (about 1 in 200 traces each) and that the two windows capture the end point of a gradual transition rather than a single switch. [DIR009, REL033, REL035, POS005, POS002, LAG005, REL031]

Other differences: A10 declined in frequency (51 → 22 of 200 traces) and retargeted from A21/A19/A20 to A27, A15 and other activities, which is consistent with the disappearance of its earlier targets but is a separate participation change [POS001, DIR001]; A46 now accompanies the trio in every trace where it appears, while its overall presence fell from about 69 to about 57 in 100 traces [REL036, REL015].
