### Observed differences

- The two alternative activity groups swapped how often they run. In the earlier window, A21, A22 and A23 ran together in about 75 in 100 traces (in no fixed order among themselves), while A24, A25 and A26 ran together in only about 25 in 100; in the later window this reversed, with A24–A26 together in about 76 in 100 traces and A21–A23 together in about 25 in 100. The two groups practically never share a trace in either window (at most about 2 in 100). [REL013, REL025, REL027, REL034, REL042, REL049]
- The groups keep the same surroundings. A19 and A20 still precede whichever group runs (A19 before A24 in about 75 in 100 later traces, as A19 before A21 in about 73 in 100 earlier), A29 still precedes the group (about 73 in 100 in both windows), and the group still precedes A34 (about 73 in 100 in both windows). A20's outgoing transitions mirror the swap: about 75 in 100 went to A21–A23 earlier versus about 25 in 100 later, with the reverse holding for A24–A26. [REL016, REL019, REL022, REL030, REL051, REL053, DIR002]
- A06 changed from a rare, mid-trace activity to a frequent, end-of-trace one. It appears in about 17 in 100 traces earlier and in about half of the traces later; its median position moved from about 0.48 to about 0.96 of the trace; and about 90 in 100 of its outgoing transitions now go to A35, against about 12 in 100 earlier, when its successors were spread over many activities (largest single share about 21 in 100, to A33). [POS001, DIR001]
- A06 now sits between A34 and A35. Earlier, about 89 in 100 of A34's outgoing transitions went straight to A35 and A06 was not among A34's observed successors; later, about 50 in 100 go to A06 and about 48 in 100 to A35, A35 follows A34 two steps later in about 51 in 100 of cases (one step in about 49 in 100), and A35 follows A06 within one step in about 97 in 100 of the later A06–A35 pairs. [DIR005, LAG013, LAG001, REL011, REL012]
- Smaller shifts: A29's and A32's outgoing transitions became more concentrated on A19 (about 84 to 94 in 100) and on A33 (about 82 to 96 in 100), and A06 now co-occurs with the A24–A26 group in about 40 in 100 traces, against about 5 in 100 earlier. [DIR003, DIR004, REL006]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the share of cases taking two existing alternative paths flipped in the later window: the group A21, A22, A23, which ran together in about 75 in 100 earlier traces, now runs together in about 25 in 100, while the group A24, A25, A26 rose from about 25 to about 76 in 100, with the surrounding activities keeping their roles (A19 and A20 before the group, A29 before it, the group before A34). This is a shift in how often existing paths are taken rather than a replacement of one path by another: every involved activity and both groups appear in substantial shares in both windows, and only the shares between the alternatives changed. The pattern is direct and consistent across the co-occurrence, order and successor measures, each item's difference exceeds about 99.5 in 100 comparable random re-splits, and 83 control-flow items qualified against fewer than 1 expected without change, so a chance explanation of the set as a whole is implausible. Limitations: the many relation items largely restate one underlying shift rather than independent confirmations, and the pairwise measures cannot show where the choice between the two groups is made. [REL013, REL016, REL025, REL027, REL049, REL053, DIR002]

Other differences: A06 appears to have taken on a new end-of-trace role — it now occurs in about half of the traces (about 17 in 100 earlier), sits near the end, almost always directly precedes A35, and typically follows A34, which previously led straight to A35. The successor, position and gap evidence shows this directly, and it is not a by-product of the path flip, since A06's occurrence (about 53 in 100 traces) matches neither group's share. An important competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that both differences reflect a changed mix of complete case variants rather than a modified routing decision; the pairwise measures cannot distinguish these.
