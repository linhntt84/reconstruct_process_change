### Observed differences

- Successors of A31: in the earlier window, every observed outgoing transition of A31 went to A20. In the later window, about 71 in 100 of A31's outgoing transitions went to A25, while about 29 in 100 still went to A20. [DIR001]
- Order between A25 and A31: in the earlier window, both activities occurred together in every trace and always in the fixed order A25 then A31. In the later window they still occurred together in every trace, but in about 68 in 100 traces they were interleaved or repeated rather than in that strict order; the strict A25-before-A31 order remained in about 32 in 100 traces, and the reverse order never appeared in either window. [REL001]
- Repetition: about 697 outgoing transitions of A31 were observed across the 200 later traces (roughly three to four per trace), against 200 in the earlier window (about one per trace), consistent with A31 now recurring several times within a case. [DIR001]
- Spacing: in the earlier window, A25 and A31 were always exactly two event positions apart; in the later window that fixed gap largely disappeared, with the pair two positions apart in only about 12 in 100 cases and wider gaps appearing (five positions in about 5 in 100). [LAG001]
- Position variability: the relative positions of A25 and of A31 each became about twice as variable (standard deviation roughly 0.06 before versus 0.12 in the later window), with slightly later medians and near-unchanged mean positions. [POS001, POS002]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that, in the later window, A25 and A31 are no longer executed once per case in a fixed sequence (A25, then A31, then on to A20) but are repeated or interleaved — consistent with a repeating cycle between A25 and A31: A31 now leads back to A25 in about 71 in 100 of its outgoing transitions, recurs roughly three to four times per trace instead of once, and in about 68 in 100 traces the pair is interleaved or repeated, while A31 still exits to A20 in the remaining transitions (about 29 in 100). [REL001, DIR001, LAG001]

The change is shown directly by relational evidence (co-occurrence unchanged at 100 in 100 traces while the fixed order gave way to interleaving/repetition) and by a successor change at A31; the loss of the fixed two-position gap and the doubled position variability agree with the same behaviour. Five items qualified where fewer than one (about 0.14) would be expected by chance for a stable process, and each difference is larger than in about 199 of 200 random re-splits, so the ensemble is unlikely if nothing had changed. [REL001, DIR001, LAG001, POS001, POS002]

Limitations: nothing is observed about A25's outgoing transitions or about what, if anything, runs between the two activities, and the relational indicator merges "interleaved" with "repeated", so the exact shape of the cycle is unknown. An important competing explanation is a mixed population: about a third of later traces still show the old strict order, which fits both a uniform new cycle and a change affecting only part of the cases. Positions are event positions, not clock time, so no timing claim is involved. [REL001, DIR001]
