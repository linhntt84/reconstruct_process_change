### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that, in the later window, the block A33–A35–A36 that previously ran in every case between A30 and the closing group A37–A38–A39 is skipped in roughly half of the executions: about 46 in 100 later traces run the block, which then closes directly into A31 without reaching A37/A38/A39, while about 55 in 100 run A30 straight into A37/A38/A39 within 1–3 positions, and both groups appear together in only about 3 in 100 traces. The signs are direct and consistent: participation is observed directly (all traces → about 47% and 56%), co-occurrence between the groups drops from certain to about 2–3.5 in 100, the A30→closing-group distance collapses from 4–5 to 1–3 positions, and the routing profiles of A30 and of the block shift accordingly; the 51 qualifying control-flow items compare with fewer than 1 expected by chance, so the pattern as a whole is not what an unchanged process typically produces. Limitations: the earlier window contains no bypass executions, so the evidence cannot show whether this path is newly added or was previously too rare to observe, and the window aggregates cannot separate a change in individual-case routing from a shift in the mix of two coexisting variants; the position changes are in event order, not clock time, and are consistent with the changed case mix rather than proof that activities were moved.
