### Observed differences

- The relative order of two groups of activities reversed. In the earlier window, A21 and A22 always came before A30, A31 and A36 (in 100% of the 200 traces, with each pair co-occurring in every trace); in the later window, A30, A31 and A36 come before A21 and A22 in about 995 in 1000 traces, with co-occurrence still complete. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006]
- A20's routing changed: before, all of A20's outgoing transitions went to A22; in the later window they split almost evenly over A30, A31 and A36 (about 33 in 100 each), with A22 chosen in only about 1 in 200. [DIR001]
- A21's routing changed: before, A21's outgoing transitions spread over A36, A30 and A31 (roughly 38%, 34% and 28%); in the later window, A21 leads almost always to A25 (about 995 in 1000), and A25 immediately follows A21 — a gap of one step in 99.5% of cases, versus four steps before. [DIR002, LAG002]
- The exits of the A30/A31/A36 block changed accordingly: before, each of the three led to one of the others or to A25 (A25 taking roughly 28–36 in 100 outgoing transitions from each); in the later window, each leads to one of the others or to A22 (A22 about 31–34 in 100), with A25 nearly never chosen. [DIR003, DIR004, DIR005]
- Positions shifted consistently with this reversal: A21 and A22 now occur later in the trace (mean relative position about 0.53 to 0.62, and 0.38 to 0.47), while A30, A31 and A36 now occur earlier (about 0.59 to 0.41 each); the gap from A20 to A22 grew from one step to four steps in almost all traces. [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, LAG001]
- The six gap items measured in the old direction (e.g., A21 before A30, A30 before A25) rest on a single after-window observation each, because those ordered pairs became rare; they are consistent with the reversal but carry little weight alone. [LAG003, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006, LAG007, LAG008]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that, in the later window, the segment A22–A21 and the block A30/A31/A36 exchanged their order after A20: in the earlier window the flow ran A20 → A22, then later A21, then A30/A31/A36 in varying order, closing with A25; in the later window it runs A20 → A30/A31/A36 (in varying order), then A22, then A21, with A25 immediately after A21. The activities involved are A20, A22, A21, A30, A31, A36 and A25. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, LAG001, LAG002]

Supporting signs: the order reversal is observed directly and near-deterministically in all six activity pairs (100% before versus about 99.5% after, on 200 traces per window), and every difference is larger than in about 995 of 1000 random re-splits; the successor changes at A20, A21 and the block's exits, the position shifts and the two gap changes all point the same way. The screening qualified 24 control-flow items where roughly 0.28 would be expected by chance without a change (56 units tested), so the pattern as a whole is far beyond what an unchanged process typically produces. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, LAG001, LAG002, POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005]

Limitations: the evidence shows the reversal between the groups but not the exact internal wiring — for instance, whether untested activities sit between A22 and A21 in the later window — and the old-direction gap items rest on one after-window observation each. A narrower set of reroutings at A20, at the block's exits and at A21 that produces the same orderings cannot be distinguished from a wholesale exchange of the two segments. [LAG003, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006, LAG007, LAG008, DIR001, DIR002]
