### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a structural reordering: the block that runs exactly one of A14, A15 or A20 per case now executes after A22 — interleaved with A23 in no fixed order and before A27 — whereas earlier it executed between A40 and A21, before A21, A22 and A23, with A23 following A22 immediately. As a result, A40 now leads directly to A21, and the formerly fixed immediate chain A22→A23→A27 is interrupted in about half of the later cases [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006, POS001, POS002, POS003, REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, REL007, REL008, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004].

The change is shown directly and consistently: successor profiles changed at A40, A22, A23 and at A14/A15/A20 in mutually reinforcing ways; every activity pair spanning the two regions flipped or relaxed its order; and the step-distance changes (A40→A21 from two positions to one, A22→A27 from two to three) match the same rearrangement. With 24 qualifying items against about 0.4 expected by chance, sampling alone cannot plausibly account for the set as a whole [DIR006, DIR004, DIR005, REL001, REL004, REL006, LAG002, LAG004, screening].

Limitations: positions are event positions, not clock time, so no claim about timing is made; and because positions are relative, the evidence cannot fully separate "the A14/A15/A20 block moved later" from "A21 and A22 moved earlier past it", although the block's much larger shift (about +0.27 vs about −0.03) favours the first reading [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006]. The small participation shifts within the block (a few in 100 cases) may also be sampling variation between two 200-case windows [POS001, POS002, POS003].
