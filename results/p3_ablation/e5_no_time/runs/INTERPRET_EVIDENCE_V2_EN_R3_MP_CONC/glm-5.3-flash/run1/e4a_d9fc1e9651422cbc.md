### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the five-activity group A31, A32, A37, A38 and A39 was repositioned and made an alternative to A22: in the later window a case runs either the whole group or A22 between A21 and A26, whereas earlier every case ran both, with the group placed between A20 and A23. This is shown directly by relational evidence — the group's order relative to A21 and A23 flipped while its relations to A20 and A26 stayed fixed, and A22 and the group became mutually exclusive — together with the successor changes at A20, A21 and the group members; participation and position indicators agree with the same structure. [REL008, REL009, REL010, REL011, REL012, REL015, REL016, REL017, REL018, REL019, REL020, REL021, REL022, REL023, REL024, REL025, DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006, DIR007, OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, POS002, POS004, POS006, POS007, POS008, POS009, POS010]

Screening qualified 72 of 144 tested control-flow units, roughly 100 times the fewer-than-one item expected if nothing had changed, so chance sampling cannot account for the pattern as a whole. [screening]

An important competing explanation remains: the two windows may capture a shifted mix of case populations (for example, different case types routed differently) rather than a modified process, and the lag items — each backed by one later-window observation — are too thin to separate these readings. [LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006, LAG007, LAG008, LAG009, LAG010]

Other differences: only minor shifts remain — the mean event position of A20 moved slightly later (0.32 to 0.35) and that of A26 slightly earlier (0.67 to 0.65), most plausibly side effects of the group's relocation rather than independent changes. [POS001, POS005]
