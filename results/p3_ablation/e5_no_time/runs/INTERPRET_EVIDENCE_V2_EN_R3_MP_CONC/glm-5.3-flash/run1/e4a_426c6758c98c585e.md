### Observed differences

- Routing out of each activity collapsed to a single successor. In the earlier window, A15 led to A18 in about 62 in 100 observed transitions and otherwise to A19, A20 or A21; A18 led to A27 in about 53 in 100 and otherwise to A19, A20 or A21; A26 split its exits evenly between A19 and A17. In the later window each source has one successor in about 995–1000 of 1000 transitions: A15→A18, A17→A15, A18→A19, A19→A21, A20→A27, A21→A20 and A26→A17. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006, DIR007]
- Pairwise order flipped or locked in. Earlier, A19 preceded A15 in about 75 in 100 traces and preceded A18 in about 88 in 100, while A15/A21, A17/A19 and A18/A20 appeared in both orders in roughly equal shares. In the later window each of these pairs runs in one direction only: A15 before A19 and A18 before A19 in about 995 in 1000 traces, A15 before A21 and A17 before A19 in 100 in 100. [REL001, REL003, REL004, REL006, REL007, REL008]
- Step distances became fixed and mutually consistent. Earlier, distances were spread over 1–5 steps (for example A15→A21 over 1–3 steps, A15→A27 over 2–5); in the later window each pair sits at one distance — immediate for A26→A17, A15→A18, A19→A21, A21→A20 and A20→A27 (versus about 47–62 in 100 before), and exactly 2–5 steps for the remaining pairs — all matching the single chain A26→A17→A15→A18→A19→A21→A20→A27. [LAG001, LAG004, LAG005, LAG017, LAG020, LAG022, LAG025]
- Formerly common reverse orders disappeared. A19→A15, A19→A18, A20→A18, A21→A18 and A26→A20 still occurred within five steps in about 106–176 of 200 earlier traces, but in only 1 of 200 later traces. [LAG014, LAG015, LAG019, LAG021, LAG028]
- Participation did not change: every activity involved occurs in essentially all traces in both windows (pair co-occurrence 1.0 throughout; the six position-tracked activities appear in 100 in 100 traces in both windows). [REL001-REL008, POS001-POS006]
- Mean event positions shifted consistently with a reordering: A15, A17 and A18 now sit earlier in the trace (mean relative position about 0.64→0.59, 0.59→0.56 and 0.68→0.62), while A19, A20 and A21 sit later (about 0.59→0.65, 0.68→0.71 and 0.64→0.68); these are event positions, not clock times. [POS001-POS006]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the ordering constraints between A15, A17, A18, A19, A20 and A21 — with A26 at the start and A27 at the end — changed from a flexible order to one fixed sequence, A26 → A17 → A15 → A18 → A19 → A21 → A20 → A27. In the earlier window several pairs ran in both orders (A15/A21 and A17/A19 about 50/50; A19 before A15 in about 75 in 100 traces) and each activity had several successors; in the later window every pair keeps a single order in essentially all traces, every source has one dominant successor, and the step distances of more than twenty activity pairs all match this one chain. Because all activities still occur in essentially every trace in both windows, this is a reordering of the same activities, not their addition, removal or bypass. [DIR001-DIR007, REL001-REL008, LAG001-LAG029]

The change is shown directly by relational and distance evidence rather than by frequency shifts alone, and it is internally consistent: 50 control-flow items qualified where about 0.47 would be expected by chance among the 95 tested units, and nearly all items rest on about 200 traces per window, so sampling variation is an unlikely explanation for the pattern as a whole. [REL001-REL008, LAG001-LAG029, DIR001-DIR007]

A competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that the earlier window simply mixed several coexisting orderings of which only one remains in the later sample, rather than a single ordering rule having been tightened; both look identical here. The five vanished reverse-order items rest on a single later trace each, and POS/LAG use event positions, so no statement about clock-time timing is possible. [LAG014, LAG015, LAG019, LAG021, LAG028, POS001-POS006]

Other differences: none — the position shifts are part of the same reordering. [POS001-POS006]
