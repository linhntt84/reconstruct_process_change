### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the two activity groups swapped their order around the A24–A28 middle section: in the earlier window the low-frequency group (A14, A18, A21, A22, A23) ran first and handed off to A24, after which A28 led into the every-trace group (A15, A16, A17, A19, A20), which passed flow on towards A29; in the later window the every-trace group ran first and handed off to A24, A28 led into the low-frequency group, and that group passed flow to A29. The support is direct and consistent: all 45 relation items reverse in the same direction, the twelve position profiles shift coherently (each group by about a quarter to a third of the trace, A24 and A28 each by about 0.13), and the successor changes at A14/A18/A21–A23, A15–A20, A28 and A39 all match one and the same exchange; with 69 qualifying control-flow items against roughly 0.65 expected in that family for an unchanged process, the ensemble is far beyond what random re-splits of stable behaviour produce. Limitations: the evidence rests on relative event positions and pairwise co-occurrence rather than clock time, and it shows that the groups changed place but not the mechanism; A39 has no position profile, so its role is inferred only from successor shares. A competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that only the entry and exit routing changed — which group A39 feeds, which group A28 releases, and which group hands off to A24 or A29 — while each group's internal sequence stayed fixed; the observed reversal would look identical. Other differences: the low-frequency activities' participation shifted slightly (A14 from about 21 to 16.5 of 100 traces, A23 from about 16 to 20.5), a difference two 200-trace samples can easily show without any change; it is not part of the main hypothesis. [POS001, POS010]
