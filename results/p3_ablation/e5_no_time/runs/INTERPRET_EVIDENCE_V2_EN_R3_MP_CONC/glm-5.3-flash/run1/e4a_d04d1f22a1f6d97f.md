### Observed differences

- A22 was executed exactly once in every trace of the earlier window (participation 100%, 200 of 200 traces); in the later window it appears in only about 51 in 100 traces, still at most once per trace. [OCC001]
- A27, A28 and A33 were not observed at all in the earlier window (0 of 200 traces each); in the later window each appears in about 49 in 100 traces, and they always occur together — every pairwise co-occurrence is about 49 in 100, with no trace containing one of them without the others. [OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, REL012, REL013, REL014]
- A21's outgoing transitions changed from leading exclusively to A22 to a split: about 51 in 100 still go to A22, while the rest go to A28 (about 18 in 100), A33 (about 17) and A27 (about 15). [DIR001]
- The new activities sit between A21 and A23: A21 precedes each of them in the traces where they occur, and each of them precedes A23; among themselves both orders appear in similar proportions (for example, A27 before A28 in about 23 in 100 traces and A28 before A27 in about 27 in 100), so the block has no fixed internal order. [REL002, REL003, REL004, REL009, REL010, REL011, REL012, REL013, REL014]
- The distance from A21 to A23 grew from 2 steps in all earlier traces to 2 steps in about 51 in 100 later traces and 4 steps in about 49 in 100. [LAG001]
- A22 and A23 always co-occurred with A22 first in the earlier window; in the later window A22 precedes A23 in about 51 in 100 traces, while A23 appears without A22 in the remaining ~49 in 100 (and likewise A21 appears without A22 in ~49 in 100). [REL001, REL005]
- The new activities occur around the middle of the trace (median relative position about 0.48–0.50). [POS001, POS002, POS003]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that, in the later window, about half of the executions take a new route in which A22 is not executed and a block of three activities — A27, A28 and A33 — runs between A21 and A23 in no fixed internal order, while the other half still follow the earlier route A21 → A22 → A23. This looks like A22 having become optional (skipped in roughly 49 in 100 later traces) while the three-activity block was inserted on the alternative path, rather than a re-weighting of paths that existed in both windows: A27, A28 and A33 were entirely absent from the earlier window and A22 was present in every trace, which rules out a simple shift in how often existing paths are taken. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, DIR001, REL012, LAG001]

The screening qualified 26 control-flow items where an unchanged process would be expected to produce about 0.4, so the ensemble is far beyond what random splitting produces, and every item reaches an empirical strength of about 0.995. The change is shown directly and consistently across indicator families: the participation flips rest on large supports (200 versus about 98–102 traces), A21's outgoing profile redistributes in exactly the 51/49 pattern, the relational items place the block between A21 and A23, and the A21→A23 distance grows from 2 to 4 steps precisely in the share of traces that take the new route. [OCC001, DIR001, REL009, LAG001, POS001]

Limitations: with only two aggregate windows, the evidence cannot show whether the roughly even split is a stable routing choice at A21 or a transitional state, nor what determines which route a case takes; the internal sequencing of A27, A28 and A33 appears unfixed, so the ordering logic inside the block remains undetermined. An important competing explanation that cannot be fully excluded is that the new activities existed in the process earlier but were not exercised in the before window — the 0-of-200 participation makes this unlikely, but it cannot be ruled out from these samples alone. [OCC002, DIR002, REL012]
