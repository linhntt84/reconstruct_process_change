### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, the five alternatives A14, A18, A21, A22 and A23 each ran in about one case in five (16–23% of traces), together covering essentially every case, and appeared around the first third of the case (mean relative position about 0.33); in the later window they still run at similar frequencies but appear around 0.68–0.71 of the case length [POS001, POS005, POS008, POS009, POS010].
- A15, A16, A17, A19 and A20 ran in every case in both windows, but their mean relative position moved from about 0.63 in the earlier window to about 0.39 in the later one [POS002, POS003, POS004, POS006, POS007].
- All observed orderings between the two groups and the connectors reversed completely: A24 preceded each of A15, A16, A17, A19 and A20 in 100% of their joint appearances in the earlier window and followed them in 100% in the later window, and the same complete reversal holds for A28 [REL012, REL018, REL024, REL033, REL038, REL013, REL019, REL025, REL034, REL039].
- Order between the two groups reversed as well: A14 preceded A15, A16, A17, A19, A20, A24 and A28 in about 21 in 100 cases earlier and followed each of them in about 17 in 100 cases in the later window; matching reversals appear for A18, A21, A22 and A23 against the other group and against A24/A28 [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, REL007, REL008, REL026, REL030, REL040, REL044].
- Successors were rewired accordingly: A14, A18, A21, A22 and A23 led exclusively to A24 in the earlier window and exclusively to A29 in the later window, while A15, A16, A17, A19 and A20 sent about 14–27% of their outgoing transitions to A29 earlier and none later, with A24 taking that share (about 17–25%); A28's outgoing transitions moved entirely from A15–A20 to the alternatives group, and A39's moved in the opposite direction [DIR001, DIR005, DIR008, DIR009, DIR010, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR006, DIR007, DIR011, DIR012].
- The connectors themselves sit later in the case than before: A24 moved from a mean relative position of about 0.36 to about 0.48, and A28 from about 0.54 to about 0.66 [POS011, POS012].

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that two segments of the process exchanged their places around the connectors A24 and A28, which kept their sequence between them. In the earlier window the alternatives segment (one of A14, A18, A21, A22, A23 per case) ran first, fed by A39, and exited to A24, after which A28 led into the segment A15/A16/A17/A19/A20, which exited to A29; in the later window A39 feeds A15/A16/A17/A19/A20, which run first and exit to A24, A28 then leads into the alternatives segment, and that segment exits to A29 [DIR012, DIR001, DIR011, DIR002, POS001, POS002, REL012, REL013].

The reading is direct and consistent: 69 control-flow items qualified against roughly 0.65 expected if nothing had changed, so chance cannot account for the ensemble; the order reversals are complete rather than partial (A24 before A15 in all 200 earlier cases, in none later); the positional shifts match the rewiring; and participation barely moved — each alternative in about one case in five and A15–A20 in every case in both windows — indicating a reordering of existing behaviour rather than activities being added, removed or run at different rates [REL012, POS001, POS002, DIR001].

Limitations: most items re-describe the same reorder from different activity pairs, so they are not independent confirmations; positions are event positions rather than clock time, so no claim about timing follows; and A29's own position is not observed, so its place in the later window rests on the successor changes alone [DIR001, POS001, REL012]. A competing explanation the evidence cannot fully exclude is that each window mixes several behaviours and only their shares shifted, but within each window the observed orders are uniform, which favours a single exchange of the two segments [REL001, REL012].
