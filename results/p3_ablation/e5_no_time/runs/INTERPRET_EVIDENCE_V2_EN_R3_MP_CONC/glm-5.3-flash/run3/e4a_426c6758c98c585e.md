### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, the activities A15, A17, A18, A19, A20 and A21 were executed in a variable order: each had several frequently taken successions (for example, A15 was followed by A18 in about 62 in 100 of its observed outgoing transitions and by A19, A20 or A21 in about 12–14 in 100 each; A18 was followed by A27 in about 53 in 100; A19 led back to A17 in about 28 in 100). In the later window, each of these activities has essentially one successor — A26→A17, A17→A15, A15→A18, A18→A19, A19→A21, A21→A20 and A20→A27 each account for about 995–1000 in 1000 of the observed transitions — so the block runs as one fixed sequence from A26 to A27. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006, DIR007]
- The order between pairs changed accordingly while the activities still always occur together: A19 preceded A15 in about 75 in 100 traces before but follows it in virtually all traces after; A19 preceded A18 in about 88 in 100 before, now A18 precedes A19; A21 preceded A18 in about 70 in 100 before, now reversed. Pairs that previously appeared in both orders in similar proportions (A15/A21 about 50/50, A17/A19 50/50, A18/A20 about 47/53) are now in one fixed order in virtually every trace. Co-occurrence is 100 in 100 traces for every pair in both windows, so participation did not change. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, REL007, REL008]
- Distances between the activities became deterministic and match that single chain: A15 reaches A18 within one step in about 62 in 100 cases before versus virtually all after; A17 reaches A19 at exactly three steps after (previously spread over one to three steps); A15 reaches A20 at four steps and A26 reaches A21 at five steps in virtually all after cases, where previously these distances were spread over several values. [LAG001, LAG003, LAG005, LAG008, LAG029]
- Event positions shifted in the directions the new sequence implies: A15, A17 and A18 now sit slightly earlier in the trace (mean relative position about 0.64 to 0.59, 0.59 to 0.56 and 0.68 to 0.62), while A19, A20 and A21 sit slightly later (about 0.59 to 0.65, 0.68 to 0.71 and 0.64 to 0.68). [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006]
- A few distance items concern successions that are no longer taken (A19→A15, A19→A18, A20→A18, A21→A18, A26→A20); each rests on a single observation in the later window and is weak on its own. [LAG014, LAG015, LAG019, LAG021, LAG028]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the ordering constraints among A15, A17, A18, A19, A20 and A21 changed: in the earlier window these activities — entered from A26 and followed by A27 — were executed in a largely unfixed order with several alternative successions, whereas in the later window they run as a single fixed sequence A26→A17→A15→A18→A19→A21→A20→A27. The support is direct and consistent: relational items show order reversals and mixed orders becoming fixed while every pair still co-occurs in every trace; successor profiles collapse from three or four options to one; and pairwise distances become deterministic at exactly the values the single chain implies. The volume of qualifying items (50 against roughly 0.47 expected if nothing had changed) cannot be explained by sampling variation as a whole. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006, DIR007, REL001, REL003, REL004, REL006, REL008, LAG003, LAG008, LAG029]

This is a change in ordering constraints, not an exchange of positions and not a change in participation: every pair of these activities still occurs together in every trace in both windows. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, REL007, REL008]

Limitations: the evidence cannot show whether the earlier mixing was free ordering within one variant or several coexisting variants, nor whether the later sequence is a newly enforced order or one previously rare pattern that now dominates. [REL003, REL004, REL007] The handful of distance items about abandoned successions rest on a single after-window observation and were not relied upon. [LAG014, LAG015, LAG019, LAG021, LAG028]
