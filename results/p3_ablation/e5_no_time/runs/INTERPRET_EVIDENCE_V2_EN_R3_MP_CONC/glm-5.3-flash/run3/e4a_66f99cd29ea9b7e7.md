### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, A20's outgoing transitions went to A30 (about 40 in 100), A31 (about 33) and A36 (about 28), and A22 was not observed as a direct successor; in the later window, about 99 in 100 of A20's outgoing transitions go to A22, and A20 and A22 are adjacent in about 99 in 100 traces, where before they were four steps apart in every trace. [DIR001, LAG001]
- In the earlier window, A21 was followed directly by A25 in every trace; in the later window, A21's outgoing transitions go to A30 (about 36 in 100), A31 (about 33) and A36 (about 30), with A25 at about 1 in 100, and the A21-to-A25 distance grew from adjacent to four steps apart in about 99 in 100 traces. [DIR002, LAG004]
- The three activities A30, A31 and A36 changed their common exit: before, each led on to A22 in roughly 29–38 in 100 of its outgoing transitions; in the later window, A22 is almost never their successor (about 1 in 200) while A25 is (about 31–36 in 100). [DIR003, DIR004, DIR005]
- The order between this group of three and A21/A22 flipped completely: before, A30, A31 and A36 each ran before A21 and before A22 in 100 in 100 traces; in the later window, A21 and A22 each run before all three in 99 in 100 traces (about 2 in 200 traces keep the old order). Both activities of every pair still occur together in every trace in both windows, so participation itself did not change. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006]
- Event positions moved accordingly: A22's mean position shifted from about 0.47 to about 0.38 of the trace and A21's from about 0.62 to about 0.53, while A30, A31 and A36 shifted from about 0.41 to about 0.58–0.59. [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the segment formed by A30, A31 and A36 changed its placement in the flow. In the earlier window it ran between A20 and A22 — A20 branched into the three activities, which exited to A22, while A21 later led directly to A25; in the later window the same three activities run between A21 and A25 — A20 leads directly to A22, A21 branches into the three, and the segment exits to A25. All seven activities still occur in essentially every trace in both windows, so this is a reordering of existing activities, not activities becoming optional, repeated or more or less frequent. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, LAG001, LAG004]

The support is direct and consistent: the successor profiles change at exactly the boundary activities (A20, A21 and the three segment activities), all six activity-pair orders flip in the same direction, the two adjacency changes (A20–A22 becoming consecutive, A21–A25 becoming separated by the three activities) match the rerouting, and the position shifts of all five activities point the same way. The 22 qualifying items also far exceed the roughly 0.42 expected for an unchanged process at these thresholds. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, LAG001, LAG004, POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005]

Limitations: the evidence shows nothing about what runs between A22 and A21 in either window, and the internal ordering of the three-activity segment is only weakly characterised in the later window, where the corresponding distance items rest on about 1–2 traces each. The evidence also cannot rule out that the reroutings at A20 and at A21 are two independent changes rather than one segment relocation, since it shows the before and after relations but not the change event itself. [LAG002, LAG003, LAG005, LAG006, REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006]

Other differences: none beyond the main hypothesis; the remaining distance items (A20 with A31 and A36, and the segment activities with A22) are consistent with the same relocation but rest on about 1–2 after-window traces each and carry little weight. [LAG002, LAG003, LAG005, LAG006]
