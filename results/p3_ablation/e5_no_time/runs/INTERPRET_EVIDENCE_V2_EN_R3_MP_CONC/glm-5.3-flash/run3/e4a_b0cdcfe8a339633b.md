- In the earlier window, six activities formed two mutually exclusive groups between A25 and A26: A28, A30 and A31 each ran in about 49 in 100 traces, A32, A33 and A34 in about 52 in 100; group-mates always ran together, the two groups never appeared in the same trace, and each activity occurred at most once per trace. ### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the two activity groups between A25 and A26 — {A28, A30, A31} and {A32, A33, A34} — are no longer alternatives: where earlier each case executed exactly one of the two groups (about half of cases each), in the later window nearly every case executes both, first A28/A30/A31 in mixed internal order, then A32/A33/A34 in mixed internal order, between A25 and A26. This rests on direct relational evidence (cross-group co-occurrence rising from 0 to about 98 in 100 with a fixed first-before-second order), on matching edge changes (A25's direct transitions into the second group and the first group's direct transitions into A26 disappear, while transitions from A28/A30/A31 into A32/A33/A34 appear), on participation rising from about half to nearly all cases, and on step-gap growth consistent with the second group now running after the first. The volume of qualifying items (51 against about 0.59 expected if nothing had changed) also shows the differences as a whole exceed what chance produces in a stable process. [REL015, REL019, REL022, DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, OCC001, OCC004, LAG001, LAG004]

Limitations: execution of the two groups appears near-mandatory rather than strictly mandatory (about 1–2 in 100 later cases still miss one group), and the evidence cannot rule out that a small share of cases still follows the earlier alternative behaviour; because positions are step-based and pair distances are only captured up to five steps, it also cannot show whether other activities now run between the two groups. [OCC001, OCC004, LAG001, LAG004]
