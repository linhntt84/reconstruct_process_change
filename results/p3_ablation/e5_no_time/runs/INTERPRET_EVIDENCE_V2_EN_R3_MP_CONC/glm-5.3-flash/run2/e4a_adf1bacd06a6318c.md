### Observed differences

- After A15, the flow chose between two alternative three-activity branches, and this choice flipped between the windows. In the earlier window, about 75 in 100 traces ran A15 into A16, A17 and A18 (the three always occurred together in the same trace), while A19, A20 and A21 occurred together in only about 25 in 100. In the later window this reversed: the A16–A18 group appeared together in about 25 in 100 traces and the A19–A21 group in about 75 in 100. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, REL007, REL022]

- A15's outgoing transitions show the same redistribution directly: before, roughly 27%, 23% and 26% of its outgoing transitions led to A16, A17 and A18, and only about 14%, 4% and 8% to A19, A20 and A21; in the later window these became about 9%, 9% and 8% versus about 25%, 22% and 29%. [DIR001]

- The two groups were mutually exclusive alternatives in both windows — no trace contained activities from both groups (co-occurrence 0 throughout) — and within each active group the three activities ran together without a fixed order (for example, A16/A17 before split about 38% versus 37% between the two orders; A19/A21 in the later window about 34% versus 42%). The change is therefore in how often each existing branch is taken, not in which activities exist or in their ordering constraints. [REL009, REL014, REL018, REL007, REL013, REL022, REL023, REL025]

- A25 was present in essentially every trace in both windows but switched the group it runs with: before it co-occurred with A16/A17/A18 in about 75 in 100 traces (appearing without them only in the remaining ~25); in the later window it co-occurred with A19/A20/A21 in about 75 in 100. [REL012, REL017, REL021, REL024, REL026, REL027]

- A15 itself was present in all traces in both windows, and no activity showed within-trace repetition in either window, so no activity was added, removed or made recurring. [REL001, REL004, OCC001, OCC004]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the routing shares after A15 between two pre-existing alternative branches flipped: in the earlier window roughly 75 in 100 cases continued from A15 into the branch A16–A17–A18 (executed together, in no fixed order, alongside A25) and roughly 25 in 100 into the branch A19–A20–A21; in the later window the shares were reversed to about 25 and 75 in 100, with A25 accompanying whichever branch ran. [OCC001, OCC004, DIR001, REL004, REL022, REL012, REL024]

Supporting signs: 35 evidence items qualified against about 0.53 expected for an unchanged process, far beyond what chance produces; occurrence, direction and relation indicators all show the same flip directly and consistently; both branches were observed in a substantial share of traces in both windows (the rarer branch in about 50 of 200 traces on each side), no activity disappeared, and within-group order stayed mixed, so the change is confined to how often each existing path is taken. Note that the many relation items largely re-describe this one underlying shift rather than confirming it independently. [DIR001, OCC004, REL009, REL022]

Limitations: the evidence cannot rule out that the two windows simply sampled a different mix of pre-existing case variants rather than a changed routing rule; it establishes the share flip itself, not its cause. [DIR001, REL009]

Other differences: the positional distance from A15 to A19 spread out — among traces containing both, the share at a one-step gap fell from about 54 in 100 to about 33, while three-step gaps rose from about 20 to about 39; this is a position-based shift consistent with the branch change, not evidence of a clock-time move. [LAG001]
