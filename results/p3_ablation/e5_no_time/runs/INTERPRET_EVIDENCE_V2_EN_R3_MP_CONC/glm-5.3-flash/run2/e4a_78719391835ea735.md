### Observed differences

- Participation is essentially unchanged: A15, A17, A18, A20 and A24 occur in every case in both windows, and the three branch activities A14, A16, A19 together appear in 200 of 200 cases in each window (60, 69 and 71 before; 65, 58 and 77 after), consistent with one branch per case and a near-stable split (about 30/35/36 in 100 before vs 33/29/38 in 100 after). [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006, DIR001, DIR003, DIR006]

- The two groups exchanged their place in the case. In the earlier window A14, A16 and A19 sat at about one-third of the trace (mean relative position 0.34–0.35) and A15, A17 and A18 at about two-thirds (0.64); in the later window A15, A17 and A18 sit at about 0.38 and A14, A16 and A19 at about 0.67–0.68, while A20 (0.38→0.44) and A24 (0.57→0.64) moved only slightly later. [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006, POS007, POS008]

- All 21 measured pairs across the two groups reversed their order. For example, A20 preceded A15 in 100% of earlier cases but follows it in 100% of later cases; A14 preceded A15, A17, A18, A20 and A24 whenever it ran in the earlier window (about 30 in 100 cases) and follows them in the later window (about 32–33 in 100); the same reversal holds for A16 and A19 against A15, A17, A18, A20 and A24. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, REL007, REL008, REL009, REL010, REL011, REL012, REL013, REL014, REL015, REL016, REL017, REL018, REL019, REL020, REL021]

- Successors flipped accordingly: A14, A16 and A19 each led to A20 in 100% of observed outgoing transitions before and lead to A25 in 100% in the later window; A15, A17 and A18 lost A25 as a successor (about 30–38 in 100 transitions before) and gained A20 (about 28–37 after); A24 now leads to A14/A16/A19 (about 29–39 in 100) instead of A15/A17/A18 (about 30–37 before); A35 now leads to A15/A17/A18 instead of A14/A16/A19. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006, DIR007, DIR008]

- Step distances changed consistently with a three-activity block swapping sides: in the later window A24 reaches A25 in 2 steps instead of 4, and A35 reaches A20 in 4 steps instead of 2. [LAG001, LAG002]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that two activity groups exchanged their positions around the stable A20 … A24 sequence while participation stayed unchanged. In the earlier window a case ran A35, then one of A14/A16/A19, then A20, later A24, then A15/A17/A18 (in varying order) and A25; in the later window it runs A35, then A15/A17/A18, then A20, later A24, then one of A14/A16/A19 and then A25 directly. [REL008, REL015, REL018, DIR001, DIR003, DIR006, DIR007, DIR008, LAG001, LAG002]

Supporting signs: the screening qualified 39 control-flow items where about 0.46 would be expected without any change, far beyond what an unchanged process produces; every item's difference exceeds about 99.5% of random re-splits; the reordering is shown directly and consistently from four angles (order relations, positions, successors, step distances); and participation and the branch split stayed stable, so this is a placement change rather than activities becoming optional or existing paths being taken with different frequencies. [DIR001, DIR007, POS002, REL008, LAG001]

Limitations: the many pair-level items largely describe one underlying reordering rather than independent changes, and the evidence covers only the measured activities — a broader resequencing that also moved unmeasured activities (for example between A20 and A24) cannot be ruled out. POS and LAG are position-based, but REL and DIR show the order change directly, so the hypothesis does not rest on positions alone. [POS001, LAG001, REL001, DIR001]

Other differences: the choice among the three branch activities shifted slightly (A16 about 35→29 in 100 cases, A19 about 36→39), a difference consistent with sampling variation between two 200-case windows. [POS001, POS003, POS006]
