### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, A25, A26 and A31 each occurred exactly once in every trace (mean 1.0 occurrence per trace, present in 100% of traces, no repeats). In the later window, each of the three occurs about 3.5 times per trace, still in every trace, and about 68 in 100 traces now contain repeats of them. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003]
- The flow out of A31 changed: earlier, all of its outgoing transitions went to A20; in the later window, about 71 in 100 outgoing transitions go to A25 while about 29 in 100 still go to A20 (based on roughly 700 outgoing transitions). [DIR001]
- The relation between A25 and A31 changed from a fixed sequence to a mixed one: earlier, both co-occurred in every trace and A25 always came before A31 in a single pass; in the later window they still co-occur in every trace, but in about 68 in 100 traces their execution is interleaved or repeated rather than one A25-then-A31 pass. [REL001]
- The tight adjacency between A25 and A31 largely dissolved: earlier the pair was always exactly two positions apart; in the later window only about 12% of observed pairs remain at that distance and about 5% sit five steps apart, with most pairs no longer within five steps. [LAG001]
- Both A25 and A31 spread out across the traces: the standard deviation of their positions roughly doubled (A25 from about 0.06 to 0.12, A31 likewise), while their median positions moved only slightly later (A25 from 0.40 to 0.43, A31 from 0.47 to 0.48) — a widening consistent with multiple occurrences per trace rather than a wholesale relocation. [POS001, POS002]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

In the later window, A25, A26 and A31 came to be executed repeatedly within cases: activities that previously ran exactly once per case in a fixed A25-before-A31 sequence now occur about 3.5 times per case, with A31 routing back to A25 in roughly 7 in 10 of its outgoing transitions while about 3 in 10 still exit to A20. The repetition is measured directly with full support in both windows (200 of 200 traces in each), the routing change rests on about 700 outgoing transitions, and the relational, adjacency and positional items all describe this same behaviour; each difference is larger than in about 995 of 1,000 random re-splits, and the eight qualifying control-flow items far exceed the roughly 0.3 expected by chance among 60 tested units, so chance sampling cannot account for the set as a whole. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, DIR001, REL001, LAG001, POS001, POS002]

Limitations: the exact shape of the repetition is not pinned down — A26 has no routing or relational evidence, so its place relative to the A25–A31 back-and-forth is unobserved, and with repeats in about 68 in 100 traces it remains possible that only a majority subset of later cases repeats while the rest still run a single pass. All items come from the control-flow family and several describe the same traces, so they corroborate one behaviour rather than providing independent confirmations. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, REL001]

Other differences: none beyond the above; the widened position spread and the loss of tight A25–A31 adjacency are facets of the same repetition behaviour rather than separate changes. [POS001, POS002, LAG001]
