### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, A14, A15 and A20 each ran in every case, exactly once per case and never repeated; in the later window each of them appears in only about 53 in 100 cases [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003].
- The three activities behave as one block: before, every pair of them co-occurred in all cases; in the later window each pair co-occurs in about 53 in 100 cases and both members are absent in about 47 in 100, with no case where only one of the pair runs [REL001, REL002, REL005].
- A36's follow-on behaviour changed: before, all of its observed outgoing transitions led to A14; in the later window about 53 in 100 still lead to A14 while about 47 in 100 lead directly to A21, a direct succession not observed in the earlier window [DIR001].
- Measured in event steps, the distance from A36 to A21 was always 4 before, with the three activities in between; in the later window it is 1 step in about 47 in 100 cases and 4 steps in the remaining cases [LAG001].
- A21 and A36 themselves still occur in every case in both windows [POS001, POS002, REL003].
- When the three activities do run in the later window, their mutual order is unchanged: A14 before A20 before A15, all after A36 and before A21 [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL005].

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the segment A14 → A20 → A15, which ran between A36 and A21 in every earlier case, became optional: in the later window about 47 in 100 cases skip the entire segment and A36 is followed directly by A21, while the remaining about 53 in 100 cases still execute the original sequence. Participation in A14, A15 and A20 fell from all cases to about 53 in 100 and they are always skipped together; A36 now routes directly to A21 in about 47 in 100 of its outgoing transitions; and the A36→A21 distance dropped from always 4 steps to 1 step in about 47 in 100 cases. The activities were not relocated: in pairs with A21 or A36 they are either co-present or wholly absent from a case, never appearing alone [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, DIR001, LAG001, REL003, REL004].

Chance context: 16 control-flow items qualified against roughly 0.35 expected for an unchanged process, so the set of differences is many times larger than chance would produce, and each item's difference exceeds about 995 in 1,000 random re-splits of the pooled windows; note that the occurrence, relation, direction and lag items describe the same traces and are not independent confirmations [screening, OCC001, DIR001, LAG001].

Limitations: the bypass is observed on A36's outgoing side, but the evidence cannot show where the skip decision originates — an upstream routing point that leaves the segment out, rather than a new choice at A36 itself, cannot be ruled out [DIR001, LAG001].

Other differences: the only remaining signals are small shifts in average event position — A21 from about 0.43 to 0.40 of the trace length and A36 from about 0.30 to 0.32 — which are consistent with cases shortening when the segment is skipped rather than a separate relocation of either activity [POS001, POS002].
