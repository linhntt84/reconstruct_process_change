### Observed differences

- Routing collapsed to single successors. In the earlier window, transitions out of A15 split across A18 (about 62 in 100) and A19, A20, A21 (about 12–14 in 100 each); transitions out of A18 went straight to A27 in about 53 in 100; and A26 led to A19 or A17 in equal shares. In the later window, each of these sources has essentially one successor (about 99.5–100 in 100 of observed transitions): A26→A17, A17→A15, A15→A18, A18→A19, A19→A21, A21→A20, A20→A27. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006, DIR007]

- Pair orders that were mixed or reversed became fixed. Earlier, A19 preceded A15 in about 75 in 100 traces and preceded A18 in about 88 in 100; in the later window A15 precedes A19 and A18 precedes A19 in about 995 in 1000. A15/A21 and A17/A19 had no fixed order earlier (about 50/50 each way) and are fully ordered later; A18/A21 flipped from A21-first (about 70 in 100) to A18-first (about 995 in 1000). [REL001, REL003, REL004, REL006, REL008]

- Distances became fixed. In the later window the consecutive chain steps lie exactly one event apart in about 995–1000 in 1000 cases (earlier about 47–66 in 100), and downstream pairs sit at exact distances: A15 reaches A27 exactly five steps later in about 995 in 1000 traces (earlier the gap spread over 2–5 steps), and A17 reaches A20 at five steps (earlier spread over 1–5). [LAG001, LAG005, LAG006, LAG009, LAG017, LAG020, LAG022, LAG025]

- Relative positions moved consistently with this: A19's mean position shifted later (about 0.59 to 0.65 of the trace) and A18's earlier (about 0.68 to 0.62), with A15 and A17 slightly earlier and A20 and A21 slightly later. [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006]

- Participation itself did not change: every tested activity pair occurs together in 100 in 100 traces in both windows, so all these activities were present in every case before and after. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, REL007, REL008]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the ordering among A26, A17, A15, A18, A19, A21, A20 and A27 was restructured into one fixed sequence A26 → A17 → A15 → A18 → A19 → A21 → A20 → A27: in the earlier window these activities (all present in every case) were executed in varying orders with several routing options from each activity, while in the later window each activity immediately follows a single predecessor and downstream pairs occur at fixed distances. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006, DIR007, REL001, REL003, REL004, REL006, REL008, LAG001, LAG005, LAG009, LAG017, LAG020, LAG022, LAG025, POS003, POS004]

The judgement rests on directness and consistency rather than item count: the order changes are shown directly by the relation, distance and successor evidence, all qualifying items describe one coherent re-sequencing, and 50 items qualified where roughly 0.5 would be expected by chance in an unchanged process, so sampling alone cannot explain the ensemble (the items are facets of one change, not independent confirmations). [REL001, REL006, LAG005, DIR003]

Limitations: the evidence cannot distinguish whether the earlier window's mixed orders reflect per-case flexibility or a mixture of a few fixed variants that collapsed to one; the after-profiles of the reversed-direction distance items rest on about one observation each and only corroborate that those directions disappeared; and positions are event positions, not clock times. [LAG014, LAG015, LAG019, LAG021, LAG028, POS001]

Other differences: none observed — all qualifying items concern this re-sequencing of the same activity group. [DIR001, REL001, LAG001, POS001]
