- The segment A14 → A15 ran in every case in the earlier window (200 of 200 traces, once per trace) but in only about 52 in 100 traces in the later window (about 105 of 200); in the remaining cases neither activity appears. [OCC001, OCC002, REL001]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that, in the later window, the segment A14 → A15 — together with its optional continuation (either A20 → A21 or A22) — is bypassed in roughly 48 in 100 cases, with A38 leading directly to A23; in the earlier window this direct succession never occurred (0 of 200 traces). In the remaining ~52 in 100 cases the earlier structure is intact, including the roughly even choice between the two alternatives, so the segment appears to have become skippable rather than removed. The hypothesis rests on the direct successor change at A38 [DIR001], the new immediate A38→A23 relation [LAG001], the coordinated participation drops for A14, A15, A20, A21 and A22 [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005], and relation profiles moving from always-co-occurring to co-occurring in about half the traces [REL001, REL005, REL006, REL010, REL011]. The set of 29 qualifying items, against fewer than one expected by chance in a stable process, is far beyond what an unchanged process typically produces [OCC001, DIR001, LAG001, REL001]. Limitations: most items describe one and the same underlying skip and are not independent confirmations, and the evidence cannot distinguish a newly added bypass from a shift in the mix of case types (for example, cases that never execute this segment appearing only in the later window), although the complete absence of the direct A38→A23 succession in 200 earlier traces makes a previously rare path unlikely.

Other differences: none beyond the small positional shifts noted above, which are consistent with the same bypass behaviour. [POS001, POS002]
