### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, each of the five activities A25, A26, A31, A32 and A33 occurred exactly once in every trace (mean 1.0 occurrence per trace, 100% participation, no repeats). In the later window, each occurs about 3 times per trace on average (mean 3.06), and in about 66 in 100 traces at least one of them occurs more than once. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the block of five activities A25, A26, A31, A32 and A33, previously executed exactly once per trace in a fixed sequence, is now repeated within traces — about three executions per trace — with A33 typically followed again by A25, so that the group appears to be run through several times per case. This is shown directly by the jump from single to about threefold execution [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005], by the new dominant A33-to-A25 transition [DIR001], and consistently across all ten activity pairs, which moved from a fixed or mixed single order to interleaved-or-repeated in about two-thirds of traces [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, REL007, REL008, REL009, REL010]; the loss of direct adjacency [LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006] and the widened position spread [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005] fit the same behaviour. With 27 qualifying control-flow items against fewer than 1 expected by chance, the volume of evidence cannot be explained by random sampling, although most items describe one underlying difference rather than independent confirmations.

Limitations: the evidence cannot show whether all five activities repeat together in every pass or whether some passes skip members, the return edge rests on a single successor change at A33, and the gaps of three to five events leave open that other, unobserved activities now run between the repetitions.
