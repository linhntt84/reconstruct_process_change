### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that, in the later window, the repeating unit formed by A02 and A04 is entered and repeated more often: A04 takes part in about 59 of 100 branch traces (about 44 before) and repeats in about twice as many traces (28 vs 15 of 200), A02 recurs in about 27 of 100 traces (about 20 before) and averages about 1.0 occurrences per trace (0.77 before), and out of A02 the observed continuations into A04 rise from about 43 to 54 in 100 while direct moves from A02 to A05 fall from about 28 to 19 in 100. This rests on direct routing evidence at A02 together with the participation and recurrence increases, each larger than roughly 91–97 of 100 random re-splits of the pooled data, and the relation, distance and position indicators all point the same way; because the branch's share of traces is unchanged (44–46 in 100) and every involved activity is well represented in both windows, this reads as a change in how often an existing repetition is taken, not as an activity being added, removed or bypassed. Limitations: the evidence carries no screening baseline, so the number of qualifying items cannot be compared with what an unchanged process would produce, the many relation and distance items describe one underlying behaviour rather than independent confirmations, and a window-to-window sampling difference remains a competing explanation that cannot be fully ruled out. [OCC002, OCC004, DIR002, REL011, LAG001, LAG007]

Other differences: within the tail, the ordering A09 before A05 became more common than the reverse, with matching shifts in the transitions out of A05 and A09; and in the A01 branch, A10 participation fell slightly while A06 rose slightly — differences at or near the size random re-splits produce, and not part of the main hypothesis. [REL034, DIR003, DIR004, LAG010, LAG012, LAG015, OCC006, OCC010, DIR001]
