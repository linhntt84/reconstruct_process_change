### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

In the later window, the choice at A06 between A15 and A16 shifted toward A16 — from roughly 58/42 to roughly 50/50 of A06's outgoing transitions — so that the repeated A06–A15 execution cycle runs fewer times per trace: A15 dropped from about 0.72 to about 0.54 occurrences per trace and A06 from about 1.25 to about 1.07 while both keep roughly the same trace participation, and A16, although no more frequent overall, tends to occur earlier and more often directly after A06. This rests on a directly measured routing shift over about 250 vs about 210 outgoing transitions, a difference larger than in about 92 of 100 random re-splits; the drop in A15 executions (about 144 → 107 per 200 traces) matches the redirected A06→A15 transitions almost exactly; and the A16 position and lag changes agree in direction and are larger than in about 90–96 of 100 random re-splits. Limitations: the corroborating items all describe the same small region and are not independent confirmations, and with 200 traces per window a share shift of this size still arises in about 8 of 100 random re-splits of unchanged behaviour, so a sampling origin cannot be fully excluded; the data also cannot show whether the routing choice or the number of repetitions per pass is the primary change. [DIR005, OCC006, OCC015, POS015, LAG023]

Other differences: The A03/A11/A12 segment is executed more often and A02 gained an outgoing share to A11 (about 10% of A02's transitions) — though an edge unobserved in the earlier window need not have been absent then; separately, A01, A05 and A08 participate less, and A13/A14 occur later and A09/A10 somewhat earlier within traces, the latter based on small samples (about 21–26 traces). These differences are reported separately and are not part of the main hypothesis. [OCC011, OCC003, OCC012, DIR002, REL024, LAG008, OCC001, OCC005, OCC008, DIR001, POS012, POS013, POS008, POS009]
