### Observed differences

- The clearest difference concerns two pairs of strict alternatives. A01 and A06 never occur in the same trace in either window, and every trace contains exactly one of them: in the earlier window A01 ran in about 54 of 100 traces and A06 in about 46, while in the later window A01 ran in about 47 and A06 in about 54. [OCC001, OCC006, REL005]
- This flip is mirrored across the partners of both activities: A01's co-occurrence with A03, A09, A10, A11, A13 and A14 each fell by about 7 percentage points (for example A01–A11 from 52% to 45% of traces), while A06's co-occurrence with the same activities rose by about the same amount (A06–A11 from 45% to 52.5%). The orderings themselves are unchanged: both activities still always precede A11, A13 and A14 whenever they co-occur. [REL002, REL010, REL013, REL028, REL060, REL063]
- A second exclusive pair moved in parallel: A04 and A12 also never occur together, and the share of traces containing one of them stayed roughly stable (about 52% before, 54% after), but the split changed — in the later window A12 ran in about 32 of 100 traces (about 25 before) while A04 fell from about 27 to about 23. Both still always precede the closing activities A11, A13 and A14, and A12 still always precedes A03. [OCC004, OCC012, REL044, REL034, REL086, REL089]
- Downstream of A12 the flow redistributed: in the later window A03 receives about 43% of A12's outgoing transitions (about 24% before) and A06 about 17% (about 2% before), while transitions to A01, A09 and A10 shrank; this is the largest directional difference in the evidence. [DIR012]
- Smaller shifts: A14 is followed immediately by A11 in about 98% of its outgoing transitions (about 91% before); A10 tends to appear slightly earlier within traces (mean normalized position about 0.50 before vs 0.47 after) and A06 slightly later (about 0.15 vs 0.20); and A12 now typically follows A02 two to three steps later rather than immediately (one-step share about 23%→15%, three-step about 17%→33%). [DIR014, LAG127, POS010, POS006, LAG024]
- The rest of the backbone is stable: A02 still occurs exactly twice in every trace, A03, A11, A13 and A14 still run in about 97–98 of 100 traces, and the remaining activities' participation and repetition moved only marginally with low empirical strength. [OCC002, OCC003, OCC005, OCC009, OCC013]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that existing alternative paths are taken with different frequencies, while the structure and ordering of the process are unchanged. At the first exclusive choice, the A06 branch is executed in about 54 of 100 traces in the later window instead of about 46, at the expense of A01; at the second exclusive choice before the closing sequence, the A12 branch is executed in about 32 of 100 traces instead of about 25, with A04 down from about 27 to about 23. The supporting signs are that both members of each pair are observed in a substantial share of traces in both windows, that the pairs never co-occur in either window, that the shifts are mirrored consistently across occurrence, co-occurrence, direction and position indicators (each affected relation moves by roughly 5–8 traces in 100 in the same direction), and that no ordering constraint between the involved activities changed. The two shifts partly move together — joint A01–A04 occurrences fell from 16% to 10% while A06–A12 rose from 11% to 17% — but the evidence cannot establish whether the same traces switched at both points. [OCC001, OCC006, REL005, OCC004, OCC012, REL044, REL034, REL089, REL003, REL061]

The main competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is ordinary sampling variation between the two 200-trace windows: individually, the core swings (about 7 in 100 traces) are exceeded in roughly one of five random re-splits of a stable process, so chance could produce any single item, and only the consistent direction across many indicators argues against it. The evidence set also provides no screening baseline, so the judgement rests on directness and consistency rather than on a comparison with the number of items expected by chance, and several qualifying lag items rest on very few observations and were not used. [OCC001, OCC012, LAG065, LAG069]

Other differences:
- A12's outgoing flow shifted toward A03 (about 24%→43% of its transitions) and A06 (about 2%→17%), away from A01, A09 and A10; the evidence cannot show whether this is an independent routing change or a side effect of the additional A12 executions. [DIR012]
- A14 is followed immediately by A11 in about 98% of its transitions (about 91% before), and A10, A06 and the A02→A12 gap show the minor positional drifts listed above; none of these alters the ordering constraints between activities. [DIR014, LAG127, POS006, POS010, LAG024]
