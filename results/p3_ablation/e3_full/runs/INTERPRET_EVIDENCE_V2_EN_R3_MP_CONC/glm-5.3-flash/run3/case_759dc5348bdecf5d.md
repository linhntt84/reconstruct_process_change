### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the branch containing A03 and A11 was no longer executed in the later window. Earlier, A03 and A11 ran in roughly 29% and 20% of traces, typically as a repeated A03↔A11 sequence entered from A02 or A06 and exiting to A12 and A19; in the later window neither activity is observed in any trace, the downstream part of the branch (A06, A12, A19) fires in fewer traces (about 29 to 22 in 100), and A06 now leads directly to A12/A19 instead of into A03. [OCC003, OCC009, DIR003, DIR006, REL035, DIR005, OCC005, OCC010, OCC016]

The change is shown directly by participation and position support falling to exactly zero for both activities while their successor profiles vanish, and the relation, routing, position and lag indicators all describe the same underlying difference rather than independent changes; a drop of this size (57 and 39 traces to zero out of 200) is far beyond what re-splitting stable traces produces. [POS003, POS010, DIR003, DIR006, REL013, REL019, LAG021, LAG022]

What the evidence cannot rule out is that the branch still exists but was simply not triggered in the later sample — zero participation only means it was not observed — and the moderate fall in A06/A12/A19 (57 to 43 traces) could partly reflect sampling variation, although its agreement with the routing and lag shifts supports the reading that the branch stopped being executed. [OCC005, DIR005]

Other differences: minor successor-share wobbles elsewhere, such as A01 shifting from about 49/51 between A07 and A08 to about 44/56, and A14 splitting about 50/50 between A09 and A10 in both windows, are within the range random re-splits produce. [DIR001, DIR008]
