### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a restructuring of the control flow around the A04/A06 loop: A03, which earlier ran about twice per trace, fed the A04→A06 loop and routed out to A05, is gone; in the later window A02 opens every trace and routes either into the A04/A06 block or straight to A01, the loop returns from A06 to A04 (or exits to A01) instead of returning through A03, and the newly observed A01 closes every trace into the unchanged final A05. This is supported directly and consistently: the participation flips (0 of 200 vs 200 of 200 traces for A01 and A02, and the reverse for A03) are far outside what randomly re-splitting stable traces produces, the new edges and orders are shown directly by the direction, relation and lag indicators, and the shortened repetition gaps match the same restructure rather than an unrelated shift. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR005, REL001, REL004, REL008, LAG001, LAG006]

Because the activity codes are anonymous, the evidence cannot show whether A01 and A02 are genuinely new steps or a re-labelled takeover of A03's routing role — this competing reading leaves the described behaviour the same. The few observed A05→A02 transitions rest on only four observations, and the rise in A04/A06 participation from 44 to 50 in 100 traces is small enough that window sampling could produce it. [DIR004, OCC004, OCC005]

Other differences: A04 and A06 appear in slightly more traces in the later window (about 50 vs 44 in 100) with minor shifts in their positions within the trace, and a handful of later traces show A05 leading back to A02, which was not observed earlier; both are minor and the latter rests on very few observations. [OCC004, OCC005, POS004, POS006, DIR004]
