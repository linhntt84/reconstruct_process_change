### Observed differences

- In the later window, A13 is executed in a larger share of cases: it appears in about 35 of 100 traces (69 of 200) versus about 26 of 100 before (51 of 200), and mean occurrences per trace rise from about 0.93 to 1.18; the rise comes from more traces containing A13, not from more repeats within a trace (about 3.4 versus 3.7 occurrences per containing trace). [OCC011, POS013]
- As a direct consequence, A13 co-occurs with every one of the fifteen other activities more often, by about 5–9.5 in 100 traces (with A01 from 26 to 35, with A04 from 12 to 20, with A10 from 10 to 15), while the share of traces containing the partner but not A13 falls by a similar amount; A13 still never appears in a trace without A01. Several of these pair shifts are larger than in about 90–99 of 100 random re-splits of the same data. [REL012, REL026, REL038, REL049, REL070, REL094, REL099, REL114]
- A13's placement and routing are unchanged: its median position in the trace stays at about 0.44 of the trace length, its outgoing transitions still lead only to A05 and A15 in near-equal shares (about 47% versus 53% later), and its relations with other activities remain almost entirely interleaved-or-repeated rather than fixed-order. [POS013, DIR008, REL012]
- Milder shifts point in the same direction: the always-together group A05/A14/A15 appears in about 43 of 100 traces later versus about 40 before, with A05 and A15 also repeating more often within traces (mean 1.89→2.30; recurrence support 0.30→0.38), and the coupled group A04/A08/A11/A12 rises from about 62–63 to 67 of 100 traces. [OCC003, OCC004, OCC007, OCC012, OCC013, REL044, REL061]
- Small decreases appear elsewhere: A01 and A06, which always occur together, fall from about 68–69 to 65 of 100 traces, and A02 from about 51 to 46 of 100; A10 stays at about 48 of 100, and A03, A09 and A16 appear in every trace throughout. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC005, OCC008, POS003]
- Routing shares and ordering are otherwise stable: every measured successor profile keeps the same alternatives with only few-point shifts (the largest, out of A01, is the share leading to A06 falling from about 52% to 46% of A01's outgoing transitions while those to A05 and A15 rise slightly), and no activity pair changes between fixed order and unordered execution. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR004, DIR005]
- Lag distributions (distances in event positions, not clock time) move only slightly: the gap from A02 to A03 shifts toward larger distances (largest-gap share about 20%→39%; strength 0.95, but on only about 40 versus 33 observations), and gaps around A01/A05/A14 lengthen slightly (strengths 0.93–0.95). [LAG004, LAG011, LAG029]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that, in the later window, A13 is executed in a larger share of cases — from about 26 to about 35 in 100 traces — while its position, its successors (A05, A15) and its ordering relations stay the same, and its extra executions occur inside traces that already contained the other activities (A13 still never appears without A01). The support is direct and consistent: the participation rise is measured on the occurrence and position indicators themselves, and all fifteen activity pairs involving A13 show the matching co-occurrence increase of about 5–9.5 in 100 traces with the partner-only share falling correspondingly, nine of them at empirical strengths of about 0.86–0.99, which random re-splits of unchanged data rarely produce. [OCC011, POS013, REL012, REL026, REL070, REL114]

The main competing explanation is a sampling fluctuation in which traces fell into the two windows: on its own, the occurrence-based difference for A13 lies within the range random re-splits produce (strength about 0.65), the fifteen pair items all express that one underlying shift rather than independent confirmations, and no chance-expectation baseline accompanies the evidence; the breadth and coherence of the relational pattern make pure noise unlikely but cannot exclude it. [OCC011, REL026]

Other differences: the milder increases for A05/A14/A15 (including more within-trace repetition of A05 and A15) and for the A04/A08/A11/A12 group, and the mild decreases for A01/A06 and A02, are consistent with the same rebalancing of how often existing paths are taken, but the evidence does not establish them as one change together with A13; the small lag shifts (for example A02 toward A03) rest on few observations. [OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC012, OCC013, REL016, REL018, LAG011]
