### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that A01 has taken over the position and relations that A02 held in the earlier window: A02, previously executed once in about 46 in 100 cases, is no longer observed, while A01 now runs once in about 50 in 100 cases at the same early point in the flow, followed by essentially the same activities (A03, A04, A05, A06, A09, A10, A11, A13) and preceding the same downstream activities (A04, A11, A12, A14, A15) that A02 used to precede. [OCC001, OCC002, DIR001, DIR002, REL003, REL010, REL011, REL013, REL014, REL016, REL023, REL024, REL026, REL027]

The support is direct and consistent: both the disappearance and the appearance concern roughly half of all cases (92 of 200 to 0, and 0 to 101 of 200), each with an empirical strength of about 0.99, the successor profiles of A02 earlier and A01 later match almost component for component, and the incoming and outgoing relations line up item by item. The main limitation is that A01 and A02 never appear in the same window, so the evidence shows role equivalence rather than identity: a removal of A02 combined with the insertion of a new, similarly placed step cannot be distinguished from a substitution. A competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that the two windows draw on different coexisting process variants rather than a change over time, although the size of the shifts makes a pure sampling artefact unlikely. [OCC001, OCC002, DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR005, DIR011, DIR013, REL001]

Other differences: A03 and A10 are executed and repeated more often in the later window (A03 mean about 1.9 to 2.2 occurrences per case; A10 participation about 45 to 53 in 100 cases), and A11's position moves later, consistent with A01 now preceding it; these are separate from the main hypothesis. The remaining activities shift participation by at most about 5 in 100 cases, differences that most random re-splits of the same data also produce.
