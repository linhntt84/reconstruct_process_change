### Observed differences

- The sharpest difference concerns A10: in the earlier window it ran in about 44 in 100 traces (88 of 200), while in the later window it appears in none of the 200 traces; before, about 82 in 100 of its outgoing transitions led to A12. This difference exceeds essentially all random re-splits of the pooled data. [OCC010, DIR009, POS010]
- In the earlier window, A04, A10 and A12 always occurred together (in 44 in 100 traces, typically A04 before A10 before A12); in the later window A04 and A12 still always occur together, now in about 58 in 100 traces, without A10. [REL036, REL038, REL064, OCC004, OCC012]
- The connection from A04 to A12 became direct: A04's transitions to A10 fell from about 55 in 100 of its outgoing transitions to none, while those to A12 rose from about 10 to 73 in 100. The distance from A04 to A12 in event positions collapsed from mostly two–three steps (about 56 and 23 in 100) to one step (about 75 in 100). [DIR004, LAG017, REL038]
- The A04/A12 branch is taken more often and the A02 branch less often: A02 fell from 56% to about 42% of traces (112 → 85 of 200). A02 and A04 were strict alternatives before and remain so after — never in the same trace, exactly one of them in every trace. [OCC002, REL013, REL021]
- A01's exits shifted the same way: transitions to A02 fell from about 47% to 28% of its outgoing transitions, those to A04 rose from 33% to 52% and to A12 from 2% to 20%, and the edge to A10 disappeared. A03, A07 and A11, which previously led mostly to A10 or A02, now lead mostly to A04 or A12, though each of these profiles rests on only about 5–10 observed transitions. [DIR001, DIR003, DIR007, DIR010]
- Smaller shifts exist around A02's position, A05's successors and several lag distributions; see "Other differences" below. [POS002, DIR005, LAG013]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that A10 was no longer executed between A04 and A12 — in the earlier window traces typically ran A04 → A10 → A12, while in the later window A04 leads directly to A12 — and that this A04/A12 branch is now chosen over its alternative A02 more often (about 58 vs 42 in 100 traces, compared with 44 vs 56 before). [OCC010, DIR004, LAG017, REL036, REL064, REL013]

The structural part is shown directly: A10 fell from 88 to 0 of 200 traces, A04's dominant successor switched from A10 to A12, and the A04→A12 distance collapsed to a single step; the share shift is shown directly in the occurrence and relation indicators, with the strict A02-or-A04 alternative structure intact in both windows, and A01's rerouting plus the rerouted predecessors of A10 point in the same direction. The many relation items largely restate the same trace-level behaviour, so they are consistent with rather than independent confirmations of this change. [OCC010, DIR004, DIR009, LAG017, REL013, DIR001, DIR003, DIR007, DIR010]

Limitations: zero participation establishes only that A10 was not observed in the later 200-trace sample, so a drastic drop in frequency short of complete disappearance cannot be excluded; and the evidence cannot decide whether the A02/A04 share shift belongs to the same modification or is a separate routing change. [OCC010, REL013]

Other differences: A02 now occurs at earlier positions within traces (mean position about 0.31 → 0.19, upper quartile from the last position to the middle) [POS002]; A05's outgoing traffic moved from A06 (about 46% → 37%) towards A07 (about 39% → 49%) [DIR005]; A12's outgoing shares redistributed towards A05 and A06 and away from A04 [DIR011]; and several lag distributions shifted modestly, e.g. A04→A07 from mostly four steps to mostly three [LAG013, LAG052].
