### Observed differences

- The starkest difference concerns A09 and A16, which exchanged their occurrence and placement profiles. Before, A09 ran in every trace, about twice per trace on average and repeated in roughly 48 in 100 traces, sitting late in the trace (mean relative position about 0.68), while A16 ran in only about 50 in 100 traces, at most once per trace, early (about 0.39). In the later window, A16 runs in every trace, about 1.9 times per trace and repeated in about 44 in 100, sitting late (about 0.70), while A09 runs in about 48 in 100 traces, never repeats, and sits earlier (about 0.45). [OCC007, OCC013, POS008, POS015]

- The wiring moved with them. Before, all observed outgoing transitions of A04 and of A10 went to A09; in the later window they all go to A16. A01, A13 and A18 previously sent about 20%, 23% and 7% of their observed outgoing transitions to A16 and none to A09; in the later window comparable shares (about 18%, 27% and 4%) go to A09, with no outgoing transitions to A16 observed from them. [DIR004, DIR007, DIR001, DIR009, DIR012]

- The two activities also exchanged their outgoing behaviour: before, A09 led to A06 (about 50% of its outgoing transitions), A05 (about 25%) and A14 (about 24%), and A16 led to A10 (about 33%), A04 (about 30%), A01 (about 21%) and A13 (about 16%); in the later window A16 leads to A06 (about 54%), A05 (about 24%) and A14 (about 22%), and A09 leads to A10 (about 35%), A04 (about 29%), A01 (about 25%) and A13 (about 11%). [DIR006, DIR011]

- Pairwise relations flipped accordingly. Before, A09 co-occurred with A01, A03, A06, A13 and A18 in every trace; in the later window only in about 48 in 100, while A16 — previously together with each in about 50 in 100 traces — now co-occurs with each of them in every trace. A15 and A16 were strict alternatives before (never in the same trace, each present in about half of them); in the later window they always run together, whereas A15 and A09, previously together in about 50 in 100 traces, never co-occur. [REL007, REL014, REL037, REL044, REL076, REL082, REL108, REL137, REL113, REL147, REL143, REL110]

- Event distances swapped in the same direction: A01 to A09 was typically 2–4 positions apart before and 1–2 apart after, while A01 to A16 was 1–2 before and 2–4 after; A09 followed A06-immediately in the earlier window and sat 3–5 apart in the later one, the reverse holding for A16 to A06; A18 to A09 was 5 apart before and 1–3 after, the reverse for A18 to A16. [LAG004, LAG010, LAG028, LAG067, LAG081, LAG084]

- Beyond this exchange, several mid-to-late activities occur less often per trace: A12 about 1.7 times per trace versus about 2.3 before, A07 about 0.85 versus 1.33 (present in about 29 in 100 traces versus 36), A05 about 0.89 versus 1.24, and A14 about 0.82 versus 1.08; A12 also sends about 50% of its observed outgoing transitions to A07 instead of about 57%, and about 25% to A08 instead of about 20%. Smaller decreases appear for A02, A10 and A11, and small increases for A03 and A17. [OCC010, OCC005, OCC004, OCC011, DIR008, OCC001, OCC008, OCC009, OCC002, OCC014]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that A09 and A16 exchanged the roles they play in the flow. Before, A09 was the mandatory, late, often-repeated step that A04 and A10 led exclusively into and that led on to A06, A05 and A14, while A16 was an optional, earlier, never-repeated step reached from A01, A13 and A18 and leading to A10, A04, A01 and A13; in the later window these roles are reversed. The exchange is shown directly by the complete successor replacement at A04 and A10 and at A01, A13 and A18, by the two activities exchanging their outgoing profiles, occurrence levels and positions, by co-occurrence with A01, A03, A06, A13 and A18 flipping from always-with-A09 to always-with-A16, by the A15 relationship flipping from never-with-A16 to always-with-A16 (while A15 and A09 went from together-in-half to never), and by event-distance distributions that swap between the two activities. Several dozen mutually consistent items across the occurrence, direction, position, relation and distance indicators back this one pattern, and the reference comparisons indicate that differences of this size arise in fewer than about 1 in 100 random re-splits of unchanged traces, so sampling variation cannot account for the pattern as a whole. [DIR004, DIR007, DIR011, DIR006, OCC007, OCC013, POS008, POS015, REL007, REL014, REL143, LAG004, LAG010]

Other differences: the lower occurrence of A12, A07, A05 and A14 in the later window, together with A12 routing a smaller share of its outgoing transitions to A07 (about 50% versus 57%) and more to A08 (about 25% versus 20%), stands outside the role exchange; smaller shifts affect A02, A10, A11, A03 and A17. [OCC010, OCC005, OCC004, OCC011, DIR008, OCC001, OCC008, OCC009, OCC002, OCC014]

An important competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that these later-segment decreases are a separate change in how often the repeated paths around A12, A07, A14 and A05 run, rather than downstream consequences of the role exchange; the evidence also cannot establish whether A09 and A16 are functionally equivalent steps. [OCC010, OCC005, OCC004, OCC011, DIR008]
