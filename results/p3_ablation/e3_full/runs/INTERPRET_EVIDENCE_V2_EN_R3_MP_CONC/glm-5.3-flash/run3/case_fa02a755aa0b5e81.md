### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence directly and consistently supports one structural hypothesis about ordering: in the earlier window the group A06/A11/A12 ran before A01, and A01 immediately preceded A05; in the later window A01 comes first, hands flow to A06, and the group A06/A11/A12 then runs and flows into A05, so A01 reaches A05 four positions later instead of immediately. The signs are direct and exception-free: A01's successor switched completely (54 of 54 transitions to A05 earlier, 43 of 43 to A06 later), the order between A01 and A11/A12 reversed in every co-occurring trace, A11 and A12 now send about 70 in 100 of their transitions to A05 instead of to A01, and the A01→A05 gap grew from 1 step to 4 steps, with A10 — which sits between A06 and the group — also moving one step closer to A05; the positional shifts agree with this rerouting. [DIR001, DIR007, DIR008, REL004, REL008, REL009, LAG001, LAG016, LAG021, LAG022, POS001, POS004, POS008, POS009]

Limitations: positions are event positions, not clock time, so only the relative order is established; the segment also appears in fewer traces later (54 vs 43 of 200), a difference of a size that random re-splits of an unchanged process still produce reasonably often, and the evidence cannot tell whether the rerouting and this frequency decline are one change or two. The competing explanation that the two windows simply draw different mixes of process variants cannot be ruled out with finite samples, although it cannot account for a complete order reversal (never in 54 earlier co-occurring traces, always in 43 later ones). [OCC001, OCC011, DIR001, REL008]
