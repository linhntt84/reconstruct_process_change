### Observed differences

- The four activities A01, A03, A04 and A08 always occur together in the same cases (no case contains one without the others) and appeared in about 50 in 100 cases in the earlier window and about 48 in 100 in the later window, so the group's presence is essentially unchanged. ### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that, in the later window, the group A01–A03–A04–A08 is traversed more strictly as a one-way sequence: A01 opens the case and passes to A03, A03 hands over to A04 immediately more often and returns to A01 less often, the direct shortcut from A01 to A08 is nearly unused, and A08 is reached almost only from A04, which in turn passes to A07 more often than before. The rerouting is shown directly by the outgoing-transition shares of A01, A03 and A04 — three activities observed in about half of the cases in both windows, always together — and is corroborated by step distances and positions rather than resting on a single indicator. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, LAG004, LAG013, LAG015, POS001, POS002, REL001]

The central differences rank above roughly 77 to 99 of 100 random re-splits, and the routing, distance and position indicators agree on one mechanism, so sampling noise is an unlikely explanation for the pattern as a whole. What the evidence cannot rule out is that part of the rerouting is a knock-on effect of the more frequent repetitions in the later group (A07 repeated more often, and A04 itself rose slightly to about 1.01 occurrences per case), and a few individual edges rest on few observations (A01 → A08 about 6 versus 1 transitions; A03 → A08 about 3 versus 10); no chance-expectation baseline for the screening as a whole is provided. [DIR003, LAG004, OCC004, OCC007]
