- In the later window, A02 and A11 — two activities that always occur together — each appear in about 40 in 100 cases, up from 30 in 100 (60 to 79 of 200 traces), while A05 appears in about 28 in 100, down from 39 in 100 (78 to 56 traces); A05 also repeats in fewer of the cases that keep it (about 15 in 100 to 8 in 100). [LAG003, LAG006, LAG021, LAG042, DIR007]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the two windows differ in how often two existing alternative branches are taken inside the cases containing the A04/A08/A09/A10 group: the branch in which A05 runs was executed in about 78 of 100 such cases before and about 54 of 100 in the later window, while the branch in which A02 and A11 run rose from about 60 to about 77 of 100, with A01 correspondingly routing fewer of its outgoing transitions to A05 (about 30% to 20%) and more to A02 and A11 (about 10% to 15% each). The support is direct and consistent: participation and routing shares are measured directly on 200 cases per window, the core items show empirical strength of about 0.96 to 0.995, and occurrence, routing and more than twenty pairwise relation items all move in the same direction; every involved activity remains observed in 28 to 53 in 100 cases in both windows, the group's own participation is unchanged, and no observed successor set appears or disappears, so this is a reallocation of case shares between existing paths rather than a rare variant or an activity being added, removed or bypassed. [OCC002, OCC004, OCC010, DIR001, REL013, REL019, REL037]

What the evidence cannot rule out is that the two windows simply captured different case mixes — for example a shift in the incoming mix of case types — rather than a modified process; the evidence also carries no screening baseline, so the judgement rests on the size, coherence and directness of the measured share shifts rather than on a comparison against the number of items expected by chance. [OCC002, OCC004, DIR001]
