### Observed differences

- A03 and A15 behave as mutually exclusive alternatives: in both windows every trace contains exactly one of the two and none contains both. Their shares flipped between windows — A15 was present in about 52 in 100 traces before (105 of 200) and in about 42 in 100 in the later window (84 of 200), while A03 rose from about 48 in 100 (95 of 200) to about 58 in 100 (116 of 200). [OCC002, OCC013, REL039]
- The routing into these alternatives shifted the same way: of A13's observed outgoing transitions, about 46 in 100 went to A03 before versus about 67 in 100 in the later window (to A15: about 54 down to 33 in 100); from A10, transitions to A03 rose from about 38 to 52 in 100 while those to A15 fell from about 58 to 43 in 100; from A11, A03 rose from about 37 to 54 in 100 and A15 fell from about 63 to 43 in 100. [DIR013, DIR010, DIR011]
- Co-occurrence with A12, which runs in every trace, mirrors the flip: A03 appeared together with A12 in about 48 in 100 traces before and about 58 in 100 after, whereas A15 with A12 fell from about 53 to about 42 in 100; partner patterns agree elsewhere, for example A03 without A05 rose from about 35 to 50 in 100 traces, and A15 without A09 fell from about 50 to 39 in 100. [REL036, REL102, REL029, REL090]
- The pair A02–A10, whose activities always occur together, appeared in about 23 in 100 traces before and about 28 in 100 in the later window (45 → 56 of 200); of A02's observed outgoing transitions, about 87 in 100 led directly to A10 before versus about 95 in 100 after. [OCC001, OCC009, REL022, DIR002, LAG017]
- A05 appeared in fewer traces (about 23 in 100 before, about 18 in 100 after) but repeated somewhat more when present (mean occurrences per trace 0.37 → 0.39; traces with repetitions 7.5 → 8.5 in 100). [OCC004, POS005]
- A06 and A13 (always together) declined from about 25 to about 21 in 100 traces, and A07 from about 28 to 24 in 100. Event positions were broadly stable — A03 and A15 kept a median normalized position of 1.0 in both windows — with the clearest positional shifts in the spacing after A01: A01→A03 pairs four steps apart rose from about 17 to 33 in 100, and A01→A15 pairs three steps apart rose from about 30 to 45 in 100. [OCC005, OCC011, OCC006, POS003, POS015, LAG002, LAG014]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the share of traces taking the A03 branch versus the A15 branch changed: A03 and A15 remain mutually exclusive alternatives, executed exactly once per trace in both windows, but in the later window A03 was executed in about 58 in 100 traces (up from about 48 in 100) while A15 fell from about 52 to about 42 in 100. The shift is observed directly in the relation between the two activities, which keeps its either/or pattern while the shares move by mirror-image amounts of about 10 in 100 traces, and it is consistent across occurrence counts, the successor distributions at A13, A10 and A11, and co-occurrence with A12 and other partners; the core differences exceed roughly 96 in 100 random re-splits of the pooled data. Limitations: the many relation items largely re-express this one underlying difference rather than confirming it independently, the windows are finite samples of 200 traces each, and no screening baseline is provided, so an unusually large sampling swing cannot be excluded with certainty. [OCC002, OCC013, REL039, DIR013, DIR010, DIR011, REL036, REL102]

Other differences: the A02–A10 block became more frequent with a more dominant immediate A02→A10 step, A05 became less common but slightly more repeated, and A06/A13 and A07 declined mildly; the A02/A10 rise coincided with A03-traces (co-occurrence with A03 up from about 9 to 16 in 100 traces, with A15 down from about 13 to 12 in 100), but the evidence does not establish whether it belongs to the same branch shift. An important competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is ordinary between-window sampling variability, which the absence of a screening baseline leaves unquantified at the item-count level. [OCC001, OCC009, DIR002, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, REL015, REL027]
