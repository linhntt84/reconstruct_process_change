### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, every trace contained exactly one of two mutually exclusive activity groups: an A03 group — A03 together with A13, and with A12 and A14 in roughly half of those traces — in about 50 of 100 traces, and an A04 group — A04, A05, A07, A09, A10 and A11 occurring together, usually with A08 and often with A06 — in the other 50. [REL013, REL022, REL025, REL027, REL035]
- In the later window, the A03 group appears in about 39 of 100 traces and the A04 group in about 34, while about 27 in 100 traces contain neither group, which never occurred earlier; the two groups remain mutually exclusive. [REL013, REL032]
- Two activities are new in the later window: A02, present in about 39 of 100 traces (78 of 200, exactly once per trace and always together with A03), and A01, present in about 28 of 100 traces (55 of 200). A14, previously observed in about 26 of 100 traces and repeated within many of them, is no longer observed at all. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC014, REL001, POS012]
- A02 sits between A03 and A13: earlier, A03's outgoing transitions led to A12 in about 51 of 100 cases and directly to A13 in about 49; in the later window they lead to A02 in about 54 and back to A12 in about 46, A02 always leads to A13, and the A03–A13 pair now occurs two positions apart (about 52 of 100 observed pairs) instead of in consecutive positions (about 50). [DIR001, DIR002, REL011, LAG003]
- A14 dropped out of the A03/A12 repetition: earlier, A12 always led to A14 and A14 back to A03, with A03 and A12 repeating at a gap of about three steps; in the later window A12 leads directly back to A03 (a pair previously seen only two steps apart) and both activities repeat at a gap of about two steps. [DIR010, DIR011, LAG001, LAG050, LAG051]
- Participation fell across nearly all branch activities: A04, A05, A07, A09, A10 and A11 from about 50 to about 34 in 100 traces, A08 from about 34 to 24, A06 from about 20 to 16, and A12 from about 26 to 19. [OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, OCC007, OCC008, OCC009, OCC010, OCC011, OCC012]
- Remaining routing and position differences are small — for example, A04's outgoing transition shares are nearly identical in both windows (dissimilarity about 0.0003, exceeded in only about 14 of 100 reference comparisons) — and are consistent with sampling variation between two 200-trace windows. [DIR003, DIR004, POS003]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the control flow around the A03/A12 repetition loop was rewired in the later window: A02 was inserted between A03 and A13 — A03 now hands over to A02, which always leads to A13, whereas earlier A03 led directly to A13 in about half of its outgoing transitions — and A14 was removed from the loop, so that A12 now feeds back to A03 directly and the repetition cycle shortened from about three steps to about two. This rests on directly observed successor changes at A03, A12, A02 and A14, on the order relations of A02 (always before A13 when both occur; co-occurring with A03 in about 39 of 100 traces), and on matching gap shifts (A03→A13 from lag 1 to lag 2, A12→A03 from lag 2 to lag 1, self-gaps of A03 and A12 from 3 to 2), consistent across the occurrence, direction, position, relation and gap indicators, with differences larger than in about 995 of 1,000 random re-splits and solid observation counts (66–105 transitions or 55–78 traces behind the key profiles). Limitations: the windows are finite samples of 200 traces each, gaps are characterised only up to five steps, and A02 is observed only as a single, non-repeating step, so the loop structure beyond that distance is not established. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR010, DIR011, REL001, REL010, REL011, LAG001, LAG003, LAG050, LAG051, OCC002, OCC014, POS001]

Other differences: the two branch groups are taken less often (about 50/50 earlier versus about 39/34 later), and the new activity A01 appears in about 28 of 100 later traces, matching the share of traces containing neither group; however, the evidence holds no relational data for A01, so whether it marks a path bypassing both branches cannot be confirmed. An important competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that this neither-share reflects a path that already existed but was not sampled in the earlier window, since zero participation only means an activity was not observed.
