### Observed differences

- Activity A02 was not observed in the earlier window (0 of 200 traces); in the later window it appears in about 58 in 100 traces, averages about 2.4 occurrences per trace, and sits at roughly 58% of the trace's event positions [OCC001, POS002].
- In the earlier window, A15's outgoing transitions led to A07 in about 52 in 100 cases, A08 in about 25 and A12 in about 23; in the later window essentially all of A15's outgoing transitions (about 474) lead to A02, and A02 in turn leads to A07 (about 52 in 100), A08 (about 27) and A12 (about 21) — nearly the same split A15 used to show [DIR011, DIR002].
- A15's three successors now occur one position further away from it: A15→A08 moved from immediate (lag 1, about half of observed pairs) to lag 2 (about two thirds), A15→A12 from lag 1 (about 54 in 100) to lag 2 (about 71 in 100), and A15→A07 from lag 1 (about 24 in 100) to lag 2 (about 31 in 100) [LAG105, LAG106, LAG110].
- The one-position shift extends to pairs downstream of A15 while upstream lags stay fixed: A11→A06 moved entirely from lag 4 to lag 5, A15→A04 from lag 2 to lag 3, and A15→A06 and A15→A09 from lag 3 to lag 4, whereas early pairs such as A01→A03 and A03→A04 kept lags of 1–2 in both windows [LAG071, LAG102, LAG104, LAG107, LAG001, LAG013].
- A02's relations changed from complete separation to routine co-occurrence: before, pairs with A02 showed only "one activity only" or "neither"; after, A02 co-occurs with A15, A05 and A11 in about 58 in 100 traces (in every trace containing those activities) and with A03 and A04, which appear in all traces, in about 58 in 100 traces [REL015, REL016, REL017, REL023, REL027].

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that A02 was inserted into the process between A15 and its successors A07, A08 and A12: in the earlier window, cases reaching A15 continued directly to one of A07, A08 or A12, while in the later window A15 always leads to A02, which then routes to A07, A08 and A12 with almost unchanged shares [DIR011, DIR002].

The reading is consistent across indicator families: A02 appears in about 58 in 100 traces after being absent, always in a trace that also contains A15 and exactly as often as A15 (about 2.4 occurrences per trace each); A15's successors moved from lag 1 to lag 2 behind it; and every activity pair spanning the insertion point shifted one position later while earlier pairs did not — differences of this size were rarely seen in random re-splits of unchanged behaviour (empirical strength about 0.99) [OCC001, OCC013, REL027, LAG105, LAG106, LAG110, LAG071, LAG102, LAG001].

Limitations: the evidence shows that A02 was not observed before, not that it did not exist, so a relabelling of an existing step cannot be excluded from anonymous codes alone; the position indicators also speak to event order, not clock time [OCC001].

Other differences:
- A12 now occurs later within traces (mean relative position about 0.66 before vs 0.73 after) and A03 slightly earlier (about 0.13 vs 0.08) [POS012, POS003].
- Participation shifted by a few percentage points for several activities (A05, A11 and A15 from about 50 to 58 in 100 traces; A10 and A13 from about 40 to 48; A09 from 51 to 48); these differences are mostly within the range random re-splits produce, so the evidence does not establish that this branch is now taken more often [OCC003, OCC007, OCC008, OCC009, OCC011, OCC013].
