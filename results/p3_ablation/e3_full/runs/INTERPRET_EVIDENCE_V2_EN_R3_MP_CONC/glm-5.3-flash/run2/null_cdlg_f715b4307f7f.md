### Observed differences

- The split at the branch after A03 reversed. A03 runs in every trace in both windows, and its observed outgoing transitions go only to A12 and A06: in the earlier window about 57 in 100 of these transitions led to A12 and about 43 to A06, while in the later window about 44 led to A12 and about 56 to A06 [POS003, DIR003].
- A06 and A12 behave as strict alternatives: in both windows every trace contains exactly one of them, never both and never neither. Their participation mirrored the routing flip — A06 appears in about 57 in 100 later traces versus about 45 earlier, A12 in about 43 versus about 56 — and neither activity repeats within a trace or changes its position (both stay near the end of the trace, mean relative position about 0.94–0.97) [REL066, OCC005, OCC011, POS006, POS012].
- A03's co-occurrence with the two alternatives moved accordingly: traces containing both A03 and A06 rose from about 45 to 57 in 100, those containing both A03 and A12 fell from about 56 to 43 [REL030, REL036].
- The shift ripples through pairs with the remaining activities: traces containing A06 but not the partner rose by roughly 4–15 in 100 (with A10 from 25 to 37, with A14 from about 28 to 42, with A15 from 35 to 49), while traces containing A12 but not the partner fell by similar amounts (with A11 from about 30 to 21, with A14 from 39 to about 29, with A15 from 47 to about 37) [REL064, REL068, REL069, REL096, REL101, REL102].
- Outside this flip, A01 became more frequent (about 67 in 100 traces versus about 62, with its median position moving to the very start of the trace) while A10 and A13 became less frequent (about 37 and 36 in 100 traces versus about 41 and 43); A01 and A10 now co-occur in fewer traces (about 16 in 100 versus 22) [OCC001, POS001, OCC009, OCC012, REL009].
- A07's successor mix shifted toward A03 (about 46 in 100 of its outgoing transitions versus about 36 earlier) and away from A02 and A12; A07 is now more often immediately followed by A03 (about 82 in 100 observed pairs at distance 1 versus 72), while A07→A12 pairs occur at greater distance (about 12 in 100 at distance 1 versus 25) [DIR006, LAG056, LAG062].

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the choice between the two alternatives A06 and A12 — which never occur in the same trace and together appear in every trace — reversed in the later window: A03's outgoing transitions now lead to A06 in about 56 in 100 cases (previously about 43) and to A12 in about 44 in 100 (previously about 57), so A06 appears in about 57 in 100 traces (previously 45) and A12 in about 43 (previously 56). This is a change in how often existing paths are taken, not a move, addition or removal: both activities are common in both windows, both branches remain in use, neither activity repeats, and neither changes position [DIR003, REL066, OCC005, OCC011, POS006, POS012]. The flip is measured directly at A03, is mirrored in the participation of both alternatives, and is consistent across many activity pairs in which A06's presence rises while A12's falls; the core differences exceed those seen in about 97–99 of 100 random re-splits of the pooled data [REL030, REL036, REL064, REL068, REL069, REL096, REL101, REL102].

The evidence file contains no screening counts, so the number of qualifying items cannot be compared with the volume expected from sampling noise alone; the judgement rests on the directness and consistency of the routing and participation measurements. An important competing explanation remains: several activities not involved in the branch (A10, A13, A14) also declined modestly in the later window, so the branch flip could be one facet of a broader shift in trace composition rather than an isolated routing change [OCC009, OCC012, OCC013].
