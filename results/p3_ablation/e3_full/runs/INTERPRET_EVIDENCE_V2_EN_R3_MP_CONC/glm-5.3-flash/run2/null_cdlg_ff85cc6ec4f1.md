### Observed differences

- In the later window, A06 and A08 are executed markedly more often per case. A06 rose from about 0.85 to about 1.18 occurrences per trace (roughly 170 → 235 executions across the 200 traces) while the share of traces containing it stayed nearly flat (about 25% → 26%, 49 → 51 traces) — about 3.5 → 4.6 passes per affected case. A08 rose from about 0.38 to about 0.64 occurrences per trace (75 → 128 executions) with participation up only from 15% to 19% — about 2.5 → 3.4 passes per affected case. These are the largest occurrence shifts of all twelve activities. [OCC006, OCC008, POS006, POS008]
- The rest of the process is comparatively stable: the frequently occurring activities (A01, A03, A07, A10, A12) stayed at about 0.48–0.54 occurrences per trace in both windows, with only small rises for A04 (0.995 → 1.075) and A09 (0.475 → 0.535), the activities the A06/A08 cycle runs between. [OCC001, OCC003, OCC007, OCC010, OCC012, OCC004, OCC009]
- The extra passes run through the A06–A08 cycle: A06's outgoing transitions grew from 170 to 235 and shifted toward A08 (about 44% → 54% of its outgoing transitions, away from A09 at about 53% → 45%), while A08 still sends about 98 in 100 of its transitions back to A06, now from 75 to 128 transitions; A09 continues to return to A04 in about 98–99% of its transitions. [DIR006, DIR008, DIR009, DIR004]
- Relational measures agree: traces containing both A06 and A08 rose from 15% to 19%, with more interleaving between them (15% → 19%), and the self-repetition and cycle gap distributions around the pair redistributed with moderate-to-high strength (A06→A06 about 0.77, A08→A04 about 0.77, A08→A06 about 0.70). [REL047, LAG033, LAG045, LAG042, LAG043, LAG035]
- Weaker, separate signals based on few traces: A02's occurrences sit at earlier event positions (median position 1.0 → 0.33; strength 0.99) and its participation fell from 12% to 9.5%; A11's outgoing transitions redistributed (to A10 from 80% to 33%, to A03 from 10% to 44%); A05's median event position moved earlier (1.0 → 0.33). [POS002, OCC002, DIR011, OCC011, POS005]

### Inference

Support level: Weak hypothesis

The evidence suggests, as a possibility, that the repetition cycle between A06 and A08 — entered from A04 and returning via A09 — is executed more times per case in the later window. A06 and A08 repeat more often within the same traces (mean occurrences per trace up by about 0.33 and 0.27 respectively) while the share of cases containing them is essentially unchanged, so the difference reflects extra passes rather than new adoption; the additional A06 transitions are directed to A08, which continues to return to A06 almost exclusively. Supporting signs: the two largest occurrence increases among all activities concern exactly this pair (empirical strengths about 0.8 and 0.9), A06's outgoing-transition volume grew in step (170 → 235) with its destination mix shifted toward A08 (strength 0.9), and co-occurrence, interleaving and self/cycle lag distributions around the pair moved in the same direction. [OCC006, OCC008, DIR006, DIR008, REL047, LAG033, LAG035, LAG042, LAG043, LAG045]

What is missing: the evidence provides no screening baseline stating how many qualifying items an unchanged process would produce, the per-activity supports are modest (about 30–51 traces for A06 and A08), and several corroborating lag items rest on roughly 20–40 observations. An important competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is a difference in case mix between the two windows — for example, more repetition-intensive cases in the later sample — which could raise repetitions per trace without any change in the process itself.

Other differences: A02 occurs at earlier event positions and slightly less often (participation 12% → 9.5%), A11's outgoing transitions redistributed toward A03 and A01, and A05's median event position moved earlier; each of these rests on roughly 10–50 traces and is not part of the main hypothesis. A position shift alone does not establish that an activity was relocated. [POS002, OCC002, DIR011, OCC011, POS005]
