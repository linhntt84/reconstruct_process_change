### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, A01 ran early in each case (median relative position about 0.17, mean about 0.26 of the event sequence); in the later window, it runs late (median about 0.67, mean about 0.65) and more consistently, with less spread. [POS001]
- The other profiled activities shifted only slightly earlier (mean relative positions: A02 about 0.31 to 0.23, A04 0.23 to 0.18, A05 0.47 to 0.38, A06 0.72 to 0.65, A07 0.52 to 0.42). [POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006]
- The order between A01 and every other activity flipped. Before, A01 usually came first (before A06 in about 91 of 100 traces, before A05 in about 80, before A07 in about 68, and roughly even with A02 and A04); in the later window, the others usually come first — A04 and A07 precede A01 in all traces, A02 in about 81 of 100, A05 in about 63, and A06 in about 49 (up from 9). Both activities of each pair still occur together in every trace in both windows, so this is a reordering, not an appearance or disappearance. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005]
- A01's close successors changed: followings within five steps from A01 to A05 fell from about 161 to 74 of 200 traces, to A06 from about 182 to 103, and to A02 from about 109 to 38, while A01 followed by A03 rose to all 200 traces (from about 143) and became usually immediate (adjacent in about 47 of 100 cases vs about 10 before). Of A01's outgoing transitions, about 47 in 100 now lead to A03 (about 7 before), and transitions to A04 and A07 were no longer observed. [LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, DIR001]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that A01 changed its place in the flow: it previously ran near the start of the case, mostly before A02, A04, A05, A06 and A07, and in the later window it runs near the end, after those five activities, usually directly preceded by A07 (which in turn follows A04) and typically followed directly by A03, so that a common ending now runs A04 → A07 → A01 → A03. The change is shown directly by A01's position shift while the other activities moved only slightly, by the order flip with all five other activities (to a fully fixed order for A04 and A07), and consistently by the redistribution of close successors and predecessors; orderings among the other activities stayed stable, and no activity was added or removed (each profiled activity occurs in every trace in both windows). Every cited difference is larger than in about 99 of 100 random re-splits of the pooled traces. [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006, REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG005, LAG011, LAG016, LAG017, LAG023, LAG028, DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR006]

Limitations: the indicators use event positions rather than clock time, so no conclusion about elapsed time follows; the evidence establishes that the relative order between A01 and the rest flipped, but it cannot distinguish whether A01 was moved to the end or the remaining activities were rearranged to complete before it. Transitions no longer observed (e.g., A01 to A04, A07 to A03) may still exist but were not taken in this sample, and A03 has no position or outgoing profile of its own in the evidence, so its placement rests only on its relations to other activities. [DIR001, DIR003, DIR006, LAG002, LAG030]

Other differences: only small, unsystematic shifts in order shares among pairs not involving A01 (a few percentage points, e.g., A04 before A05 about 73 to 66 in 100), consistent with sampling variation rather than a behavioural change. [REL006, REL010, REL011, REL012, LAG014, LAG020]
