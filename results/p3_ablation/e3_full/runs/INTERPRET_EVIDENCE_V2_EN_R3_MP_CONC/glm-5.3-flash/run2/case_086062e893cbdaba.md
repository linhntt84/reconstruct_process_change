- Activity A01 is absent from the earlier window and new in the later one: 0 of 200 earlier traces contain it, against about 46 in 100 later traces (92 of 200). ### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that a new activity, A01, was added to the process and placed into the existing flow between the activities that previously fed A05 and A10 and those activities themselves. In the earlier window A01 never occurred; in the later window it runs in about 46 in 100 traces, receives transitions from A03, A05, A06 and A11, and passes on to A05 or A10 — exactly the connections those sources previously made directly. The one-step growth in event gaps between the remaining activities (including A05-to-A10 moving from a gap of 1 to gaps of 1–2) fits a step being inserted into existing sequences, and the fact that A03, A05, A06, A09, A10 and A11 now never appear without A01 is consistent with the new step sitting upstream of that block. The change is consistent across occurrence, successor, co-occurrence and gap indicators, and the stability of A02, A04, A07 and A08 argues against a broader restructuring. Limitations: each window is a 200-trace sample, so the roughly 46-in-100 reach of A01 is approximate; some pair-level gap profiles rest on a few dozen traces; and the evidence cannot fully separate an activity inserted into pre-existing paths from a new, separately routed kind of case that happens to connect the same activities, although the new edges on both sides of A01 and the shifted gaps favour insertion. [OCC001, DIR001, DIR003, DIR005, DIR006, DIR007, REL002, REL004, REL005, REL008, REL009, REL010, LAG021, LAG024]

Other differences: the slight participation declines in A03 and A11 (about 32 to 28 in 100 traces) and in A05, A09 and A10 (about 53 to 46 in 100), together with small relative-position shifts for several activities, are minor next to the A01 change and may be sampling variation or knock-on effects of flows that now reach A10 via A01 without passing through A05.
