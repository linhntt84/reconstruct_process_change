### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that, in the later window, A12 is executed in a larger share of cases — about 32 in 100 traces versus about 25 in 100 — and that the routing out of A12 shifts, with A03 becoming the dominant successor (about 43 in 100 of A12's outgoing transitions, up from about 24 in 100) and A06 a new regular successor (about 18 in 100 versus about 2 in 100), while A01, A09 and A10 become rarer successors. The signs are direct (A12's own occurrence and position profiles plus its successor distribution, the strongest single item, exceeding about 98 in 100 random re-splits) and consistent (the same ~7-in-100 participation rise appears with every constant partner). Limitations: the many agreeing items largely re-measure the same traces rather than providing independent confirmations, the successor shares rest on only 49 versus 63 outgoing transitions, and no screening baseline is included, so the number of qualifying items cannot be compared with a chance expectation. An important competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that A12's successor mix changed only because A12 now occurs in a different mix of cases, not because the routing after A12 itself changed. [OCC012, POS012, DIR012, REL023, REL034, REL083, REL086, REL089, REL090]

Other differences: A01 and A06, strict alternatives with exactly one of them in every trace, swapped shares (A01 about 54 → 46 in 100 traces, A06 the reverse, with A06 slightly later); A04 is executed less often (about 27 → 22 in 100 traces) and leads to A03 less often; A10 occurs at slightly earlier positions. The evidence cannot establish whether these shifts are connected to the A12 change or are independent differences. [REL005, OCC001, OCC006, POS006, OCC004, DIR004, POS010]
