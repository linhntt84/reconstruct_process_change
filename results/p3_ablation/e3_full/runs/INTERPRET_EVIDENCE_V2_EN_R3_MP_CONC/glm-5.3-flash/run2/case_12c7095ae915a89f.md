### Observed differences

- In the earlier window the segment after the start ran in a fixed order: A01 led to A02 in about 87 in 100 of its outgoing transitions, A02 led to A04 in about 94 in 100, and A04 led to A03 in about 96 in 100; A02 preceded A04 in all traces, usually immediately (about 94 in 100 at a one-step gap). [DIR001, DIR002, DIR004, REL006, LAG008]
- In the later window the order between A02 and A04 is mixed: A02 still precedes A04 in about 54 in 100 traces, but A04 now precedes A02 in about 46 in 100 (before: 0 in 100). [REL006]
- Routing around this segment shifted accordingly: from A01, the direct share to A04 rose from about 6 in 100 to about 42 in 100 while the share to A02 fell from about 87 to about 50; A02 now leads directly to A03 in about 44 in 100 of its transitions (previously unobserved) and A04 leads to A02 in about 40 in 100 (previously unobserved), while A04→A03 fell from about 96 to about 49 in 100. - Positions moved consistently: A02's mean event position moved later (about 0.17 to 0.27) and A04's earlier (about 0.39 to 0.28), so both now occupy either of the two middle slots; A01, A03, A05 and A06 kept their positions. [POS002, POS004, POS001, POS003, POS005, POS006]
- Lag profiles agree: A01→A04 is now immediate in about half the cases (before: about 94 in 100 two steps later), A02→A03 and A04→A03 each split between immediate and one-activity gaps, and traces containing A02 followed by A04 within five steps fell from 200 to about 108 of 200. - The back edge A04→A01 rose from about 4 to about 10 in 100 of A04's transitions, and A04 precedes A01 in about 14 in 100 traces (up from about 7); the underlying lag profiles rest on only 14 and 27 traces. The A03→A01 lag profile rests on 6 vs 1 observations and cannot be interpreted. [DIR004, REL003, LAG014, LAG011]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the ordering constraint between A02 and A04 was relaxed. Before, every trace ran A01 → A02 → A04 → A03 with A02 always first; in the later window, A02 and A04 execute in either order (A04 first in about 46 in 100 traces, previously none), both lead directly on to A03, and A01 enters the segment through A04 directly in about 42 in 100 cases (previously about 6). Participation is unchanged — every activity still occurs in essentially all traces in both windows and no interleaving or repetition was detected — so this reads as a change in ordering constraints, not as activities being added, removed or repeated. [REL006, DIR001, DIR002, DIR004, POS002, POS004]

The change is shown directly by the relation between A02 and A04 and is consistent across successor shares, positions and lag profiles, with the key indicators exceeding about 995 of 1000 random re-splits of the pooled data; downstream activities (A03, A05, A06) are stable, localising the change to the A01–A02–A04 segment. [REL006, DIR001, DIR002, DIR004, LAG003, LAG007, DIR003]

Limitations: the items come from the same traces and are not independent confirmations, and the evidence cannot fully exclude that part of the two-directional traffic between A02 and A04 reflects added repetition rather than a pure reordering, although both activities still appear in essentially every trace. [DIR002, DIR004, REL006]

Other differences: the back edge A04→A01 became somewhat more frequent (about 4 to about 10 in 100 of A04's outgoing transitions; A04 precedes A01 in about 14 in 100 traces, up from about 7), but this rests on few observations; the apparent A03→A01 change is based on 6 vs 1 observations and is not interpretable. [DIR004, REL003, LAG014, LAG011]
