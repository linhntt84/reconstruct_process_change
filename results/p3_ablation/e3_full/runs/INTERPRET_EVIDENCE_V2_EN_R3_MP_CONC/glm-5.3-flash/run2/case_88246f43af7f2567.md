### Observed differences

- **A02 and A04 now lead directly to A12.** In the earlier window, about 91 in 100 of A02's observed outgoing transitions went to A03 (about 6 in 100 to A12), and about 67 in 100 of A04's went to A03 (about 20 in 100 to A12). In the later window, every observed outgoing transition of A02 (28) and of A04 (35) goes to A12, and A12 follows A02 or A04 within one step in essentially all observed pairs, where it previously followed two to four steps later. [DIR002, DIR004, LAG007, LAG010]

- **A03 and A11 no longer belong to that route.** A03 co-occurred with A02 in about 26 in 100 traces, with A04 in about 24 in 100 and with A12 in about 49 in 100 (A03 before A12 in about 26 in 100); all of these are now zero, as are A11's co-occurrences with A12 (previously about 24 in 100) and A04 (about 12 in 100). A03 now appears in about 30 in 100 traces (previously about 49 in 100), with about 0.67 occurrences per trace instead of about 0.96, and A11 in about 17 in 100 traces (previously about 24 in 100). [REL016, REL024, REL030, REL038, REL049, REL106, OCC003, OCC011]

- **A03 and A11 now run together with the A05/A06/A15/A16 branch.** Previously A03 never co-occurred with A05, A06, A10, A15 or A16 — with A05 the two were strict alternatives, every trace containing exactly one of them; in the later window A03 co-occurs with A05, A06, A10 and A16 in about 30 in 100 traces each (and with A15 in about 21 in 100) and never appears without A05, A06, A10 or A16. A16's outgoing transitions, previously split between A10 (about 52 in 100) and A09 (about 48 in 100), now go to A03 (about 50 in 100) and A09 (about 50 in 100); A03 leads on to A11 (about 55 in 100) or A10 (about 45 in 100), and A11 still returns to A03 in all of its observed outgoing transitions. Downstream distances grew by about one step: A10 now follows A16 about two steps later rather than immediately (immediate succession fell from about 85 in 100 pairs to zero), and it follows A06 mostly three or more steps instead of two. - **A12 is no longer a near-obligatory endpoint.** Earlier it appeared in about 49 in 100 traces and was almost always the last event (only 5 observed outgoing transitions; all position quartiles at the trace end). In the later window it appears in about 45 in 100 traces, its position is much more dispersed (at least about a quarter of occurrences now fall at the very start of a trace, while the median stays at the end), and it has 26 observed outgoing transitions, about 46 in 100 to A02 and 54 in 100 to A04. - **Participation moved toward the branch A03 joined:** A05 rose from about 51 to 56 in 100 traces and A06, A10 and A16 from about 27 to 30 in 100, while A02 fell from about 26 to 20 in 100. [OCC002, OCC005, OCC006, OCC010, OCC016]

- The region around A01, A07, A08, A13 and A14 is essentially unchanged; successor shares, positions and co-occurrences there differ only marginally between the windows. [DIR001, DIR008, DIR009, POS001, REL001, REL115]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the control flow around A03 was rerouted. In the earlier window, A02 or A04 fed into A03, which alternated with A11 and then handed over to A12, which typically ended the trace; in the later window, A02 and A04 connect directly to A12, so A03 and A11 are bypassed on that route, while A03 together with A11 now executes as part of the other branch — entered from A16, returning to A11 or continuing to A10 — and A12 has changed from a near-obligatory final activity to one that often continues to A02 or A04 and sometimes opens the trace. The activities involved are A02, A04, A03, A11 and A12, with A16 and A10 marking the new attachment point of A03. [DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR010, LAG007, LAG010, LAG042, POS011, REL031, REL038]

The signs are direct and consistent: the successor profiles of A02, A04, A03 and A16 are replaced almost completely rather than shifted slightly; the co-occurrence flips run exactly in the two directions the rerouting predicts (zero to about 30 in 100 traces with A05/A06/A10/A16, and about 49 in 100 to zero with A12); the lag, position and participation shifts (A12 directly after A02/A04, A10 two steps after A16, A03/A10/A11 later and A12 more variable in the trace, A03/A11/A02 down and A05/A06/A10/A16 up) agree with the same story; and the key differences exceed about 199 of 200 random re-splits of stable traces. Limitations: the many agreeing items describe one and the same rewiring and are not independent confirmations; the later-window successor profiles rest on limited observed transitions (28 for A02, 35 for A04, 26 for A12); and the evidence shows the rerouting itself, not what produced it. A competing explanation that cannot be fully ruled out is that both routings already existed and only their frequencies shifted drastically, with the old edges (A02→A03, A03→A12) simply unobserved in the later sample — unlikely given the completeness of the flips, but not excluded. [DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR010, LAG007, LAG010, LAG042, OCC003, OCC016, POS003, POS009, POS010, POS011, REL031, REL032, REL036]

Other differences: a few further one-step distance increases (from A06, A15 and A09 to A05 or A10) and a slight rise in A09's participation (about 11 to 13 in 100 traces) fit the same picture but rest on modest support (roughly 8 to 41 observed pairs), so they are not added to the main hypothesis. [LAG011, LAG023, LAG026, LAG033, LAG036, OCC009]
