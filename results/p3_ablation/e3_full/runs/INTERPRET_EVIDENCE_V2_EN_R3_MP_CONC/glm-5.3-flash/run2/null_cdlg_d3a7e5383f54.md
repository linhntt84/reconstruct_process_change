### Observed differences

- The clearest difference is a swap in how often two alternative path fragments are taken. In the earlier window, about 54 in 100 cases contain A04 (and then neither A03 nor A05), while about 46 in 100 contain A03 together with A05; in the later window the shares are reversed, about 46 and about 54. The two fragments never occur in the same case in either window and both remain frequent in both, so only the choice between existing alternatives shifted; A10, which occurs in every case in both windows, follows either fragment (A04 and A05 each precede A10 in all of their co-occurrences). [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, REL018, REL021, REL022, REL030, REL035, REL042]

- A11 appeared in about 22 in 100 cases in the earlier window and about 31 in 100 in the later window, while the pair A09+A12, which always occurs together when present, fell from about 48 to about 41 in 100 cases; matching this, the share of A02's outgoing transitions leading to A09 fell from about 50% to about 47%, and the share leading to A10 rose from about 50% to about 53%. [OCC007, OCC008, OCC009, REL061, DIR002]

- Repetition became less common: A02 occurred about 1.9 times per case in the later window versus about 2.0 earlier (recurring in about 41% versus 48% of cases), and A09/A12 recurred in about 22% versus 30% of cases; accordingly the fixed orders A01 before A02, A08 before A02 and A02 before A10 now hold in about 59% of cases (about 52.5% earlier), with repeated or interleaved patterns down to about 41% from about 48%. [OCC001, OCC007, OCC009, REL001, REL016, REL018]

- Local rerouting around A04: of A04's outgoing transitions, the share to A08 fell from about 36% to about 23%, while the shares to A01 (about 27% to 36%) and to A02 (about 37% to 41%) grew; step distances also redistributed (A05 now typically 3 rather than 4–5 steps after A01, A09 typically 5 rather than 3–4, A06 typically 3 rather than 2). [DIR004, LAG004, LAG005, LAG008]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that two existing alternative paths changed how often they are taken: the fragment consisting of A04 (followed by A10) was chosen in about 54 of 100 cases in the earlier window and about 46 of 100 in the later window, while the fragment in which A03 and A05 run together, again followed by A10, was chosen in about 46 of 100 and then about 54 of 100. The support is direct and consistent: all three activities appear in a substantial share of cases in both windows (46–54%), the fragments remain strictly mutually exclusive in both windows, the participation swap of A04 against A03/A05 is mirrored exactly in the A03–A05 co-occurrence and in the relation profiles involving A08 and A10, and the outgoing-transition profiles of A01 and A04 shift in the same direction. The main limitation is sample size: each window holds 200 cases, individual indicators place these differences around the 67th–88th percentile of random re-splits, and the many agreeing relation items re-express the same cases rather than independent confirmations, so an unusually lucky or unlucky pair of samples cannot be excluded. [OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, REL021, REL022, REL025, REL027, REL033, REL035, REL040, REL042, DIR004]

Other differences: In the later window, A11 ran in about 31 of 100 cases (about 22 earlier) while A09+A12 fell to about 41 (from about 48); A02 and A09/A12 repeat less often and the fixed orders A01→A02, A08→A02 and A02→A10 now cover about 59% of cases (about 52.5% earlier); after A04 the outgoing mix shifted from A08 toward A01 and A02, and several step distances from A01 to mid-trace activities changed. An important competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that these concurrent changes are not independent of the branch-share shift but side effects of a single upstream behavioural difference (for example fewer repetitions per case), nor can it fully exclude chance sampling as the source of the whole pattern. [OCC001, OCC007, OCC008, OCC009, REL001, REL016, REL018, REL061, DIR002, DIR004, LAG004, LAG005, LAG008]
