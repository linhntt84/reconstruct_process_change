### Observed differences

- The sharpest difference concerns two mutually exclusive closing activities. In both windows every trace contains exactly one of A03 or A15 — they never occur in the same trace — and each is typically the final event of its trace (median relative position 1.0). In the earlier window the split was about 48 in 100 traces containing A03 versus about 53 in 100 containing A15; in the later window it became about 58 in 100 for A03 and 42 in 100 for A15, a swing of roughly 10 in 100. Both participation changes rank above about 96 in 100 random re-splits of the pooled traces. [OCC002, OCC013, REL039, POS003, POS015]

- The rerouting is visible at the branch points feeding these alternatives. Of the outgoing transitions from A13, the share to A03 rose from about 46 in 100 to 67 in 100 (share to A15 fell from 54 to 33); from A10, the share to A03 rose from about 38 to 52 in 100 (A15 fell from 58 to 43); from A11, the share to A03 rose from about 37 to 54 in 100 (A15 fell from 63 to 43). At the two rarely observed branch points from A09 and A14 (about 10–15 outgoing transitions per window) the local split instead leaned slightly toward A15. [DIR013, DIR010, DIR011, DIR009, DIR014]

- A second, smaller difference: the block in which A02 is directly followed by A10 — the two always appear together, A02 first — ran in about 23 in 100 traces in the earlier window and about 28 in 100 in the later window, and became almost always directly consecutive (direct succession in about 87 → 95 in 100 of the pair's occurrences). [OCC001, OCC009, REL022, LAG017, DIR002]

- Participation of A06, A07 and A13 each slipped a few points (25 → 22, 28 → 24 and 25 → 22 in 100 traces); these differences are unremarkable against the random re-splits. [OCC005, OCC006, OCC011]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that, in the later window, the two existing alternative closing paths were taken with different frequencies: the variant closing with A03 was taken in about 58 of 100 traces (previously about 48), while the variant closing with A15 fell from about 53 to 42 in 100. The reading is direct: the occurrence and relation indicators measure the two activities' participation and their strict either/or relation, and the routing indicators show the same move out of A10, A11 and A13 — measured within the traces that actually reach those branch points, so a mere shift in the upstream case mix does not by itself explain it. Because both alternatives were common in both windows and neither appeared, disappeared or was bypassed, this is a change in how often existing paths are taken, not a path being added, removed or replaced. [OCC002, OCC013, REL039, DIR010, DIR011, DIR013]

Limitations: the evidence set includes no screening baseline, so the usual comparison of evidence-item counts against chance expectations cannot be run, and each window is a finite sample of 200 traces. The slight opposite moves at the A09 and A14 branch points rest on about 10–15 observations and remain unresolved, and the evidence cannot show whether the shift is spread evenly across upstream routes or concentrated in some of them — an important competing explanation it cannot fully rule out. A hint that A03's own successor pattern changed (from only A12 to mostly A01) rests on 1 versus 5 outgoing transitions and cannot be interpreted. [DIR003, DIR009, DIR014]
