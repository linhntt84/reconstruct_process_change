### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that a new activity, A01, was added and embedded in the recurring cycle between A02 and A12. In the earlier window that cycle ran directly, with A02 and A12 typically following one another immediately and A06 also feeding in; in the later window A01 executes exactly once per case, early on average and always before the A04/A05 stage, receiving handovers from A02, A12 and A06 and returning control mainly to A02 and A12, so that the previously immediate A02↔A12 successions now often span two or more steps. Every indicator family shows the same picture — occurrence (0 of 200 traces to 200 of 200), new early positions, new edges in both directions, and all eleven A01 relations flipping from "A01 never present" to "always or usually co-occurring" — and the key items sit at the top of the reference comparisons, so a rare variant or sampling fluctuation is implausible. Limitations: the evidence cannot pin down a single insertion point, since A01 both receives from and leads to several activities, and it cannot rule out that A01 is a re-coded existing step rather than a genuinely new one, as the identifiers are anonymous codes. [OCC001, POS001, DIR001, DIR002, DIR006, DIR012, REL001, REL003, REL004, REL011, LAG010, LAG073]

Other differences: the frequency of two mutually exclusive branches shifted — the A06 path rose from about 48 to about 59 of 100 cases while the A03/A08/A10 branch fell from about 53 to about 41, with the A07/A11 and A09 declines mirroring the reduced use of that branch rather than those activities becoming optional. An important competing explanation the evidence cannot fully exclude here is sampling variation between the two windows, although the observed share shifts exceed most random re-splits of the pooled traces.
