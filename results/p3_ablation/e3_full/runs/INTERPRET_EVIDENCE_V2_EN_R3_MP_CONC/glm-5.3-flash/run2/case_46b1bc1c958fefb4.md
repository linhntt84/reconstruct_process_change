### Observed differences

- The largest difference concerns A01 and A02. In the earlier window, A02 ran in about 46 of 100 cases while A01 was not observed at all; in the later window this is reversed — A01 runs in about 50 of 100 cases and A02 is not observed. Both differences are larger than what random re-splits of the two windows typically produce. [OCC001, OCC002]
- A01 and A02 act as strict alternatives in both windows: they never occur in the same trace, and in roughly half of all cases neither of them runs (about 54 of 100 before, 50 of 100 after). [REL001]
- Both occupy the same place in the trace: A02 previously appeared at a mean relative position of about 0.14 of the trace, A01 now appears at about 0.19 — both near the start. [POS001, POS002]
- Their outgoing behaviour matches closely. A02 previously led mainly to A03 (about 32% of its outgoing transitions), A06 (~22%), A11 (~14%), A13 (~11%), A04 (~9%) and A10 (~7%); A01 now leads to nearly the same set: A06 (~24%), A03 (~19%), A10 and A11 (~15% each), A13 (~11%), A05 (~8%) and A04 (~7%). [DIR001, DIR002]
- Relations with the rest of the process are mirrored. Before, A02 co-occurred with A03, A04, A11, A12, A14 and A15 in about 44–46 of 100 traces and, whenever present together with A04, A12, A14 or A15, always ran before them; in the later window, A01 co-occurs with the same activities in about 47–50 of 100 traces and again always precedes A04, A12, A14 and A15. [REL002, REL003, REL010, REL011, REL013, REL014, REL015, REL016, REL023, REL024, REL026, REL027]
- Incoming edges were redirected: activities that previously transitioned on to A02 (A03, A05, A06, A09, A10, A11, A13) now transition to A01 with comparable shares. [DIR003, DIR005, DIR006, DIR009, DIR010, DIR011, DIR013]
- Separately, A03 and A10 repeat more often in the later window: A03's mean occurrences rose from about 1.9 to 2.2 per trace (repeated in about 53 vs 45 of 100 traces), and A10 now runs in about 53 of 100 cases (up from 45) with about 1.2 occurrences per trace (up from 0.9). [OCC003, OCC010, LAG001, REL034]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that A01 took over the role previously played by A02 — an optional, early activity executed in roughly half of the cases, always instead of the other one. In the earlier window this position was filled by A02 (about 46 of 100 cases, never together with A01); in the later window it is filled by A01 (about 50 of 100 cases, never together with A02). The change is shown directly and consistently: A01 now sits at A02's early position, leads to essentially the same successor set, co-occurs with the same main-path activities (A03, A04, A11, A12, A14, A15) at nearly the same rates, keeps A02's ordering role of always preceding A04, A12, A14 and A15, and the transitions that previously led to A02 now lead to A01. [OCC001, OCC002, REL001, POS001, POS002, DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, REL002, REL003, REL010, REL011, REL013, REL014, REL016, REL023, REL024, REL026, REL027]

A competing explanation the evidence cannot fully rule out is that this is a relabelling of the same step rather than a behavioural substitution (the activity codes are anonymous), or that A01 was added and A02 dropped as two independent changes that happen to occupy the same position; the cause of the swap is not observable in this evidence. [DIR001, DIR002, REL001]

Other differences: A03 now repeats more often (mean about 1.9 → 2.2 occurrences per trace; repeated in about 53 vs 45 of 100 traces) [OCC003, LAG001]; A10 occurs more often and in more traces (participation about 45 → 53 of 100; mean about 0.9 → 1.2 per trace) and co-occurs more with A03, A11, A12, A14 and A15 [OCC010, REL034, REL091, REL092, REL094, REL095]; A11 tends to appear slightly later in the event sequence (mean relative position about 0.48 → 0.53), which is a positional shift and does not by itself show a change in clock timing [POS011]. These shifts are not part of the A01/A02 substitution hypothesis.
