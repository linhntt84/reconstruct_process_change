### Observed differences

- Activity A01 was not observed at all in the earlier window (0 of 200 traces). In the later window it runs in about 30 in 100 traces (59 of 200), about 0.66 times per trace, typically around 40% of the way through the trace. [OCC001, POS001]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that a step, A01, was added between A12 and its successors A04 and A13, on the segment that A12 executes together with A07. In the earlier window the segment ran A12 directly into A04 or A13 (52/48); in the later window every observed A12 execution is followed by A01, which then routes to A04 or A13 with nearly the same split, while A01 runs only in traces that also run A12 and A07, at a mid-trace position. The successor change is observed directly at both endpoints, and the occurrence, co-occurrence, position and distance items all describe this one underlying shift rather than independent findings. [DIR007, DIR001, OCC001, REL011, POS001, LAG050]

Limitations: the evidence contains no screening baseline, so the number of qualifying items cannot be compared with what an unchanged process would produce; and with two finite samples it cannot be excluded that A01 existed before but was simply not exercised, rather than being genuinely added — although a previously unseen activity with 131 occurrences that captures all of A12's outgoing traffic is hard to explain as sampling noise. [OCC001, DIR007]

Other differences: the repeating part of the process ran somewhat less in the later window — A04 and A13 averaged 1.53 vs 1.84 occurrences per trace, A07 and A12 averaged 0.66 vs 0.88, and A10's trace participation fell from 52 to 46 in 100. The added step does not by itself explain these reductions, and they may partly reflect sampling variation. [OCC004, OCC005, OCC009, OCC010, POS010]
