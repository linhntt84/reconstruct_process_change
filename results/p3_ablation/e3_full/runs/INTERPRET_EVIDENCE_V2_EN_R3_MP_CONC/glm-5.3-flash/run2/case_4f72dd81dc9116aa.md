### Observed differences

- Participation swapped between two pairs of activities. In the earlier window A05 ran in every case (about 4.2 occurrences per case) and A10 in about 47 in 100 cases (about 1.9 occurrences); in the later window A10 runs in every case (about 4.1 occurrences) and A05 in about 50 in 100 cases (about 2.2 occurrences). A08 fell from about 68 in 100 cases to about 34 in 100, while A16 rose from about 30 in 100 to about 68 in 100. [OCC004, OCC006, OCC008, OCC012]
- The same four activities swapped their typical place in the case: A05 and A08 moved from a mean relative position of about two-thirds of the case to about one-third, and A10 and A16 moved from about one-third to about two-thirds. [POS004, POS007, POS009, POS015]
- The flow into the two anchors was rewired. Earlier, all of A06's outgoing transitions led to A05; in the later window all lead to A10. A11 and A15 now send about half of their outgoing transitions to A10 instead of A05, and A14 now sends about 53 in 100 to A05 instead of about 51 in 100 to A10. [DIR004, DIR008, DIR010, DIR011]
- The outgoing branches of the two anchors each exchanged one target: A05 now leads to A17 (about 52 in 100 of its outgoing transitions) and A08, where it previously led to A08 and A09; A10 now leads to A16 and A09, where it previously led to A16 and A17. [DIR003, DIR007]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the segments anchored on A05 and on A10 exchanged their places in the process. In the earlier window A05 was mandatory, sat around two-thirds into the case, was reached from A06, A11 and A15, and led on to A08 and A09; A10 was optional (about 47 in 100 cases), sat around one-third in, was reached from A14, and led to A16 and A17. In the later window these roles are exchanged: A10 is mandatory, sits around two-thirds in, is reached from A06, A11 and A15, and leads to A16 and A09, while A05 is optional (about 50 in 100 cases), sits around one-third in, is reached from A14, and leads to A08 and A17. A08 and A16 stayed attached to their anchors and moved with them, A08 becoming rarer and A16 about twice as frequent. [OCC004, OCC006, OCC008, OCC012, DIR003, DIR004, DIR007, DIR008, DIR010, DIR011, POS004, POS007, POS009, POS015, REL060]

The change is shown directly by successor and predecessor changes rather than inferred from frequencies alone, and it is reflected consistently across occurrence, co-occurrence, position and step-distance items, with dozens of items reaching the maximum empirical strength (a difference larger than in 199 of 200 random re-splits of unchanged data). Limitations: the evidence shows an exchange of roles and positions but cannot distinguish a genuine relocation of these segments from a re-routing in which the earlier A05-path is now executed as the A10-path and vice versa; positions are event positions, not clock time; and no chance-expectation baseline was provided, so the judgement rests on this directness and consistency. [DIR004, DIR007, DIR010, REL060, POS004, LAG003, LAG005]

Other differences: A11 and A12 show marginally later mean positions with only moderate strength, most plausibly sampling fluctuation, and a few lag items rest on very few observations (for example A04–A08 with 1 versus 25 traces, and A13–A04 with 2 versus 3) and should not be read as changes; the remaining moderate-strength lag shifts involve the swapped activities and their neighbours and fit the same rewiring. [POS010, POS011, LAG014, LAG047, LAG021, LAG038]
