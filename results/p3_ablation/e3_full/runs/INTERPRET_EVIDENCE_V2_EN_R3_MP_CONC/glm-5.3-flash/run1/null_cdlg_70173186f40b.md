### Observed differences

- The clearest difference sits between two sets of activities that never occur in the same trace. In the earlier window, about 60 in 100 traces contained A04 together with A14 (these two always occur together, with A13 alongside in about 30 in 100 traces), while about 40 in 100 contained A07 together with A12 (also always together). In the later window the split is reversed: A04/A14 in about 48 in 100 traces, A07/A12 in about 52 in 100. The two sets still never share a trace, so the boundary between them is unchanged — only its usage split moved. [REL048, REL053, REL086, REL088, REL123, OCC003, OCC006, OCC011, OCC013]

- Routing out of A02 — which follows the opening A01→A02 pair in every trace — shifted accordingly: the share of A02's outgoing transitions leading to A04 fell from about 31% to about 21%, while the shares leading to A03 rose from about 43% to about 50% and to A07 from about 20% to about 22%. [DIR002, REL001, POS001]

- The A03/A17 pair, which always occurs together (A03 leads almost exclusively to A17), was executed more often and repeated more: present in about 47 in 100 traces before versus about 54 in 100 in the later window, with mean occurrences per trace up from about 1.5 to about 2.3 and repeats in about a third versus about half of the traces containing them. Its companions A05 (about 29 → 41 in 100 traces), A06 and A10 also became more frequent, and from A17 the share of transitions to A05 rose to about 50%. [OCC002, OCC016, OCC004, OCC005, OCC009, REL045, REL070, DIR009]

- Inside the A07/A12 set, A15 grew the most (about 22 → 38 in 100 traces; it occurs only alongside A07/A12) and took a larger share of A07's outgoing transitions (about 20% → 30%) at the expense of A09 (about 30% → 20%); A09, A11 and the always-together pair A08/A16 also appear in more traces as their set became more common. [OCC014, OCC008, OCC010, OCC007, DIR005, REL089, REL083]

- Smaller shifts: A02 itself repeated slightly more often (mean about 1.9 → 2.2 occurrences per trace, still in every trace); A04 and A13 appeared at later relative positions within traces (mean relative position about 0.68 → 0.74 and about 0.64 → 0.72); and immediate A02→A03 follows became less common (about 19% → 14% of A02→A03 pairs within five steps). [OCC001, POS004, POS012, LAG019]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the two alternative branches after the opening A01→A02 sequence are taken with different frequencies in the later window: the A04/A14 branch (A04 and A14 always together, A13 in about three in ten traces) fell from about 60 in 100 traces to about 48, while the A07/A12 branch rose from about 40 to about 52, so its optional members (A09, A11, A15, A08/A16) also appear in more traces. The support is direct and consistent: the pairs A04–A07, A04–A12, A07–A14 and A12–A14 never co-occur in either window while their exclusive-occurrence shares swap almost exactly; every branch member's trace participation moves with its branch; and A02's outgoing routing shifts in the same direction. All involved activities are observed in tens to about 200 traces in both windows, and the mutual-exclusion structure itself is identical in both windows, so this is a re-weighting of existing alternatives rather than activities or paths appearing or disappearing. [REL048, REL053, REL088, REL123, OCC003, OCC006, OCC011, OCC013, DIR002]

A competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is a difference in case mix between the two windows (for example, case types that favour one branch); no case attributes are available, and no screening baseline is provided against which the number of qualifying items could be compared to chance. [DIR002, REL048]

[OCC002, OCC016, OCC004, OCC005, OCC009, REL032, REL035]
