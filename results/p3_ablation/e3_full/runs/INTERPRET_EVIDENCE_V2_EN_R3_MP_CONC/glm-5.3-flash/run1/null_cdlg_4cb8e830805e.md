### Observed differences

- The activities that change most form a repeating chain: A07 leads almost exclusively to A15 and A16 (about 48%/52% of A07's outgoing transitions in the earlier window, 51%/49% in the later), A16 leads mainly to A11 (about half) and A12 (about a quarter), A12–A14 lead on to A09 or A10, and A10 is sometimes followed again by A07 or A16 within a few events. [DIR004, DIR008, DIR005, DIR006, DIR007, LAG049, LAG051]
- In the later window, every recurring activity of this chain occurs more often per case while the share of cases containing it hardly moves: mean occurrences per case rose for A07 from 3.8 to 4.6, A15 from 1.8 to 2.4, A16 from 2.0 to 2.3, A11 from 0.99 to 1.17, A12 from 0.49 to 0.58 and A10 from 0.42 to 0.53 (gains of roughly 15–30%), while participation stayed at about 18–25 in 100 cases for each (A07: 23 → 24.5; A15: 22.5 → 22; A11 and A12 unchanged). [OCC007, OCC010, OCC011, OCC012, OCC014, OCC015]
- Activities outside this chain that occur at most once per case barely moved: A01, A03 and A08 each slipped only from 50 to 48 in 100 cases (means 0.50 → 0.48). [OCC001, OCC003, OCC008]
- A04, which precedes the chain, repeated slightly more (0.92 → 1.01 occurrences per case, participation 50 → 48 in 100), and among its observed outgoing transitions the share going to A07 rose from about 42% to 50% while the share going to A08 fell from about 50% to 42%. [OCC004, DIR003]
- Corroborating signs: A07 was executed about 756 times in the earlier window versus about 923 in the later one (its outgoing-transition count, roughly +22%), and the distance profiles between consecutive occurrences of A07 and of A16, as well as the distances from A10 back to A07 and to A16, all changed markedly relative to random re-splits. [DIR004, LAG034, LAG099, LAG049, LAG051]
- At the end of the chain, exits tilted from A09 toward A10: from A12, the share of outgoing transitions going to A10 rose from about 39% to 47% (A09 fell from 61% to 53%), with the same direction more weakly from A13 (45% → 49%) and A14 (49% → 53%); A09 itself appeared in fewer cases (16 → 12.5 in 100), a small and unremarkable difference. [DIR005, DIR006, DIR007, OCC009]
- Outside the chain, A05 was contained in fewer cases (about 16 → 9.5 in 100, i.e., 32 → 19 of 200) and A02 in more (22 → 29 in 100, 44 → 58); no relational evidence covers either activity. [OCC002, OCC005]
- Around the case opening, the A01 → A03 order became more dominant (A01 before A03 in about 35% → 40% of all cases, the reverse order down from about 15% to 8.5%), A01's direct moves to A08 nearly vanished (6% → 1% of A01's transitions, while A01 → A03 rose 59% → 64%), A03's moves shifted from A01 (27% → 16%) toward A08 (3% → 11%), A01's relative position moved earlier (mean 0.08 → 0.03) and A03's later (0.17 → 0.22), and distances to A08 shifted in opposite directions (A01 → A08 lengthened, A03 → A08 shortened). [REL001, DIR001, DIR002, POS001, POS002, LAG004, LAG015]

### Inference

Support level: Weak hypothesis

Main hypothesis: in the later window the repeating segment A07 → A15/A16 → A11–A14 → A09/A10, entered from A04, was cycled through more times per case — about the same share of cases ran it, but each of A07, A15, A16, A11, A12 and A10 was executed roughly 15–30% more often per case, A04 repeated slightly more with its transitions tilting toward A07, and the distance profiles between consecutive repetitions and along backward edges changed. Supporting signs are the uniform "more occurrences, stable participation" signature across all recurring activities of the segment, the ~22% growth in A07's execution count, and the changed self-repetition and backward distances. [OCC004, OCC007, OCC010, OCC011, OCC012, OCC014, OCC015, DIR003, DIR004, LAG034, LAG099, LAG049, LAG051]

What is missing: the evidence reports no chance baseline (screening expectation), so the number of qualifying items cannot be checked against what an unchanged process would produce; the individual occurrence differences are of sizes that random re-splits match or exceed in roughly a third to two thirds of cases (empirical strengths about 0.35–0.65), and all items draw on the same cases, so their agreement is weaker than the item count suggests. The aggregates also cannot distinguish a uniform gain of about one extra cycle per affected case from a few cases repeating the segment many more times — this sampling explanation cannot be ruled out. [OCC007, OCC010, OCC011, OCC012, OCC014, OCC015]

Other differences: exits from A12/A13/A14 tilted from A09 toward A10, with A09 in fewer cases, though weakly; A05 was contained in fewer cases and A02 in more, with no relational evidence on either; and the A01/A03 ordering, rerouting and position shifts may form a separate change — relative-position shifts alone do not show that an activity was moved, and longer cases (as under the main hypothesis) would mechanically push early activities' relative positions earlier. [DIR005, DIR006, DIR007, OCC002, OCC005, OCC009, REL001, DIR001, DIR002, POS001, POS002, LAG004, LAG015]
