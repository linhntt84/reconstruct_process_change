### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that, in the later window, part of the process is exercised more often and earlier — A05, A09, A10, A11 and A12, where A09, A11 and A12 gain mainly through more repetitions within the traces that contain them and A05 and A10 also appear in somewhat more traces — while another part is exercised less often and later — A01, A07, A15 and, more weakly, A08, with A01, A07 and A15 both in fewer traces and with fewer repetitions. All these activities remain observed in a substantial share of traces in both windows, so this is a change in how often existing behaviour is exercised and repeated, not in whether activities appear at all. [OCC001, OCC004, OCC006, OCC008, OCC010, OCC011, OCC013, POS005, POS008, POS009, POS012, POS014, REL007, REL080]

The signs are direct and consistent: the occurrence changes rest on 76–144 traces per window per activity, the position shifts match the occurrence direction for every activity in both groups, and the relation items corroborate the same activities; the routing change at A10 is also unusually large for a no-change re-split (empirical strength about 0.97). [DIR009, OCC004, OCC008, POS005, POS009, REL004, REL009]

Limitations: each individual shift is moderate — empirical strengths of roughly 0.6–0.9 mean a fair share of random re-splits of unchanged data produce differences of similar size — and no screening baseline is provided to compare the number of qualifying items with chance, so sampling variation between the two 200-trace windows cannot be fully ruled out; the coherence across activities and indicators, not any single item, carries the inference. The position measure counts event positions, so the later or earlier placement may partly follow mechanically from the changed repetition counts rather than being an independent relocation, and participation and co-occurrence indicators overlap, so agreeing items are not independent confirmations. [OCC001, OCC004, POS001, POS005, REL007]
