### Observed differences

- The activities and the routing structure are the same in both windows, but two pairs of mutually exclusive route blocks swapped how often they are taken. In the earlier window, 45 in 100 traces contained the block A01–A06–A08 (A01 always before A08, A06 alongside) and 55 in 100 the alternative block A03–A04–A11–A12; in the later window this is 53 vs 47. Every trace contains exactly one of the two blocks in both windows — the blocks never co-occur and never both miss, measured on all 200 traces per window. [REL002, REL007, REL022, OCC001, OCC003]
- The second exclusive choice shifted the same way: the block A05–A09 (A05 always before A09) versus A02 moved from about 45.5/54.5 to 52/48 per 100 traces; A05 and A09 always appear together, and A02 never appears with either. [REL014, REL042, OCC002, OCC005]
- Across the four resulting trace families, the combination "A01–A06–A08 with A05–A09" grew from about 20 to 28 in 100 traces, mainly at the expense of "A03–A04–A11–A12 with A02" (about 29 to 22 in 100), while the mixed combinations stayed near 25–26 in 100. [REL004, REL012, REL001, REL023]
- Otherwise the control flow is stable: successor profiles barely move (A03 is followed by A12 in about 97% of its outgoing transitions in both windows, A11 by A04 in 99–100%) and activity positions are essentially unchanged (A12's median position is 0.50 in both windows), so the difference is confined to how often each existing path is taken. [DIR003, DIR011, POS012]
- Activities that can repeat did so somewhat less per trace in the later window: A07 fell from 0.52 to 0.41 mean occurrences with participation flat at about 25 in 100 traces, A10 from 0.66 to 0.52, and A06 from 0.97 to 0.94 although participation rose to 53 in 100. [OCC007, OCC010, OCC006]
- Partly as a result, A06's successor share flipped: A06→A07 fell from 53% to 41% while A06→A08 rose from 44% to 53% of roughly 190 outgoing transitions. [DIR006]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the relative frequencies of existing execution paths shifted, with no structural change: in the later window, traces more often run the variants containing A01, A06, A08 (from 45 to 53 in 100) and the variants containing A05, A09 (from about 45 to 52 in 100), and correspondingly less often the alternative variants containing A03, A04, A11, A12 (from 55 to 47) and A02 (from about 55 to 48). This is shown directly by the exclusive co-occurrence relations measured on all 200 traces per window, it is consistent across every occurrence, relation and direction item, and the unchanged successor profiles and activity positions rule out added, removed, bypassed or reordered activities — only the shares between existing alternatives moved. The limitations are the finite samples (200 traces per window) and moderate per-item strength (the key differences exceed those of only about 80–90 in 100 random re-splits), and no screening baseline is included, so an unlucky sampling imbalance between the two windows cannot be fully excluded, although the lockstep alignment of all items makes pure sampling noise unlikely; the evidence also cannot indicate why the shares moved. [REL002, REL014, OCC001, OCC003, DIR003, POS012]

Other differences: Repetition intensity of the repeating activities decreased somewhat beyond what the share shift explains — A07 (0.52→0.41 mean occurrences at flat participation), A10 (0.66→0.52) and slightly A06, A03 and A12 — which also moved A06's successor share from A07 to A08; a few pairwise gaps redistributed slightly (A05→A11 pairs four events apart fell from 89% to 67% of 27→18 observations; A02→A10 pairs three events apart from 100% to 90%). [OCC007, OCC010, OCC006, DIR006, LAG032, LAG012]
