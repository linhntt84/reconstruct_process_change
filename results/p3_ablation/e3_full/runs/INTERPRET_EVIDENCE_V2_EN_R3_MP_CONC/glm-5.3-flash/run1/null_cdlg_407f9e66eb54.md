### Observed differences

- The mix of cases shifted modestly: in the later window, A04 appears in about 50 in 100 traces (about 57 before), A07 in about 51 (about 44 before), A05 in about 39 (about 34 before) and A06 in about 33 (about 37 before); A03, A09 and A13 each edge down by about 2 in 100 (about 54 to 52). [OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, OCC008, OCC012]
- A04 and A07 are mutually exclusive alternatives — in both windows every trace contains exactly one of them — and their split moved from roughly 57/43 to roughly 50/50 in favour of A07. [REL036, OCC003, OCC006]
- Upstream routing moved the same way: of A02's outgoing transitions, the share leading to A07 rose from about 20% to about 24% while the share to A03 fell from about 50% to about 45%; of A01's outgoing transitions, the share to A04 fell from about 4% to about 2.5%. [DIR001, DIR002]
- Further downstream, of A13's outgoing transitions the share to A05 rose from about 47% to about 54%, while the shares to A06 (about 28% to 23%) and A10 (about 25% to 23%) fell; this is the difference most unusual for random splits, with about 93 in 100 reference comparisons smaller. [DIR008]
- Co-occurrence shifts mirror these participation changes rather than adding independent confirmation: every activity pair containing A04 co-occurs less often (A01–A04 about 57 to 50 in 100), and pairs containing A07 or A05 co-occur more often (A01–A07 about 44 to 51; A05–A13 about 34 to 39). [REL003, REL006, REL014, REL046, REL050]
- A02 still occurs in every trace but repeats slightly less often (about 2.1 to 2.0 occurrences per trace on average). [OCC001]
- The many positional-gap differences are scattered and partly contradictory (the A08–A06 gap shifts towards longer lags while the A11–A06 gap shifts towards shorter ones), and the strongest of them rest on very few paired observations (as few as 3–20 traces); one directional item (A07→A01) rests on a single observed transition. These do not establish a change in ordering or placement. [LAG005, LAG017, LAG043, LAG047, LAG070, DIR004]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that existing paths were taken with different frequencies in the later window: cases containing A04 became less frequent (about 57 to 50 in 100) while cases containing its mutually exclusive alternative A07 became more frequent (about 44 to 51), and further along the process cases containing A05 became more frequent (about 34 to 39) while A06 became less frequent (about 37 to 33). [OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, REL036, DIR008]

Supporting signs: the participation components change directly; the A04/A07 swap is complementary, with the two shares summing to all traces in both windows, so only the split between existing alternatives moved; and the transition-share shifts at A01, A02 and A13 point the same way. All activity pairs involving these four activities shift coherently, both alternatives are well observed in both windows, and their repetition counts do not change materially — a pattern consistent with existing paths being taken with different frequencies rather than paths being added, removed or bypassed. [OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, REL036, DIR001, DIR002, DIR008]

Limitations: the shifts are modest (about 5–7 in 100 cases), and with 200 traces per window the key items sit around the top quarter of random re-splits, so sampling variation is not excluded for any single item; the evidence set also reports no chance-expectation baseline, so the number of qualifying items cannot be checked against the count expected for an unchanged process. The evidence cannot show whether the A04/A07 swap and the A05/A06 shift are one coupled redistribution or two independent frequency changes. The main competing explanation — ordinary between-window sampling variability — is made unlikely by the one-sided coordination of the shifts but cannot be fully ruled out. [OCC003, OCC004, OCC006, DIR008]

Other differences: A03, A09 and A13 are each marginally less frequent (about 54 to 52 in 100) and A02 repeats slightly less often, differences close to what random splits produce; the scattered positional-gap shifts are mutually inconsistent and mostly based on few observations, best read as sampling noise. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC008, OCC012, LAG005, LAG017, LAG047, LAG070]
