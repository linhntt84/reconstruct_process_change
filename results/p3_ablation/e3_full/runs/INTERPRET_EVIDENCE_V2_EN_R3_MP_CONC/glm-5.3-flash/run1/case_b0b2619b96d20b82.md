### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that A01 and A04 exchanged places in the repeating fragment around A06. Before, the fragment ran A04 → A01 → A06, with A06 either returning to A04 or continuing to A05, and A01 was the near-obligatory member (about 52 in 100 traces) while A04 appeared mainly on repeated passes (about 24 in 100). In the later window the fragment runs A01 → A04 → A06 with the return edge into A01, and the roles are reversed: A04 is now the near-obligatory member (about 52 in 100 traces) and A01 appears mainly on repeated passes (about 20 in 100). [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, OCC001, OCC003, REL003]

The change is seen directly in the successor relations, and it is consistent: every lag distribution between the two activities and A02, A05 and A06 shifted by exactly one position in the predicted direction, the occurrence and position profiles of A01 and A04 mirror each other, and A02, A03, A05 and A06 remained stable, so the difference is not a sampling fluctuation in one indicator. [LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG005, LAG011, POS001, POS002, REL002, REL010, OCC002, OCC005]

Limitations: each window is a sample of 200 traces, and A01's later profile rests on about 40 traces, so the fine shape of its rarer behaviour is uncertain; no screening expectation is included in the evidence, so the formal comparison against items expected without change cannot be made. A competing reading the evidence cannot fully exclude is two coordinated single-step relocations (A01 moved one step later, A04 one step earlier, with the loop's entry point changed) rather than one mutual exchange; the observed edges fit both readings, with the plain exchange being the more parsimonious. [OCC001, LAG002]
