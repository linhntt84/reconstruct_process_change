### Observed differences

- The take-up of two strict alternatives swapped: in the earlier window A01 ran in about 54 of 100 traces and A06 in about 47 of 100; in the later window A01 ran in about 47 and A06 in about 54. The two never occur in the same trace in either window (co-occurrence 0 in both), the same direction appears in their co-occurrence with A02, which runs in every trace, and A06 also appears at slightly later event positions on average (mean about 0.15 vs 0.20 of trace length). ### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that existing alternative paths are taken with different frequencies. The strict alternatives A01 and A06 swapped take-up (A01 from about 54 to 47 in 100 cases, A06 from about 47 to 54), and A12 — which runs alongside either side in a minority of cases — took part in more cases (about 25 to 32 in 100) and was more often followed by A03 or A06 and less often by A01, A09 or A10. The swap is directly visible in mirrored occurrence counts and in a relation profile with zero co-occurrence in both windows, and the A12 change appears consistently across occurrence, relation, direction and position indicators, its outgoing redistribution being larger than in about 98 of 100 random re-splits. Limitations: no screening baseline is included, so the number of qualifying items cannot be compared with what an unchanged process would produce; the individual frequency differences are moderate (about 7 in 100 cases on 200-trace windows); and the evidence cannot show whether the A01/A06 swap and the A12 increase are one underlying change or two. Sampling variation between the two windows therefore remains a competing explanation, chiefly for the smaller shifts. [OCC001, OCC006, REL005, OCC012, DIR012, REL023]

Other differences: A04 became slightly less frequent with a smaller outgoing share to A03, A10 appears at slightly earlier event positions, and several activity-pair lag distributions shifted, most of them resting on small observation counts. [OCC004, DIR004, POS010, LAG003, LAG024]
