### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, an occurrence of A01 was followed by A04 in about 50 in 100 of its roughly 380 outgoing transitions, by A03 in about 29 in 100 and by A02 in about 22 in 100. In the later window, A01 was followed by A04 in every one of about 380 outgoing transitions; handovers to A02 or A03 were no longer observed. [DIR001]
- In the earlier window, A04 was followed by A01 in about 50 in 100 of its outgoing transitions, by A02 in about 26 in 100 and by A03 in about 23 in 100. In the later window, A04 was followed by A03 in about 53 in 100 and A02 in about 47 in 100, with no observed return to A01 in roughly 380 transitions. [DIR002]
- A06 was followed by A01 or A04 in roughly equal shares in the earlier window; in the later window it was followed by A01 in all about 180 transitions. [DIR003]
- The order of A01 and A04 was mixed in the earlier window (A01 first in about 25 in 100 traces, A04 first in about 28 in 100, interleaved or repeated in about 47 in 100). In the later window, A01 came before A04 in about 58 in 100 traces and the reverse order was never observed. [REL003]
- A01 moved to the front of the trace (median relative position from about 0.45 to 0.0, mean from 0.40 to 0.29), while A04 moved later (mean from about 0.40 to 0.50, and it no longer appears in the first quarter of traces). [POS001, POS003]
- A03 occurs in every trace in both windows. In the earlier window it directly followed A01 in about 55 in 100 traces and A04 in the rest; in the later window it always follows A04 at a distance of one step. [REL002, REL010, LAG003, LAG011]
- Step distances between activity pairs shifted by about one step across all 18 measured pairs (each difference larger than in about 995 of 1000 random re-splits): for example, A01→A03 is now always two steps apart instead of one or two, and A04→A01 within five steps appears in about 84 of 200 traces at a distance of four steps, down from about 150 of 200 traces at a distance of one step. ### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the flow between A01 and A04 was rerouted from a flexible, two-way sequence into a fixed one. In the earlier window, A01 and A04 could follow each other in either order, each could hand over to the optional block A02→A05→A06 or exit to A03, and the block could be entered from either of them and return to either of them. In the later window, A01 always hands over to A04; A04 no longer returns directly to A01 but either exits via A03 or enters the block A02→A05→A06, which returns to A01; A03 is now reached only from A04. This is shown directly by the successor profiles of A01, A04 and A06 (each based on roughly 180–380 transitions, each difference larger than in about 995 of 1000 random re-splits), by the fixed A01-before-A04 order replacing a previously mixed order, and by position and distance shifts of A01, A04 and almost every activity pair in exactly the direction this rerouting predicts. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, REL003, POS001, POS003, LAG003, LAG009, LAG011]

Limitations: the handovers that are no longer observed (A04→A01, A01→A02, A01→A03, A06→A04) are unobserved rather than proven removed; if they still exist, they are at most very rare in the later window. The position and pair-distance items largely restate the same rerouting rather than independently confirming it. A competing explanation the evidence cannot fully rule out is that the two windows sample different mixes of trace variants of an unchanged process; the size and consistency of the successor changes (roughly 50/50 splits becoming 0/100) make a pure sampling explanation unlikely. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, LAG009]

Other differences: the participation of the block activities A02, A05 and A06 and the amount of A01/A04 repetition show only small shifts (about 47% to 42% participation; recurrence 0.47 to 0.42) that fall within the range produced by random re-splitting, so no change in how often the block runs is established beyond the rerouting itself. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005]
