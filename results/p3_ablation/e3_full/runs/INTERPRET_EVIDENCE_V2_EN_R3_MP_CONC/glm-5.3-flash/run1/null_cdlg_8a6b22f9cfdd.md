### Observed differences

- In the later window, A01 takes part in more traces: about 21 in 100 versus about 16 in 100 before (41 vs 32 of 200 traces). Where it appears it also occurs more often (mean occurrences per trace about 0.66 vs 0.44), with repeated occurrences in about 15 vs 10 in 100 traces; consistently, more outgoing transitions from A01 were observed (132 vs 88). [OCC001, POS001, DIR001]
- In the later window, A03 takes part in fewer traces: about 43 in 100 versus about 51 in 100 before (86 vs 102 of 200 traces). A01 and A03 never occur in the same trace in either window, so they behave as mutually exclusive alternatives. [OCC002, REL002]
- In the later window, several further activities shifted participation: A09 (about 31 vs 25 in 100), A10 (about 32 vs 25), A13 (about 13 vs 9) and A14 (about 21 vs 16) rose, while A11 fell (about 17 vs 20 in 100) and increasingly appeared without its earlier partners — its co-occurrence with A01, A09 and A14 dropped from about 11, 20 and 11 to about 7, 17 and 7 in 100 traces. [OCC006, OCC007, OCC008, OCC010, OCC011, REL010, REL075, REL085]
- A02 and A07, present in every trace in both windows, sit at later event positions in the later window: A02's median position moved from about one third to about three fifths of the trace, A07's from about two thirds to four fifths; a position shift alone does not show that an activity was moved. [POS002, POS005]
- Some routing shares among observed transitions changed: A08's outgoing transitions went from an even split between A11 and A01 (about 49% each) to about two thirds towards A01; A06's transitions concentrated further on A12 (about 96% vs 88%); A14→A07 pairs three events apart rose from about two thirds to about nine tenths of observed pairs, while immediate A11→A09 successions fell from about three quarters to about half. [DIR003, DIR004, LAG048, LAG063]

### Inference

Support level: Weak hypothesis

The evidence suggests a redistribution of execution frequencies between existing alternatives, centred on A01 and A03: in the later window A01 is executed in a larger share of traces (about 21 vs 16 in 100) and repeats more often within a trace, while A03 is executed in a smaller share (about 43 vs 51 in 100). The two never occur together, both are well observed in both windows, and only the shares between them (and traces containing neither, about 33 → 37 in 100) shift, which fits existing alternative paths being taken with different frequencies rather than one path replacing another. The same redistribution appears consistently across indicator families — every relation involving A01 shows its presence up by about 4–5 in 100 traces, every relation involving A03 shows a fall of about 8, and A14 and A13, which follow A01, rose in parallel — together with A09 and A10 up and A11 down, increasingly without A01, A09 or A14. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC006, OCC007, OCC008, OCC010, OCC011, REL002, REL010, REL075, REL085, DIR001]

The hypothesis remains weak because the evidence provides no screening baseline, so the number of qualifying items cannot be compared with the number expected if nothing had changed; the individual differences are modest (about 4–8 in 100 traces), and many agreeing items re-express the same marginal change (all A01 relations reflect one participation increase), so they are not independent confirmations. Some qualifying lag items also rest on fewer than about 15 traces per window and carry little weight. The competing explanation that cannot be ruled out is sampling variation between two 200-trace samples across the many tested activities and pairs. [OCC001, REL001, LAG017, LAG049]

Other differences: the later event positions of A02 and A07, the shift of A08's outgoing transitions towards A01 (about half to two thirds), the stronger concentration of A06 on A12, and several redistributed event gaps (for example A14→A07 and A11→A09) are present but are not part of the main hypothesis; the evidence cannot show whether they are independent changes or reflections of the participation shifts above. [POS002, POS005, DIR003, DIR004, LAG048, LAG063]
