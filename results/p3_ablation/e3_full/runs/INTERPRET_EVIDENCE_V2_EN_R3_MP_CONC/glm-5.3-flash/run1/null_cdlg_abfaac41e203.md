### Inference

Support level: Weak hypothesis

The evidence suggests, but does not establish, a modest repositioning of activities within cases on an otherwise unchanged structure: in the later window A02 and A04 tend to be executed somewhat later in each case and A03, A05 and A06 somewhat earlier, so several precedence shares move by about 3–7 in 100 cases — most visibly, A03 now precedes A02 in about 55 in 100 cases (49 before) and A04 precedes A05 in about 83 in 100 (90 before) — while the activities involved, their participation and the direct-succession profiles stay the same. The supporting signs are that position, order and distance indicators move in the same direction for essentially every activity pair involved, and the strongest items (A05's position, the A04–A05 and A02–A05 order shares, the A01→A04 spacing) show differences larger than in about 90–97 of 100 random re-splits. The hypothesis remains weak because every shift is small (2–7 in 100 cases; position changes of 0.02–0.04 of the case length), the successor profiles offer no structural corroboration, items describing the same shift are not independent confirmations, and with 49 activity- and pair-level tests a coherent pattern of small differences can also arise if the later window simply contains more traces of an existing variant mix — a competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out. Other differences: successor shares move only slightly and are not part of the positional hypothesis — A02's transitions to A01 fall from about 16 to 10 in 100, A03's and A04's transitions to A01 each rise by about 6 in 100, and A05's transitions to A02 rise from about 39 to 49 in 100, though the latter rests on only 23 and 39 traces.
