### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, A26, A17, A15, A18, A19, A21, A20 and A27 each occurred in every trace and always in the same sequence, A26 → A17 → A15 → A18 → A19 → A21 → A20 → A27: each of these activities had exactly one observed successor, and the gap between pairs was constant (for example A15 was always 4 steps before A20, A18 always 2 steps before A21). [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006, DIR007, LAG003, LAG012]
- In the later window the same activities still occur in every trace, but pairs that were strictly ordered before now appear in both orders: A19 precedes A18 in about 85 in 100 traces and A19 precedes A15 in about 74 in 100 (both were 0 in 100 earlier), A21 precedes A18 in about 68 in 100, while A15/A21 and A18/A20 now split roughly 50/50. [REL006, REL001, REL008, REL003, REL007]
- Successor relations changed accordingly: A18, which previously always led to A19, is now followed directly by A27 in about 52 in 100 of its outgoing transitions; A26 now leads to A19 in about 47 in 100 (previously always to A17); A19 now leads to A17 in about 24 in 100. [DIR003, LAG013, DIR007, DIR004]
- Some original adjacencies survive but are no longer exclusive: A17 still leads to A15 in about 56 in 100 of its outgoing transitions, A15 to A18 in about 68 in 100 and A21 to A20 in about 67 in 100, with the remaining transitions spread over several other activities. [DIR002, DIR001, DIR006]
- Step gaps became variable: the A15-to-A20 distance, previously always 4 steps, now ranges over 1–4 steps, and A15 and A19 are within 5 steps of each other in only about 52 of 200 later-window traces (200 of 200 earlier), although both still occur in every trace. [LAG003, LAG002]
- The two groups drifted toward each other in trace position: A15, A17 and A18 now occur later (A18's mean relative position rose from about 0.62 to 0.68), while A19, A20 and A21 occur earlier (A19 fell from about 0.65 to 0.59); the groups' mean positions now nearly coincide (A17 0.59 vs A19 0.59; A15 0.64 vs A21 0.64; A18 0.68 vs A20 0.68). [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the fixed ordering between the two activity groups was replaced by variable ordering: in the earlier window A26, A17, A15, A18, A19, A21, A20 and A27 always ran as one fixed chain, while in the later window A19 and A21 often precede A15 and A18, A18 is often immediately followed by A27, and A26 is often immediately followed by A19 — with no change in participation, since each of these activities still occurs in essentially every trace in both windows. [REL001, REL006, REL008, DIR003, DIR007, LAG013]

The hypothesis rests on direct relational evidence — strict one-way order becoming mixed orders — corroborated by successor changes, dispersed step distances and the convergence of the groups' positions. Its scale cannot be explained by sampling noise: 45 control-flow items qualified against fewer than 1 expected for an unchanged process across 95 tested units (roughly 100 times the chance expectation), every item's difference exceeds those seen in about 199 of 200 random re-splits, and the core relations are backed by all 200 traces in each window. [REL001–REL008, DIR001–DIR007, POS001–POS006, LAG001–LAG024]

Limitations: the evidence shows which adjacencies persist and that orders are now mixed, but not the exact new control flow — whether the two groups became fully interchangeable, run interleaved, or follow a small set of new fixed sequences. A competing explanation it cannot rule out is that the mixed orders stem from a subset of cases taking newly added re-ordered paths rather than from a uniform relaxation of the ordering. All positional evidence uses event positions rather than clock time, so no statement about timing is possible, and successor shares refer to outgoing transitions rather than to cases. [REL003, REL007, DIR003]
