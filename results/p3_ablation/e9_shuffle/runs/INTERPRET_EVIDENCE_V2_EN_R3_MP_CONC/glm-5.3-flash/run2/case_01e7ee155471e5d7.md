### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the group of three activities A30, A31 and A36 — still executed together in every trace, in varying internal order — changed its placement: in the earlier window it ran directly after A21 and led to A25, while in the later window it runs directly after A20 and leads to A22, so the surrounding sequence changed from A20 → A22 → … → A21 → group → A25 to A20 → group → A22 → … → A21 → A25. This is shown directly by the six reversed pair relations between A21/A22 and the group, by the successor changes at A20, A21, A30, A31 and A36, and by the step distances A20→A22 (1→4) and A21→A25 (4→1), which match a three-activity group changing sides; the positional shifts agree, and every item's difference exceeds about 199 of 200 random re-splits of the pooled data. With 24 flagged items against roughly 0.41 expected by chance among the 83 tested units, the set as a whole is far beyond what an unchanged process produces [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, LAG001, LAG002, POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005].

The evidence cannot distinguish whether the group moved earlier or A21 and A22 moved later past it, since both describe the same reversals, and it does not show what runs between A22 and A21, so that part of the new layout is inferred from positions only; the six one-trace lag profiles add little weight on their own [LAG003, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006, LAG007, LAG008, POS001, POS002].
