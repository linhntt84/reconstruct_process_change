### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that A01 took over the role previously played by A03: A03 is no longer executed, and in the later window A01 occupies the same point in the flow — entered from A09 and A11, followed mainly by A11, A12, A10 and A09 — at a similar relative position and in a similar share of traces (about 45–50 in 100). The appearance and disappearance are observed directly, the incoming and outgoing transitions were rewired from A03 to A01 with nearly matching shares, and A01's co-occurrence and ordering pattern with A05, A09, A10, A11 and A12 reproduces what A03 had. [OCC001, OCC002, POS001, POS002, DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, REL007, REL008, REL009, REL010, REL011]

Nineteen items qualified against roughly 0.33 expected by chance among the 67 tested units, so the set as a whole is not what an unchanged process typically produces; the items are, however, largely overlapping views of the same traces rather than independent confirmations. [OCC001, DIR003, REL010]

An important competing explanation cannot be ruled out: because activity codes are anonymous, the evidence cannot distinguish a genuine replacement of A03 by a new activity from a relabelling of the same step. Also, A03's zero participation strictly means only that it was not observed in the later window, and all items come from the control-flow family, so timing and resource aspects are outside this evidence. [OCC002, POS002]

Other differences: A05's participation rose from about 64 to about 75 in 100 traces and it now co-occurs with A01 in about 44 in 100, and a few new weak edges (about 1–2 in 100 of outgoing transitions) from A09 and A01 towards A13, A15 and A02 appeared; these are consistent with the substitution but could partly reflect sampling variation. [REL007, REL002, DIR003, DIR001]
