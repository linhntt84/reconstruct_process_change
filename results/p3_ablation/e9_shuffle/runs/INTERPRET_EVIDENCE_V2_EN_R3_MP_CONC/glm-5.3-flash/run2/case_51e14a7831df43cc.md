### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports one specific structural possibility: A09 and A16 exchanged their places in the process flow. In the earlier window, A09 was a mandatory, roughly twice-occurring, late activity feeding A05, A14 and A06, while A16 was an optional, single-occurrence, mid-trace activity feeding A13, A10, A04 and A01; in the later window these roles are reversed, with A16 mandatory, repeated and late, feeding A05, A06 and A14, and A09 optional, single-occurrence and mid-trace, feeding A13, A10, A04 and A01. [OCC001, OCC002, POS001, POS002, DIR003, DIR006, DIR002, DIR004, DIR001, DIR005]

The support is direct and consistent: every one of the 39 qualifying items involves A09 or A16 and shows the same mirrored exchange across occurrence, position, successor, distance and co-occurrence indicators, so the items largely reflect one underlying difference rather than separate changes. The item count is about 100 times the roughly 0.39 items expected for an unchanged process, so the set as a whole is not what random re-splits produce, and each item's difference also exceeds about 199 of 200 reference re-splits. [REL012, REL003, REL004, REL009, REL013, LAG006]

Limitations: the traces show the exchange of the two activities' roles but cannot distinguish a genuine swap from a re-labelling of these two activity labels, which would leave the traces identical; POS and LAG use event positions rather than clock time, so no statement about timing is possible; and a few lag items rest on fewer than about 40 observations per window, although the same mirrored pattern appears in pairs with large support. [LAG007, LAG014, LAG010]

Other differences: none observed — all qualifying items concern A09 or A16 and fit the single exchange. [REL005, REL011, REL015, LAG004, LAG011]
