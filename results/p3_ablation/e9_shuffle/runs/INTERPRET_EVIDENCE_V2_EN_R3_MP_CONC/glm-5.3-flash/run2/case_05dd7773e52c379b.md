### Observed differences

- Participation rose sharply for six activities: A28, A30, A31, A32, A33 and A34 each appeared in roughly half of the cases in the earlier window (about 48–52 in 100) and in nearly all cases in the later window (about 98–100 in 100). A25 and A26 were present in every case in both windows. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, POS001, POS002]
- In the earlier window these six formed two mutually exclusive triples, {A28, A30, A31} and {A32, A33, A34}: within a triple the activities always ran together (about 48–52 in 100 cases) but in no fixed order, while across triples they never ran together — a case contained one triple or the other (e.g., A28 and A32 co-occurred in 0 of 100 relevant cases). In the later window all six activities run together in about 98 in 100 cases. [REL013, REL014, REL015, REL018, REL025, REL026]
- Ordering in the later window: every first-triple activity precedes every second-triple activity in about 98 in 100 of their joint cases (e.g., A30 before A34 in 98 in 100), whereas within each triple both orders still occur in similar proportions (A28 before A31 in about 44 in 100, A31 before A28 in about 55 in 100). A25 precedes and A26 follows all six in about 98–100 in 100 cases. [REL021, REL014, REL002, REL004, REL010, REL007]
- Positional distances grew accordingly: pairs linking A25 or A26 to the opposite triple moved from gaps of 1–3 positions to gaps of 4–5 positions (e.g., A25 to A32: about 30/36/34 across lags 1/2/3 before, about 50/48 across lags 4/5 after). [LAG001, LAG002, LAG004, LAG006]
- Relative positions shifted as expected for a longer case: A25 earlier (mean relative position about 0.67 to 0.62) and A26 later (about 0.80 to 0.83); first-triple activities shifted slightly earlier (A28 about 0.74 to 0.68) and second-triple activities slightly later (A32 about 0.73 to 0.77). [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS006]
- Direct successions changed: A25's outgoing transitions moved from spread over both triples to almost only the first triple (A30 about 0.38, A28 about 0.31, A31 about 0.30, with A32 and A34 nearly gone), and first-triple activities lost their direct transition to A26 (from A28, the share to A26 fell from about 0.36 to about 0.005) while gaining transitions into the second triple. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004]
- Scale of the signal: 51 control-flow items qualified out of 118 tested units, against roughly 0.59 expected by chance alone if nothing had changed. [screening]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the two three-activity blocks that previously alternated became sequential stages: in the earlier window a case executed, between A25 and A26, exactly one of the blocks {A28, A30, A31} or {A32, A33, A34}, with no fixed order inside a block; in the later window nearly every case executes both blocks in sequence — first A28/A30/A31, then A32/A33/A34 — so the second block's activities became effectively mandatory and each case gained about three activity executions. This is shown directly: cross-block pairs such as A28–A32 went from never co-occurring to co-occurring in about 98 in 100 cases in a fixed order, A25 now leads almost exclusively into the first block, first-block activities no longer hand over directly to A26 but to the second block, and positional gaps between the blocks and the endpoints grew by roughly three steps, matching an inserted block. [REL015, REL021, REL014, DIR001, DIR002, LAG001, LAG006]

Limitations: the evidence cannot rule out that the later window mixes case variants — for example a both-blocks variant covering about 98 in 100 cases while single-block cases persist in the remainder — nor can it show why the sequence changed; DIR shares are computed over outgoing transitions rather than cases, so the routing picture is indicative rather than exact. The many qualifying items largely describe this one underlying difference and are not independent confirmations of it. [screening, OCC004, OCC005]
