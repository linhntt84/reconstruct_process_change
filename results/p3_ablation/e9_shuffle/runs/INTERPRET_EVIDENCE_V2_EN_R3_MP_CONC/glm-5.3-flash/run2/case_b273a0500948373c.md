### Observed differences

- The activities split into two mutually exclusive groups whose usage reversed between the windows. In the earlier window, A19, A20 and A21 each ran in about 82 in 100 traces (163 of 200), while A16, A17 and A18 each ran in about 19 in 100 (37 of 200); in the later window, A16–A18 each ran in about 79 in 100 (158 of 200) and A19–A21 in about 21 in 100 (42 of 200). [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006]
- The two groups are alternatives, not sequential stages: cross-group pairs such as A16 with A19 or A18 with A20 never occurred in the same trace in either window. [REL009, REL011, REL019]
- A15 and A25 appeared in every trace in both windows, and A15 always preceded the group activities; what changed is which group executes. [REL001, REL012, REL005]
- A15's outgoing transitions redistributed accordingly: towards A16, A17 and A18 from about 19 to about 79 of 100 observed outgoing transitions, and towards A19, A20 and A21 from about 81 to about 21. [DIR001]
- Within each group, activities co-occur in both orders in similar proportions (in the later window, for example, A16 before A17 in about 42 in 100 traces versus A17 before A16 in about 38 in 100), and this unordered co-execution pattern was the same in both windows. [REL007, REL008, REL013, REL022]
- A25's co-occurrence partners switched with the branch: earlier it ran alongside the A19–A21 group (with A20 in about 82 in 100 traces), later alongside the A16–A18 group (with A16 in about 79 in 100). [REL026, REL012, REL024]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a change in how often two existing alternative paths after A15 are taken: the path on which A16, A17 and A18 execute together rose from about 19 in 100 cases to about 79 in 100, while the alternative path carrying A19, A20 and A21 fell from about 82 in 100 to about 21 in 100; A25, present throughout, now accompanies the A16–A18 path instead of the A19–A21 path. The shift is shown directly by A15's outgoing distribution and by the participation of all six branch activities; both branches remain observed in substantial shares in both windows (the rarer one still in 37–42 of 200 traces), no activity was added, removed or newly repeated (recurrence is zero in both windows), and each difference exceeds about 199 of 200 random re-splits of the pooled data. Screening qualified 34 control-flow items where an unchanged process would produce about 0.5, so chance sampling cannot account for the pattern as a whole. [OCC001, OCC004, OCC006, DIR001, REL009, REL012, REL026, screening]

Limitations: the many relation items largely re-express one underlying branch shift rather than serving as independent confirmations, and the evidence cannot rule out that the two windows simply contain different mixes of cases (for example a shifted variant population) rather than a changed routing decision. [OCC001, DIR001, REL019]

Other differences: none beyond this branch shift; the unordered co-execution within each group and the constant presence of A15 and A25 were the same in both windows. [REL001, REL007, REL022, REL012]
