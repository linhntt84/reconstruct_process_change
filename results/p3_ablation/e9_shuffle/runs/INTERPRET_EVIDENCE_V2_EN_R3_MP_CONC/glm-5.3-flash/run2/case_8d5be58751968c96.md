### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, every one of the 200 traces contained all of A30, A33, A35, A36, A37, A38, A39 and A31 (100% participation for each) [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, POS001, POS002].
- The earlier ordering was fixed between blocks but not within them: A30 preceded each middle activity in about 98–100 in 100 traces, the trio A33/A35/A36 always ran before the trio A37/A38/A39 (about 97–100 in 100), each middle activity preceded A31 in about 97–100 in 100, and within each trio either order occurred in roughly half the traces [REL001, REL007, REL013, REL015, REL025, REL026].
- In the later window, the six middle activities each appear in only about 47–57 in 100 traces (A33, A35 and A36 about 47; A37, A38 and A39 about 56–57), while A30 and A31 still appear in every trace [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, POS001, POS002].
- In the later window the two trios became mutually exclusive: pairs spanning the trios co-occurred in essentially every earlier trace but in only about 2–3 in 100 later traces; about 44 in 100 later traces contain only the first-trio activity and about 53 in 100 only the second-trio activity [REL015, REL017, REL020, REL022, REL024]. Within each trio the activities still co-occur whenever they appear (about 47 in 100 traces for the first trio, about 56 for the second), still without a fixed internal order [REL013, REL018, REL025, REL027].
- Routing changed accordingly: earlier, A30's observed outgoing transitions led almost exclusively to A33/A35/A36, while in the later window about 53 in 100 of them lead directly to A37/A38/A39; A33, A35 and A36, which earlier showed no direct transition to A31, now lead to it in about 24–34 in 100 of their observed outgoing transitions [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004].
- Step distances shrank accordingly: the positional gap from A30 to each second-trio activity and from each first-trio activity to A31 spanned 4–5 steps earlier and 1–3 steps in the later window [LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG007, LAG010].

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the previously mandatory sequential middle segment became two mutually exclusive alternatives: in the earlier window every case ran A30 → (A33/A35/A36, unordered) → (A37/A38/A39, unordered) → A31, whereas in the later window a case executes only one of the two trios — roughly 46–47 in 100 cases run A30 → A33/A35/A36 → A31 and roughly 55–57 in 100 run A30 → A37/A38/A39 → A31 — so one trio is bypassed in each case [REL020, REL024, DIR001, DIR003, LAG001, LAG004, OCC001, OCC004]. The hypothesis is shown directly and consistently across indicator families: participation of all six activities halved, spanning pairs collapsed from near-universal co-occurrence to about 2–3 in 100, new transitions bypass the skipped trio, and the corresponding step distances fell from 4–5 to 1–3; the 57 flagged control-flow items are roughly seventy times the number expected without a change (about 0.8), so chance sampling cannot explain the pattern as a whole [DIR001, LAG001, OCC001, REL020]. An important competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that these exclusive variants existed before at very low frequency and only became frequent — none appears among the 200 earlier traces, but a finite sample can miss a rare variant [REL015, REL020].

Other differences: A30's mean relative position moved slightly later (about 0.62 to 0.67) and A31's slightly earlier (about 0.82 to 0.80), and the six middle activities shifted by about 0.02–0.07 in relative position; these follow from traces becoming shorter once one trio is skipped and do not indicate separate relocations [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006, POS007, POS008]. The changed gap profiles for pairs spanning the two trios (for example A33–A37 and A35–A38) rest on only 4–5 later-window observations and are weakly supported [LAG005, LAG006, LAG008, LAG009, LAG011, LAG012].
