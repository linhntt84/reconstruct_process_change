### Observed differences

- Four activities became far more frequent: in the earlier window A22 ran in 54 of 100 traces and A31, A32 and A37 in about 46 of 100 each; in the later window A22 runs in every trace and A31, A32 and A37 in about 995 of 1000. A20, A21, A23 and A26 appear in every trace in both windows. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, POS001, POS002, POS004, POS005]
- A22 and the group A31/A32/A37 were strict alternatives before — they never appeared in the same trace (A22 alone in 54 of 100 traces, the group alone in 46 of 100) — whereas in the later window they run together in about 995 of 1000 traces, always with the group before A22. [REL011, REL012, REL013]
- The group's order relative to A23 and A21 flipped: before, A23 and A21 each preceded A31/A32/A37 in every case where the group ran; after, the group precedes both in about 995 of 1000 traces. A21, earlier leading to A22 in 54 of 100 cases or into the group otherwise, now always leads directly to A22. - One behaviour is unchanged: A31, A32 and A37 still appear in both orders in similar shares, so their internal order remains unfixed in both windows. [REL020, REL021, REL022]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the group A31/A32/A37, previously an optional branch run in about 46 of 100 cases instead of A22 and placed between A21 and A26, became part of every case and now runs directly after A20 and before A23, while A22 also became mandatory — the earlier either-or choice between A22 and the group disappeared. The observed order changes from A20 → A23 → A21 → (A22 or A31/A32/A37) → A26 in the earlier window to A20 → A31/A32/A37 → A23 → A21 → A22 → A26 in the later window. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, REL011, REL012, REL013, REL014, REL015, REL016, REL006, REL007, REL008, DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, LAG001, LAG002]

Chance is an unlikely explanation: 41 items qualified against roughly 0.49 expected for the 99 tested units if nothing had changed, and each item's difference exceeds about 995 in 1000 random re-splits of the pooled data. The placement change is shown directly by successor changes (A20 now leads into the group, the group now leads to A23), by the order flips around A23 and A21, and by the A20→A23 distance growing from one to four steps, consistently across occurrence, relation, direction, lag and position indicators, with near-complete support in both windows (about 200 in 200 traces, and 92 versus 199 traces for the group's earlier versus later participation). [REL014, REL015, REL016, DIR001, DIR003, LAG001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004]

Limitations: the evidence covers only relations and positions among the tested activities, so unobserved activities sitting between them cannot be excluded, and positions are event indices rather than clock times; the evidence also cannot distinguish a relocation of the group within one process from a later window dominated by a variant in which the group always runs up front. [REL014, REL015, REL016, POS004, POS006, POS007, POS008, LAG001]

Other differences: none beyond the main change — the small position shifts of A20, A21, A22 and A26 (about 0.02–0.06 of trace position) follow from the group's new placement, and the group's internal order remains unfixed in both windows. [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS005, REL020, REL021, REL022]
