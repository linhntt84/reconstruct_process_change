### Observed differences

- The clearest difference is that two groups of activities exchanged the half of the case in which they run, while participation stayed essentially unchanged. In the earlier window, A20, A22 and A23 each occurred in all 200 cases, about once per case, in the later half of the case (mean relative position about 0.63); in the later window they again occur in all cases but in the earlier half (about 0.38) [POS002, POS004, POS005, DIR002, DIR004, DIR005].
- Conversely, A19, A21 and A24 moved from the earlier half (mean relative position about 0.34–0.36; each in 60–73 of 200 cases) to the later half (about 0.66–0.68; each in 59–79 of 200 cases) [POS001, POS003, POS006].
- Successors flipped accordingly. Earlier, A24, A19 and A21 led almost exclusively to A25 (about 97–98 in 100 of their outgoing transitions); in the later window they lead to A30 (about 95–98 in 100) [DIR006, DIR001, DIR003]. A29 earlier led to A20, A23 or A22 (about 37, 33 and 30 in 100) and later to A24, A19 or A21 (about 38, 32 and 29 in 100) [DIR007]. A40 earlier led to A21, A24 or A19 (about 33, 34 and 28 in 100) and later to A22, A20 or A23 (about 34, 31 and 28 in 100) [DIR008]. A20, A22 and A23 earlier ended in A30 in about 29–37 in 100 of their outgoing transitions; later they lead to A25 in about 27–37 in 100 [DIR002, DIR004, DIR005].
- Step distances match this exchange. A40 reached A25 after 2 steps in about 95 in 100 cases earlier, but after 4 steps in about 92 in 100 later; A29 reached A30 after 4 steps (about 93 in 100) earlier and after 2 steps (about 99 in 100) later [LAG009, LAG005]. The immediate steps A24→A25 and A21→A25 (71 and 69 cases) nearly disappear (2 and 1 cases), A29→A21 becomes immediate in 58 of 59 cases, and A40 now reaches each of A20, A22 and A23 within 1–3 steps in about 200 cases, where earlier only 1 case showed such a link [LAG003, LAG001, LAG004, LAG006, LAG007, LAG008, LAG002].
- Pairwise orders reversed across about twenty activity pairs. A25 ran before A20, A22 and A23 in about 99 of 100 co-occurring cases earlier and after them in about 99 of 100 later; the same reversal holds between A29 and those three; A21, A24 and A19 each ran before the block earlier (for example A21 before A20 in 34 of 35 co-occurring cases) and after it later (A20 before A21 in 29 of 30); A19 ran before A29 and the block earlier and after them later [REL015, REL018, REL008, REL009, REL016, REL019, REL006, REL014, REL007, REL017, REL005, REL021, REL020, REL001, REL003].
- A25 and A29 kept their places between the two groups, shifting only slightly later (mean relative position about 0.38 → 0.45 and 0.57 → 0.64) [POS007, POS008].

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that two segments exchanged their order while keeping their internal structure. In the earlier window a case ran A40, then one of the alternatives A21, A19 or A24 (in roughly equal shares), then A25, then A29, then A20, A22 and A23 in varying order, then A30; in the later window a case runs A40, then A20, A22 and A23 in varying order, then A25, then A29, then one of A21, A19 or A24, then A30 [DIR008, DIR007, DIR006, DIR001, DIR003, DIR002, DIR004, DIR005, LAG009, LAG005, REL015, REL009].

The exchange is shown directly and consistently: all eight observed outgoing-transition profiles of the involved activities flipped to the neighbours the exchange predicts, about twenty relation pairs reversed with large margins, and the distance changes fit exactly (A40→A25 lengthening from 2 to 4 steps, A29→A30 shortening from 4 to 2 steps, loss of the old A24/A21→A25 adjacencies, gain of A29→A21 and A40-to-block adjacencies). Participation of every involved activity is essentially unchanged, which rules out explanations through activities being added, removed or repeated differently. The screening also produced 46 qualifying items where about 0.69 would be expected without a change, and the qualifying items show differences larger than in about 995 of 1,000 random re-splits [screening, DIR006, DIR007, DIR008, REL015, REL009, LAG009, LAG005, POS002].

Limitations: positions and lags use event positions, not clock time, so no conclusion about timing follows; many items describe the same underlying reordering and are not independent confirmations; the A25→A29 link itself is not directly observed but inferred from positions and surrounding relations [POS007, POS008, LAG005]. An important competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that, besides the reordering, the choice among the three alternatives also shifted slightly, or that these small shifts are only sampling variation in 200-case windows [POS003, POS006, POS001].

Other differences: apart from small shifts in how often the alternatives occur (A21 in 70 → 59 of 200 cases, A24 in 73 → 79, A19 in 60 → 66), no further differences appear; the mixed internal order of the A20/A22/A23 block and the roughly equal choice among A21/A19/A24 look similar in both windows [POS003, POS006, POS001, DIR002, DIR004, DIR005, DIR007, DIR008].
