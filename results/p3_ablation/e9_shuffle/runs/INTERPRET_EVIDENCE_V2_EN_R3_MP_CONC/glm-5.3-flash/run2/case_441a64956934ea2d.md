### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that A01 and A13 exchanged their places in the control flow. Before, A01 was an early, frequently repeated activity fed by A02, A04, A07 and A15 and leading to A15, A08 and A07, while A13 was a rare, late activity cycling with A06; in the later window these roles are exchanged, with A13 early and frequent (about 61 in 100 traces, fed by A02/A04/A07/A15, leading to A15/A08/A07) and A01 late and rare (about 28 in 100 traces, fed by A06 and leading back to A06). The exchange is shown directly by the swapped successor sets [DIR001, DIR006] and the redirected predecessors [DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, DIR007], and it is consistent across the occurrence, position, relation and lag indicators [OCC001, OCC002, POS001, POS002, REL005, REL012, LAG004, LAG008]. Screening tested 92 control-flow units and 38 items qualified, against roughly 0.46 expected if nothing had changed, so a set of differences this large is not what an unchanged process typically produces [DIR001, DIR006, OCC001, POS002].

Limitations: the evidence shows an exchange of roles between the two activity codes but cannot rule out that the same work was simply recorded under the other code after the change point; DIR shares are computed over outgoing transitions rather than cases, and positions are event positions rather than clock times. [DIR001, DIR006, POS001, POS002]

Other differences: none stand apart from the exchange; the remaining items (A06's relation with A13, A08's lags with A01 and A13, and the A01→A06 and A13→A06 lags) follow from it — for example, A01 now leads directly to A06 at lag 1, where A13 previously did. [LAG002, LAG009, LAG010, LAG011, REL010]
