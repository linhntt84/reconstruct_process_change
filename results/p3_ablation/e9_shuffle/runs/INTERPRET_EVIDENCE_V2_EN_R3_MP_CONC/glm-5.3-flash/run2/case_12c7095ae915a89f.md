### Observed differences

- The order between A02 and A04 changed from fixed to mixed. Earlier, both activities occurred in every trace and A02 came first in 100% of the 200 traces; in the later window they still occur together in every trace, but A02 comes first in about 54 in 100 cases and A04 first in about 46 in 100. [REL001]
- Routing after A01 changed: earlier about 87 in 100 of A01's outgoing transitions led to A02 and only about 6 to A04; in the later window about 50 lead to A02 and about 42 lead directly to A04. A04 now follows A01 within one step in about 49 in 100 of the traces containing both, against about 6 in 100 earlier. [DIR001, LAG002]
- Routing after A02 changed: earlier about 94 in 100 of A02's outgoing transitions led to A04; in the later window about 50 lead to A04 and about 44 lead directly to A03, a successor A02 did not have earlier (A03 within one step after A02 rose from 0 to about 44 in 100). [DIR002, LAG003]
- Routing after A04 changed: earlier about 96 in 100 of A04's outgoing transitions led to A03; in the later window about 49 lead to A03 and about 40 lead back to A02, and A03 follows A04 within one step in only about 49 in 100 of the traces containing both, against about 96 in 100 earlier. [DIR003, LAG004]
- Event positions shifted accordingly: A02's mean position moved later (about 0.17 to 0.27, upper quartile 0.2 to 0.4) and A04's earlier (about 0.39 to 0.28, median 0.4 to 0.2), both with more spread; the A01–A02 gap loosened from always one step to one step in about 57 in 100 later traces. Participation itself did not change — A02 and A04 still appear in essentially all traces in both windows. [POS001, POS002, LAG001, REL001]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the fixed sequence between A02 and A04 was relaxed into a mixed order. Earlier, traces ran A01, then A02, then A04, then A03; in the later window, about 46 in 100 cases run A04 before A02, so that A01 leads directly to A04 (about 42 in 100 of A01's outgoing transitions) and A03 directly follows A02 (about 44 in 100 of A02's outgoing transitions) instead of always following A04. The order change is shown directly by the A02–A04 relation with full support in both windows (200 of 200 traces each), while their co-occurrence stays at 100 in 100 — this is a change in ordering constraints, not activities appearing, disappearing or becoming optional. Every other item fits this single reordering: the new direct transitions, the shifted one-step and two-step gap shares, and the opposite position shifts of A02 and A04; each difference is larger than in about 995 of 1000 random re-splits of the pooled data. Screening produced 10 qualifying control-flow items where about 0.1 would be expected without a change, so the set as a whole cannot be explained by chance sampling, and the new order appears in about 46 in 100 cases, far too often to be a rare variant surfacing by sampling. A competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that the later window mixes two fixed variants with opposite A02/A04 order rather than a genuinely free order within each case; both would produce the same profiles. [REL001, DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, POS001, POS002]

Other differences: A05 and A06, earlier about 2 and 1 in 100 of A01's outgoing transitions, are no longer observed among them in the later window (an unobserved edge does not show they left the process); a transition from A04 back to A01 appears at about 10 in 100 of A04's outgoing transitions, and small three-step gaps emerge (for example A04 before A03 at distance three in about 8 in 100). These are minor and may be side effects of the same reordering. [DIR001, DIR003, LAG004]
