### Observed differences

- The relative order of two activity blocks flipped. In the earlier window, A07 preceded A05 in about 48 of 100 cases and A09 preceded A05 in about 48 of 100 (in every case where the pair co-occurred); in the later window, A05 preceded A07 in about 55 of 100 and A05 preceded A09 in about 55 of 100, again in every co-occurrence. ### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the late block of the case was reordered: A05 and A06, which earlier executed after A09 and typically concluded the case (A09 handed over to A05 or A06, adjacent and in either order, with each of them ending about half of the traces), now execute before A07 and A09, so that a typical case in the later window runs A01 → A04 → A05 → A06 → A07 → A09, with A09 the closing step in most traces and A04 passing the case to A05 instead of A07. The order changes are observed directly in both windows on about 96 versus 109 traces per activity pair; the position shifts of all four activities, the successor changes and the step-distance changes all agree with the same rewiring; 28 qualifying control-flow items against roughly 0.26 expected by chance cannot be explained by random re-splitting, and every item's difference exceeds about 995 of 1000 random re-splits. Because the orders A05-before-A07 and A05-before-A09 were not observed at all in the earlier window (share 0), this is a new ordering constraint rather than a shift in how often existing paths are taken. The evidence cannot rule out that the later window samples a variant that already existed earlier but was too rare to appear; a few peripheral readings (A09's outgoing profile rests on 12 observations, the A07–A06 distance on 27) are weak, but the hypothesis does not rest on them. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, DIR001, DIR002, DIR004, LAG002, LAG004]

Other differences: each activity of the block appears in slightly more traces (about 48 in 100 before versus 55 in 100 after), and small back-transitions such as A04→A01 (about 23% of A04's outgoing transitions after, 10% before) and A05→A04 (about 19%) suggest occasional repetition in the later window.
