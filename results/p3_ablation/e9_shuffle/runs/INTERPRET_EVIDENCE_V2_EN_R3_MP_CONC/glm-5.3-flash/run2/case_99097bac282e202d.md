### Observed differences

- Two mutually exclusive groups of activities swapped how often they run. Earlier, A21, A22 and A23 each ran in about 75 in 100 traces (and almost always together), while A24, A25 and A26 each ran in about 25 in 100 (also together); cross-group pairs shared a trace in at most about 2 in 100 cases. In the later window the shares are reversed: A21–A23 each about 25 in 100, A24–A26 about 75 in 100, with the same mutual exclusivity. - A02 fell from about 9 to about 1 in 100 traces; its after-window outgoing profile rests on only 2 transitions. [OCC001, DIR001]
- Step distances between consecutive activities shifted slightly upward: A20→A24 at 1 step fell from about 54 to 33 in 100 while 3 steps rose from about 20 to 39 in 100, and A32→A33 at 1 step fell from about 56 to 39 in 100 with 2 steps rising to about 14 in 100; these are event positions, not clock times. [LAG001, LAG002, LAG003]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the choice between two existing, mutually exclusive paths changed frequency: before, the path carrying A21, A22 and A23 ran in about 75 in 100 traces and the alternative path carrying A24, A25 and A26 in about 25 in 100; in the later window the shares are reversed (about 25 vs 75 in 100). The reading is direct and consistent across occurrence, relation and direction indicators: all six activities occur in a substantial share of traces in both windows, the two groups never run together in either window, each group's members appear together and in both orders (so no fixed internal order and no reordering or relocation is involved), and A20 and A30, present in essentially every trace, co-occur with whichever group runs. [OCC003, OCC006, REL013, REL015, REL034, REL039, REL007, REL011, DIR003]

The evidence cannot show why the shares flipped: a changed routing rule and a changed mix of cases reaching the choice point would look identical here, and the exact decision structure (one branch point versus separate skips) is not identifiable from these indicators. [OCC003, OCC006, REL015, REL034]

Other differences:
- A10 forms a secondary, expanded late-trace segment — reached from A32 in about 20 in 100 of A32's outgoing transitions and followed by A33 in about 90 in 100 of its own — which the path-share hypothesis above does not cover; based on 42 traces. [OCC002, POS001, DIR002, DIR004]
- A02 nearly disappeared (about 9→1 in 100 traces), on very few observations. [OCC001, DIR001]
- Step gaps between consecutive activities shifted slightly toward larger distances, consistent with more activities now lying between them once the second path runs more often. [LAG001, LAG002, LAG003]
