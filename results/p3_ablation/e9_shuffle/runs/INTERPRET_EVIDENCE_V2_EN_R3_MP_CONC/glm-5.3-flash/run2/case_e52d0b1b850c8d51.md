### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

Screening qualified 59 control-flow items where fewer than 1 would be expected without a change, so the ensemble cannot be explained by random sampling of stable behaviour. The evidence supports the hypothesis that, in the later window, two previously near-mandatory segments are skipped in a large share of cases: the block A19 → A25 → A20, executed together in every earlier case, is omitted in about 45 in 100 later cases — all three absent jointly, order preserved when present — and when it is skipped, A41 passes control directly to A26 instead of to A19; in addition, the A06 step between A14 and A15 is omitted in about three quarters of later cases, with A14 passing directly to A15. [OCC005, OCC006, OCC007, REL016, REL017, REL022, DIR008, LAG008, OCC002, DIR005, DIR003]

The supporting signs are direct and consistent: participation measured on all 200 traces per window, joint absence of the block, successor redistribution at A41 and A14, and unchanged ordering among remaining executions, which points to skipping rather than reordering or position swaps. An important competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that the later window mixes in a case type that never executes these segments, rather than the same cases sometimes skipping them; the apparent growth of A04, A08 and A10 also rests on few earlier observations. [OCC001, OCC003, OCC004, REL016, REL017, REL022]

Other differences: A04, A08 and A10 (with A02 in small shares) now occur in a minority of traces where they were previously rare and appear as minor new successors of A14, A15, A44 and A46; A26 sits slightly earlier in the trace, and several activity-pair distances shifted by about one step. Many qualifying items describe the same underlying skip behaviour and are therefore not independent confirmations.
