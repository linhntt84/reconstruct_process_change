### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, A30, A31 and A36 each occurred about once per trace (means about 1.0) and repeats were almost unseen (roughly 0–1 in 200 traces). In the later window, each occurs about 3.5 times per trace, and about 68 in 100 traces contain at least one repeat of each; every trace still contains all three activities in both windows, so the activities repeat rather than appearing, disappearing or becoming optional [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003].
- Fixed orders broke up into interleaving: before, A14 preceded A30 in about 99 in 100 traces, A14 preceded A36 in about 99 in 100, and A30 preceded A36 in about 98 in 100 (each pair co-occurs in every trace). In the later window, these fixed orders hold in only about 32 in 100 traces — matching the roughly one third of traces without repeats — while the pairs are interleaved or repeated in about 68 in 100 [REL003, REL004, REL005].
- A36's successor changed: before, about 97 in 100 of its outgoing transitions led to A25; in the later window about 70 in 100 lead to A30 and about 28 in 100 to A25, out of roughly 700 observed transitions versus about 200 before — consistent with a return to A30 on repeated passes and a single exit to A25 at the end [DIR001].
- The close succession of A36 after A30 largely dissolved: before, A36 followed within two steps of A30 in about 98 in 100 measured cases; in the later window only about 12 in 100 do, with the remaining measured gaps spread over three to five steps [LAG002].
- Relative positions (event order, not clock time) spread out: position variability for A30 and A36 roughly grew (standard deviations from about 0.07 to about 0.12 in both), and A14's typical position moved slightly earlier (mean about 0.14 to 0.11) [POS001, POS002, POS003].
- A05 still occurs only alongside A30/A36, in about half of traces in both windows, but its fixed precedence weakened: A05 preceded A30 in about 54 in 100 traces before versus about 16 in 100 after, with interleaving or repetition rising from near zero to about 35 in 100; the gap from A05 to A14 lengthened from mostly one to two steps to mostly two to three steps [REL001, REL002, LAG001].

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the pass formed by A30, A31 and A36 — previously executed exactly once per trace after A14 in the fixed order A14 → A30 → A36, with A36 then leading to A25 — is now repeated about 3.5 times per trace: A36 returns to A30 between passes (about 70 in 100 of its outgoing transitions) and proceeds to A25 on the final pass (about 28 in 100), and the strict orders A14 → A30, A14 → A36 and A30 → A36 survive only in the roughly one third of traces without repeats, becoming interleaved in the rest [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, DIR001, REL003, REL004, REL005].

The hypothesis is direct and consistent: repetition is measured outright on activities present in every trace in both windows, which rules out explanations based on participation or a rare variant appearing in one window; the pair relations, the successor profile and the adjacency gaps all move in the same direction, each difference exceeding about 995 in 1000 random re-splits; and 14 qualifying items against roughly 0.49 expected for an unchanged process makes chance an implausible account of the evidence as a whole — though the items largely describe one underlying behaviour rather than independent changes [OCC001, DIR001, LAG002, REL005].

Other differences: A05, which previously preceded the block in essentially all traces where it co-occurred, now interleaves with it in about two thirds of those traces, and its gap to A14 lengthened; A14's relative position also shifted slightly earlier [REL001, REL002, LAG001, POS001]. Limitations: there are no occurrence or relational items for A14 or A05, so whether they join the repeated passes or still run once cannot be determined, and A31's place in the repeated pass is unobserved; positions use event order, not clock time. An important competing explanation the evidence cannot exclude is that the later window mixes unchanged single-pass traces with heavily repeated ones — about one third of traces still show the old single-pass pattern — rather than changing every trace uniformly [REL003, POS002].
