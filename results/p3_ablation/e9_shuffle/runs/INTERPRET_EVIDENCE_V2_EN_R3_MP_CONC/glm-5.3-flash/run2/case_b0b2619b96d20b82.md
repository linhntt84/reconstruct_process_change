### Observed differences

- The direct connections between A01, A04 and A06 reversed. In the earlier window, all observed outgoing transitions of A01 led to A06, all of A04 led back to A01, and A06 led to A05 in about 54 in 100 transitions and to A04 in about 46 in 100. In the later window, all of A01's transitions lead to A04, all of A04's lead to A06, and A06 leads to A05 in about 57 in 100 and to A01 in about 43 in 100. The cycle thus ran A01 → A06 → A04 (→ A01) earlier and runs A01 → A04 → A06 (→ A01) later, while the exit from A06 to A05 stayed in place. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003]
- Pairwise distances flipped in the same direction. A01–A06 were adjacent (within one step) in about 30 in 100 of the traces containing the pair earlier and about two steps apart later (about 29 in 100); A01–A04 and A04–A06 moved from about two steps apart (about 31 in 100) to adjacent (about 29–35 in 100), and the mirrored pairs show the reverse pattern. [LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006]
- Participation swapped between A01 and A04. A01 appeared in about 52 in 100 traces earlier versus about 20 in 100 later (mean occurrences per trace about 0.96 versus 0.39; repeated in about 24 versus 9 in 100 traces). A04 rose from about 24 to about 52 in 100 traces (mean about 0.44 versus 0.90; repeated in about 10 versus 20 in 100). A06 stayed at about 52 in 100 traces in both windows. [OCC001, OCC002, REL002, REL003]
- Co-occurrence relations flipped accordingly. A01 and A06 usually appeared together earlier (about 52 in 100 traces) but only about 20 in 100 later, with A06 now appearing without A01 in about 32 in 100; A04 and A06 moved from about 24 in 100 together (A06 alone about 28) to about 52 in 100 together, with A04 before A06 in about 32 in 100; and for A01–A04, "A01 without A04" (about 28 in 100) became "A04 without A01" (about 32 in 100). [REL001, REL002, REL003]
- Typical positions exchanged: A01 sat at the beginning of its traces earlier (median relative position 0) and around one third in later (median about 0.33); A04 moved from about one third (median 0.33) to the beginning (median 0). [POS001, POS002]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence directly supports a reordering of the cycle over A01, A04 and A06: in the later window it runs in the opposite order (A01 → A04 → A06, closing back to A01) instead of A01 → A06 → A04, with A06 keeping its exit to A05. A01 and A04 thereby exchanged roles: A04 now opens the episode (about 52 in 100 traces, at the start position) and A01 occurs mainly when the cycle repeats (about 20 in 100 traces), whereas earlier A01 opened it (about 52 in 100) and A04 marked repetition (about 24 in 100). [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, LAG001, LAG002, LAG004, REL002, REL003, POS001, POS002, OCC001, OCC002]

Supporting signs: the successor changes are observed directly for all three activities; every adjacency flips exactly as a reversal predicts, including the across-repetition distances (about four steps earlier, five later); and the participation, co-occurrence and position shifts all match the same role exchange. Chance is an unlikely explanation for the set as a whole: 16 control-flow items qualified where about 0.1 would be expected without a change, each difference exceeding about 199 of 200 random re-splits — though the items describe one underlying difference, not independent confirmations. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, LAG002, LAG004, LAG005, screening]

Other differences: none that stand apart from this reordering; the share of traces containing none of A01, A04, A06 stayed at about 48–49 in 100, and A06's exit to A05 was stable (about 54% versus 57% of its outgoing transitions). [REL002, REL003, DIR003]

A competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that the change originates outside the observed activities — for example, a rerouted entry into the cycle from an unobserved predecessor could produce the same edge and position shifts, since the data show which edges changed but not the full surrounding model; A01's later profile also rests on only about 40 traces. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, OCC001]
