### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the block A15–A17–A18 and the alternative step (exactly one of A14, A16, A19) exchanged sides around the unchanged A20 → A24 segment: earlier, a case ran A35 → A15/A17/A18 (unfixed order) → A20 → A24 → one of A14/A16/A19 → A25; in the later window it runs A35 → one of A14/A16/A19 → A20 → A24 → A15/A17/A18 (unfixed order) → A25. A15, A17 and A18 moved from before A20 to after A24, and the alternative step moved from after A24 to before A20, while A35, A20 → A24 and A25 kept their anchor roles. [REL016, REL008, REL018, REL009, REL019, REL015, DIR001, DIR003, DIR006, DIR007, DIR008, LAG001, LAG002]

The support is direct and consistent: relational items show complete order reversals (several from 100 in 100 traces to 0), the successor profiles of A35, A24, A14, A16 and A19 changed exactly as this reordering requires (profiles resting on roughly 56–200 traces per window), and the step-distance changes match a three-activity group changing sides. The volume also far exceeds chance: 40 items qualified where about 0.46 would be expected for an unchanged process, and every item's difference exceeds about 995 of 1,000 random re-splits. [REL016, DIR008, LAG001, LAG002, DIR001]

An important competing explanation remains: both arrangements could have existed as rare variants in both periods with only their frequencies flipping. This is unlikely — the reversed order is entirely absent from the opposite window (0 of 200 traces for pairs such as A17 and A24) — but very rare variants would not be visible in 200-trace samples, and the internal order within the A15/A17/A18 group is not fully characterised by the relational items. [REL016, DIR002, DIR004, DIR005]

Other differences: the routing among the three mutually exclusive alternatives shifted in frequency — A14 rose from about 31 to about 44 in 100 cases, while A16 (about 38 to 29) and A19 (about 32 to 28) declined — a change in how often existing paths are taken, alongside the reordering. [OCC001, POS001, POS003, POS006]
