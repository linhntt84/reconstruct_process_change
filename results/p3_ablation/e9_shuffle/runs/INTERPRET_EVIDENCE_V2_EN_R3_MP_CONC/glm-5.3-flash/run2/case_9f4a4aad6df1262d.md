### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, A31, A22, A20, A23, A24, A26, A25 and A32 ran as one fixed chain (A31→A22→A20→A23→A24→A26→A25→A32): each source activity had essentially one successor (about 93–100 in 100 of its outgoing transitions), and within every pair of the six middle activities the chain order held in about 98–99 in 100 traces. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006, DIR007, REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, REL007, REL008]
- In the later window, this fixed order is gone. A24 now precedes A23 in about 83 in 100 traces (previously about 1–2), A24 precedes A20 in about 73 in 100 (previously about 2), and A26 precedes A23 in about 68 in 100 (previously about 1–2); other pairs have become genuinely mixed, with A26 before A20 in about 51 in 100, A25 before A23 in about 52 in 100 and A24 before A22 in about 47 in 100. [REL006, REL001, REL008, REL003, REL007, REL004]
- New direct successions appeared in the later window: A31 leads to A24 in about 47 in 100 of its outgoing transitions (before: only A22), A23 leads to A32 in about 49 in 100 (before: only A24), A22 leads to A24 in about 27 in 100 (before: about 99 in 100 to A20), and A24, A26 and A25 each now also lead back to A20/A22/A23 in roughly 30–50 in 100 of their transitions. [DIR007, DIR003, DIR002, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006]
- Step distances that were constant became variable: A31 to A24 was always 4 positions apart and is now 1 position apart in about 47 in 100 cases; A23 to A32 was 4 positions apart and is now 1 position apart in about 49 in 100; A20 to A32 was always 5 positions apart and is now spread over 2–5. [LAG027, LAG013, LAG005]
- Relative positions shifted consistently with the reordering: A20, A22 and A23 occur later in the trace (mean relative position up by about 0.03–0.06), while A24, A25 and A26 occur earlier (down by about 0.02–0.06). [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006]
- Participation did not change: all six activities still co-occur in 100 in 100 traces in both windows, and the interleaved/repeated share stays at about 1–2 in 100, so the difference is ordering, not added, removed or repeated activities. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, REL007, REL008]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the fixed sequential ordering among A20, A22, A23, A24, A25 and A26 was relaxed into mixed orders: in the earlier window they always executed as the chain A31→A22→A20→A23→A24→A26→A25→A32, while in the later window several pairs run mostly or partly in the opposite order, and new direct successions such as A31→A24 and A23→A32 now carry about 47–49 in 100 of the source's outgoing transitions. This is a change in ordering constraints rather than an exchange of positions, because most pairs appear in both orders in substantial shares instead of a clean reversal. [REL001, REL003, REL004, REL006, REL007, DIR003, DIR007, LAG027, LAG013]

The reordering is shown directly by the relational and successor evidence, with the lag-distance and position changes agreeing in direction; participation, co-occurrence and repetition shares are stable, so added, removed, skipped or repeated activities are not plausible competing readings. The volume of qualifying items (49 control-flow items against roughly 0.66 expected if nothing had changed) shows the differences as a set cannot be a sampling artefact of the two 200-trace windows. [REL001, REL004, REL006, REL008, DIR002, DIR004, DIR005, screening]

Limitations: the items overlap heavily — the same reordering is reflected in REL, DIR, LAG and POS alike — so they are not independent confirmations, and the position indicators alone would not establish movement. A few lag items rest on only about one before-window observation (for example A25→A20 and A24→A22) and were not relied on. The evidence cannot distinguish a general relaxation of the sequence from one specific new routing structure (for example a partial reversal of the block plus added skip edges); the exact target model is not identifiable from these indicators. [LAG019, LAG015, POS001, POS004]
