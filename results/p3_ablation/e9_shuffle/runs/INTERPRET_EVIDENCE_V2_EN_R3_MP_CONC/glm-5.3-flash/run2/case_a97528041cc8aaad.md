### Observed differences

- Two groups of three activities exchanged their places in the running order. ### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that two three-activity segments exchanged their order in the later window: earlier the case ran A40 → A20/A22/A23 (around 40% into the trace) → A25, and only afterwards A29 → A19/A21/A24 (around two-thirds in) → A30; later it runs A40 → A19/A21/A24 (around 36% in) → A25 and afterwards A29 → A20/A22/A23 (around 64% in) → A30. The change is shown directly and consistently: near-complete order reversals between the groups and A25/A29 with co-occurrence unchanged, successor changes at A19/A21/A24 (A30 → A25) and at A20/A22/A23 (A25 → A30), predecessor changes at A40 and A29, position shifts of roughly a quarter to a third of the trace for all six activities, and the corresponding loss and gain of immediate adjacencies. All involved activities remain well observed in both windows (A20/A22/A23, A25, A29 and A40 in essentially all traces; A19, A21 and A24 in about 28–44 in 100), so this is a reordering of existing behaviour rather than one path replacing another. Screening produced 87 qualifying items against about 1 expected without a change, so the pattern as a whole cannot be attributed to sampling variation. Limitations: positions are event positions, not clock time, so the evidence shows reordering within the case rather than a timing change; and the aggregate view cannot distinguish a rewiring of a single flow from a shift in the mix of two order-variants, since the order is near-deterministic within each window. The exchange is also not perfectly symmetric — A19 now appears in about 44 in 100 traces (32 earlier) while A21 and A24 appear in about 29 and 28 (39 and 32 earlier).
