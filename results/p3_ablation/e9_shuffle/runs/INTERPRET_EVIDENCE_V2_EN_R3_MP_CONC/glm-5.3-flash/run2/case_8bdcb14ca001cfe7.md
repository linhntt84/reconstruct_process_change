### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that, in the later window, repetition moved from the A03/A14 group to A09: A09 is now executed about 4.3 times per case (previously about 1.9) and has become the dominant successor of both A13 and A15, while A03 and A14 — which still always appear together, now joined by A11 in every such case — occur at most once per case, mostly with A11 first, instead of repeating and interleaving. [OCC002, OCC001, OCC003, DIR004, DIR006, REL001, REL002, REL004]

The hypothesis is shown directly and consistently: the occurrence items capture the repetition shift outright, the transition items show the redirection to A09, and the relation and position items show the new single-pass, ordered co-occurrence and A11's higher, earlier participation. Screening qualified 29 items on 86 units where about 0.43 would be expected if nothing had changed, so chance cannot account for the set, and every item's difference exceeds about 199 of 200 random re-splits; the profiles rest on substantial supports (for example 124–135 traces behind A09's profile, 94–101 behind the A03/A14 pair). [OCC002, DIR004, REL004, POS002]

Limitations: the evidence shows that A09 occurs more often but not where the extra occurrences come from (a newly repeating segment versus more passes through existing ones), and an important competing explanation it cannot rule out is that the shift is driven by a subset of cases following a new variant rather than by a uniform change across all cases; the aggregate profiles cannot separate these. [OCC002, LAG007]
