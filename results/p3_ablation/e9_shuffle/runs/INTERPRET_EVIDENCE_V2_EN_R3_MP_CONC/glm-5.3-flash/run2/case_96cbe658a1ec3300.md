### Observed differences

- The relative order of two groups of activities flipped. In the earlier window, A26 and A27 each ran before A35, A36 and A41 in about 98–99 of 100 traces; in the later window, A35, A36 and A41 each ran before A26 and A27 in about 97–98 of 100. Both members of every pair appeared together in all 200 traces of each window, so participation itself did not change. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006]
- Successors were rewired consistently with that flip. In the earlier window, A25 led almost exclusively to A27 (about 98 in 100 of its observed outgoing transitions), A26 led to A41, A35 or A36 (about 28–38 in 100 each), and A35, A36 and A41 each led mainly to A30 (about 28–34 in 100). In the later window, A25 leads to A35, A36 or A41 (about 31–33 in 100 each), A26 leads almost exclusively to A30 (about 98 in 100), and A35, A36 and A41 each lead mainly to A27 (about 31–33 in 100). [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005]
- Event positions moved with the flip: A26's mean position rose from about 0.53 to 0.62 and A27's from about 0.38 to 0.47, while A35, A36 and A41 fell from about 0.58 to about 0.42. [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005]
- Step distances match the same move: A25 and A27 were adjacent in about 99 of 100 earlier traces and are 4–5 steps apart in about 99 of 100 later traces; A26 and A30 were 4–5 steps apart in nearly all earlier traces and are adjacent in about 99 of 100 later traces. [LAG001, LAG003]
- The remaining distance items fit the same reversal but are thinly supported: A35 now sits 1–3 steps after A25 and A27 sits 1–3 steps after A35 (about 199 of 200 later traces each, against a single earlier observation each), while directed pairs such as A26→A41, A36→A30 and A26→A35 have only 1–2 supporting observations in the later window, as expected once the order reversed. [LAG002, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006, LAG007, LAG008, LAG009, LAG010]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a structural reordering in which the three-activity block A35, A36, A41 — executed in varying internal order — changed place with the segment containing A27 and A26: in the earlier window the flow ran A25 → A27 and later A26 → {A35/A36/A41} → A30, whereas in the later window it runs A25 → {A35/A36/A41} → A27, with A26 leading directly to A30. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, LAG001, LAG003]

Supporting signs: all six activity pairs flip from a near-deterministic order to its opposite with unchanged full co-occurrence, so this is a reordering of activities that still always run, not activities appearing or disappearing; all five observed successor profiles change exactly as this rewiring predicts; the two well-supported distance items match the block being inserted after A25 and removed from before A30; and 26 items qualified in screening against roughly 0.63 expected for an unchanged process (control-flow family), far beyond what random re-splits produce. The items largely reflect one underlying trace behaviour and should be read as one consistent signal, not independent confirmations. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, LAG001, LAG003]

Limitations: the items do not cover the region between A27 and A26, so the evidence cannot show whether other activities there also moved with the block; the block's internal ordering cannot be compared reliably because several of its directed pairs rest on only 1–2 observations in one window; and "the block moved earlier" versus "A27 and A26 moved later" are equivalent descriptions of the same rewiring, which the data cannot distinguish. [LAG002, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006, LAG007, LAG008, LAG009, LAG010]

Other differences: none that stand apart from this reordering; the low-support distance items are all consistent with it. [LAG002, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006, LAG007, LAG008, LAG009, LAG010]
