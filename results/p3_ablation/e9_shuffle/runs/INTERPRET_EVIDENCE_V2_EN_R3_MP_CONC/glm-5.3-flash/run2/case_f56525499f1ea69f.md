### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, each of the six activities A33, A35, A36, A37, A38 and A39 ran in about 49–52 in 100 traces; in the later window, each runs in about 99 in 100 traces. [OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, OCC007, OCC008, OCC009]
- In the earlier window these six formed two mutually exclusive groups: pairs within {A33, A35, A36} and within {A37, A38, A39} always co-occurred (about 49–52 in 100 traces, matching a roughly 50/50 split between the groups), while cross-group pairs never shared a trace. In the later window, cross-group pairs co-occur in about 97–99 in 100 traces. [REL037, REL042, REL049, REL040, REL045, REL046, REL048]
- In the later window the groups run in a fixed sequence: an A33/A35/A36 activity precedes an A37/A38/A39 activity in about 97 in 100 co-occurring traces, while the order within each group stays mixed (roughly 50/50 both ways, as before). [REL040, REL043, REL046, REL048, REL037, REL049]
- The groups sit between the same anchors as before: A14 and A30 precede them (about 98–99 in 100 after), A31 follows them (about 98 in 100 after), and A15/A16 straddle them in a mixed order (about 60 in 100 before-order, 40 in 100 interleaved). Distances grew as expected if the other group was inserted: A30 to the A37/A38/A39 activities moved from 1–3 steps to 4–5 steps, and the A30→A31 succession within five steps, seen in essentially all 200 before-window pairs, is seen in only about 5 traces afterwards. - Edges rewired accordingly: before, A30's outgoing traffic split about half to each group; in the later window about 97 in 100 of its outgoing transitions go to A33/A35/A36. A33/A35/A36 now lead to A37/A38/A39 (about 30–35 in 100 of their outgoing) rather than to A31, which fell from about 30–36 in 100 to about 1 in 100. [DIR006, DIR007, DIR008, DIR009]
- Relative positions shifted consistently: A33/A35/A36 occur earlier (mean position about 0.75 to about 0.68), A37/A38/A39 slightly later (about 0.73 to about 0.77), A30 earlier (about 0.67 to 0.62) and A31 later (about 0.80 to 0.82). [POS006, POS007, POS008, POS009, POS010, POS011, POS004, POS005]

Outgoing profiles of the rare activities A02 and A08 also differ, but rest on only 9–28 transitions. [OCC001, LAG001, DIR002, OCC002, OCC003, DIR001, DIR003]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the later window replaced an exclusive choice between two activity groups with sequential execution of both: in the earlier window a trace ran either {A33, A35, A36} or {A37, A38, A39} (about 49 vs 52 in 100 traces, never both), while in the later window essentially every trace runs both groups in order — A33/A35/A36 first, then A37/A38/A39 — between A30/A14 and A31. This rests on direct relational evidence (cross-group co-occurrence rising from zero to about 98 in 100 with a consistent group order), the near-universal participation of all six activities, the matching rewiring of edges at A30 and the group activities, and pairwise distance growth that fits the insertion of the other group. The 89 qualifying items against about 1 expected for an unchanged process cannot be explained by chance as a whole, and the items agree with one another across indicators. Limitations: all evidence is control-flow based on event positions rather than clock time, and the evidence cannot rule out that the two windows contain different mixes of case types (for example, a variant that always runs both groups dominating the later sample) rather than a modified routing decision within one process. [REL040, REL045, REL048, OCC004, OCC007, DIR006, DIR008, LAG002, LAG003]

Other differences: A04 participation fell from about 31 to 14 in 100 traces with a looser relation to A16, and A06/A10 became somewhat more frequent; these are not part of the main hypothesis. [OCC001, LAG001, DIR002, OCC002, OCC003]
