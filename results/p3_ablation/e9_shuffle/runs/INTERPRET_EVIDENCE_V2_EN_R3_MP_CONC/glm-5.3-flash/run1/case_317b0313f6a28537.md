### Observed differences

- A block of three activities — A19, A20 and A25 — now runs much earlier in the case. In the earlier window their mean position was about 0.60–0.61 of the trace; in the later window it is about 0.32–0.34, while their participation barely moved (A19 in 78 → 72 of 200 traces, A20 in 63 → 70, A25 in 62 → 64). [POS001, POS002, POS003]
- Their order relative to A26 and A27 inverted. Before, A26 and A27 always came first when they co-occurred with these activities (e.g., A26 before A19 in about 38 in 100 traces, the reverse never); in the later window A19/A20/A25 come first in about 30–36 in 100 traces — essentially every co-occurrence — and the old order has disappeared. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006]
- Flow into and out of the block was rewired. Before, A19, A20 and A25 sent most of their observed outgoing transitions to A28 (about 47–56%) and A32 (about 43–48%); in the later window about 88–94% go to A26. A45, which previously fed A26 directly (about 85% of its transitions), now leads to A20, A19 and A25 (about 29%, 29% and 27%), with A26 no longer among its observed direct successors; A26 still follows A45, but two to three steps later instead of immediately. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR006, LAG007]
- The late-stage remainder became a fixed chain. A27 previously split its transitions across A28 (~47%), A19 (~19%), A20 (~16%) and A25 (~16%), and A28 split between A32 (~50%) and A19/A25/A20; in the later window about 98% of A27's transitions go to A28 and about 96% of A28's go to A32. Gaps agree: A27→A28 and A28→A32 are now almost always immediate (lag 1 in about 98 and 96 in 100, previously about 48 and 51), and A32 follows A27 one step sooner (lag 2 in about 95 in 100, previously lag 3 in about 96). [DIR004, DIR005, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006]
- A15 occurs less often per case: about 1.9 → 1.6 occurrences per trace on average, recurring in about 47% → 38% of traces, while still appearing in every trace. [OCC001]
- Smaller shifts: A26, A27 and A28 each sit slightly later (mean position up about 0.02–0.03). Three lag items (A19→A32, A25→A28, A25→A32) suggest these pairs are no longer adjacent, but their later-window profiles rest on only 1–3 observations and are weak. [POS004, POS005, POS006, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a structural reordering centred on A19, A20 and A25: a block that previously ran late in the case — after A26 and A27, around 60% of the trace, passing flow on to A28 and A32 — now runs early, before A26, around 33% of the trace, fed by A45 and passing flow on to A26. In parallel, A27, A28 and A32 became a direct fixed sequence (A27→A28→A32), losing their branches to the relocated block. The change is shown directly by positional evidence (mean positions of the three activities dropping by about 0.28), relational evidence (all six pairs with A26/A27 flip from block-after to block-before), successor changes in the routing profiles, and lag evidence (the A45→A26 gap widening to 2–3 steps; A27→A28 and A28→A32 becoming immediate). Participation of the moved activities stayed roughly stable, so this reads as a relocation with rewiring rather than activities appearing or disappearing. With 26 qualifying items against roughly 0.6 expected by chance for 121 tested units, the ensemble is far beyond what random re-splits of a stable process produce. Limitations: positions and lags are measured in event order, not clock time, so "earlier" means earlier in the sequence; the six order flips and three position shifts describe one underlying relocation rather than independent confirmations; and the after-profiles of LAG001, LAG002 and LAG003 rest on only 1–3 observations. A competing reading the evidence cannot rule out is that a late-stage variant disappeared while an early-stage variant appeared, rather than one structure being rewired — the two windows look alike under either. Other differences: A15 now repeats less often (about 1.9 → 1.6 occurrences per trace; recurrence in about 47% → 38% of traces, participation unchanged at 100%); this reduction in repetition is not part of the relocation hypothesis. [OCC001]
