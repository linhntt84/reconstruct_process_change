### Observed differences

- Participation did not change: A15, A17, A18, A19, A20 and A21 each appear in essentially all traces in both windows (200 of 200), and every studied pair co-occurs in 100% of traces in both windows, so all differences below concern order and routing rather than activities appearing or disappearing [REL002, REL006, POS001, POS004]
- Pairwise order became fixed. In the earlier window many pairs ran in both directions — A18 preceded A19 in only about 12 in 100 cases, A15 preceded A19 in about 26 in 100, and A15/A21 as well as A17/A19 were each split roughly 50/50 — while in the later window a single direction holds almost everywhere, with A18 before A19, A15 before A19, A15 before A21, A17 before A19 and A18 before A20 each in about 99–100 in 100 cases [REL001, REL003, REL004, REL006, REL007, REL008]
- Successors concentrated onto one path. Earlier, outgoing transitions were spread over several successors (A15: A18 about 62 in 100, A19 about 14, A20 and A21 about 12 each; A18: A27 about 53 in 100; A19: A21 about 55 in 100), whereas in the later window every source has essentially one successor — A26→A17, A17→A15, A15→A18, A18→A19, A19→A21, A21→A20, A20→A27 — each taken in about 99–100 in 100 observed transitions [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006, DIR007]
- Step distances became deterministic and reverse orders disappeared. Earlier, gaps spread over roughly two to five steps (A26 to A15: about 26/24/20/31 in 100 at two/three/four/five steps), while in the later window each pair sits at one gap matching the chain above (A26→A15 at two steps in about 99 in 100, A15→A21 at three, A17→A20 at five); reverse-order pairs within five steps nearly vanished, for example A19 before A15 in about 149 traces earlier versus 1 trace later [LAG024, LAG004, LAG009, LAG020, LAG014]
- Relative event positions shifted consistently with the new sequence: A15, A17 and A18 sit slightly earlier in the later window (A18 mean position about 0.68 to 0.62) and A19, A20 and A21 slightly later (A19 about 0.59 to 0.65), so the later mean positions line up with the chain order above [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

Chance cannot account for the evidence as a whole: 50 control-flow items qualified where an unchanged process would produce about 0.47, and every item's difference exceeds about 199 of 200 random re-splits of the pooled traces. The evidence supports the hypothesis that the ordering constraints among A26, A17, A15, A18, A19, A21, A20 and A27 tightened from a mixed order to one fixed sequence: in the earlier window these activities had several alternative successors, mixed or reversed pairwise orders and variable step distances, while in the later window they run as the single chain A26 → A17 → A15 → A18 → A19 → A21 → A20 → A27, with consecutive pairs one step apart, all eight studied pairs in one direction, and every measured gap matching that same chain. This is a change in ordering constraints (a mixed order becoming fixed), not an exchange of positions, and participation stayed at 100% of traces throughout [DIR001, DIR003, DIR007, REL001, REL006, LAG025, LAG029, POS004]

The relational and directional evidence shows this directly and consistently, but the eight REL, seven DIR and roughly two dozen LAG items largely describe one underlying behaviour, so they are not independent confirmations. The evidence cannot rule out that the earlier window's mixed orders reflect a shifting mix of pre-existing fixed-order variants rather than flexible ordering within individual cases, and a few LAG items on reverse pairs rest on a single after-window observation [REL003, LAG014, LAG021]
