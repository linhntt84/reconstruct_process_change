### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the block A14 → A20 → A15, sitting between A36 and A21, changed from optional to executed in nearly every case: in the earlier window about 47 in 100 traces ran A36 → A14 → A20 → A15 → A21 while the rest skipped the block and passed directly from A36 to A21, whereas in the later window about 995 in 1000 traces run the block and the direct A36 → A21 step survives in only about 1 in 200 traces. Supporting signs: the participation of all three block activities rose in step (about 47% to about 99.5%, backed by 94–199 traces per window); relational evidence places A36 before A14, A14 before A20, A20 before A15 and the block before A21 in about 995 in 1000 later traces; A36's successor shifted from A21 (about 53% of outgoing transitions) to A14 (about 99.5%); and the A36-to-A21 distance moved from one step (about 53 in 100 traces) to four steps (about 995 in 1000). The pattern is unlikely to arise from sampling alone: 22 control-flow items qualified against roughly 0.41 expected by chance, and every item's difference exceeds 199 of 200 random re-splits of the pooled data. One competing explanation cannot be ruled out: the direct A36 → A21 route still appears in about 1 in 200 later traces, so the evidence cannot tell whether the skip option was removed or is only rarely taken, nor whether the routing itself changed or only the mix of cases reaching this segment.
