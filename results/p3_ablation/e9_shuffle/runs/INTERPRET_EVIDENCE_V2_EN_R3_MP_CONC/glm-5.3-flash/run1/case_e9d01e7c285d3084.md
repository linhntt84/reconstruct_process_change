### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that A30, A31 and A36 became repeated activities in the later window: each moved from a single execution per trace to about 3.5 occurrences per trace, repeating within a trace in about 68 in 100 cases, while still appearing in every trace. The repetition is measured directly for all three activities with full support in both windows, and the surrounding evidence is consistent with it: every fixed ordering that linked these activities to A14 or A05, or to each other, turned into interleaved/repeated orderings whose share (about 68 in 100) matches the recurrence share, and A36 now leads mostly to A30 instead of A25, consistent with the repeated activities alternating. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, DIR001]

Screening qualifies 14 items against an expectation of fewer than 0.5 without change (all in the control-flow family), and every item's difference exceeds about 199 of 200 random re-splits of the pooled data, so the collection of differences is unlikely for an unchanged process. Limitations: the many REL, LAG and POS items largely mirror the same repetition behaviour rather than independent confirmations; no relational item covers A31, so the evidence cannot verify that it repeats in the same traces or within the same repeated block as A30 and A36; and the rerouting of A36's exits from A25 towards A30 could be an independent routing change occurring alongside the repetition rather than a consequence of it. [screening, DIR001, OCC002]

Other differences: the distance between A05 and A14 lengthened (one-step gaps fell from about 36 to 17 in 100 pairs, three-step gaps rose to about 45 in 100) and A14's relative position shifted slightly earlier with more variability; these may be knock-on effects of the repetition, but the evidence does not tie them directly to it. [LAG001, POS001]
