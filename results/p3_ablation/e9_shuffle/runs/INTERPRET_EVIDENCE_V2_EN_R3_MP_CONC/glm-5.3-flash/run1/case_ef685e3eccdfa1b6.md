### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, A25, A26, A28, A30, A31, A32, A33 and A34 each appeared in essentially every trace (200 of 200). In the later window, A25 and A26 still appear in all traces, but A28, A30 and A31 each appear in only about 47 in 100 traces, and A32, A33 and A34 in about 56 in 100. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006]
- In the earlier window, A28, A30 and A31 always occurred together, A32, A33 and A34 always occurred together, and the first group always ran before the second (for example, A30 preceded A33 in 100% of traces). In the later window, every first-group/second-group pair shares a trace in only about 2 in 100 cases, while within-group co-occurrence is preserved (A28 with A30 or A31 in about 47 in 100 traces; A32 with A33 or A34 in about 56 in 100). [REL020, REL016, REL017, REL013, REL014, REL018, REL025, REL026, REL027]
- Within both groups the order was already unfixed in the earlier window and remains so (for example, A28 before A31 in about 22% of later traces and A31 before A28 in about 24%), so no ordering constraint within the groups changed. [REL013, REL014, REL018, REL025, REL026, REL027]
- A25 continues to precede whichever group executes (A25 before A28 in about 47 in 100 later traces, before A33 in about 56 in 100), and A26 continues to follow it (A28 before A26 in about 47 in 100; A33 before A26 in about 56 in 100). [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL005, REL007, REL011]
- Direct successors changed: in the later window A30, A31 and A28 each lead directly to A26 in roughly a quarter to a third of their outgoing transitions (about 36%, 26% and 34%), edges not observed earlier, while their transitions into the other group (such as A30 to A33 or A31 to A34) nearly disappear; A25 now leads to A32, A33 or A34 in about 54% of its outgoing transitions, which it never did earlier. [DIR003, DIR004, DIR002, DIR001]
- The step distance from A25 to second-group activities and from first-group activities to A26 shrinks from 4–5 steps in essentially all earlier observations to 1–3 steps in about 97 in 100 later observations, consistent with the other group being skipped rather than with an activity being relocated. [LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG011, LAG004, LAG008]
- Relative event positions shift only slightly (A25's mean position from about 0.62 to about 0.67 of the trace, A26's from about 0.83 to about 0.81, the six middle activities converging near 0.74); these are position-share changes consistent with shorter traces, not evidence that an activity was relocated. [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006, POS007, POS008]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the segment between A25 and A26 changed from two sequentially executed blocks into two almost mutually exclusive alternatives: in the later window, about 45 in 100 traces contain only the group A28, A30, A31 (in an unfixed internal order), about 54 in 100 contain only the group A32, A33, A34, and about 2 in 100 still contain both, whereas earlier every trace executed both groups in sequence with A25 always first and A26 always last. In participation terms, one of the two blocks is now skipped in almost every trace. [OCC001, OCC004, REL020, REL014, REL001, REL007, DIR003, LAG001]

The support is direct and consistent: 58 control-flow items qualified against fewer than 1 expected if nothing had changed, so chance sampling cannot explain the ensemble; the participation drops, the collapse of cross-group co-occurrence alongside preserved within-group co-occurrence, the new direct edges from A25 into the second group and from the first group into A26, and the shortened step gaps all describe the same structure. Because the both-groups path was universal earlier and is nearly absent later, this is a participation change (a block now skipped), not a redistribution of shares between pre-existing alternative paths. [screening, OCC001, REL020, DIR001, DIR003, LAG001, POS004]

Limitations: the evidence cannot distinguish a single routing choice between the two groups from two independent skip behaviours (for example, only the second block having become optional), and the residual both-group traces rest on roughly 4 observations per cross-group pair. Within-group order remains unfixed in both windows, and no ordering change is claimed there. [REL020, LAG007]

Other differences: only secondary consequences of the main change — slight relative-position shifts of A25 and A26, and lag redistributions for cross-group pairs that now co-occur in about 2 in 100 traces (about 4 observations each, e.g., A28 with A34, A30 with A33), which should be treated as weak. [POS001, POS002, LAG007, LAG009, LAG010]
