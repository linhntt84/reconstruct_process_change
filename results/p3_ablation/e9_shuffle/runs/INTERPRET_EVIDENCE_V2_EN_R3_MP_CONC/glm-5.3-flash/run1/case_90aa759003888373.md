### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

In the later window, the block A14 → A20 → A15 between A36 and A21 is skipped in roughly 47 in 100 traces: A36 is then followed directly by A21, while in the remaining about 53 in 100 traces the full chain A36 → A14 → A20 → A15 → A21 runs exactly as before. A36 and A21 continue to occur in every trace, and the three block members never occur without each other — the block is no longer executed mandatorily in every case. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, DIR001, LAG001, REL001, REL002, REL005]

The screening produced 15 qualifying control-flow items where only about 0.35 would be expected without a change, so chance sampling cannot explain the pattern as a whole. The routing change is shown directly (a new A36→A21 transition and a distance-1 A36→A21 pair), the participation drop is confined to the block members, their joint skipping is consistent across all pairs, and the order within the block is preserved when it runs. Note that the 15 items largely reflect one underlying trace-level difference — the skipped block — so they are not independent confirmations. [DIR001, LAG001, REL003, REL004, REL006, REL007, REL008, REL009, POS001]

Limitations: the evidence cannot show what distinguishes the skipping traces or whether the block's work is handled by behaviour outside the tested activities, and it cannot distinguish "the block became optional" from "a new direct A36→A21 path was added", which look identical here; the exact 53/47 split also carries sampling uncertainty in 200-trace windows.
