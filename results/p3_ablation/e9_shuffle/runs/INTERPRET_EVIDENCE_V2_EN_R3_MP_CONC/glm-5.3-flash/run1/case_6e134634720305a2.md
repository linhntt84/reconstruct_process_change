### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the repetition cycle through A30, A31 and A36 was largely switched off. Before, these three activities formed a repeating block — each ran about 3–4 times per trace, with A36 returning to A30 in about 70 in 100 of its outgoing transitions, producing the interleaved/repeated A30–A36 pattern; in the later window each runs about once, A36 proceeds to A25 instead, and A30 → A36 became a fixed order, typically two positions apart. The change is shown directly by the routing, relation and lag evidence, it is consistent across all eleven qualified items, and eleven items is far above the roughly 0.5 expected by chance across the 94 tested units, so chance sampling cannot account for the set as a whole [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, DIR002, REL001, LAG001, DIR001]. A limitation: the evidence cannot distinguish a complete removal of the loop from a drastic reduction to the roughly 1–2 in 100 traces that still show repeats, and A31's role in the cycle is inferred from its occurrence pattern matching A30 and A36 together with the stable A30→A31 edge, since no outgoing profile of A31 is included.

Other differences: none beyond manifestations of the same behaviour — the narrowed positional spread of A30 and A36 follows from the repetition stop [POS001, POS002], and two lag items on A36→A30 and A36→A36 point the same way but rest on only one and two observations in the later window, adding little weight on their own [LAG002, LAG003].
