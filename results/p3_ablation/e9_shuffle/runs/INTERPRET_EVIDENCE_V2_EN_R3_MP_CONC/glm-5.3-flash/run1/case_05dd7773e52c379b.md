### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the segment between A25 and A26 changed from an exclusive choice to a full sequence: in the earlier window each case executed either the trio A28–A30–A31 (about 49 in 100 cases) or the trio A32–A33–A34 (about 51 in 100), never both, whereas in the later window both trios run in about 98–100 in 100 cases — first A28/A30/A31, then A32/A33/A34, with no fixed order inside each trio and A25 and A26 still bracketing the segment. [REL015, REL019, REL013, REL025, OCC001, OCC004]

The support is direct and consistent: the relational items show the change itself (cross-trio co-occurrence from 0 to about 98 in 100, fixed first-to-second order, A25 now leading into the first trio and the first trio rarely into A26), the participation items show all six activities becoming near-mandatory, and the distance, position and successor items all match one trios-inserted-before-the-other reading. The 51 qualifying items are many times the roughly 0.59 expected by chance among the 118 tested units, and every item's difference exceeds about 995 in 1000 random re-splits, so sampling variation cannot explain the pattern as a whole (representative items [OCC001, REL015, LAG003, DIR001]). An important competing explanation remains: from these window-level aggregates one cannot tell whether the earlier either-or pattern was a per-case routing choice or a mixture of two case variants; also, direction shares cover only observed transitions, so an unobserved edge such as A25 to A33 in the later window is not proof that the edge was removed.
