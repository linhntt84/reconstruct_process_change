### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis of a reordering between the group A35, A36, A41 and the activities A26, A27: in the earlier window the group ran before A26 and A27 (entered from A25 and leading to A27, with A26 leading directly to A30), whereas in the later window A26 and A27 run before the group, A25 leads directly to A27, A26 leads into the group, and the group leads to A30. This rests on direct relational evidence — all six activity pairs flip from one fixed order to the other with about 200 traces per window and unchanged co-occurrence [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006] — and is consistent with the position shifts [POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006], the successor changes [DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006, DIR007] and the lag changes [LAG005, LAG009]. With 33 qualifying items against roughly 0.76 expected without change, the evidence set as a whole is not what an unchanged process typically produces. A competing explanation the evidence cannot fully rule out is that the two windows sample two coexisting orderings with different frequencies rather than a single modified sequence; several lag items rest on very few observations (2 in the later window) and were not relied upon.

Other differences: A06 is executed in fewer traces (about 27 → 12.5 of 100) and its outgoing transitions spread across more targets, while A02 appears in more traces (about 4.5 → 11.5 of 100); these participation changes are not part of the main hypothesis. [OCC002, OCC001, DIR001, POS001]
