### Observed differences

- The choice after A20 flipped between two alternative activity groups. In the earlier window, about 82 of A20's outgoing transitions per 100 led to A24 (29%), A26 (29%) or A25 (24%), and only about 19 per 100 to A21/A22/A23; in the later window, about 79 per 100 led to A23 (29%), A21 (29%) or A22 (22%), and about 21 per 100 to A24/A25/A26. [DIR003]
- Trace participation moved with the same flip: A21, A22 and A23 ran in about 19 of 100 traces before versus about 79 of 100 after; A24, A25 and A26 fell from about 82 of 100 to about 21 of 100 (each estimate backed by 37–164 traces per window). [OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, OCC007]
- Order relations mirror this: A20 precedes A21/A22/A23 in about 77–79% of later traces (about 18% earlier) and precedes A24/A25/A26 in about 21% (about 81% earlier); the two trios never share a trace in either window, and within each trio the members always occur together. [REL009, REL010, REL011, REL012, REL013, REL014, REL015, REL017, REL023, REL036]
- Downstream activities switched partners: A30, present in essentially every trace in both windows, co-occurred with A24/A25/A26 in about 82% of earlier traces and now co-occurs with A21/A22/A23 in about 79%; A32 and A33 moved from about 52% co-occurrence with the old group to about 55% with the new one. [REL020, REL033, REL038, REL045, REL021, REL027, REL039, REL040]
- A10 became rarer and earlier: it appears in about 16.5% of traces before versus 7% after, its median relative position moved from 0.86 to 0.36, and its outgoing transitions spread from ~97% to A33 to a mix led by A15 (~21%); the later profile rests on only 14 transitions. [POS001, DIR002]
- Smaller shifts: A32's outgoing traffic moved from A10 (about 20% → 1%) to A33 (about 79% → 93%), and A02 participation rose from about 2% to 9% with a broader successor set, though only 4 and 18 observations back A02. [DIR004, OCC001, DIR001]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the share of traces taking each of two existing alternative activity groups after A20 flipped: in the earlier window roughly 4 in 5 traces ran A24, A25 and A26 after A20 (with A21, A22 and A23 in the remainder), while in the later window roughly 4 in 5 traces run A21, A22 and A23 (with A24, A25 and A26 in the remainder). A20 and A30 run in essentially every trace in both windows; A30 now co-occurs with the new group (about 79% of traces) instead of the old one (about 82%). [DIR003, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, OCC007, REL012, REL020, REL038]

The support is direct and consistent rather than merely voluminous: the routing change at A20 is observed with full support in both windows, the precedence relations reverse exactly as the participation figures predict, and the screening produced 60 qualifying control-flow items where about 0.9 would be expected without a change, with every item's difference exceeding about 99.5% of random re-splits, so chance cannot account for the pattern as a whole. [DIR003, REL009, REL012, OCC002]

Limitations: both groups remain in use in the later window (about 21% for A24–A26), so this is a shift between existing alternatives rather than an addition or removal, and many items describe the same underlying flip rather than independent confirmations. The evidence cannot show whether the flip stems from a modified routing condition or from a changed mix of cases, and it offers no information about causes. [OCC005, OCC007, REL017]

- A32's outgoing transitions moved from A10 (about 20% → 1%) to A33 (about 79% → 93%). [DIR004]
