### Observed differences

- In the later window, A01 appears where it was previously absent: it occurred in none of the 200 earlier traces but in about 59 of 200 later traces (about 30 in 100), averaging roughly two occurrences per trace that contains it, repeating within a trace in about 14 in 100 traces, and sitting around 40% of the way through the trace (median relative position about 0.41). [OCC001, POS001]
- Routing around A12 changed completely: in the earlier window A12's observed outgoing transitions went to A04 in about 52 of 100 cases and to A13 in about 48 of 100; in the later window essentially all 131 observed outgoing transitions from A12 go to A01. A01's own outgoing transitions in the later window split between A04 (about 53 in 100) and A13 (about 47 in 100) — almost exactly the split A12 showed earlier; A01 had no observed outgoing transitions in the earlier window because it was not observed at all. [DIR002, DIR001]
- The step distance from A12 to A04 and to A13 moved about one step farther: earlier these pairs were mostly 1–2 steps apart (roughly 12–14 in 100 traces at lag 1 and lag 2 each), later they sit at 2–3 steps (about 21–24 in 100 at lags 2 and 3), with 1-step occurrences disappearing. [LAG002, LAG004]
- The relations changed accordingly: A12 and A01 never co-occurred earlier (A12 appeared without A01 in about 33 of 100 traces), while later they co-occur in about 30 of 100 traces (A12 first in about 16, interleaved or repeated in about 14) and A12 no longer appears without A01. A01 co-occurs with A04 and with A13 in about 30 of 100 later traces each — always interleaved or repeated rather than in one fixed order — where earlier they never co-occurred. [REL002, REL001, REL003]
- Distances between repeated occurrences of the same activity also shifted about one step farther: A12→A12 moved from 4 steps (about 46 in 100) to 5 steps (about 49 in 100), and A04→A04 and A13→A13 moved from lags 3–5 to lags 4–5 (for example, A13→A13 at lag 5 rose from about 10 to about 25 in 100). [LAG003, LAG001, LAG005]
- Participation of the pre-existing activities barely moved: A04 and A13 each appear in about half the traces in both windows, and A12 in about 33 of 100 earlier versus 30 of 100 later traces. [REL001, REL002, REL003]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that A01, not observed in the earlier window, now takes part in the flow as a step between A12 and its successors A04 and A13. Earlier, A12 led directly to A04 (about 52 of 100 transitions) or A13 (about 48 of 100); in the later window A12 leads to A01 in essentially all of its observed transitions, and A01 in turn leads to A04 (about 53 in 100) or A13 (about 47 in 100), preserving the earlier branch split. The distance from A12 to A04/A13 grew by about one step, and the distances between repeated occurrences of A12, A04 and A13 each shifted about one step farther — the pattern expected when one extra step sits on the traversed path. Since participation of A04, A13 and A12 stayed roughly the same, this is not existing paths being taken with different frequencies but a new step appearing on them. Twelve items qualified in screening against roughly 0.17 expected if nothing had changed, and the successor, distance, relation and occurrence items all describe this same behaviour. [DIR002, DIR001, LAG002, LAG004, LAG001, LAG003, LAG005, REL001, REL002, REL003, OCC001]

Limitations: zero participation in the earlier window only shows that A01 was not observed then, so the evidence cannot distinguish a newly added activity from an existing one that was previously not executed or too rare to appear in 200 traces. The co-occurrences of A01 with A04 and A13 are all interleaved or repeated rather than in one fixed order, and A01 itself repeats within about 14 of 100 later traces, so the evidence does not pin down a single fixed placement beyond the observed successor and distance relations. [OCC001, REL001, REL003]

Other differences: none stand apart from this pattern; the shifts in repetition distances and A01's repeated occurrences fit the same behavioural change rather than forming independent differences. [LAG001, LAG003, LAG005, OCC001]
