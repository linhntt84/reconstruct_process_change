### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the control flow around A02 was restructured into a configuration centred on A03: A02, which earlier ran in every trace and repeated in about 59 in 100 of them, is no longer observed, while A03 now runs exactly once per trace; A05 hands over to A03 instead of A02, A03 routes onward to A06 or A07 much as A02 did, and the repetition that earlier alternated between A02 and A06 (A02→A06→A02) is now carried by A06 repeating itself before the trace closes with A07. [OCC001, OCC002, REL001, DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, LAG003]

The screening found 21 qualifying control-flow items against roughly 0.12 expected for an unchanged process, so the volume of signals cannot be explained by random re-splits. The hypothesis rests on direct transition-level evidence (A05→A02 replaced by A05→A03; A06→A02 replaced by A06→A06 and A06→A07), an all-or-nothing participation swap across 200-trace windows, and consistent relational and distance evidence (A05 always before A03, A03 before A07 in about 99 in 100 traces, A06 self-repeats now consecutive and A06→A07 mostly one step apart). [DIR003, DIR004, REL005, REL007, LAG003, LAG004, screening]

Limitations: zero participation shows only that A02 was not observed in the later sample, and several items describe the same underlying traces rather than independent confirmations. An important competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that A02 still executes at a very low rate outside these 200 sampled traces, which would make this a drastic routing shift away from A02 rather than its complete disappearance. [OCC001]

Other differences: the A05→A07 distance, earlier concentrated at two and four steps, now spreads across one to five steps, and the A05→A06 and A06→A07 gaps shifted toward smaller lags. These fit the restructure as downstream effects but are event-position observations, not clock-time shifts. [LAG001, LAG002, LAG004]
