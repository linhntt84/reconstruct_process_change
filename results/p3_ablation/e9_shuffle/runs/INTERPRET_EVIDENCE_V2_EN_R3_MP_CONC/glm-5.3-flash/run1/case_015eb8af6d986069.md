### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the ordering constraints in the mid-process segment changed: A31, A22, A20, A23, A24, A26, A25 and A32, previously executed in a variable order, are now executed as the fixed chain A31 → A22 → A20 → A23 → A24 → A26 → A25 → A32. The support is direct and consistent: the pairs still co-occur in 100 of 100 traces in both windows, so participation is unchanged and only the order shifted (A24 before A20 fell from 73 to 0.5 of 100 while A20 before A24 rose to 98.5); lag distributions collapsed from spreads to single distances; every successor profile along the chain converged to one dominant target; and the position shifts agree with the new sequence. With 82 qualifying items against roughly 1 expected by chance, this volume of flagged behaviour cannot be explained by random re-sampling, though several items describe facets of the same shift rather than independent confirmations. [REL019, REL021, REL014, REL016, LAG017, LAG012, LAG022, LAG029, DIR013, DIR008, DIR007, DIR009, DIR010, DIR012, DIR011]

The evidence cannot rule out that only the mix of pre-existing trace variants shifted rather than the ordering rule itself, since the "new" order already occurred in a minority of earlier traces (A23 before A26 in 30 of 100); the lag and position indicators also use event positions, so no claim about clock timing is possible. [REL021, LAG017]

Other differences: A08 became far less frequent and later-positioned with diversified successors — a participation and placement change outside the main hypothesis; A10 occurs in more traces (7.5→22 of 100) and A02/A04 show largely new successor profiles, with A14, A45 and A47 gaining small new branches; these latter observations rest on roughly 15–50 observed transitions each and could partly reflect sampling variation between two finite windows.
