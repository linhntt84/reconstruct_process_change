### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, A30, A31 and A36 each ran exactly once in essentially every trace (mean about 1.0 occurrence per trace, repeats in fewer than 1 in 100 traces, participation 100%). In the later window, each of the three runs about 3.5 times per trace, with repeats in about 68 in 100 traces, while participation stays at 100%. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003]
- A36's exits were rerouted: before, about 98% of its outgoing transitions led to A25; in the later window about 71% lead back to A30 and only about 28% to A25. [DIR005]
- The adjacency "A36 followed by A30 within five steps" was seen in only 1 trace before and in 136 of 200 traces in the later window. [LAG002]
- The strict one-pass ordering dissolved: A30 preceded A36 in 98.5% of traces before versus 31.5% after, with interleaved/repeated executions rising from 1.5% to 68.5%; the same shift holds for A14 with A30 (99% to 31.5%) and A14 with A36 (99.5% to 32%). [REL005, REL003, REL004]
- The spacing between A30 and A36, previously almost always 2 positions apart (97% of pairs), spread out: only about 12% of pairs remain at lag 2 and about 5% sit at lag 5. [LAG001]
- A30 and A36 keep roughly their median positions (about 0.40 and 0.48 of the trace) but their spread roughly doubles (std about 0.06 to 0.12); A14 also becomes more spread and slightly earlier. [POS003, POS004, POS002]
- The chain edges A30 → A31 and A31 → A36 remained dominant in both windows (about 97.5% before, 98.7% after). [DIR003, DIR004]
- At A05, exit shares shifted: transitions to A14 fell from about 35% to 18% while transitions to A07 rose from about 19% to 39%; A05 also co-occurs with A30/A36 in a fixed order less often (about 54% to 17% of traces). [DIR001, REL001, REL002]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that a repetition cycle was introduced around the A30 → A31 → A36 sequence: in the later window, A36 typically leads back to A30 instead of proceeding to A25, so the sequence A30 → A31 → A36 repeats within the case — A30, A31 and A36 rise from one execution per trace to about 3.5, with repeats in roughly 68 in 100 traces — and the previously fixed one-pass order between A14 and A30/A36 gives way to interleaved, repeated executions. [DIR005, LAG002, OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, REL005, REL003, REL004]

The support is direct and consistent: the rerouting at A36 and the appearance of the A36 → A30 adjacency show the cycle edge itself; the near-identical occurrence increases for all three activities, the order dissolution at exactly the ~0.68 repetition share, and the spread-out A30–A36 spacing all match one underlying difference, while the chain edges A30 → A31 and A31 → A36 stay intact; the screening produced 19 qualifying items against about 0.5 expected for an unchanged process, far beyond what chance produces. [DIR005, LAG002, OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, REL005, REL003, REL004, LAG001, DIR003, DIR004]

Limitations: the evidence shows the cycle's internal edges but not directly how the repetition is entered or how the exit to A25 (still about 28% of A36's exits) is chosen, and about 32 in 100 later traces still show no repeats, so the evidence cannot establish whether every trace loops; the before-window absence of the A36 → A30 pair rests on a single observation, so the "new edge" reading leans mainly on the rerouting at A36. [DIR005, LAG002]

Other differences: at A05, the choice of successor shifted towards A07 and away from A14, and A05 occurs slightly earlier in traces; this routing shift is not part of the repetition hypothesis. [DIR001, POS001]
