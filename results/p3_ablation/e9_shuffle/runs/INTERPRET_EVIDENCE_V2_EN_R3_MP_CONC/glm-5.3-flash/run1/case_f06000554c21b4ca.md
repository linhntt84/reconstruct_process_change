### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

In the later window, the block of three activities A35, A36 and A41 runs earlier in the trace: it previously executed after A26 and A27 — entered from A26 and closing into A30 — and now runs directly after A25 and before A27, closing into A27, while A30, which previously concluded that block, now immediately follows A26 near the end of the trace. The internal sequence of the trio remains variable in both windows (each member leads to the other two in similar shares), and all six activities still occur in every trace, so this reads as a relocation of an existing block rather than activities being added, removed or made optional. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005]

The supporting signs are direct and consistent: relational evidence shows the order reversal for all six activity pairs, each backed by about 200 traces in both windows; the position shifts, the successor changes at A25, A26 and the trio, and the two fully supported distance changes all point to the same placement; and 26 qualifying items against about 0.63 expected without change means chance sampling cannot account for the pattern as a whole. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, LAG001, LAG003, POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005]

Limitations: the evidence cannot fully separate a changed ordering constraint from a complete replacement of the executed variant by one with the reversed order, since the reversal is near-total in the later window; eight lag items rest on only 1–2 observations in one window and were not used to carry the hypothesis; and positions are event positions rather than clock times, though the relational and successor evidence shows the movement directly.
