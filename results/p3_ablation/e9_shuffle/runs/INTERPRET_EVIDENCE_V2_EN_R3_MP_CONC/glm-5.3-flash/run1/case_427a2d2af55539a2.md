### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the segment between A39 and A24 was restructured: the earlier behaviour, in which exactly one of three mutually exclusive activities (A14, A15 or A16) executed between A39 and A24 in each case, gave way in the later window to a behaviour in which three activities (A21, A22 and A23) each execute once in every case, in no fixed order, between A39 and A24. This is a structural change — activities left the observed behaviour and others entered it, and exclusive alternatives became an always-executed block without a fixed ordering constraint — not a shift in how often existing paths are taken. The change is shown directly and consistently across indicator families: appearance and disappearance with full-trace support [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006], the successor change at A39 [DIR007], relational evidence placing A39 before and A24 after the new block in all cases [REL021, REL022, REL024, REL025, REL026, REL027], and the A39-to-A24 distance growing from 2 to 4 steps [LAG001]. With 48 qualifying items against roughly 0.5 expected if nothing had changed, chance cannot explain the pattern as a whole, and every item's difference exceeds about 995 of 1000 random re-splits; note, however, that the many relation items largely restate this one underlying structural change rather than confirming it independently.

What the evidence cannot rule out: zero participation only means an activity was not observed, so A14, A15 and A16 may still exist in the process but were not exercised in the later window (and A21, A22 and A23 may have existed earlier without being exercised); the data also cannot distinguish a single replacement from two coincident changes, one removing the old alternatives and one adding the new block. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006]

Other differences: none that stand apart from this change; the slightly later position of A24 follows from three activities now preceding it instead of one. [POS007, LAG001]
