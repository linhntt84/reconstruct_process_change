### Observed differences

- The direct successors of the three recurring activities flipped. In the earlier window, all ~190 observed executions of A01 were followed by A06, and all ~90 observed executions of A04 were followed by A01; in the later window, all 77 observed A01 executions are followed by A04, and all ~180 observed A04 executions are followed by A06. A06's outgoing transitions moved from a split of about 54 in 100 to A05 and 46 to A04, to about 57 to A05 and 43 to A01 — the exit to A05 kept roughly the same share while the A04 branch became an A01 branch. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003]

- Participation moved in opposite directions for A01 and A04. A01 appeared in about 52 in 100 traces before versus about 20 in 100 in the later window (mean occurrences per trace 0.96 vs 0.39), while A04 rose from about 24 to about 52 in 100 traces (0.45 vs 0.90 occurrences per trace). A06 stayed stable, at about 52 in 100 traces and roughly 190 occurrence events in both windows. [OCC001, OCC002, DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, REL002, REL003]

- The pairwise relations follow the same pattern. Before, about 28 in 100 traces contained A01 and A06 without A04, and A01 preceded A06 in about 28; in the later window, about 32 in 100 traces contain A04 and A06 without A01, with A04 preceding A06 in about 32. A04–A06 co-occurrence rose from about 24 to about 52 in 100 traces while A01–A06 co-occurrence fell from about 52 to about 20; A01 and A04 still co-occur in about 20–24 in 100 traces, but the solo member flipped from A01 (about 28 in 100 before) to A04 (about 32 in 100 after). [REL001, REL002, REL003]

- Typical gaps flipped by one event position for every ordered pair among the three activities: pairs that were adjacent before (A01→A06, A04→A01, A06→A04, each at gap 1) now sit two positions apart, while pairs two positions apart before (A01→A04, A04→A06, A06→A01) are now adjacent; the small components at gaps of 4–5 in both windows fit repeated passes of the same sequence. These lag shares rest on roughly 40–104 paired observations per window. [LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006]

- A04 now tends to appear earlier in the trace (median position about 0.33 before vs 0 in the later window) and A01 later (median 0 before vs about 0.33). [POS001, POS002]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the fixed order of the repeating sequence through A01, A06 and A04 reversed between the windows: in the earlier window it ran A01 → A06 → A04 and closed back to A01, with an exit from A06 to A05; in the later window it runs A01 → A04 → A06 and closes back to A01, with the same exit from A06 to A05 — in effect, A04 and A06 exchanged their places in the cycle. The change is shown directly by the successor profiles of all three activities, and it is consistent across every indicator family: A04 now precedes A06 in about 32 in 100 traces where previously A01 preceded A06, all six pairwise gaps flip between one and two positions exactly as the exchange predicts, and A04's typical position moved earlier while A01's moved later. Chance is an unlikely explanation for the pattern as a whole: 16 items qualified where roughly 0.1 would be expected without a change, and every item's difference exceeded about 995 of 1,000 random re-splits. [OCC001, OCC002, DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, REL001, REL002, REL003, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006, POS001, POS002]

An accompanying participation shift belongs to the same picture: A01 now appears in far fewer traces (about 20 vs 52 in 100) and A04 in many more (about 52 vs 24), with A06 stable. This is what one would expect if traces now enter the sequence at A04, so that A01 appears only when a pass runs the full cycle to its close, but the entry point itself is not observed, so the shift could equally be an independent change in how often this segment is executed. [OCC001, OCC002, REL001, REL002, REL003]

Two limitations remain: POS and LAG are based on event positions within traces rather than clock time, so "earlier" and "later" refer to trace positions only, and some lag profiles rest on about 40 paired observations per window; the evidence also cannot rule out that the participation change is a separate routing change that coincides with, rather than follows from, the reordered sequence. [LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006, POS001, POS002, OCC001, OCC002]
