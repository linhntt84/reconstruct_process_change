### Observed differences

- The dominant difference is a swap in how often two alternative activity blocks run. In the earlier window, A21, A22 and A23 executed together in about 75 in 100 traces, while A24, A25 and A26 executed together in about 25 in 100; in the later window the shares reversed (about 25 versus about 76 in 100). The two blocks never share a trace in either window (cross-block cooccurrence is essentially 0), confirming they are alternatives. [OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, OCC007, OCC008, REL013, REL015, REL022, REL029, REL034, REL039]
- A20 and A30 appear in every trace in both windows, but their surroundings flipped. A20's outgoing transitions went from mostly A21 (26%), A23 (25%) and A22 (23%) to mostly A26 (28%), A24 (25%) and A25 (22%). A30 co-occurred with A21 in about 75 in 100 traces before but only about 25 in 100 after, and with A24 about 26 in 100 before versus about 76 in 100 after. [DIR003, REL007, REL011, REL018, REL031, REL036, REL040, REL043]
- A32 ran in a stable share of traces (about 64 in 100 before, 67 in 100 after) but changed company: cooccurrence with A21/A22/A23 fell from about 48 to about 15 in 100 traces, while cooccurrence with A24/A25/A26 rose from about 17 to about 52 in 100. A32 still leads mostly to A33 in both windows, with A32→A10 growing from about 1% to about 20% of its outgoing transitions. [REL019, REL026, REL037, REL041, DIR004]
- Within-pair step distances shifted: A20→A24 within one step fell from 54% to 33% of observations while 2–3 step gaps grew, and A32→A33 within one step fell from 56% to 39% while two-step gaps rose from 3% to 14%. These are event-position distances, not clock times. [LAG001, LAG003]
- A10 became more frequent (about 6 in 100 traces before, 21 in 100 after), appears later in traces (median position 0.60 → 0.86) and its outgoing transitions now concentrate on A33 (about 90% after versus 18% before). A02 nearly disappeared (about 9 in 100 traces before, 1 in 100 after). [OCC002, POS001, DIR002, OCC001]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the relative frequency of two alternative paths flipped: in the earlier window roughly 75 in 100 cases took the branch containing A21, A22 and A23 (which always executed together, after A20), and roughly 25 in 100 took the alternative branch containing A24, A25 and A26; in the later window the shares were reversed at about 25 versus 75 in 100. Every activity involved is observed in a substantial share of traces in both windows, and only the shares between the alternatives shifted, matching the pattern of existing paths being taken with different frequencies rather than activities being added, removed or bypassed. [OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, OCC007, OCC008, REL013, REL015, REL022, REL029, REL034, REL039, DIR003]

Supporting signs: 62 items qualified against fewer than 1 expected by chance for the 183 tested units, so chance cannot account for the item count as a whole, and every item's difference exceeds about 99.5 in 100 random re-splits of the pooled data. The participation changes, the mutual exclusivity with flipped shares, and A20's successor shift all describe the same flip consistently, though they are overlapping views of one underlying behaviour rather than independent confirmations; A30 and A32 flipped their association from the A21–A23 block to the A24–A26 block in the same direction. The evidence cannot locate the change within the window boundary, and it cannot separate a change in routing from a shift in the mix of cases entering the process, which is the main competing explanation. [REL007, REL018, REL019, REL036, REL037, DIR003]

Other differences: A10 participation roughly tripled (about 6 in 100 → 21 in 100 traces), it appears later in traces (median position 0.60 → 0.86) and its outgoing transitions now go almost exclusively to A33 (about 90% versus 18% before), though this rests on modest support (11 versus 42 outgoing transitions). A02 fell to near-absence (about 9 in 100 → 1 in 100 traces) and its outgoing profile changed completely, but with only two after-traces behind it this is too weak to interpret. The growth in 2–3 step gaps for pairs such as A20→A24 and A32→A33 is consistent with the branch flip but is a positional observation, not evidence of clock-time movement. [OCC002, POS001, DIR002, OCC001, DIR001, LAG001, LAG003]
