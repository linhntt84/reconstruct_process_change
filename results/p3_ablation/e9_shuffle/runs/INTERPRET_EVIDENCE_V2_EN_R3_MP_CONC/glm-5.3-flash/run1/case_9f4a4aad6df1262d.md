### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The 49 qualifying control-flow items are far above the roughly 0.66 expected by chance among the 132 tested units, so the pattern as a whole is not what an unchanged process typically produces. The evidence supports the hypothesis that the fixed sequential ordering of the block A22, A20, A23, A24, A26, A25 between A31 and A32 was relaxed in the later window: A24, which previously ran after A20 and A23, now typically executes earlier — often directly after A31 or A22 and before A20 and A23 — while A23, which previously preceded A24, A26 and A25, now typically executes later, near A32; pairs such as A23/A25, A20/A26 and A22/A24 lost their fixed order and now occur in both orders. The order reversals show this directly, the opposite position shifts of A24 (earlier) and A23 and A20 (later) agree with it, and the redistributed successors of A31, A23 and A25 and the spreading of the fixed distances fit the same picture. Participation itself did not change — every block activity still occurs in every trace in both windows — so this is a change in ordering constraints, not activities being added, removed or taken with different frequencies. Other differences: none beyond this reordering; the remaining direction and lag items involve the same activities and match the same pattern. [DIR006, LAG013, LAG018, LAG005]

Limitations: the items largely describe one underlying reordering rather than independent changes, and the evidence cannot distinguish a genuinely flexible order from a new fixed sequence with A23 and A24 exchanged, because some pairs keep a clearly dominant order in the later window (A24 before A23 in about 83 in 100 traces); a mixture of traces keeping the old order and traces following a new order is also compatible with the mixed shares.
