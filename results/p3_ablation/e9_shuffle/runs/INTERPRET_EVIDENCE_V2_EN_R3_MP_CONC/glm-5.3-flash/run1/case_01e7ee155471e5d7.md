### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a placement change: the block A30–A31–A36, executed in varying internal order, and the segment containing A22 and A21 exchanged their relative order, and A25 changed its attachment. In the earlier window the flow ran A20 → A22 → … → A21 → {A30/A31/A36} → A25; in the later window it runs A20 → {A30/A31/A36} → A22 → … → A21 → A25, with A25 now immediately following A21 instead of closing the group. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, LAG001, LAG002, POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005]

Supporting signs: the order reversal is shown directly by six activity-pair relations that flip from 100% one way to about 995 in 1000 the other way, and it is consistently corroborated by the successor changes, the step-distance changes and the position shifts; every involved activity occurs in essentially all traces in both windows, so this is not a participation or path-frequency change; and 24 control-flow items qualified against roughly 0.41 expected by chance, far beyond what an unchanged process typically produces. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, LAG001, LAG002, POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005]

Limitations: the relation between A22 and A21 itself is not observed and A22 has no outgoing profile in the evidence, so the wiring of the middle segment is inferred from positions; six LAG items rest on a single later-window observation each, though the relations they reflect are independently shown by REL and DIR. A competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is two independent reroutings (A20 into the group, the group into A22, and A21 into A25) rather than one block relocation, since both would produce the same observable order.
