### Observed differences

- The order of a group of six activities — A20, A22, A23, A24, A25 and A26, each present in essentially every trace in both windows — changed from unfixed to fixed. In the earlier window many pairs occurred in both orders (for example, A23 came before A24 in only about 12 in 100 traces, A20 came before A24 in about 25 in 100, and A22/A24 were split roughly 50/50). In the later window, every one of these pairs runs in a single order in about 98–99 in 100 traces. [REL007, REL002, REL005, REL004, REL008, REL009, REL003, REL006]
- Direct transitions were concentrated accordingly. In the earlier window each of these activities had several frequent successors (after A23, the next activity was A32 in about 46 in 100 transitions, A26 in 18, A25 in 16, A24 in 12); in the later window each has essentially one successor, forming the chain A31 → A22 → A20 → A23 → A24 → A26 → A25 → A32, with each link taken in about 92–100 in 100 transitions. [DIR005, DIR003, DIR004, DIR006, DIR008, DIR007, DIR009]
- Step distances now match that chain: A31 reaches A23 in exactly 3 steps in all observed cases (previously spread over 3–5 steps), A20 reaches A25 in 4 steps (previously 1–4), and A23 reaches A32 in 4 steps (previously usually 1). Pairs running against the chain, such as A24 followed by A23 or A25 followed by A23, dropped to a single observation in the later window. [LAG028, LAG005, LAG015, LAG017, LAG021]
- Positions within the trace shifted consistently with the reordering: A22, A20 and A23 now occur slightly earlier (A23's mean position fell from about 0.68 to 0.62), while A24, A26 and A25 occur slightly later (A24's mean rose from about 0.59 to 0.65). [POS003, POS002, POS004, POS005, POS007, POS006]
- Outside this block, A08 became much rarer (present in about 32 in 100 traces earlier, about 13 in 100 later), occurs later in the trace, and its outgoing transitions became more varied (the A15 share fell from about 91 in 100 to 48); A10 became more frequent (about 3 in 100 traces earlier, 12–13 in 100 later). Both rest on modest counts (64 vs 26 and 6 vs 25 traces). [OCC001, POS001, DIR001, REL001, OCC002]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the block of activities A22, A20, A23, A24, A26 and A25 — entered from A31 and closing into A32 — changed from being executed without a fixed order between them to a single fixed sequence, A31 → A22 → A20 → A23 → A24 → A26 → A25 → A32. The relational evidence shows this directly: every tested pair moved from occurring in both orders to one order in about 98–100 in 100 traces (for example, A23 before A24 rose from about 12 to 98 in 100; A20 before A24 from about 25 to 99 in 100). [REL007, REL002, REL005, REL004, REL008, REL009, REL003, REL006]

The hypothesis is corroborated by consistent non-relational signs: each block activity's outgoing transitions collapsed to essentially one successor, all measured step distances fit the same chain, and the position shifts run in the directions the new sequence predicts. Screening found 58 qualifying items in the control-flow family against fewer than 1 expected if nothing had changed, so this ensemble is not what random re-sampling of a stable process produces. [DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006, DIR007, DIR008, DIR009, LAG028, LAG005, LAG011, LAG015, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006, POS007]

An important competing explanation the evidence cannot fully rule out: the earlier window's mixed orders could reflect either free ordering within each case or a mixture of several fixed variants that was replaced wholesale — the earlier successor profiles show several substantial next activities per block activity (for example, A22 led to A20 in about 55 in 100 transitions but also to A24 in 24 and A26 in 14), which is compatible with both readings. A few lag items also rest on a single after-window observation, though the hypothesis does not depend on them. [DIR004, DIR005, DIR006, LAG017, LAG021]

Other differences: the decline of A08 (fewer traces, later position, more varied successors) and the rise of A10 are separate from the reordering and rest on small later-window counts (26 and 25 traces respectively). [OCC001, POS001, DIR001, REL001, OCC002]
