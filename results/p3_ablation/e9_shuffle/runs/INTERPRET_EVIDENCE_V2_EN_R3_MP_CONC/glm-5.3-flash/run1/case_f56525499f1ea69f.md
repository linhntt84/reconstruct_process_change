### Observed differences

- In the later window, six activities — A33, A35, A36, A37, A38 and A39 — each run in about 98–99 in 100 traces; in the earlier window each ran in only about 49–52 in 100 [OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, OCC007, OCC008, OCC009].
- In the earlier window these six formed two mutually exclusive triples: {A33, A35, A36} ran together in about 49 in 100 traces and {A37, A38, A39} in about 52 in 100, and essentially every trace contained exactly one of the two triples. In the later window all six co-occur in about 98 in 100 traces [REL037, REL039, REL042, REL046, REL049, REL051].
- The order across the two triples is now fixed — each of A33, A35, A36 precedes each of A37, A38, A39 in about 97–98 in 100 later traces — while the order inside each triple stays mixed, with both orders in roughly equal shares in both windows [REL039, REL041, REL043, REL046, REL037, REL038, REL050].
- The block sits between A30 and A31: earlier, A30 preceded the running triple and the triple preceded A31 whenever one ran (about half of all traces); in the later window these relations hold in about 97–99 in 100 traces [REL025, REL028, REL030, REL031, REL034, REL036]. Successor profiles agree: A30 now leads mainly into A35, A33 and A36 (about 30–37% of its outgoing transitions each, versus a spread over all six before), and A33, A35 and A36 now lead onward to A37, A38 and A39 and almost never directly to A31 [DIR006, DIR007, DIR008, DIR009]. The span widened: distances from A30 to A37/A38/A39 and from A33/A35/A36 to A31 moved from mostly 1–3 steps to 4–5 steps, A30's mean relative position moved earlier (about 0.67 to 0.62) and A31's later (about 0.80 to 0.82) [LAG003, LAG005, LAG006, LAG008, POS004, POS005].
- A14 precedes all six in about 98 in 100 later traces (about 50 in 100 before); A15 and A16 now co-occur with the block almost always, preceding it in about 6 in 10 traces and being interleaved with it in about 4 in 10 [REL007, REL012, REL013, REL019, REL021].
- A04 became rarer and less tied to A16: participation fell from about 31 to about 14 in 100 traces, its dominant transition to A16 dropped from about 81% to 36% of its outgoing transitions, and the one-step A04→A16 distance became less common (only 15 later observations) [OCC001, DIR002, LAG001].
- Two rare activities became somewhat more frequent: A06 from about 3 to 11 in 100 traces and A10 from about 3 to 12 in 100, each based on fewer than 25 traces per window [OCC002, OCC003].

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the two triples {A33, A35, A36} and {A37, A38, A39} stopped being mutually exclusive alternatives and became jointly executed, mandatory blocks in a fixed sequence A30 → {A33, A35, A36} → {A37, A38, A39} → A31, with the order inside each triple still unfixed, A14 preceding the block and A15/A16 preceding or interleaved with it: participation of all six activities rose from about half of traces to nearly all, pairwise co-occurrence between the triples went from never to about 98 in 100 with near-full support in both windows, and the successor and distance indicators show the same placement directly [OCC004, OCC009, REL039, REL046, REL025, REL031, DIR006, DIR007, LAG003]. That this is not a sampling artefact is supported by the screening comparison — 89 control-flow items qualified where about 1 would be expected without change — and by the agreement across participation, co-occurrence, direction and distance indicators [REL039, OCC004]. The hypothesis does not cover the roughly 1–2 in 100 later traces that still run only one triple, and it cannot rule out that the two windows sample different case mixes (for instance a variant running both triples becoming dominant) rather than a modification of the same process [REL039, REL046].

Other differences: A04 participation fell from about 31 to about 14 in 100 traces and its dominant link to A16 loosened [OCC001, DIR002, LAG001]; the rare activities A06 and A10 became more frequent, though on small numbers of traces [OCC002, OCC003].
