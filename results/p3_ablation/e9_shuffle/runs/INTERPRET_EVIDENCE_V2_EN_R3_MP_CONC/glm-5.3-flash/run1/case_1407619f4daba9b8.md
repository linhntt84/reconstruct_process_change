### Observed differences

- Four activities became near-universal: A22 ran in about 54 in 100 traces in the earlier window and in all traces in the later window; A31, A32 and A37 each ran in about 46 in 100 traces earlier and in about 995 in 1000 traces later, without repeating within a trace in either window. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004]
- In the earlier window, A22 and each of A31, A32, A37 were mutually exclusive alternatives — they never appeared in the same trace (roughly 54 vs 46 in 100). In the later window they co-occur in about 995 in 1000 traces, with A31, A32 and A37 each executed before A22. [REL011, REL012, REL013]
- The group A31/A32/A37 changed its place in the flow: earlier, A23 and A21 came before the group (in the traces where both ran); in the later window the group comes before A23 and before A21 in essentially all traces, while it still precedes A26 as before. The gap from A20 to A23 grew from the immediately next event to four events later — consistent with the three group activities now lying between them. [DIR001, LAG001]
- A21's successor changed: earlier it led to A22 in about 54 in 100 cases and to a group member in the rest; in the later window it always leads to A22, and A21 reaches A26 two events later in every trace (A21 → A22 → A26). [DIR002, LAG002, REL005, REL010]
- The internal order of A31, A32 and A37 is unfixed in both windows: each pair occurs in both orders in near-equal shares before and after. [REL020, REL021, REL022]
- Mean event positions shifted accordingly: A31, A32 and A37 moved earlier (about 0.59 to about 0.40 of the trace), A23 moved later (about 0.39 to 0.46), A21, A22 and A26 moved slightly later, and A20 slightly earlier. [POS006, POS007, POS008, POS004, POS002, POS003, POS005, POS001]
- Scale check: 41 of 99 tested control-flow units qualified, where about 0.5 would be expected if nothing had changed. [screening]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the exclusive branch between A22 and the group A31/A32/A37 was replaced by a flow in which both always execute, with the group relocated earlier. In the earlier window, a trace took either the A22 path (about 54 in 100 cases) or the A31/A32/A37 path in mixed order (about 46 in 100), both sitting between A21 and A26. In the later window, every trace runs A20 → A31/A32/A37 (mixed order) → A23 → … → A21 → A22 → A26: A22 and the group changed from alternatives into sequential stages, and the group moved from after A21 to between A20 and A23.

The hypothesis rests on direct relational and successor evidence, not on marginal frequencies alone: co-occurrence of A22 with A31/A32/A37 rose from zero to about 995 in 1000; the order relative to A23 and A21 flipped; the observed successors of A20 and A21 changed accordingly; and the A20→A23 distance grew from 1 to 4 events, matching the group's new position. Participation changes match, and 41 qualifying items against roughly 0.5 expected by chance make sampling noise implausible as an explanation of the pattern as a whole. Many items describe the same underlying restructuring through different indicators, so they should be read as one consistent picture rather than independent confirmations. [REL011, REL012, REL013, REL014, REL015, REL016, REL006, REL007, REL008, DIR001, DIR002, LAG001, LAG002, OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, screening]

Limitations: positions are event positions, not clock times, so "moved earlier" concerns sequence order only. The evidence covers only the flagged activities and cannot show whether unobserved activities between A23 and A21 were also affected. One competing explanation remains: a rare pre-existing variant with this always-both behaviour that the earlier window's 200 traces did not sample cannot be fully ruled out, although the earlier window shows no trace of it.
