### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that, in the later window, two pre-existing alternative blocks of activities are taken with swapped frequencies: A21, A22 and A23, which ran together in about three of four traces in the earlier window, run together in only about one of four in the later window, while A24, A25 and A26 rise from about one of four to about three of four. Both blocks keep their members executing without a fixed internal order, remain mutually exclusive with each other, and are entered from the same predecessors — A19, A20 and A29 now precede A24/A25/A26 where they previously preceded A21/A22/A23 — so the change reads as existing paths being taken with different frequencies, not as activities being added, removed or reordered [OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, OCC007, OCC008, OCC009, REL025, REL034, REL049, REL055, REL037, DIR002, REL016, REL051]

The swap is shown directly and consistently: participation and co-occurrence flip in mirror image on the two sides, and 92 of 220 tested control-flow units qualified against roughly 1 expected for an unchanged process, so the ensemble is far beyond what random re-splits of a stable process produce; each item also exceeds about 99.5 in 100 reference differences. Many relation items restate the same trace behaviour, so they corroborate one another without being independent confirmations. An important competing explanation remains: a changed case mix in which two pre-existing variants are simply sampled in different proportions would look identical at this level, and the roughly 25 in 100 later traces still running the old block could be such a continuing variant [OCC004, OCC007, REL037, REL049, DIR002, screening]
