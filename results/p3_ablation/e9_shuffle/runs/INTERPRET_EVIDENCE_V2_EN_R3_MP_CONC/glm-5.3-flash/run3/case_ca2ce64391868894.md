### Observed differences

- Three activities disappeared: A01, A02 and A03 were each present in all 200 earlier traces but in none of the 200 later ones. A01 and A02 occurred exactly once per trace; A03 occurred about 2.1 times per trace and repeated in about 55 in 100 traces. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, POS001, POS002, POS003]
- A04 shows the mirror image: not observed earlier, it occurs exactly once in every later trace, always at the final position (relative position 1.0 with no spread) — the slot A01 used to occupy. [OCC004, POS004, REL003, REL009, REL014]
- The ending changed: earlier, A05 always led to A02, A02 always led to A01, and A01 was always last; in the later window, A05 leads to A04 in 100 in 100 cases, and A07 precedes A04 in every trace. [DIR001, DIR003, REL010, REL019, REL021]
- The repetition cycle was rewired: earlier, A08 always fed back into A03, and A03 split its outgoing steps between A06 (about 53 in 100) and an exit to A07 (about 47 in 100); in the later window, A08 leads directly to A06 (about 46 in 100) or exits to A07 (about 54 in 100). [DIR002, DIR004, LAG005, LAG006]
- Every distance through the cycle shortened by one step: A08→A06 and A08→A07 from lag 2 to 1, A06→A07 and A08→A05 from 3 to 2, A06→A05 from 4 to 3, and the within-cycle repeats A06→A06 and A08→A08 from 3 to 2. [LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006, LAG007]
- The cycle itself still fires at a similar rate: A06 and A08 each appear in about 55 in 100 earlier traces and about 50 in 100 later ones, repeating in roughly half of those (57 of 111 earlier vs 46 of 101 later); A06 and A07 also sit earlier in the trace than before (mean relative position about 0.27 vs 0.18, and 0.48 vs 0.30). [REL016, REL020, REL022, LAG002, LAG007, POS005, POS006]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the process was structurally restructured around its cycle and its ending. In the earlier window the cycle ran A03 → A06 → A08 → A03 (A08 returning to A03 in all cases; A03 either continuing the cycle via A06 or exiting to A07), and every trace closed with A05 → A02 → A01, A01 always last. In the later window, A01, A02 and A03 are no longer observed; the cycle alternates directly between A06 and A08, with the exit to A07 now taken from A08, so each passage through the cycle is one step shorter; and A05 leads to the new activity A04, which is mandatory and always final, as A01 was. The loop's incidence stayed roughly stable (about 55 in 100 traces earlier, about 50 in 100 later), so this is a rewiring and shortening of an existing cycle plus a replacement of the closing sequence, not a shift in how often existing paths are taken. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, POS004, LAG001, LAG003, REL016, REL022]

The support is direct and consistent: the participation changes are absolute (100 → 0 and 0 → 100 in 100 traces, on 200 traces per window), the rerouting is visible in the successor profiles of A05 and A08, all seven lag distributions through the cycle shift by exactly one step, and the screening flagged 43 control-flow items where fewer than one (0.24) would be expected by chance in an unchanged process. [OCC001, OCC003, OCC004, DIR003, DIR004, LAG001, LAG003, screening]

Limitations: the evidence does not show what feeds the cycle in the later window, since the predecessor that used to reach A03 is not among the reported changes; it also cannot rule out that A04 is a relabelled or merged replacement of the removed closing activities rather than a genuinely new step, or that the cycle rewiring and the ending replacement were two independent changes rather than one. [DIR002, DIR004, OCC003, OCC004, POS001, POS004]
