### Observed differences

- The three activities A30, A31 and A36 changed from exactly one execution per case to repeated executions. Before, each appeared in 100% of cases with about 1.0 occurrences per trace and repeats in at most about 2 in 100 traces; in the later window each still appears in every case but averages about 3.5 occurrences per trace, with repeats in about 68 in 100 traces. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003]
- The strict ordering around these activities dissolved. A14 still co-occurs with A30 and with A36 in every case, but A14 before A30 fell from about 99 in 100 traces to about 32 in 100, A14 before A36 from about 98 in 100 to about 32 in 100, and A30 before A36 from about 98 in 100 to about 32 in 100; interleaved or repeated executions, almost absent before (0–2 in 100), now account for about 68 in 100 traces for each pair. [REL003, REL004, REL005]
- With A05, which runs in about half of the cases in both windows (about 55 in 100 before, 52 in 100 after), the picture is the same: A05 before A30 or A36 fell from about 53–54 in 100 to about 16 in 100, while interleaved or repeated executions rose to about 35 in 100. [REL001, REL002]
- A36's dominant successor changed: before, about 97 in 100 of A36's outgoing transitions led to A25; in the later window about 70 in 100 lead to A30 and about 28 in 100 to A25, plus a few rare new successors. Observed outgoing transitions rose from about 200 to about 700, consistent with A36's higher execution count. [DIR001, OCC003]
- The close A30→A36 succession largely disappeared: the two-position gap made up about 97 in 100 A30→A36 successions before (three positions for the rest) but only about 12 in 100 in the later window, with about 5 in 100 at the five-position maximum. [LAG002]
- Secondary shifts: A30's and A36's positions within the case became more variable (spread about 0.07 → 0.12 of case position) and A14's position moved slightly earlier; the distance between A05 and A14 shifted somewhat to larger gaps. These are event-position measures, not clock times. [POS001, POS002, POS003, LAG001]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that, in the later window, A30, A31 and A36 are executed repeatedly — about 3.5 times per case instead of once, with repeats in roughly two of three cases — and that the former single-pass ordering around them dissolved into interleaving: A14 (in every case) and A05 (in about half of the cases) no longer strictly precede one A30 and one A36, and A36 now typically passes to A30 (about 70 in 100 of its transitions) rather than to A25. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL001, REL002, DIR001]

Supporting signs: the repetition is measured directly on all 200 traces in both windows (participation 100% throughout, mean occurrences 1.0 → about 3.5, recurrence from about 0 to about 68 in 100); every item's difference exceeded 199 of 200 random re-splits of the pooled data; and fourteen items qualified against roughly half an item expected by chance for the 99 units tested, so sampling fluctuation cannot account for the pattern as a whole. The relational, directional and spacing items all point the same way. [OCC001, OCC003, REL003, DIR001, LAG002]

Limitations: the ordering items largely restate the repetition (interleaving is what repeats of A30 and A36 around a single A14 produce), so they are consistent signs rather than independent confirmations; the evidence cannot show whether the repeats follow a fixed alternating structure or are unstructured re-executions, only that the old tight two-step A30→A36 spacing is mostly gone; and A31's repetition comes with no relational or positional items, so its place in the sequence is unobserved. [OCC002, LAG002, REL005]

Other differences: only minor by-products beyond this hypothesis — A14 now sits slightly earlier within the case and the A05–A14 distance moved slightly to larger gaps; no activity appeared or disappeared (participation of A30, A31, A36 and A14 stayed at 100% and A05 at about half of cases in both windows). [POS001, LAG001, REL001, OCC001]
