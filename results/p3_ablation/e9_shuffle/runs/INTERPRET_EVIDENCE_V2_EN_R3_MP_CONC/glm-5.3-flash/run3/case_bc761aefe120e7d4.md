### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, A30, A31 and A36 each occurred about 3.4–3.5 times per trace and appeared in every trace; in the later window they still appear in every trace but only about once per trace (about 1.0–1.05 occurrences), and the share of traces with repeated executions of each activity fell from about 75 in 100 to about 1 in 100 [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003].
- Routing out of A36 shifted accordingly: earlier, about 70 in 100 of A36's observed outgoing transitions returned to A30 and about 29 in 100 went to A25; in the later window, about 93 in 100 go to A25 and only about 4 in 100 return to A30 [DIR003].
- The chain itself is stable in both windows: A30 leads to A31 in about 97–99 in 100 observed transitions and A31 leads to A36 in about 98–99 in 100 [DIR001, DIR002].
- The relation between A30 and A36 changed from repeated or interleaved execution to a fixed order: earlier the two were interleaved or repeated together in about 75 in 100 traces (A30 strictly before A36 in about 25 in 100); in the later window A30 precedes A36 in about 98 in 100 traces, with both co-occurring in every trace in both windows [REL001].
- The average relative positions of A30 and A36 barely moved (A30 about 44% to about 42% of the trace, A36 about 49% in both), but their spread roughly halved, consistent with the block now running once instead of several times [POS001, POS002].
- The gap from A30 to the next A36 concentrated at two steps, rising from about 12 in 100 observed pairs earlier to about 89 in 100 later; the self-repetition gap items for A30 and A36 point the same way but rest on only 1–2 observed pairs in the later window [LAG002, LAG001, LAG003, LAG004].

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the repeated execution of the A30 → A31 → A36 block stopped: in the earlier window the block ran about three to four times per case, with A36 returning to A30 in about 70 in 100 of its outgoing transitions, while in the later window it runs once per case, with A36 proceeding to A25 in about 93 in 100 transitions and back to A30 in only about 4 in 100 [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, DIR003, REL001]. The volume of qualified items is hard to explain by chance: 13 control-flow items qualified where about 0.46 would be expected if nothing had changed [screening]. The hypothesis is direct and consistent: all three activities remain in every trace, so none became optional or disappeared; the internal edges A30→A31 and A31→A36 keep their dominant shares; and the average positions of A30 and A36 are unchanged — together these rule out removal, replacement or a swap of the activities as competing explanations [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, DIR001, DIR002, POS001, POS002]. Limitations: the occurrence, relation and routing items all describe one underlying trace behaviour, so they are not independent confirmations; the self-repetition gap items rest on only 1–2 later-window observations [LAG001, LAG003, LAG004]; and the evidence cannot distinguish a fully removed loop from one that is still taken in the roughly 1–2 in 100 traces that still show repeats [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003].

Other differences: the remaining qualified items are noise-level shuffles among rare outgoing edges of A30 and A31, with every affected component at or below about 0.5 in 100 transitions in both windows; they do not indicate a separate change [DIR001, DIR002].
