### Observed differences

- The largest difference is a reversal in how often two alternative groups of activities run. In the earlier window, A16, A17 and A18 each took part in about 75 in 100 traces, while A19, A20 and A21 each took part in about 25 in 100; in the later window these shares reversed, to about 25 in 100 for each of A16–A18 and about 76 in 100 for each of A19–A21. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006]

- The two groups were mutually exclusive alternatives in both windows: A16 and A19 never occurred in the same trace (0 of 200 traces in either window), every trace contained exactly one of the two groups, and the three members of a group always ran together — A16 with A17 in about 75 in 100 earlier traces versus about 25 later, and A19 with A20 in about 25 earlier versus about 76 later. [REL009, REL007, REL013, REL022, REL023, REL025]

- Ordering inside each group showed no fixed sequence in either window: A16 preceded A17 in about 38 in 100 earlier traces and followed it in about 37, and in the later window A19 preceded A21 in about 34 in 100 and followed it in about 42. [REL007, REL023]

- A15 and A25 ran in every trace in both windows and bracketed the choice: A15 came before whichever group ran (in the later window it preceded A19, A20 or A21 in all of their roughly 76-in-100 joint appearances), and A25 co-occurred with the running group — with A16 in about 75 in 100 earlier traces versus about 25 later, and with A19 in about 25 earlier versus about 76 later. [REL001, REL004, REL005, REL006, REL012, REL024]

- A15's outgoing transitions redistributed accordingly: earlier, about 27 in 100 of its observed outgoing edges led to A16, 23 to A17 and 26 to A18, against roughly 13 to A19, 4 to A20 and 8 to A21; later, about 25 led to A19, 22 to A20 and 29 to A21, against 9 to A16, 9 to A17 and 8 to A18. [DIR001]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the exclusive choice after A15 flipped its shares: the path through A16, A17 and A18 (with A15 before and A25 after) fell from about 75 in 100 traces to about 25 in 100, while the alternative path through A19, A20 and A21 rose from about 25 to about 76 in 100. [OCC001, OCC004, REL009, REL022, DIR001]

Supporting signs: the participation changes are large, symmetric and consistent across the occurrence, relation and direction indicators; the surrounding structure is intact in both windows — the groups remain mutually exclusive, keep no fixed internal order, and A15 and A25 keep running in every trace with no repetition — which fits a re-weighting of two existing paths rather than activities being added, removed, bypassed or repeated. Even the smaller shares rest on about 50 traces, and every item shows a difference larger than in about 995 of 1,000 random re-splits of the pooled traces. [OCC001, OCC004, REL009, REL007, REL022, REL001, REL012, DIR001]

Screening comparison: 35 control-flow items qualified out of 106 tested units, against roughly 0.53 expected by chance if nothing had changed; a count this many times larger cannot be explained by chance as a whole, although the many relation items largely restate one underlying flip and are not independent confirmations. [screening]

Limitations: with 200 traces per window the exact shares carry sampling error, and the evidence cannot rule out that the later window simply contains a different mix of the two pre-existing case variants — a population shift — rather than a change in the routing itself. [OCC001, REL009]

Other differences: the positional gap between A15 and A20 shifted slightly toward smaller distances — a gap of three event positions occurred in about 52 in 100 earlier observations versus about 34 in 100 later, while gaps of one or two positions rose from about 48 to about 66 in 100; the change is small on the indicator's own scale (about 0.08), and since positions are not clock time, this suggests only that A20 tended to sit somewhat earlier within its group, not that the activity was relocated. [LAG001]
