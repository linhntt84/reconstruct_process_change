### Observed differences

- The relative order of two groups of activities reversed. In the earlier window, A26 and A27 each ran before A35, A36 and A41 in about 99 in 100 traces; in the later window, each of A35, A36 and A41 ran before each of A26 and A27 in about 98–99 in 100 traces. All six activity pairs still occurred together in every trace in both windows. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006]

- Direct successors were rewired at the boundaries of these groups. Earlier, A25 led to A27 in about 99 in 100 of its observed outgoing transitions, A26 led into A35, A36 or A41 (roughly one third each), and each group member led to A30 in about 28–37 in 100 transitions; in the later window, A25 led into A35, A36 or A41 (about one third each), the group members led to A27 in about 29–34 in 100 transitions, and A26 led directly to A30 in about 99 in 100 transitions. - Event positions shifted consistently: A26 moved from a mean relative position of about 0.53 to about 0.62 and A27 from about 0.38 to about 0.47, while A35, A36 and A41 each moved earlier, from about 0.58–0.59 to about 0.41. [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005]

A25 reached A27 within one step in about 99 in 100 traces earlier but at four steps in about 93 in 100 traces later; A26 reached A30 at four steps in about 93 in 100 traces earlier but within one step in about 99 in 100 later. The old bridging pairs (A26 to A35/A36/A41, and the group members to A30) occurred within five steps in roughly 200 traces earlier but in only 1–2 traces later, whereas A25→A35 and A35→A27 rose from 1 trace to about 199 traces, at one to three steps. ### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the group of activities A35, A36 and A41 exchanged places with the part of the flow containing A27 and A26: in the earlier window the flow ran from A25 to A27, then after A26 through A35/A36/A41 to A30; in the later window A25 feeds the group directly, the group feeds A27, and A26 leads straight to A30. The support is direct and consistent: all six activity-pair relations flipped in the same direction with near-complete shares (about 98–99 in 100) while co-occurrence stayed at 100% in both windows, so the activities did not appear or disappear; the successor changes at A25, A26 and the group members show the same rewiring; the position and step-distance shifts agree; every item's difference exceeds what was seen in about 199 of 200 random re-splits; and the screening produced 26 qualifying items against fewer than one expected without a change, which chance cannot explain as a whole. Limitations: most items reflect one underlying reordering rather than independent confirmations; the evidence cannot distinguish whether the group moved earlier or the A27/A26 part moved later, since both describe the same relational change; several distance items rest on only 1–2 observations in one window and are individually weak; and the group's internal execution order is variable in both windows, so no internal reordering is claimed. A competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that the windows sample two different process variants with different orderings; the near-complete reversal makes a pure sampling artefact unlikely, but the residue of about 1–2 in 100 traces that keep the old order in the later window is not explained by the main hypothesis. Other differences: a few rare outgoing transitions, each about 1 in 200, appear or disappear at A25, A35, A36 and A41 (for example A41 towards A06, A43, A44 or A08, and A36 towards A02, A22, A23 or A46); they are minor and not part of the main hypothesis. [DIR001, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005]
