### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, six activities — A33, A35 and A36 in one group, A37, A38 and A39 in the other — each ran in only about half of the cases (49–53 in 100) and averaged about 0.5 occurrences per case; in the later window, each runs in about 99 in 100 cases, once per case, almost never repeated. [OCC005, OCC006, OCC007, OCC008, OCC009, OCC010]
- In the earlier window these two groups were alternatives: pairs spanning the groups (for example A33 with A37, or A36 with A39) co-occurred in only about 1 in 100 cases, while exactly one group was present in about 99 in 100 cases. In the later window, these same pairs co-occur in about 98 in 100 cases. [REL039, REL040, REL041, REL045, REL046, REL047, REL050, REL051, REL052]
- In the later window the order between the groups is fixed: in about 95–98 in 100 joint cases an activity of the first group completes before an activity of the second; within each group the order stays unfixed (A33 before A35 in about 46 in 100 later cases, A35 before A33 in about 50 in 100). In the earlier window no cross-group order was observable because the groups rarely shared a case. [REL037, REL039, REL044, REL050, REL052, REL056, REL059]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the two middle activity groups — {A33, A35, A36} and {A37, A38, A39} — changed from alternatives to a fixed sequence: in the earlier window a case executed exactly one of the two groups (each in about half of the cases, both in about 1 in 100), while in the later window virtually every case executes both, first group before second, with the order inside each group unfixed; each of the six activities thereby changed from optional (about half of the cases) to effectively mandatory (once in nearly every case). Supporting signs: the participation of all six roughly doubled in agreement; the nine cross-group pairs moved from near-exclusive to near-universal co-occurrence with a fixed order; within-group pairs kept their mixed order while co-occurrence rose to about 98–99 in 100; and the relations to A14, A30, A31, A47 and A48, the longer distances to A31, and the changed outgoing transitions of A30 and the first-group activities all agree with this one underlying difference — though these agreeing items partly reflect the same trace behaviour rather than independent confirmations. With 109 qualifying items against about 1 expected by chance in the control-flow family, sampling variability cannot account for the evidence as a whole. A competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is a shift in the case mix (the later window may simply contain more cases of a kind that requires both groups) rather than a routing change for all cases; the indicators also use event positions, so no claim about clock-time timing is made. Other differences: A04 is now usually skipped — present in about 26 in 100 cases (before about 59), later in the trace, with A14 leading directly to A15 in most of its outgoing transitions — a direct relational finding that is not part of the main hypothesis. A02, A08 and A10 became somewhat more frequent (about 6–8 to about 18–23 in 100 cases) but remain rare and rest on few observations, and the A45–A46 and A47–A48 endings are slightly less exclusive than before.
