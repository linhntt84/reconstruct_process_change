### Observed differences

- The frequencies of A01 and A07 moved in opposite directions: in the earlier window A01 appeared in about 28 in 100 traces (about 0.5 occurrences per trace, repeats in 11 in 100 traces), while in the later window it appeared in about 53 in 100 traces (about 1.1 occurrences per trace, repeats in 29 in 100). A07 went the other way, from about 51 in 100 traces (about 1.0 occurrence per trace) to about 29 in 100 (about 0.6). A03, A04, A05 and A06 kept similar participation in both windows. [OCC001, OCC002, POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004]

- The successor profiles of A01 and A07 essentially exchanged: before, about 94 in 100 of A01's outgoing transitions went to A07, and A07 branched to A05 (43 in 100) and A06 (41 in 100); in the later window, about 94 in 100 of A07's outgoing transitions go to A01, and A01 branches to A05 (57 in 100), A06 (18 in 100), A04 (14 in 100) and A03 (11 in 100). [DIR001, DIR006]

- A05's outgoing flipped from A01 to A07 (96 in 100 before, 91 in 100 after), and the pair distances match a reversed small loop: before, A01 followed A05 immediately and A05 followed A01 about two steps later; in the later window A05 immediately follows A01 and A01 follows A05 about two steps later. [DIR004, LAG001, LAG009, LAG011, LAG013]

- The A03/A04 block now returns to A01 instead of A07: before, A03 and A04 sent roughly 40–50 in 100 of their outgoing transitions to A07 and at most 3 in 100 to A01; in the later window they send about 34–39 in 100 to A01 and 2 in 100 or fewer to A07, with A01 now following A03/A04 within one to two steps instead of three to four. A05 and A07, which often appeared apart before, now always co-occur (about 29 in 100 traces). [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, REL007, REL008, REL009]

- Within traces, A06 moved earlier (mean relative position about 0.96 before, about 0.48 in the later window) and A07 moved later (about 0.51 to about 0.69). [POS003, POS004]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that A01 and A07 exchanged their positions and roles in the cyclic core of the process. Before, the cycle ran A05 → A01 → A07 with A07 as the hub: it closed a small loop back through A05 (A07 → A05 → A01 → A07) or continued to A06 and the A03/A04 block, which returned to A07. In the later window the cycle runs A07 → A01 → A05 → A07 with A01 as the hub: it branches to A05 or to A06/A03/A04, which now return to A01. This is shown directly by the near-complete reversal of the successor profiles of A01, A05 and A07, corroborated by lag-distance shifts in the same direction, by A01 appearing in about twice as many traces while A07 appears in about half as many, and by the co-occurrence flip from "A03/A04/A06 without A01" to "A03/A04/A06 without A07". The volume of qualifying items (35 against fewer than 0.3 expected without a change, all control-flow) and the uniform empirical strength of about 0.995 make sampling noise an unlikely explanation. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR006, LAG001, LAG009, LAG011, LAG012, LAG013, OCC001, OCC002, REL005, REL006, REL007, REL009]

An important competing explanation remains: the window-level profiles cannot show whether individual traces were rewired in place or whether the later window blends a changed mix of trace variants (for example, a variant running the A06/A03/A04 circuit from A01 instead of A07); the aggregates are consistent with both. [REL006, REL007, REL009]

Other differences: A06 gained a direct transition to A05 (about 18 in 100 of its outgoing transitions in the later window, none observed before), A03 and A04 lead to A06 more often (about 25 in 100 versus under 10 in 100 before), and A01 pairs are more often interleaved or repeated (11 to 29 in 100 traces), consistent with A01 repeating more often. [DIR005, DIR002, DIR003, REL001, OCC001]
