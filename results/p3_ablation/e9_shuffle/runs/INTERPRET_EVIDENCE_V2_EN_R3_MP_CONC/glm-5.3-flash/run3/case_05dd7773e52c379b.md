### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, six activities — A28, A30, A31, A32, A33 and A34 — each appeared in only about half of the cases (roughly 49–52 in 100); in the later window, each of them appears in about 98–99 in 100 cases. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006]
- Before, these six formed two mutually exclusive triples: {A28, A30, A31} ran in about 49 in 100 cases and {A32, A33, A34} in about 52 in 100. Members of the same triple always occurred together, while members of different triples never shared a case (co-occurrence 0 in all nine cross-pairs). [REL013, REL014, REL018, REL025, REL026, REL027, REL016, REL017, REL021, REL023]
- In the later window, both triples run together in about 98 in 100 cases, always in the same macro-order: every activity of {A28, A30, A31} precedes every activity of {A32, A33, A34} (about 98 in 100 for each cross-pair). The order inside each triple remains unfixed in both windows, with both orders in roughly equal shares. [REL015, REL016, REL017, REL019, REL020, REL021, REL022, REL023, REL024, REL014, REL018, REL026]
- A25 and A26 occur in every case in both windows. Before, A25 preceded whichever triple was selected and A26 followed it; in the later window, A25 precedes all six block activities (about 99 in 100) and all six precede A26 (about 99 in 100). [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, REL007, REL008, REL009, REL010, REL011, REL012]
- The segment between them grew longer: positional distances from A25 to the second-block activities and from the first-block activities to A26 moved from 1–3 steps before to 4–5 steps in the later window, and relative positions shifted accordingly — A25, A28, A30 and A31 sit earlier (mean position about 0.74 → 0.68) while A32, A33, A34 and A26 sit later (about 0.73 → 0.77). [LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006, POS001, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006, POS007, POS008]
- Direct successors changed accordingly: A28, A30 and A31 now almost never lead directly to A26 (for example A28→A26 fell from about 36 in 100 of A28's outgoing transitions to under 1 in 100) and instead lead to A32, A33 and A34; A25 now leads almost exclusively to A28, A30 and A31 rather than directly to A32, A33 or A34. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the two previously alternative activity blocks — {A28, A30, A31} and {A32, A33, A34} — are both executed in the later window, in that order, between A25 and A26, whereas before, each case executed exactly one of the two blocks (about a 49/52 split). This is a change from alternatives to jointly executed, sequentially ordered blocks rather than a shift in how often stable paths are taken: the nine cross-block pairs went from never co-occurring to co-occurring in about 98 in 100 cases with a consistent order, and participation of all six activities rose from about half of cases to nearly all. [REL016, REL017, REL021, REL023, REL024, OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006]

The screening comparison supports treating this as a real change: 51 control-flow items qualified against fewer than 1 expected by chance in an unchanged process, and the occurrence, relational, lag, successor and positional items all describe one coherent restructuring — a longer, mandatory middle segment between A25 and A26 — rather than isolated anomalies. [REL021, LAG006, DIR002, POS008, OCC006]

Limitations: the evidence cannot show whether the two blocks now form one merged stage or two mandatory consecutive stages, and the internal sequencing within each block remains variable in both windows. A competing explanation that cannot be fully ruled out is that the fixed order between the two blocks existed before but was unobservable while they never co-occurred; the data show the joint execution is new, not necessarily the inter-block order itself. [REL014, REL018, REL025, REL026, REL027]

Other differences: none beyond the above; the position shifts, longer lags and successor changes are consequences of the same restructuring rather than separate behavioural changes.
