### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, A14, A15 and A20 do not appear in any of the 200 traces. In the later window, A15 occurs in about 41 in 100 traces, A14 in about 29 and A20 in about 30, each at most once per trace and around the middle of the case (mean event position about 0.51 of the trace length). [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, POS001, POS002, POS003]
- The three new activities are mutually exclusive alternatives: no later trace contains two of them, and for each pair the share of traces containing neither matches the participation of the third activity, so exactly one of the three runs in almost every later trace. [REL001, REL002, REL006, OCC001, OCC002, OCC003]
- The new step sits between A22 and A24: whenever one of the three co-occurs with A22 it follows A22, and whenever it co-occurs with A24 it precedes A24. The event distance from A22 to A24 grows from 2 steps in essentially all earlier traces to 3 steps in about 995 in 1000 later traces. [REL003, REL005, REL007, REL009, REL010, REL012, LAG002]
- Routing redistributes accordingly: earlier, A22 led only to A23 and A23 only to A24; in the later window, A23 leads to A24 in about 57 in 100 of its outgoing transitions and to A15, A14 or A20 in the remaining 43, while A22 leads to A23 in about 44 and to one of the new activities in about 56. - The position of the new step relative to A23 varies case by case: A15 precedes A23 in about 24 in 100 later traces and follows it in about 17, with similar mixed orders for A14 (about 16 vs 14) and A20 (about 17 vs 13). Consistently, the A22→A23 gap is 2 steps in about 56 in 100 later traces (always 1 before), and the A23→A24 gap is 2 steps in about 43 in 100 (always 1 before). - A22 still occurs in every trace in both windows; its median event position shifts slightly earlier, from about 0.48 to about 0.46 of the trace length. [POS004]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that, in the later window, one additional step is executed in almost every case inside the segment between A22 and A24, where the earlier window showed the fixed two-step chain A22 → A23 → A24. The additional step is one of three mutually exclusive activities — A15 in about 41 in 100 later traces, A14 in about 29 and A20 in about 30 — always executed after A22 and before A24, placed between A22 and A23 in a majority of cases and between A23 and A24 in the remainder. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, REL001, REL002, REL006, REL003, REL005, REL007, REL009, REL010, REL012, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, REL004, REL008, REL011, DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005]

The support is direct and internally consistent: participation rises from zero to roughly 29–41 in 100 traces for each of the three activities, the A22–A24 step distance grows by exactly one step in nearly all traces, and the outgoing transitions of A22 and A23 redistribute in proportions that match the relational and gap evidence. With 27 qualifying items against roughly 0.4 expected for an unchanged process, chance alone cannot account for the pattern as a whole. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, DIR004, DIR005, REL004, REL008, REL011]

Limitations: zero participation in the earlier window strictly establishes only that A14, A15 and A20 were not observed then, so the evidence cannot distinguish a newly added step from one that existed but was never triggered before — although at 0 of 200 versus roughly 30–41 of 200 traces, sampling fluctuation is an implausible explanation. The evidence also cannot fix a single placement for the new step, since it appears on both sides of A23. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, REL004, REL008, REL011]
