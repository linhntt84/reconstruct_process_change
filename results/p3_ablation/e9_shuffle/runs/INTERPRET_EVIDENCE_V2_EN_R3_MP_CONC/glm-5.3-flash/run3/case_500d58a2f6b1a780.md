### Observed differences

- The clearest difference is in repetition. In the earlier window, A25, A26 and A31 each ran about 3.4 times per trace and repeated within a trace in about 75 in 100 cases; in the later window each runs about once per trace and repeats in only about 1 in 200 cases, while all three still appear in every trace in both windows. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003]
- The exit routing of A31 changed: in the earlier window about 71 in 100 of its observed outgoing transitions went to A25 and about 29 in 100 to A20; in the later window about 96 in 100 go to A20 and only about 4 in 100 to A25. [DIR001]
- The relationship between A25 and A31 changed from overlapping repetition to a fixed single pass: earlier the two were interleaved or repeated in about 75 in 100 traces (A25 before A31 in the remaining ~25 in 100); later A25 precedes A31 in about 995 of 1000 traces with virtually no interleaving. [REL001]
- Distances agree: A31 now follows A25 two steps later in about 92 in 100 traces (earlier about 12 in 100), while A25 following A31 within five steps has almost disappeared — observed in only 1 of 200 later traces versus about 150 earlier. [LAG001, LAG002]
- The relative positions of A25 and A31 barely moved (A25 median about 0.43 to 0.40; A31 about 0.48 to 0.48), but their spread roughly halved (standard deviation about 0.12 to 0.06 for both), as expected when a variable number of passes is replaced by one fixed pass. [POS001, POS002]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the repeated execution of the block involving A25, A26 and A31 stopped. In the earlier window these activities cycled — A31 typically returned to A25 (about 71 in 100 of its outgoing transitions), producing about three to four passes per trace and interleaving of A25 and A31 in about three quarters of traces. In the later window the cycle is no longer taken: A31 proceeds to A20 in about 96 in 100 of its outgoing transitions, each of the three activities runs once per trace, and A25 precedes A31 in a fixed single pass with a two-step gap in about 92 in 100 traces. This is a change in repetition (the activities stopped repeating), not a shift of share between alternative complete paths. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, DIR001, REL001, LAG001, LAG002]

The signs are direct and mutually consistent: participation stayed at 100% in both windows, so the activities did not become optional or disappear; the collapse of recurrence support (about 75 in 100 to about 1 in 200) and of the A31→A25 edge (about 71 in 100 to 4 in 100) is observed directly; and the screening produced 9 qualifying items against roughly 0.3 expected without change — about thirty times the chance expectation — so the item set is unlikely for an unchanged process. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, DIR001, REL001]

Limitations: all items describe one underlying trace behaviour, so they corroborate a single difference rather than nine independent ones. The evidence shows only A31's outgoing edges, so the new predecessor of A25 and the exact way the single pass is entered are not directly observed, and A26 has no directional or relational item of its own. A competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that the iteration count was capped by a change upstream of A25 rather than at the A31→A25 edge itself. [DIR001, LAG002, OCC002]

Other differences: none beyond the above; the narrowing of the position distributions of A25 and A31 (spread roughly halved, central position essentially unchanged) is consistent with the same single-pass behaviour. [POS001, POS002]
