### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that, in the later window, A02 sits between A15 and the activities A07, A08 and A12: A15, which previously led directly to one of those three, now leads to A02, and A02 in turn leads to A07, A08 or A12. Because this occurs inside a repeating segment, every within-segment distance grew by one position (A15→its successors from 1 to 2; A07→A07/A08/A12 and A15→A15 from 4 to 5), and A02's roughly 2.4 occurrences per trace in the traces that contain it fit about one insertion per pass of the segment. [DIR002, DIR001, LAG005, LAG006, LAG007, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG008, OCC001]

The change is observed directly in the successor profiles (A15's targets collapsed onto A02; A02's targets match A15's former ones), is corroborated by consistent lag shifts and by the new co-occurrence pattern, and the volume of flagged items far exceeds what an unchanged process produces (16 control-flow items against roughly 0.2 expected from 41 tested units), so chance sampling cannot explain the pattern as a whole. [DIR002, DIR001, REL004, REL001, REL002, REL003, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG008]

Limitations: A02's zero participation in the earlier window shows only that it was not observed then, so a previously unobserved step that became frequent cannot be distinguished from a genuinely added one; the 16 items largely describe one underlying trace difference rather than independent confirmations; and a few lag items rest on few observations (for example A07→A12 on 8 earlier and 13 later traces). [OCC001, LAG003]
