### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, the relative order of A15, A17, A18, A19, A20 and A21 was flexible: A15/A21 and A17/A19 each ran in both orders roughly 50 in 100 traces, A19 ran before A18 in about 88 in 100 traces and before A15 in about 74 in 100, and A21 ran before A18 in about 70 in 100. [REL003, REL004, REL006, REL001, REL008]
- In the later window, a single fixed order prevails: A15 before A21 and A17 before A19 in all 200 traces, and A15 before A19, A18 before A19, A18 before A21 and A18 before A20 in all but about 1 in 200 traces. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, REL007, REL008]
- Successors concentrated accordingly: earlier, A26's outgoing transitions split about 50/50 between A19 and A17, A18 led most often to A27 (about 53 in 100) and A19 most often to A21 (about 55 in 100); in the later window each of A26, A17, A15, A18, A19, A21 and A20 has essentially one successor — A26→A17, A17→A15, A15→A18, A18→A19, A19→A21, A21→A20, A20→A27 — at about 995–1000 in 1000 of outgoing transitions. [DIR007, DIR003, DIR004, DIR002, DIR001, DIR006, DIR005]
- Distances between pairs match one chain: the consecutive pairs above now sit one step apart in nearly every trace, whereas earlier the same pairs were spread across distances of one to five steps; longer pairs land exactly where the chain predicts (for example A26→A21 and A15→A27 at five steps in about 995 in 1000 later traces). [LAG025, LAG006, LAG001, LAG017, LAG022, LAG020, LAG029, LAG005]
- The reverse pairs all but vanished: A19 before A15, A19 before A18, A20 before A18 and A21 before A18 (within five steps) each appeared in roughly 100–180 earlier traces but in only 1 later trace. [LAG014, LAG015, LAG019, LAG021, LAG028]
- Relative positions moved consistently with the new order: A15, A17 and A18 now occur earlier in the trace (A18's mean relative position fell from about 0.68 to about 0.62), while A19, A21 and A20 occur later (A19 rose from about 0.59 to about 0.65). Participation itself did not change: every activity involved appears in essentially all traces in both windows. [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006, REL002]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the ordering constraints between A15, A17, A18, A19, A20 and A21 changed: in the earlier window these activities ran in flexible, partially reversed orders, while in the later window they execute in the single chain A26 → A17 → A15 → A18 → A19 → A21 → A20 → A27, each activity followed by exactly one successor. The change is shown directly by the pairwise order shares, the concentration of each activity's outgoing transitions on one successor, and the near-deterministic distances between pairs; fifty control-flow items qualified against roughly 0.47 expected for an unchanged process, so the pattern as a whole is not what random re-splits produce, and each item's difference exceeds about 995 of 1000 random re-splits [REL006, DIR003, LAG027]. Because participation is unchanged — all involved activities occur in essentially every trace in both windows — this is a change in ordering, not in which activities run [POS003, REL002].

The evidence cannot rule out that this fixed chain already existed as a minority variant before and the share of variants shifted, rather than a new constraint being imposed; DIR shares are measured over outgoing transitions rather than cases, and LAG/POS use event positions, so no claim about clock-time timing is made. A few distance items rest on a single later trace [LAG014, LAG015, LAG019, LAG021, LAG028], but the corresponding order flips are shown independently with full support [REL001, REL006, REL007, REL008].

Other differences: none — the remaining items repeat the same ordering pattern for the other pairs of the chain [LAG007, LAG026, LAG018, DIR005].
