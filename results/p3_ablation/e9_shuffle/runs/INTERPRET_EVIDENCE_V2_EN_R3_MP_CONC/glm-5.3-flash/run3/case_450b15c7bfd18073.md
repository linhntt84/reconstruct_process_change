### Observed differences

- In the earlier window every trace ran the same chain: A22 was present in all 200 traces, A21's only observed outgoing transition went to A22, A22 always preceded A23, and A23 followed A21 exactly two event positions later in every case [OCC001, DIR001, REL001, REL005, LAG001].
- In the later window A22 appears in only about 51 in 100 traces (102 of 200), and A21 occurs without A22 in about 49 in 100 traces; A21's outgoing transitions are now split between A22 (about 51%) and the three activities A28, A33 and A27 (about 18%, 17% and 15% of observed outgoing transitions) [OCC001, REL001, DIR001].
- Three activities not observed at all in the earlier window — A27, A28 and A33 — each now appear in about 49 in 100 traces (98 of 200), exactly once per trace when present, around the middle of the trace (median position roughly 0.48–0.50 of trace length) [OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, POS001, POS002, POS003].
- A22 and the three new activities are mutually exclusive in the later window: A22 never co-occurs with A27, A28 or A33, while the three new activities always appear together (each pair co-occurs in about 49 in 100 traces) [REL006, REL007, REL008, REL012, REL013, REL014].
- The new trio runs between A21 and A23: A21 precedes each of the three in about 49 in 100 traces, each of the three precedes A23 in about 49 in 100 traces, and the A21-to-A23 distance is four event positions in about 49 in 100 later cases versus two positions in all earlier cases [REL002, REL003, REL004, REL009, REL010, REL011, LAG001].
- Within the trio there is no fixed order: each pair appears in both orders in similar proportions (for example A27 before A28 in about 23 in 100 traces and A28 before A27 in about 27 in 100), and each trio member's observed outgoing transitions lead both to A23 and to the other two members; when A22 does occur, it still precedes A23 as before [REL012, REL013, REL014, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, REL005].

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that, in the later window, about half of the cases take a new route from A21 to A23 that skips A22 and instead executes A27, A28 and A33 — each once, in no fixed internal order — while the other half still follows the original A21 → A22 → A23 chain. The screening produced 26 qualifying items where about 0.4 would be expected for an unchanged process (all in the control-flow family), so chance cannot account for the pattern as a whole; the hypothesis is shown directly by relational and distance evidence — mutual exclusivity between A22 and each new activity, A21 before the trio and the trio before A23, and the A21-to-A23 distance shifting from two to four positions in about 49 in 100 cases — and it is consistent across the occurrence, direction and position indicators [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, REL006, REL007, REL008, REL002, REL009, LAG001, DIR001].

Limitations: the qualifying items largely re-describe one underlying trace behaviour and are not independent confirmations, and the direction shares are computed over outgoing transitions rather than cases [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004]. An important competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that the later window pools two coexisting variants — the new activities were simply not observed in the earlier 200 traces — rather than a change occurring between the windows [OCC002, OCC003, OCC004].
