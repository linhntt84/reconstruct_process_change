### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the sequence A41 → A19 → A25 → A20 → A26, which in the earlier window ran in only about half of the cases — with A41 passing directly to A26 and the block A19/A25/A20 skipped in the rest — was executed in nearly every trace in the later window; the part of the process formed by A19, A25 and A20 is no longer skipped. The signs are direct and mutually consistent: participation of all three activities rose from about 48 to about 995 in 1000 traces while their mutual order stayed the same; A41's outgoing traffic moved from a roughly even split between A26 and A19 to almost exclusively A19; the step distances from A41 (1, 2, 3 and 4 steps to A19, A25, A20 and A26) fit one fixed chain; and A26 now consistently follows the block instead of co-occurring with it in only half the traces. [OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, REL018, REL019, REL021, REL022, REL025, DIR006, LAG018, LAG019, LAG020, LAG021]

The screening comparison indicates this is not a sampling artefact: 62 evidence items qualified against roughly 0.84 expected by chance for the 169 tested control-flow units, far beyond what an unchanged process typically produces [screening]. Limitations: the items are strongly interdependent — most describe the same trace-level change, so they are not independent confirmations; and the evidence only shows that the direct A41→A26 skipping path was observed in about 0.5 in 100 later traces, not whether it remains possible in principle. A competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that the mix of cases reaching this part of the process shifted, rather than the routing itself having been altered.

Other differences: A06 became far more frequent (about 24 to about 73 in 100 traces), sits earlier in the trace, now typically feeds directly into A16 and receives a new transition from A15; A08 became rarer (about 20 to about 8 in 100 traces). These changes are not explained by the main hypothesis. [OCC001, POS001, DIR001, LAG002, DIR003, LAG009, OCC002]
