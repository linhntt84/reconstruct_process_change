### Observed differences

- Before, A20 fed directly into A22: A22 was A20's only observed successor (100 in 100 of its outgoing transitions) and the pair sat one step apart in all 200 traces. In the later window, A20 instead flows into A30, A31 and A36 in near-equal shares (about a third each), A22 receives only about 0.5 in 100, and the remaining A20→A22 pairs sit four steps apart. [DIR001, LAG001]
- The order of two segments reversed. Before, A21 and A22 each came before A30, A31 and A36 in 100 in 100 traces; in the later window, A30, A31 and A36 come first in 199 of 200 traces. All activities still co-occur in every trace in both windows, so nothing became optional or absent. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006]
- A21 now leads almost exclusively to A25 (about 99.5 in 100 of its outgoing transitions, one step away), whereas before it spread over A36, A30 and A31 (about 38, 34 and 28 in 100) and reached A25 only four steps later. [DIR002, LAG002]
- The three block activities changed their exit: before, A30, A31 and A36 each passed on to A25 in roughly 28–37 in 100 of their outgoing transitions; in the later window they pass on to A22 (about 31–34 in 100) and virtually never to A25. [DIR003, DIR004, DIR005]
- Relative positions moved accordingly: A30, A31 and A36 each shifted earlier (median from about 0.58 to about 0.41 of the trace), A22 later (about 0.37 to 0.47) and A21 later (about 0.53 to 0.63). [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005]
- Lag profiles spanning the old ordering (for example A21→A30 or A30→A25) are supported by only 1 of 200 traces in the later window, consistent with those ordered pairs no longer occurring within five steps; their later-window profiles rest on a single observation each. [LAG003, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006, LAG007, LAG008]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the segment A22 → A21 and the three-activity block A30/A31/A36 swapped places: before, traces ran A20 → A22 → A21 → (A30, A31, A36 in varying order) → A25; in the later window they run A20 → (A30, A31, A36 in varying order) → A22 → A21 → A25, with A21 now directly preceding A25. [DIR001, DIR002, LAG001, LAG002, REL001, REL004, POS001, POS003]

Supporting signs: the order reversal is shown directly and consistently on all six activity pairs, each flipping from 100 in 100 to about 99.5 in 100 in the same direction with full 200-trace support in both windows; the boundary successors change exactly as the swap predicts (A20 now enters the block, the block now exits to A22, A21 exits to A25); the two cross-boundary distances mirror each other (A20→A22 from one to four steps, A21→A25 from four to one); and participation is unchanged (co-occurrence 1.0 throughout), so this is a reordering of the same activities rather than activities being added, removed or made optional. With 24 qualifying items against roughly 0.41 expected if nothing had changed, the item set as a whole is far beyond what random re-splits of stable behaviour produce. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, LAG001, LAG002]

Limitations: the six lag items bridging old and new order rest on a single later-window observation each, so they cannot verify behaviour inside the block; positions are event positions, not clock times; and about 1 in 200 later traces still shows the old order. A competing explanation the evidence cannot fully exclude is an additional change in how the A30/A31/A36 block orders internally, since block-internal lags are effectively unobserved in the later window; all observed items, however, are consistent with a pure swap of the two segments. [LAG003, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006, LAG007, LAG008]

Other differences: none beyond the residual old-order traces noted above; the slight widening of the position spread of the moved activities accompanies the same reordering. [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005]
