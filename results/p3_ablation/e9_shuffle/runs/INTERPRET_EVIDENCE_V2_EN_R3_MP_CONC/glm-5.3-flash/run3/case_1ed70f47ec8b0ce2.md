### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that two segments of the case exchanged their positions. In the earlier window, the slot between A35 and A20 held exactly one of A14/A16/A19 (always leading to A20), while the slot between A24 and A25 held A15, A17 and A18 running consecutively in varying order; in the later window the contents of these slots are exchanged — A35 is followed by A15/A17/A18 in varying order leading to A20, and A24 is followed by exactly one of A14/A16/A19 leading to A25. A35, A20, A24 and A25 kept their roles throughout. [REL008, REL015, REL018, REL009, REL016, REL019, REL001, REL021, DIR001, DIR007, DIR008, POS002, POS001, LAG001, LAG002]

The hypothesis is supported directly and consistently: all 21 observed pair relations flipped, the two groups' position distributions moved in opposite directions by about a quarter to a third of the case, the successor changes sit precisely at the two segment boundaries, and both distance changes point the same way, while participation of every involved activity is unchanged. With 39 qualifying items against roughly 0.46 expected for an unchanged process, chance sampling cannot account for the evidence as a whole, and each item's difference exceeds about 995 of 1000 random re-splits of the same windows. [REL001, REL015, POS002, POS001, DIR001, DIR008, LAG001, LAG002]

The evidence cannot distinguish whether the two groups moved as intact blocks or through separate relocations of the six activities that produce the same pair-wise flips, and it covers none of the activities that run between A20 and A24, so any change there would be invisible.

Other differences: the split across the mutually exclusive A14/A16/A19 moved a few points (A16 from about 35 to 29 in 100 cases, A14 from about 30 to 33, A19 from about 36 to 39); with 200 cases per window this is within normal sampling variation and is not part of the hypothesis above.
