### Observed differences

- The largest difference is a reversal in the choice between two alternative activity blocks that sit between A15 and A25. In the earlier window, about 82 in 100 traces contained A19, A20 and A21 together, while about 19 in 100 contained A16, A17 and A18 together; in the later window the shares are about 21 in 100 for A19–A21 and about 79 in 100 for A16–A18. The two blocks never occur in the same trace in either window. [OCC011, OCC014, REL001, REL004, REL010, REL023]
- A15's outgoing transitions flipped accordingly: earlier, roughly 29 in 100 of its outgoing edges led to A19, 29 to A21 and 24 to A20 (only about 3–9 to A16/A17/A18); in the later window, roughly 29 led to A18, 29 to A16 and 22 to A17, with A19/A20/A21 down to about 6–8 in 100. [DIR012]
- The blocks are unchanged as units: A16, A17 and A18 always appear together when they appear (co-occurrence equals participation in both windows), in mixed order — in the later window A16 precedes A17 in about 42 in 100 and follows it in about 38 in 100 of the traces containing both — and the same held for A19–A21 earlier; A15 always precedes the selected block, which in turn precedes or interleaves with A25. [REL001, REL008, REL013, REL023]
- Event positions barely move: the median relative position of the six activities shifts by at most about 5 in 100 of trace length, so the difference is in how often each path is taken, not in where the activities sit. [POS002, POS004, POS005, POS007]
- A10 and A11 are executed more often per trace: mean occurrences rose from about 1.7 to about 1.9 while both still appear in every trace; the share of traces with repeats stayed at about 43 in 100. [OCC009, OCC010, DIR009]
- Smaller downstream shifts: A27 and A28 now appear in about 69 in 100 traces (up from about 61); A26 leads to A25 in about 45 in 100 of its outgoing transitions (up from 37) and to A29 in about 55 (down from 63); A23 leads to A22 in about 44 in 100 (up from 38). [OCC021, OCC022, DIR021, DIR019]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the selection frequency between two alternative blocks between A15 and A25 shifted: the block A16–A17–A18, executed as a complete triple after A15, rose from about 19 in 100 traces to about 79 in 100, while the alternative block A19–A20–A21 fell from about 82 in 100 to about 21 in 100. Because both blocks are observed in substantial shares in both windows, remain mutually exclusive, and keep the same internal mixed order and the same framing by A15 and A25, this reads as existing paths being taken with different frequencies, not as activities being added, removed, bypassed or relocated. [OCC011, OCC014, DIR012, REL001, REL004, REL023]

The signs are direct and consistent: the participation change of about 0.605 for all six activities, the flip in A15's successor profile, and the relational co-occurrence reversals all describe the same single behavioural difference (they are not independent confirmations), each with an empirical strength of about 0.995 — larger than in about 199 of 200 random re-splits of the pooled data. Limitations: the evidence cannot show where the choice between the blocks is made (it may lie upstream of A15), and it cannot rule out that the two windows simply sampled different case-type mixes, which would look identical in these aggregates; the evidence also includes no chance-expectation baseline for the number of flagged items, so the judgement rests on the directness and consistency of the relational and directional items rather than on a screening comparison. [DIR012, REL001, REL010, POS002]
