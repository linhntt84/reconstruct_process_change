### Observed differences

- The sharpest difference concerns the fixed sequence A19 → A25 → A20. In the earlier window each of the three activities appeared in about 94–95 of 200 traces (roughly 0.47–0.48 occurrences per trace); in the later window each appeared in about 199 of 200 traces (about 1.0 occurrence per trace). Their relative positions barely moved (medians around 0.32–0.39 of the trace in both windows), so the block runs at the same place in the flow but far more often. [OCC018, OCC019, OCC024, POS001, POS002, POS003]
- The block's neighbours changed from sometimes running without it to virtually always running with it. Before, A26, A41 and A42 each occurred without A19/A20/A25 in about 53 in 100 traces; afterwards the block co-occurs with each of them in about 995 in 1000 traces. A45, which previously co-occurred with A19 in only about 28 in 100 of its traces, now never appears without the block. [REL003, REL004, REL005, REL008, REL009, REL010, REL012, REL013, REL014, REL006, REL011, REL015]
- Ordering around the block is stable in both windows: A19 precedes A25 and A25 precedes A20 in about 98 in 100 joint occurrences, with A41 before the block and A20 before A26. What changed is A41's successor: before, A41 was followed directly by A26 in about 52 in 100 of its executions; afterwards A19 follows A41 in about 92 in 100 and A26 almost never. [REL001, REL002, REL007, DIR019, DIR025, DIR020, DIR041]
- Accordingly, the distance from A41 to A26 grew: before, A26 followed A41 after one step in about 52 in 100 cases (four steps in about 42 in 100); afterwards it follows after four steps in about 89 in 100 cases — exactly the three block activities now standing between them. [LAG010]
- Repetition increased for A42, A44 and A45: A42 rose from 1.60 to 1.72 occurrences per trace (traces containing repeats from about 37 to 46 in 100), and A44 and A45 from 0.76 to 0.885 with repeat shares up from about 12 to 21 in 100. [OCC040, OCC042, OCC043, LAG013, LAG016]
- Smaller shifts: several existing routing shares moved (after A03, A13 gained over A12; after A21 and A24, A31 gained over A24; after A40, A41 gained over A39; after A45, A42 gained over A46), participation rose slightly for A02 (about 7 to 12 in 100 traces) and fell for A08 (about 14 to 8 in 100) and A18 (about 31 to 25 in 100). A47's outgoing profile changed almost completely, but it rests on only 7 versus 6 observed transitions. [DIR003, DIR021, DIR024, DIR040, DIR045, OCC002, OCC008, OCC017, DIR047]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the sequential block A19 → A25 → A20, which sits between A41 and A26 in the flow, was executed in only about 47 in 100 cases in the earlier window and is executed in virtually every case (about 199 of 200) in the later window, with A41 previously passing directly to A26 when the block was skipped. The participation jump is direct and large for all three activities, with differences larger than in about 995 of 1000 random re-splits; the relational items show the block now co-occurring with A26, A41 and A42 in nearly every trace; A41's dominant successor switched from A26 to A19; and the A41-to-A26 distance grew from one to four steps, matching the three block activities. Because the internal order A19 → A25 → A20 and the surrounding order are the same in both windows, this reads as an existing block being executed far more often, not as a reordering or a replacement of paths. Limitations: the evidence cannot rule out that the two windows draw on different case mixes (for example a subpopulation whose cases always contain the block) rather than a change in the process itself, and one later-window trace still skips the block. [OCC018, OCC019, OCC024, REL001, REL002, REL007, DIR041, LAG010]

Other differences: A42, A44 and A45 now repeat more often within a trace; several routing shares between existing alternatives shifted modestly; participation of A02 rose while that of A08 and A18 fell; and the near-complete turnover in A47's outgoing transitions is based on very few observations and is likely sampling noise.
