### Observed differences

- The clearest difference is in routing among A31, A32 and A37. In the earlier window, about 39 in 100 outgoing transitions of A31 went to A37 and about 28 in 100 went to A32; in the later window these shares are roughly reversed, with about 27 going to A37 and about 39 to A32. Over the same traces, transitions from A32 to A37 rose from about 30% to 39% of A32's outgoing transitions while A32→A31 fell from about 38% to 29%, and from A37 the share to A31 rose from about 30% to 37% while the share to A32 fell from about 37% to 31%. [DIR017, DIR018, DIR023]
- Consistently, the direct succession A31→A37 became less immediate: among pairs where A31 is followed by A37 within five steps, immediate succession dropped from about 74 to 57 in 100, with two-step gaps rising from about 26 to 43 in 100. In the reverse direction, A37→A31 became more often immediate (about 63 to 69 in 100). [LAG001, LAG002]
- A31 and A37 still co-occur in every trace in both windows, still occur in both orders (A31 first in about 53% of traces before, 47% after), and their average positions are essentially unchanged at about 0.40 of the trace in both windows. [REL001, POS001, POS002]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that, in the later window, successions from A31 to A37 run less often directly and more often via A32: the direct A31→A37 transition loses share (about 39% to 27% of A31's outgoing transitions) while A31→A32 (about 28% to 39%) and A32→A37 (about 30% to 39%) gain, and immediate A31→A37 successions fall from about 74 to 57 in 100 with two-step gaps rising in their place; as part of the same redistribution, A37→A31 becomes somewhat more frequent and more often immediate. [DIR017, DIR018, DIR023, LAG001, LAG002]

This behaves like a shift in how often existing paths are taken rather than a replacement of one path by another: A31, A32 and A37 each appear in every trace in both windows, all six transitions between them are observed in both windows, the direct edge is still used (about 27%), the activities keep their average positions, and A31 and A37 still co-occur in all traces in both orders. The routing, lag and order items all point the same way, with differences exceeding those of about 69 to 99 in 100 random re-splits; however, they plausibly mirror one underlying trace behaviour, so they corroborate rather than independently confirm the hypothesis. [REL001, POS001, POS002, LAG001, LAG002, DIR017, DIR018, DIR023]

An important competing explanation that cannot be fully ruled out is sampling variation between the two 200-trace windows: the evidence carries no chance-expectation baseline, so the possibility that this cluster is an unusually aligned random re-split, while unlikely given the strengths above, cannot be quantified. [DIR017, LAG001]
