### Observed differences

- The dominant difference is a swap between two alternative activity groups. In the earlier window, A21, A22 and A23 each ran in about 75 in 100 traces, while A24, A25 and A26 each ran in about 25 in 100; in the later window the shares are reversed, with A21–A23 at about 25 in 100 and A24–A26 at about 75 in 100 (49–152 supporting traces per window, empirical strength 0.995 for all six). [OCC021, OCC022, OCC023, OCC024, OCC025, OCC026]
- The groups are alternatives, not replacements: cross-group pairs such as A21 with A24 share a trace in only about 0–1 in 100 cases in both windows, while members within each group run together — A21 with A22 in about 75 in 100 traces before versus 25 in 100 after, and A24 with A25 in about 25 in 100 before versus 76 in 100 after. [REL020, REL022, REL029, REL041, REL046]
- The split sits directly after A20, which occurs in every trace in both windows: before, about 75 in 100 of A20's outgoing transitions led to A21, A22 or A23 and about 25 to A24–A26; after, about 25 led to A21–A23 and about 75 to A24–A26. Whichever group runs, A20 precedes it, in both windows. [OCC020, DIR020, REL011, REL014]
- A30, which also occurs in every trace (about 1.7 occurrences per trace in both windows), switched company: before it co-occurred with A21–A23 in about 75 in 100 traces; after it co-occurs with A24–A26 in about 75 in 100. [OCC030, REL025, REL043]
- A10 became more frequent and more narrowly placed: about 6 in 100 traces before (11 of 200) versus about 21 in 100 after (42 of 200); its median position within the trace moved from about 0.60 to about 0.86 with a much narrower spread, and it now typically follows A20 or A24–A26 (A20 before A10 in about 19 in 100 traces after versus 3 in 100 before). About 90 in 100 of A10's observed outgoing transitions now go to A33 (about 18 in 100 before), and A32 now leads to A10 in about 20 in 100 of its outgoing transitions, previously unobserved. [OCC010, POS001, REL001, REL005, DIR010, DIR032]
- Smaller shifts: A02 fell from about 9 in 100 to about 1 in 100 traces (only 2 traces after), and A06 and A08 also became rarer, but each rests on fewer than 20 traces per window; A15 and A16 still occur in every trace but recur more often (about 36–37 in 100 traces before versus 46–47 after); A17 and A18 rose about 5 points and A31 fell about 2–3 points in participation. [OCC002, OCC006, OCC008, OCC015, OCC016, OCC017, OCC018, OCC031]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that two existing alternative paths swapped how often they are taken: the path in which A21, A22 and A23 run together after A20, alongside A30, dropped from about 75 in 100 cases to about 25 in 100, while the alternative path in which A24, A25 and A26 run together in the same slot rose from about 25 in 100 to about 75 in 100. The support is direct and consistent across three levels: participation swings of roughly 50 percentage points for all six activities, mirrored flips in within-group co-occurrence, and a matching flip in the routing out of A20. Because both groups remain observed in about a quarter of traces in each window, A20 still precedes whichever group runs, and repetition levels are essentially unchanged, this reads as the same two paths being taken with different frequencies rather than activities being added, removed or bypassed. [OCC021, OCC022, OCC023, OCC024, OCC025, OCC026, DIR020, REL011, REL014, REL020, REL022, REL041]

Limitations: no screening baseline was provided, so the number of qualifying items could not be checked against the count expected for an unchanged process; the judgement rests on the size, directness and consistency of the differences. The evidence also cannot distinguish a changed branching decision after A20 from a shift in the mix of cases entering this part of the process, and it offers no insight into why the split moved. [DIR020, OCC021]

Other differences: A10's participation roughly quadrupled (about 6 to 21 in 100 traces), it now appears late in traces typically after A20 or A24–A26, its outgoing flow concentrates on A33, and a new A32→A10 transition appeared; whether this belongs to the same underlying change or is independent cannot be determined from the evidence. Smaller, weakly supported shifts concern A02, A06 and A08 (rarer, few observations), a modest rise in recurrence of A15 and A16, and participation changes of a few points for A17, A18, A31, A32 and A33.
