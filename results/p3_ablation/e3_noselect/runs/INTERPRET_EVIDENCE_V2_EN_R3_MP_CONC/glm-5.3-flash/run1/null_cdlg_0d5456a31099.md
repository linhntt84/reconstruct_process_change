### Observed differences

- Successor choice at A02 shifted toward A04: in the earlier window, about 43 in 100 of A02's outgoing transitions went to A04, 29 to A09 and 28 to A05; in the later window, about 54 in 100 go to A04, 27 to A09 and only 19 to A05. A02 was executed more often overall (154 vs 201 outgoing transitions), and the extra executions went mainly to A04 (about 66 vs 109 transitions), while A02→A05 changed little in absolute terms (about 43 vs 38). [DIR002]
- A02 repeated more often within traces: mean occurrences per trace rose from 0.77 to about 1.0 while the share of traces containing A02 barely moved (44 vs 46 in 100 traces), so the increase comes from traces where A02 runs more than once (recurrence support 0.20 vs 0.27). [OCC002]
- A04 was used more and repeated more: it appears in about 27 in 100 later traces versus about 20 in 100 earlier (54 vs 39 traces), mean occurrences rose from 0.33 to 0.55, and within-trace repeats, still uncommon, nearly doubled (recurrence support 0.075 vs 0.14). [OCC004]
- The A05/A09 pair shifted toward A08: A05's outgoing transitions led to A08 in about 51 in 100 earlier versus 59 in 100 later (and to A09 correspondingly less often), and A09's led to A05 in about 51 versus 59 in 100 (to A08 less often); both items describe the same activity pair. [DIR003, DIR004]
- All other measured differences are small — participation changes of at most about 3.5 in 100 traces (e.g., A01 56 vs 54 in 100, A10 30 vs 26 in 100) with low empirical strength (0.15–0.54) — consistent with sampling variation between two 200-trace windows. ### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that, in the later window, execution became more concentrated on the A02–A04 route with more repetition of both activities: A02 was followed by A04 in about 54 in 100 of its outgoing transitions (up from about 43) while its alternative successor A05 fell to about 19 in 100 (from 28), A02 occurred about 1.0 times per trace on average (up from 0.77) at nearly unchanged participation, and A04 appeared in about 27 in 100 traces (up from about 20) with more within-trace repeats. The routing change is measured directly on A02's successor distribution and matches the occurrence side: the growth in A02 executions (154 vs 201 outgoing transitions) and the rises in A04's participation and recurrence point the same way, though these items likely reflect one underlying shift rather than independent confirmations [DIR002, OCC002, OCC004]. Limitations: the empirical strengths (0.91–0.97) are not 1.0, so a stable process could still produce differences of this size when two 200-trace samples are re-split at random; the repetition component rests on modest counts (A04 present in 39 vs 54 traces, repeats in roughly 15–28 traces per window); and no screening baseline was provided, so the expected number of qualifying items under no change could not be checked. An important competing explanation the evidence cannot fully rule out is that these differences are an unusually large random fluctuation in path shares.

Other differences: the A05/A09 pair routes more often to A08 in the later window (A05→A08 about 51 vs 59 in 100; A09→A05 about 51 vs 59 in 100, i.e., A09→A08 down), a moderate signal (empirical strength about 0.70) that the evidence cannot separate from sampling variation and that is not part of the main hypothesis [DIR003, DIR004].
