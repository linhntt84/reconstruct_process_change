### Observed differences

- The main difference concerns six activities that form two trios between A25 and A26: A28/A30/A31 and A32/A33/A34. In the earlier window each trio ran in only about half of the cases (49–52 in 100 traces); in the later window each runs in nearly all cases (98–100 in 100) [OCC019, OCC020, OCC021, OCC022, OCC023, OCC024].
- In the earlier window the two trios were strict alternatives: cross-trio pairs such as A28 with A32 occurred together in 0 of 100 traces, with exactly one trio or the other present per case (about 49 vs 52 in 100). In the later window these cross-trio pairs occur together in about 98 in 100 traces, always with the A28/A30/A31 trio before the A32/A33/A34 trio [REL015, REL016, REL017, REL019, REL022, REL024]. Within each trio the order stays mixed (both orders in similar proportions) in both windows [REL013, REL018, REL025].
- Routing changed accordingly. A25, present in every trace in both windows, earlier split its outgoing transitions roughly evenly over all six block activities (about 15–19 in 100 each); in the later window it leads almost only to A28, A30 and A31 (about 30–38 in 100 each), with transitions to A33 no longer observed and to A32/A34 seen in only about 1 in 100 [DIR016]. The first trio earlier passed to A26 in roughly a third of its outgoing transitions; in the later window that transition is essentially unobserved and the trio instead flows into A32/A33/A34 [DIR018, DIR020, DIR021].
- Positions shifted coherently: the A28/A30/A31 trio moved earlier (mean position about 0.74 to 0.68), the A32/A33/A34 trio later (about 0.73 to 0.77), and A26 later (about 0.80 to 0.83) [POS003, POS006, POS002]. Step distances agree: pairs that were 1–3 steps apart (A25 to the second trio; the first trio to A26) now sit 4–5 steps apart [LAG004, LAG007, LAG010, LAG013].
- Smaller, separate differences: A10 and A11 repeat slightly more often (mean about 1.55 to 1.66 occurrences per trace) while A38 repeats less (about 1.76 to 1.60); there are small branch-share shifts of roughly 5 in 100 cases at A20/A27/A29 and at A37 between A14 and A15, and A16 routes to A25 more often (about 49% to 61% of its outgoing transitions) [OCC009, OCC010, OCC027, DIR014, DIR026, DIR010].

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the two three-activity blocks between A25 and A26 — A28/A30/A31 and A32/A33/A34 — changed from mutually exclusive alternatives into two sequentially executed, effectively mandatory stages: in the earlier window a case ran exactly one of the two blocks (about 49 vs 52 in 100 cases), while in the later window virtually every case runs both, first A28/A30/A31 and then A32/A33/A34, with A26 now following only after the second block. This is shown directly: cross-block pairs went from never co-occurring to co-occurring in about 98 in 100 traces in a fixed order, participation of all six activities roughly doubled, the direct routes A25→A32/A33/A34 and A28/A30/A31→A26 disappeared, and positions and step distances shifted coherently [REL015, REL019, OCC019, OCC022, DIR016, DIR018, POS003, POS006, LAG004, LAG007]. A competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is a shift in the mix of case types between the windows (for example, the later sample containing mainly cases whose variant includes both blocks) rather than a changed routing rule; the within-block order also remains unfixed, so the change concerns when the blocks run, not the order inside them [REL013, REL025].

Other differences: slightly more repetitions of A10 and A11 and fewer of A38, small branch-share shifts at A20/A27/A29 and A37 (A14 vs A15), and more frequent routing from A16 to A25; these are minor and not part of the main hypothesis [OCC009, OCC010, OCC027, DIR014, DIR026, DIR010].
