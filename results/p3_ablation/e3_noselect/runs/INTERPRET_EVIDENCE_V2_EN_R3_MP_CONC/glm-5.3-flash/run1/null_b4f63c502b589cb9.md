### Observed differences

- The clearest difference concerns how often A15 and A16 are repeated. In the earlier window both activities appeared in every trace and averaged about 1.8 occurrences per trace, with a repeat present in about 44 of 100 traces; in the later window they still appeared in every trace but averaged about 1.6 occurrences, with a repeat in about 36 of 100 traces. Both differences are larger than about 93% of those seen in random re-splits of the pooled data. [OCC014, OCC015]
- The edges around this pair moved the same way. In the earlier window A15 passed to A16 in about 66% of its outgoing transitions and to A08 in about 34%; in the later window the split was about 61% and 39%. A16 returned to A15 in about 45% of its outgoing transitions before and about 37% after, while its exit to A39 grew from about 26% to 30%. Entry into the pair was stable: A14 passed to A15 in about 97% of its outgoing transitions in both windows. [DIR014, DIR015, DIR016]
- A second group moved the opposite way: A43 and A44 rose from about 0.76 to about 0.89 occurrences per trace, their participation from about 60 to 62 of 100 traces and their repeat share from about 13 to 19 of 100 traces (differences larger than about 90% of random re-splits); A41 rose from about 1.6 to 1.7 occurrences. Consistently, A41 sent about 46% of its outgoing transitions to A43 before and about 49% after (away from A42), and A44 returned to A41 in about 38% of cases before and 42% after (away from A45). [OCC035, OCC037, OCC038, DIR041, DIR042, DIR044]
- Routing between the near-mandatory activities A20, A22 and A23 shifted: A20 sent about 27% of its outgoing transitions to A22 before and about 39% after (to A23 down from about 39% to 30%); A22 sent about 23% to A23 before and about 35% after (to A20 down from about 40% to 29%); A29 sent about 27% to A20 before and about 37% after; A23 sent about 30% to A30 before and about 37% after. All these activities occur in essentially every trace in both windows, so existing paths are taken with different frequencies rather than replaced; A22 also gained a few repeats (about 5 of 200 traces, from none). [DIR020, DIR022, DIR023, DIR029, OCC019]
- The successor profiles of A02, A04, A06, A10 and A46 look almost completely different between windows, but each rests on only about 5–17 outgoing transitions per window, so these differences carry little weight. [DIR002, DIR004, DIR006, DIR010, DIR046]
- Remaining occurrence differences are small (at most about 5 percentage points of participation, on counts of roughly 10–72 traces, e.g. A10 up from 5% to 8.5%, A19 down from 35% to 30.5%). [OCC009, OCC017, OCC021]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the A15–A16 cycle is repeated fewer times per trace in the later window: both activities remain mandatory (present in every trace in both windows), but their average occurrences fell from about 1.8 to 1.6 each, the share of traces with a repeat fell from about 44 to 36 in 100, and the loop-backs A15→A16 and A16→A15 lost share to the exits A15→A08 and A16→A39, while the entry A14→A15 stayed at about 97%. [OCC014, OCC015, DIR014, DIR015, DIR016]

Supporting signs: the change appears directly in the occurrence components (mean count and repeat share down at unchanged participation) and in the direction profiles of both activities; it rests on large supports (200 of 200 traces; 320–366 outgoing transitions per window); and the occurrence and direction items tell one consistent story rather than being independent confirmations. Limitations: with 200 traces per window, a repetition-rate difference of this size cannot be fully separated from sampling variability, and the evidence cannot indicate why the cycle is iterated less often. [OCC014, OCC015, DIR015, DIR016]

Other differences:
- The A41–A43–A44 cycle developed in the opposite direction: A43 and A44 are executed more often per trace (about 0.76 → 0.89) and repeat more often, with more traffic from A41 into A43 and from A44 back to A41; the direction evidence here is somewhat weaker. [OCC035, OCC037, OCC038, DIR041, DIR042, DIR044]
- Routing shares between A20, A22 and A23 (all present in nearly every trace in both windows) shifted towards A20→A22 and A22→A23, and A22 gained a small number of repeats. [DIR020, DIR022, DIR023, DIR029, OCC019]
- The apparent wholesale changes in the successors of A02, A04, A06, A10 and A46 are based on too few observations to interpret. [DIR002, DIR004, DIR006, DIR010, DIR046]
