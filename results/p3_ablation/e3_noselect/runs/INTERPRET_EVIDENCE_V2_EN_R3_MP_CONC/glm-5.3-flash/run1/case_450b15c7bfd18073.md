### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the process gained an either-or choice in the segment between A21 and A23: in the earlier window every trace ran the fixed sequence A21 → A22 → A23, whereas in the later window about half of the traces still run A21 → A22 → A23 and the other half replace A22 with the three previously unobserved activities A27, A28 and A33, which execute between A21 and A23 in varying order (no fixed order among the three, each of them preceding A23 in the traces where it appears); A22 and the trio never occur in the same trace. The change is shown directly and consistently: relational items record flips of about 0.49 on a 0–1 scale in co-occurrence and order for A21, A22 and A23 versus the trio; the successor redistribution at A21 and the new outgoing edges of A27, A28 and A33 show the new routing; participation shows A22 falling from all traces to about half and the trio rising from none to about half; and the A21→A23 distance moved from always 2 event steps to 2 or 4 steps, matching an insertion of two extra activities. Evidence from all five indicator types (occurrence, direction, position, relation, lag) describes this one difference. An important competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that the trio path existed before as a rare variant that was not exercised in the earlier 200-trace sample, so the change could be a shift in the mix of two pre-existing variants rather than a newly inserted block; the exact split (about 51/49) is also a sample estimate. [OCC018, OCC019, OCC020, OCC025, DIR014, DIR017, DIR018, DIR023, POS004, REL001, REL005, REL006, REL009, REL012, LAG001]

Other differences: the small participation shifts (about 4–6 percentage points) and moderate successor-share shifts (up to about 14 percentage points) involving A01–A08, A16–A19, A24, A29–A32 and A40 are not part of this hypothesis; their size and reference strength are also compatible with sampling variation between two 200-trace windows.
