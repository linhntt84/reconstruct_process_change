### Observed differences

- Most of the flagged items are negligible. For example, the outgoing-transition shares from A16 and from A44 are practically identical in the two windows (differences below 0.0001, and no random re-split produced a smaller difference), and a few other direction and occurrence items are almost as weak [DIR010, DIR027, DIR003, DIR006].

- The largest participation movement is a mirrored drift between two blocks of four activities. In the earlier window, A01, A03, A04 and A05 each appeared in about 46 in 100 traces while A02, A06, A07 and A08 each appeared in about 55 in 100; in the later window the first block rose to about 53 and the second fell to about 47 in 100, with the two block shares summing to 100 in 100 traces in both windows. Each single-activity change is about 8 in 100 traces, larger than in roughly 82 in 100 random re-splits. Successor shares inside the blocks also shifted, e.g. from A02 the share of observed outgoing transitions to A08 fell from about 38% to 24% while A07 rose from about 28% to 39% [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, OCC007, OCC008, DIR002].

- The strongest single item concerns the branch after A41. In the earlier window about 55 in 100 of A41's observed outgoing transitions went to A43 and about 45 to A42; in the later window this reversed to about 47 to A43 and 53 to A42 (based on roughly 340 outgoing transitions per window). A42 then appeared in about 61 vs 66 in 100 traces and repeated slightly more often (mean occurrences about 0.77 vs 0.91), while A43 and A44, whose figures are identical, slipped from about 63 to 61 in 100 traces (mean occurrences 0.94 vs 0.82). This difference is larger than in about 96 in 100 random re-splits, but its absolute size is modest [DIR025, OCC027, OCC028, OCC029].

- Two smaller mirrored shifts of the same kind appear elsewhere. From A17, the share of outgoing transitions to A19 rose from about 22% to 35% while A18 fell from about 29% to 24% and A28 from about 49% to 40%; accordingly A18 appeared in about 52 vs 44 in 100 traces and A19 in about 48 vs 56. From A36 and A37 the preferred successor swapped (transitions from A36 to A37 fell from about 56% to 46%; from A37 to A29 fell from about 56% to 46%), with A34 and A35 down from about 51 to 48 and A36 and A37 up from about 50 to 53 in 100 traces; the direction items here are larger than in about 85–89 in 100 random re-splits, the small participation changes only about 31 in 100 [OCC017, OCC018, DIR011, DIR021, DIR022, OCC020, OCC021, OCC022, OCC023].

- Repetition rose slightly for a few activities: A10 and A11 still appeared in every trace in both windows, but traces containing a repeat rose from about 35 to 41 in 100 — a weak signal, larger than in only about 55 in 100 random re-splits. A38's participation rose from about 14 to 21 in 100 traces, but this rests on only 27 vs 41 supporting traces, and A39's repeats rose from about 14 to 21 in 100 traces [OCC009, OCC010, OCC024, OCC025].

### Inference

Support level: Insufficient evidence

The evidence is insufficient to infer a change in the process. Every observed difference is small — participation shifts of at most about 8 in 100 traces per activity and successor-share shifts of at most about 13 percentage points — and even the strongest item, the redirection of A41's outgoing transitions from A43 toward A42, would be exceeded by about 5 in 100 random re-splits of a stable process, so sampling variation between the two 200-trace windows remains a plausible explanation [DIR025, OCC027].
