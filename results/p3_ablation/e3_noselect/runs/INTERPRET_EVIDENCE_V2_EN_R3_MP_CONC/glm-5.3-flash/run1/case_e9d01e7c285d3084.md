### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, A30, A31 and A36 each occurred exactly once in every trace (mean about 1.0 occurrence per trace, participation 100%); in the later window, each occurs about 3.5 times per trace, with repeats in about 68 in 100 traces (previously essentially none), while participation remains 100% [OCC027, OCC028, OCC033].
- In the earlier window, A36 was followed by A25 in about 97 in 100 of its outgoing transitions; in the later window, A36 is followed by A30 in about 70 in 100 and by A25 in only about 28 in 100, while A30→A31 (about 98%) and A31→A36 (about 97–99%) stay unchanged [DIR036, DIR030, DIR031].
- Pairs that previously followed a fixed order now appear interleaved or repeated: A14 before A30 fell from about 99.5% of traces to 32%, A14 before A36 from about 98.5% to 32%, and A30 before A36 from about 98% to 32%, with "interleaved or repeated" rising to about 68 in 100 for each pair; A05 before A30 and A05 before A36 fell from about 53–54% to about 16% [REL004, REL005, REL006, REL002, REL003].
- A30 and A36 now occur at later and much more variable event positions (A30 mean 0.41→0.44, spread 0.07→0.12; A36 mean 0.48→0.49, spread 0.07→0.12), while A14 (0.14→0.12) and A05 (0.08→0.06) sit slightly earlier; the A30→A36 gap was two steps for about 98 in 100 observed pairs before and for about 12 in 100 after, with some pairs now five or more steps apart [POS003, POS004, POS002, POS001, LAG002].

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the segment A30 → A31 → A36, previously executed exactly once per trace in a fixed order, is now repeated within traces: on average about 3.5 passes per trace, with about 68 in 100 traces running it more than once, A36 returning to A30 in about 70 in 100 of its outgoing transitions, and the exit to A25 preserved at about 28 in 100 — almost exactly one exit per 3.5 passes. The occurrence indicators show this directly (participation unchanged at 100% while mean occurrences rise from about 1.0 to about 3.5, a difference larger than in about 199 of 200 random re-splits), the successor profile shows the new dominant A36→A30 loop-back, and the order, position and gap indicators all change in the way expected if the segment repeats rather than if single executions were reordered [OCC027, OCC028, OCC033, DIR036, DIR030, DIR031, REL004, REL005, REL006, POS003, POS004, LAG002]. Limitations: the indicators are based on event positions rather than clock time, several items describe the same underlying behaviour and are not independent confirmations, and the evidence cannot show what determines the number of passes or whether every later case loops; no chance-expectation baseline is included in the evidence, so the judgement rests on the size, directness and consistency of these differences.

Other differences: the modest redirection within the A05/A07/A09 cluster (the path into A14 now starts more often from A07/A09 than from A05), the slightly earlier positions of A05 and A14, the mild increase in A15/A16 repetition, and the small participation shifts for A02, A08, A22 and A21 are not part of the loop hypothesis; they could be side effects of the lengthened traces or separate small routing changes, which this evidence cannot distinguish [DIR005, DIR007, LAG001, POS001, POS002, OCC015, OCC016, OCC002, OCC008, OCC022, OCC021].
