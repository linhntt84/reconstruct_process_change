### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that, in the later window, the flow through the A24/A25/A26 group is routed differently, so that the exit towards A30 is typically reached via A25 instead of via A24. In the earlier window the dominant route ran A26 → A24 → A30 (A26 sent about 50 in 100 of its transitions to A24; A24 sent about 39 in 100 to A30); in the later window it runs A24 → A26 → A25 → A30 (A24 sends about 41 in 100 to A26, A26 about 44 in 100 to A25, A25 about 49 in 100 to A30), and A20 feeds A24 about three times as often as before (about 5 vs 14 in 100) while feeding A25 less (about 10 vs 4 in 100). Supporting signs: the change is shown directly by the successor profiles of A24, A25 and A26 — the three largest routing shifts in the evidence, with empirical strengths about 0.89–0.99 — it is corroborated upstream by A20's routing shift, and the five strongest routing shifts all concern sources in the same A20–A26 block, which a coincidence of unrelated fluctuations would not typically produce. All involved activities and paths remain well observed in both windows and the group's participation rose only slightly (about 22 vs 26 in 100 traces), so this is a shift between existing alternatives rather than activities being added, removed or bypassed. [DIR020, DIR023, DIR024, DIR025, DIR026, OCC022, OCC023, OCC024]

The main competing explanation that cannot be ruled out is sampling variability: the three key profiles rest on moderate samples (about 44–53 outgoing transitions per source and window), and the evidence provides no screening baseline stating how many qualifying items an unchanged process would produce, so a coincidental cluster of share shifts in one region remains possible. [DIR024, DIR025, DIR026]
