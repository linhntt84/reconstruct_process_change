### Observed differences

- Out of activity A02, outgoing transitions were split evenly between A03 and A05 in the earlier window (50% each). In the later window, about 57.5% of these transitions go to A05 and about 42.5% to A03 [DIR001].
- Out of A03, the earlier window split evenly between A04 and A05 (50% each). In the later window, about 57.5% go to A04 and about 42.5% to A05 [DIR002].
- Out of A05, the earlier window split evenly between A03 and A04 (50% each). In the later window, about 57.5% go to A03 and about 42.5% to A04 [DIR003].
- The three shifts are equal in size (about 7.5 percentage points each) and point in a coordinated direction: the later window favours the branch sequence A02→A05, A05→A03, A03→A04 over the alternatives A02→A03, A03→A05, A05→A04. Each routing profile rests on about 200 outgoing transitions per window, and each difference is larger than in about 81 of 100 random re-splits of the pooled data — moderately unusual for a single indicator, but not extreme [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003].

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that existing branches are taken with different frequencies in the later window. At three decision points (out of A02, A03 and A05), even 50/50 splits became roughly 57.5/42.5, together favouring the branch sequence A02→A05→A03→A04 over A02→A03→A05→A04. The support is direct (routing shares measured at the exact decision points), consistent (three shifts of identical size in a coordinated direction), and based on substantial samples (about 200 outgoing transitions per source per window); every involved activity and edge is observed in both windows, so this is a redistribution of shares between existing alternatives, not a replaced or added path [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003].

Limitations: the shift is modest, and each item alone is exceeded by about 19 in 100 random re-splits, so a single indicator would not stand; the three items may partly express one underlying trace behaviour rather than three independent confirmations, and no screening baseline was provided to compare the number of qualifying items against a chance expectation [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003].

An important competing explanation the evidence cannot fully rule out is sampling variability between the two finite windows; the coordinated direction across three decision points makes pure chance less plausible, but the per-item strength leaves room for it [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003].
