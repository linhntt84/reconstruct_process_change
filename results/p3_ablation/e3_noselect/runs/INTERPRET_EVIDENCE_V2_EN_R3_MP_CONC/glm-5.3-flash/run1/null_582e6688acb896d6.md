### Observed differences

- The clearest difference concerns the choice points after A23, A30 and A31. In the earlier window, of the cases leaving A23 about 53 in 100 continued to A31 and about 46 to A30; in the later window about 55 in 100 continue to A30 and about 41 to A31. After A30, continuation to A31 rose from about 46 to about 56 in 100, while continuation to A24 fell from about 52 to about 42. After A31, continuation to A24 rose from about 47 to about 57, while continuation back to A30 fell from about 53 to about 43. Each shift rests on about 200 outgoing transitions per window and is larger than in about 96–97 of 100 random re-splits. [DIR023, DIR029, DIR030]
- Both visiting orders of A30 and A31 remain in use, and all four activities run in essentially every case in both windows (participation 1.0 for A23, A24 and A30; about 200 outgoing transitions for A31), so this is a change in how often existing paths are taken, not in paths appearing, disappearing or being bypassed. ### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that, in the later window, the existing paths through the cycle formed by A23, A30, A31 and A24 are taken with different frequencies: cases more often continue from A23 to A30, from A30 to A31 and from A31 to A24 — that is, they more often visit A30 before A31 — while the alternative order via A31 first is taken less often, with each of the three choice points shifting by about 10 percentage points and both orders remaining in use. The support is direct and consistent: the change is measured on the outgoing transitions of A23, A30 and A31 themselves, all three shifts agree in direction, each rests on about 200 outgoing transitions, and each difference exceeds about 96 of 100 random re-splits; the unchanged, near-universal participation of all four activities rules out that activities or paths were added, removed or bypassed. Limitations: the three edges are traversed by the same cases and are therefore not independent confirmations, each individual shift is moderate in size, and no screening baseline is present to check the family-wide chance expectation. [DIR023, DIR029, DIR030, OCC022, OCC023, OCC026]

The main competing explanation is an ordinary sampling imbalance between the two windows in how often cycle-taking cases were drawn; the re-split evidence makes this unlikely for each edge taken alone, but it cannot be excluded with certainty. [DIR023, DIR029, DIR030]

- Shifts of the same kind — existing alternatives taken with different frequencies — appear around A01, A05, A07, A09 and A14, around A20, A22 and A28, and after A03 and A13, as described above; they are not part of the main hypothesis. [DIR001, DIR005, DIR007, DIR009, DIR020, DIR022, DIR003, DIR013]
- The rare activities A04, A08 and A10 lost participation (each now in about 5 of 100 traces, previously about 9–10), though the underlying counts are small, and A15/A16 repeat slightly more often. [OCC003, OCC007, OCC009, OCC014, OCC015]
