### Observed differences

- Activity A01 was executed in every earlier case (200 of 200, once per case, and as the first event in the large majority of cases — median position 0). In the later window it appears in none of the 200 cases. This difference is larger than all but about 1 in 200 random re-splits of the pooled data. [OCC001, POS001]
- Activity A04 was executed in about 51 of 100 earlier cases; in the later window it also appears in none of the 200 cases. [OCC004, POS004]
- With A01 and A04 unobserved, their branching disappeared: earlier, A01's outgoing transitions led to A03 (about 46%), A04 (about 34%), A02 (about 16%) and rarely to A06 or A05 (about 5% combined), and A04's led to A02 (about 70%), A06 (about 19%) and A01 (about 11%). In the later window neither activity shows any observed outgoing transition. [DIR001, DIR004]
- Cases in the later window begin directly with A02 or A03: A02's mean event position moved from about 0.53 (median about 0.67) to 0, and A03's from about 0.44 (median 0.5) to 0, so each is now the first event whenever it occurs. [POS002, POS003]
- Routing from the surviving activities simplified: A02 was followed by A06 in about 79% of its earlier outgoing transitions (A04 about 14%, A01 about 7%) and in 100% of later ones; A03 was followed by A05 in about 95% of earlier transitions (A01 about 5%) and in 100% of later ones. [DIR002, DIR003]
- Earlier, A01 co-occurred with A02 in about 51 of 100 cases, with A03 in about 49 and with A04 in about 51, usually before them, and A04 co-occurred with A02 in about half the cases in both orders. In the later window A01 co-occurs with no activity, A04 never appears, and in every case neither of the two occurs. A02 and A03 never share a case in either window. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006]
- A05 and A06 still occur at similar rates (A05 about 49% earlier vs about 54% later; A06 about 51% vs about 47%), but no outgoing transition was observed for either in the later window; earlier such transitions were seen in only 14 cases for A06 and 6 for A05, so this observation rests on few cases. [OCC005, OCC006, DIR005, DIR006]
- The participation shifts among A02, A03, A05 and A06 (about 4–5 in 100) are of the size that random re-splits of unchanged behaviour produce (empirical strength about 0.53). [OCC002, OCC003, OCC005, OCC006, REL004]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

A hypothesis directly and consistently supported by the evidence is that the later window no longer executes A01 and A04: A01, previously the entry activity present in all 200 earlier cases, and A04, previously in about half of them, are unobserved in all 200 later cases, and cases now begin directly with A02 or A03, each followed by a single observed successor (A02 to A06, A03 to A05). The signs are direct — participation falls from 100% and about 51% to zero on 200-case windows, with empirical strength about 0.995 — and consistent across occurrence, direction, position and relation indicators, including the simultaneous disappearance of the edges from A02 and A03 into A01 and A04. The main limitation is that zero participation only means the activities were not observed: the evidence cannot distinguish a genuine removal of the A01/A04 part from a change in what the log records, and no screening baseline is included to compare the number of qualifying items against a chance expectation, although a drop from 200 of 200 to 0 of 200 is difficult to explain by sampling alone. [OCC001, OCC004, POS001, POS002, POS003, DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, REL001, REL002, REL003]

Other differences: the shares of the remaining alternatives moved slightly (A02 and A06 down about 4–5 in 100, A03 and A05 up by the same amount, empirical strength about 0.53, in line with sampling noise), and A05 and A06 show no observed outgoing transitions in the later window where earlier only a handful were seen (6 and 14 cases); neither observation is part of the main hypothesis. [OCC002, OCC003, OCC005, OCC006, REL004, DIR005, DIR006]
