### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that, in the later window, existing alternative paths are taken with different frequencies, with no activity added or removed. Most clearly, flow in the A01/A05/A07/A09 segment moved onto the stepwise chain A01→A05→A07→A09 (and onward to A14) and away from the skipping successions A01→A09 and A05→A14 and the reverse successions A07→A05 and A09→A07. The same behavioural family appears elsewhere: A22, A36, A37 and A44 run in roughly 9 more traces per 100 than before, A23, A38, A39, A45 and A46 in roughly 9–10 fewer, and A10 now favours A21 over A20. [DIR001, DIR005, DIR007, DIR009, DIR010, OCC010, OCC020, OCC021, OCC022, OCC023, OCC035, OCC036, OCC037, OCC038, OCC043, OCC044, OCC045]

The supporting signs are that the differences come directly from the routing and participation indicators, that several items agree in direction within each segment, and that the largest differences are unusual for random re-splits of unchanged data (for A01→A05, A05→A07, A07→A09 and A43→A44, only about 2–5 in 100 reference comparisons reach this size). In the A01 segment every involved activity keeps about 50 in 100 participation and every edge is observed in both windows, which fits a shift between existing paths rather than a replacement of one path by another. The evidence cannot rule out sampling noise for the smaller shifts (for example A24, A25), nor can it show whether the segment-level changes share a common source or are independent local changes. [DIR001, DIR005, DIR007, DIR043, DIR022, DIR025]
