### Observed differences

- The cycle around A06 ran in the opposite direction in the later window. In the earlier window, A06's observed outgoing transitions led to A05 in about 54 of 100 cases and to A04 in about 46, with A04 then always leading to A01 and A01 always leading back to A06. In the later window, A06 leads to A05 in about 58 of 100 cases and to A01 in about 42, with A01 then always leading to A04 and A04 always leading back to A06. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003]
- Step distances mirror this reversal exactly: earlier, A06→A04, A04→A01 and A01→A06 were typically immediate (one position apart) while A06→A01, A01→A04 and A04→A06 were typically two positions apart; in the later window each pair shows the opposite distance. [LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006]
- Participation and repetition exchanged between A01 and A04: A01 appeared in about 52 of 100 traces earlier versus about 20 in the later window (mean occurrences per trace about 0.96 versus 0.39; repeated in about 24 versus 8 of 100 traces), while A04 appeared in about 24 versus about 52 of 100 traces (about 0.45 versus 0.90 occurrences per trace; repeated in about 10 versus 20). - The ordering relations moved in step: earlier, about 28 of 100 traces contained A01 before A06 with no A04 at all, and A04 never appeared without A01; in the later window, about 32 of 100 traces contain A04 before A06 with no A01, and A01 never appears without A04. - A03 and A05 showed no qualifying change, with participation of about 48–52 of 100 traces in both windows. [OCC002, OCC004]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that A01 and A04 exchanged their places around A06: in the earlier window, cases ran A01 before A06 (about 28 of 100 traces without any A04), and the segment between repeated A06 executions ran A04 then A01; in the later window, cases run A04 before A06 (about 32 of 100 traces without any A01), and that segment runs A01 then A04. With this exchange, A01 became much less frequent (about 20 of 100 traces versus about 52) and A04 much more frequent (about 52 versus about 24), and the activity that repeats within a case changed from A01 to A04. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, REL001, REL002, REL003, OCC001, OCC003]

The supporting signs are direct and consistent: the successor changes at A01 and A04 are complete flips observed on many transitions (192 versus 77 observed outgoing transitions for A01; 89 versus 181 for A04), not shifts between coexisting alternatives; all six LAG items and all three REL items show the same reversal; and every qualifying difference is larger than in about 995 of 1000 random re-splits of the pooled data. A06's own frequency, position and exit to A05 stayed stable, localising the change to the order between A01 and A04. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006, OCC005, POS003]

Limitations: only the outgoing transitions of A01, A04 and A06 were profiled, so how cases reach this cycle from the rest of the process is not observed, and the evidence cannot show whether the upstream hand-over into the cycle changed as well; several items also describe the same traces, so they corroborate rather than independently confirm the change. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003]

Other differences: none beyond the above; A03 and A05 kept essentially the same participation in both windows. [OCC002, OCC004]
