### Observed differences

- The dominant difference is a reversal of execution order between two groups of activities. In the earlier window, exactly one of the three alternatives A19, A21 and A24 ran in almost every case (about 30, 35 and 37 in 100 cases respectively) and did so about a third of the way into the trace (mean relative position about 0.34–0.36), before A25 and A29, while the mandatory trio A20, A22 and A23 — present in every trace — ran about two thirds in (about 0.63–0.64), after A29. In the later window this is reversed: A20, A22 and A23 sit at about 0.38, before A25 and A29, and A19, A21 and A24 sit at about 0.66–0.68, after A29 and just before A30. Each of the six position shifts spans roughly a quarter to a third of the trace length and exceeds the differences seen in 199 of 200 random re-splits; the mix among the three alternatives also shifted slightly (A21 about 35→30 in 100, A24 about 37→40 in 100). [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006, OCC018, OCC020, OCC023]

- Pairwise order relations between the two groups flip almost completely: A25 ran before A20 in about 995 in 1,000 traces containing both, and now A20 runs before A25 in about 995 in 1,000; A29 ran before A20 in about 99 in 100 and now A20 runs before A29 in about 99 in 100; the same near-complete reversal holds for A19, A21 and A24 against A20, A22 and A23, against A25 and against A29 — roughly twenty pairs, each measured on all 200 traces in both windows. [REL001, REL010, REL013, REL014, REL015, REL018, REL021, REL026, REL027, REL031, REL032, REL036, REL037]

- Relations that a reordering should not touch are stable: A20, A22 and A23 still co-occur in every trace with no fixed order among themselves (each order in roughly half of the traces) in both windows; A25 still precedes A29 in about 99 in 100 in both; A40 still precedes and A30 still follows the whole section; and A19, A21 and A24 still practically never occur in the same trace. [REL011, REL012, REL025, REL040, REL017, REL044, REL041, REL002, REL005, REL020]

- Distances and successors move exactly where the reversal implies: A29 now reaches A30 in about 2 steps instead of about 4 [LAG013]; A40 is followed by A20 and by A23 within five steps in essentially every later trace versus 1 earlier trace [LAG015, LAG008]; A21 and A24 are no longer followed by A25 within five steps (69→1 and 71→2 observations) [LAG003, LAG009]; A19, A21 and A24 now lead to A30 in about 95–97 in 100 of their observed outgoing transitions instead of to A25 (about 94–97 in 100 before) [DIR019, DIR021, DIR024]; A29's outgoing transitions now go to A24, A21 and A19 (about 38, 29 and 32 in 100) where they previously went to A20, A23 and A22 (about 37, 33 and 30 in 100) [DIR029]; A40's outgoing transitions now go to A22, A20 and A23 instead of A21, A19 and A24 [DIR040].

- Away from this reordering, a few participation and repetition shifts appear: A08 falls from about 17 to 7 in 100 cases [OCC007]; A39 and A42 occur and repeat more often (A39 participation about 49→58 in 100, repetition 17→23 in 100) while A43 and A44 occur and repeat less (repetition about 20→12 in 100 each) [OCC036, OCC039, OCC040, OCC041]; A15 and A16 repeat slightly less often (mean occurrences about 1.6→1.5) [OCC014, OCC015]; the outgoing mixes of A02 and A06 changed markedly but rest on only about 10–23 outgoing transitions each [DIR002, DIR006].

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that two blocks of activities exchanged their relative order around A25 and A29: the mandatory trio A20, A22 and A23, which previously ran after A29, now runs before A25, while the alternative activity — whichever of the mutually exclusive A19, A21, A24 a case contains — now runs after A29, immediately before A30, instead of before A25; A40 still opens and A30 still closes this section in both windows, and the order within each block is unchanged. [REL014, REL015, REL027, REL032, REL036, POS002, POS005, POS001, POS003]

The supporting signs are direct and consistent: about twenty order relations between the two groups reverse from near-universal one way to near-universal the other, each on full 200-trace support per window and larger than in 199 of 200 random re-splits, while position shifts, five-step distances and successor changes all move in the direction and by the amount the exchange predicts, and relations outside the swap stay fixed — so this is a reordering of existing blocks, not activities appearing, disappearing or repeating differently. The occurrence, position, lag and successor items describe facets of one underlying reordering rather than independent confirmations. [REL001, REL014, REL015, REL032, REL011, REL040, LAG013, LAG015, DIR019, DIR029, DIR040]

Limitations: the evidence shows relative order within traces, so it cannot tell which of the two blocks was displaced against fixed milestones outside these activities — only that their relative order reversed — and positions are event positions rather than clock times, so no timing claim is possible; with no chance-expectation baseline provided, sampling variation cannot be strictly excluded, though roughly twenty near-complete reversals make it implausible. [REL014, REL015, POS002]

Other differences: A08's participation dropped from about 17 to 7 in 100 cases [OCC007]; A39 and A42 now occur and repeat more often while A43 and A44 occur and repeat less often [OCC036, OCC039, OCC040, OCC041]; A15 and A16 repeat slightly less often [OCC014, OCC015]; the outgoing-transition mixes of A02 and A06 changed markedly on few observations [DIR002, DIR006]; and the successor preferences between A31, A34 and A35 reversed, with shares shifting by about 10 points [DIR034, DIR035].
