### Inference

Support level: Weak hypothesis

The evidence suggests that repetition involving A01, A13 and A14 increased in the later window: all three activities appear in somewhat more traces and, more distinctly, occur more often per trace with higher within-trace recurrence, and A08 now routes to A01 in about two thirds of its outgoing transitions instead of about half [OCC001, OCC010, OCC011, DIR004]. The three occurrence profiles move in the same direction with similar magnitudes, which is consistent with one shared mechanism, but they could also reflect a single sampling fluctuation rather than three independent signals [OCC001, OCC010, OCC011]. What is missing is relational evidence (activity-pair order or co-occurrence) showing directly how these activities are connected or that a repeated section is traversed more often; the routing support rests mainly on one item, and A14's outgoing traffic in fact moved away from A08 toward A09, which does not fit a simple picture of one strengthened cycle [DIR001, DIR004, DIR008]. An important competing explanation is ordinary sampling variability between the two 200-trace windows: differences of this size arise in a sizable minority of random re-splits, and with 19 indicators tested a few elevated values are expected even without any change, while the evidence provides no screening baseline to check the item count against that chance expectation [OCC001, OCC002, OCC006, OCC007, OCC010, OCC011, DIR003, DIR004, DIR008].

Other differences: the lower participation of A03 (about 51 to 43 in 100 traces) and the higher participation of A09 and A10 (about 25 to 30 and 25 to 32 in 100) are not explained by the main hypothesis, nor are the routing concentrations A06→A12 and A14→A09 [OCC002, OCC006, OCC007, DIR003, DIR008].
