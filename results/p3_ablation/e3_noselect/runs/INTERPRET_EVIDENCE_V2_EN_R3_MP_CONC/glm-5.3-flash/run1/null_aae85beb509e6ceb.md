### Observed differences

- The preferred order of A30 and A31 between A23 and A24 flipped. In the earlier window, about 52 in 100 of A23's outgoing transitions went to A31 and about 46 in 100 to A30; from A31, about 53 in 100 continued to A30; from A30, about 52 in 100 went straight to A24 — a dominant route A23→A31→A30→A24. In the later window all of these reversed: about 55 in 100 of A23's transitions went to A30 (A31 down to about 42), from A30 about 56 in 100 went to A31 (straight to A24 down to about 43), and from A31 about 54 in 100 went to A24 (back to A30 down to about 43) — a dominant route A23→A30→A31→A24. [DIR023, DIR030, DIR031]

- The exit from the A19–A22 loop moved one step later. Earlier, about 57 in 100 of A20's outgoing transitions went directly to A28 and about 22 in 100 to A22, while about 28 in 100 of A22's transitions returned to A20; later, the direct A20→A28 jump fell to about 43 in 100 while A20→A22 rose to about 35, and A22→A28 rose to about 59 in 100 while A22→A20 fell to about 16. [DIR020, DIR022]

- Smaller, less coordinated shifts appear around A01/A05/A07/A09 and A03/A11–A13: A01→A07 fell from about 39 to about 29 in 100 while A01→A09 rose from about 31 to about 41; A05→A07 rose from about 23 to about 37 while A05→A09 fell from about 34 to about 25; A13→A12 fell from about 42 to about 26 while A13→A11 and A13→A14 rose to about 34 and 36 in 100. [DIR001, DIR005, DIR013, DIR003, DIR007, DIR009]

- Activity participation barely moved. Every activity that ran in all traces before did so after (A23 and A31 in 200 of 200 traces in both windows), and the largest shifts are about 5–8 in 100 traces (A40 up from about 57 to 63, A10 up from about 12 to 17, A13 up from about 49 to 54, A35 down from 50 to 45, A07 down from about 52 to 48) — differences that random re-splits of two 200-trace windows regularly produce. [OCC023, OCC027, OCC036, OCC010, OCC013, OCC031, OCC007]

- Profiles of rarely observed sources are uninformative: A44 shows a complete turnover of successors but on only 8–9 outgoing transitions per window, a difference that about three quarters of random re-splits exceed; the large dissimilarities for A02 and A10 rest on only about 25–38 outgoing transitions each. [DIR044, DIR002, DIR010]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the relative order of A30 and A31 in the segment between A23 and A24 changed: in the earlier window the dominant route was A23→A31→A30→A24, in the later window A23→A30→A31→A24. This is shown directly by the successor shares of A23, A30 and A31 themselves; all three shift by roughly 8–11 percentage points in the coordinated direction, each difference lies in the top 5–10% of what random re-splits produce, and each profile rests on about 200 outgoing transitions per window. Participation and recurrence of the involved activities were essentially unchanged (A23 and A31 occur once in virtually every trace in both windows, and A24 and A30 are likewise observed in essentially every trace), so this reads as a re-weighting of two existing orders rather than activities being added, removed, skipped or repeated. [DIR023, DIR030, DIR031, OCC023, OCC027, DIR024]

Limitations: the evidence contains no screening baseline, so the number of items expected under no change cannot be checked, and a single shift of about 10 percentage points is within reach of random splits of this size; the three items also come from the same traces and are not independent confirmations, so the case rests on their agreement in direction and magnitude. The competing explanation that cannot be ruled out is therefore sampling variation between the two window samples. [DIR023, DIR030, DIR031]

Other differences:
- The exit from the A19–A22 loop moved one step later (A20→A28 down, A20→A22 and A22→A28 up), which fits the same fewer-direct-jumps pattern but could also reflect a change in how often the loop is traversed before exiting — these indicators cannot separate the two. [DIR020, DIR022]
- The shifts around A01/A05/A07/A09 and A03/A11–A13 do not point in one consistent direction and are not part of the main hypothesis. [DIR001, DIR003, DIR005, DIR007, DIR009, DIR013]
- Small participation movements of a few traces in 100 (e.g., A18 down from 25 to 21) are within sampling variation. [OCC018, OCC036, OCC010, OCC031]
- Strongly changed successor profiles of rarely observed sources (A02, A10, A44) cannot be interpreted for lack of observations. [DIR002, DIR010, DIR044]
