### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

A loop appears to have been introduced around A25, A26 and A31: a segment that previously executed exactly once per case now repeats roughly three to four times per case, with A31 typically returning to A25 instead of proceeding to A20. The supporting signs are direct and mutually consistent: occurrence counts roughly tripled with repeats appearing in about 68 in 100 cases where there were none before [OCC019, OCC020, OCC025]; A31 now leads back to A25 in about 71 in 100 of its transitions rather than always to A20 [DIR020]; the fixed single A25-before-A31 pass became interleaved or repeated in about 68 in 100 cases [REL001]; and the constant 2-step spacing vanished while position variability doubled [LAG001, POS001, POS002]. Every one of these items exceeds the differences seen in 199 of 200 random re-splits of the pooled data, and the change cannot be a rare variant surfacing, since all three activities appear in every case in both windows. Limitations: A26's involvement rests only on its occurrence pattern, and the evidence does not show what enters or triggers the repeated block, so the loop's boundaries cannot be confirmed. A competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that the extra occurrences come from a subset of cases repeating many times rather than a uniform loop, since the per-case repeat distribution is not in the evidence.

Other differences: a routing redistribution in the early part of the process, where A03 now leads more often to A04 and less to A09, A01 more to A03, and A02 more to A07 [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003]; A16 is executed in fewer cases (about 53 to 46 in 100) while A17 is executed in more (about 48 to 54 in 100), consistent with a shift in branch shares [OCC015, OCC016]; and A10 and A11 repeat slightly more often than before [OCC009, OCC010]. These are not part of the main hypothesis.
