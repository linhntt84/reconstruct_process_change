### Observed differences

- Occurrence levels are essentially stable: A01 and A04 appear in every trace in both windows at about 1.9 occurrences per trace, while A02, A05 and A06 each appear in about 47 in 100 traces in the earlier window versus about 42 in 100 in the later window (94 vs 84 of 200 traces) — a gap that random re-splits of these windows typically exceed, so it is weak evidence on its own [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005].

- In the earlier window, A01 and A04 followed each other directly in both directions: about 50 in 100 of A01's outgoing transitions went to A04 (about 29 to A03 and 22 to A02), and about 50 in 100 of A04's outgoing transitions went back to A01 (about 26 to A02 and 23 to A03); A06 split its outgoing transitions roughly evenly between A01 and A04 [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003].

- In the later window, the routing runs one way: all observed outgoing transitions of A01 go to A04; A04's observed outgoing transitions go only to A03 (about 53 in 100) and A02 (about 47 in 100), with none to A01; and A06 always leads to A01 [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003].

- The order within the A01–A04 pair changed from mixed to fixed: earlier, A01 came first in about 25 in 100 traces, A04 first in about 28, and the two interleaved or repeated in about 47; in the later window, A01 comes first in about 58 in 100 traces and A04 never comes first [REL003].

- Relative event positions moved accordingly: A01 shifted earlier (mean relative position from about 0.40 to 0.29, median from about 0.45 to 0.0, so it is now typically the first event), while A04 shifted later (mean from about 0.39 to 0.50) [POS001, POS003].

- Pair distances agree with the rewiring: A01→A04 within five steps now occurs in all 200 traces (144 before) and is always direct; A04→A01 dropped from 150 to 84 traces and is no longer direct, now spanning three intervening events; A04→A03 is now always direct (before one or two steps apart); A01→A03 is now always two steps apart, i.e. A01, A04, A03 in a row; and A06→A01 is now always direct (84 traces, before mixed distances in 94) [LAG004, LAG008, LAG010, LAG003, LAG013].

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the alternating repeat between A01 and A04 gave way to a fixed sequence: in the earlier window the two activities followed each other directly in either order (each was the other's direct successor about half the time, and A04 preceded A01 in about 28 in 100 traces), whereas in the later window A01 always hands over to A04, A04 no longer returns directly to A01 but passes to A03 or A02, and the route back to A01 now runs through A06, which always leads to A01; A01 now typically opens the trace and A04 sits in the middle [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, REL003, LAG004, LAG008, LAG013, POS001, POS003].

The hypothesis rests on direct relational evidence — successor profiles, the ordered A01/A04 relation, pair distances and relative positions all move together — and the key items show differences that random re-splits of these windows virtually never produce (empirical strength about 99.5 in 100); because several indicators describe the same traces, their agreement reflects one coherent rewiring rather than independent confirmations [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, REL003, LAG004, LAG008, POS001, POS003].

Other differences: the share of traces containing A02, A05 or A06 slipped from about 47 to about 42 in 100 (94 to 84 of 200) and A01/A04 recurrence support similarly from about 47 to about 42 in 100, but random re-splits usually produce differences of this size (empirical strength about 3 in 100), so this is not standalone evidence of change; the relative positions of A02 and A06 barely moved [OCC001, OCC002, OCC005, POS002, POS004].

An important competing explanation remains: a complete shift in the mix of pre-existing trace variants (for example, a loop-first variant disappearing from the sample) rather than a rewiring of the routing itself; the routing shares also cover only observed transitions, so an unobserved A04→A01 edge is not proof of removal, and the position and distance statements are in event order, not clock time [DIR002, REL003, LAG008].
