### Observed differences

- The dominant difference is a flip in the choice between two alternative three-activity groups after A15. In the earlier window, about 75 in 100 traces contained the group A16, A17, A18 (the three always appearing together) and about 25 in 100 contained the alternative group A19, A20, A21; in the later window, about 76 in 100 traces contain A19/A20/A21 and about 25 in 100 contain A16/A17/A18. The two groups remain mutually exclusive in both windows: a trace contains one group or the other, essentially never both. [OCC013, OCC016, REL008, REL010, REL023]

- A15's outgoing transitions mirror this flip: transitions from A15 into A16/A17/A18 made up about 75% of its observed outgoing transitions earlier and about 25% later, while transitions into A19/A20/A21 rose from about 25% to about 75%. [DIR012]

- The frame around the branch is unchanged. A15 and A25 still occur in every trace in both windows; A15 still precedes whichever group executes; and the executed group keeps a mixed relation to A25 (sometimes before, sometimes interleaved, no fixed order), with A15 preceding A25 in about 60 in 100 traces in both windows. [POS001, POS008, REL001, REL004, REL007, REL013, REL025]

- The internal character of both groups is preserved: within each group the activities still co-occur and still appear in both orders in similar proportions (for example, A16 precedes A17 in about 38 in 100 and A17 precedes A16 in about 37 in 100 of the earlier traces, versus about 13 and 11 in 100 later). [REL008, REL014, REL023, REL026]

- Smaller, separate differences: A10 and A11 repeat somewhat more often (about 1.7 versus 1.8 occurrences per trace, occurring more than once in about 36 versus 46 in 100 traces, with participation at 100% in both windows); participation of A12 and A13 rose from about 47 to 53 in 100 and of A27/A28 from about 64 to 67 in 100, while A26 fell from about 62 to 58 in 100; a few successor shares moved mildly (A01→A05 up from about 28% to 40%, A08→A07 down from about 39% to 23%, A23→A22 up from about 35% to 43%, A25→A27 up from about 49% to 55%). [OCC009, OCC011, OCC022, OCC023, DIR001, DIR008, DIR019, DIR020]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the branch after A15 routes cases differently: the share of traces running the path A15 → A16/A17/A18 → A25 fell from about three quarters to about one quarter, while the alternative path A15 → A19/A20/A21 → A25 rose from about one quarter to about three quarters. This is a change in how often existing paths are taken, not a replacement or repositioning: both groups are well observed in both windows (roughly 49–151 traces each), they stay mutually exclusive, A15 still precedes whichever group executes, and the group's mixed order relation to A25 is preserved. The flip is shown directly by relational and successor evidence and consistently across participation, direction and pairwise indicators, with the largest items at empirical strength about 0.995 (larger than in about 995 of 1,000 random re-splits). [OCC013, OCC016, DIR012, REL001, REL004, REL010, REL023, REL025]

An important competing explanation remains: the two windows may sample different case mixtures (for example, case types with different branch habits in changing proportions) rather than a re-routing within individual cases, which the evidence cannot distinguish because it contains no case attributes. The earlier-window profile of the A19/A20/A21 path also rests on only about 50 traces, so finer claims about that path's internal behaviour are weak. [OCC016, DIR012, REL010]

Other differences: the modest repetition increase at A10/A11, the small participation shifts at A12/A13 and A26/A27/A28, and the mild successor-share movements at A01, A08, A23 and A25 are not part of this hypothesis; individually they are small enough to reflect ordinary window-to-window variation. [OCC009, OCC011, OCC022, DIR001, DIR008, DIR019, DIR020]
