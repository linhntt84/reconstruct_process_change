### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that A35 and A36 exchanged their execution order inside the segment between A31 and A32: in the earlier window the segment typically ran A31 → A36 → A35 → A32, and in the later window A31 → A35 → A36 → A32. The change is shown directly by relational and positional evidence: among traces containing both activities, A36-before-A35 fell from about 60 to about 39 in 100 while A35-before-A36 rose from about 40 to about 61 [REL006]; the typical step distance from A31 to A35 fell from two to one and from A36 to A32 from two to one, with the opposite shifts for A31→A36 and A35→A32 [LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG006, LAG007]; and the successors of both activities flipped (A35→A36 from about 38 to 61 in 100 of outgoing transitions, A36→A32 from about 39 to 60) [DIR035, DIR036]. Every item involved rests on at least 87 traces and has an empirical strength of 0.88 or higher, and the direction, lag and relation indicators agree, although they partly reflect the same underlying traces. Limitations: both orders already occur in both windows, so the evidence shows a reversal of the predominant order within a mixed ordering rather than the introduction of a strict sequence; the accompanying shift in how often the segment is executed at all (about 52 to 44 in 100 traces) cannot be separated from the re-ordering; and no screening expectation was provided, so chance aggregation of the item cluster cannot be quantified, though the cross-indicator consistency makes it unlikely. [OCC031, OCC032, REL006, LAG005, LAG007]
