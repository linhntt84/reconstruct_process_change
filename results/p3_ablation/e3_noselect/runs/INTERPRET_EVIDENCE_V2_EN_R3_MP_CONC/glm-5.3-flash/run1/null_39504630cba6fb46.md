### Observed differences

- **The dominant route through the A20/A22/A23 segment reversed.** In the earlier window, the most frequent moves in this segment (entered from A29, exited via A30) were A20 → A23 (about 40 in 100 outgoing transitions), A23 → A22 (about 37) and A22 → A20 (about 39). In the later window the frequent moves ran the opposite way: A20 → A22 (about 39, up from 27), A22 → A23 (about 34, up from 22) and A23 → A30 (about 37, up from 30), and entry from A29 shifted to A20 (about 28 → 37 in 100). Each profile rests on roughly 200 outgoing transitions, with empirical strengths of about 0.80–0.99, while A20, A22 and A23 still occur about once per trace in both windows — so only the shares of existing transitions moved. [DIR020, DIR022, DIR023, DIR029, DIR030, OCC020, OCC021]

- **The A15–A16 repetition loop was iterated less often.** Both activities still appear in every trace, but mean occurrences fell from about 1.8 to about 1.6 per trace, and traces in which they repeated fell from about 44–45 in 100 to about 35–37 in 100 (strengths about 0.93–0.94). Consistently, A16's return edge to A15 fell from about 44 to 38 in 100 of its outgoing transitions while its exit to A39 rose from about 26 to 30. [OCC014, OCC015, DIR016]

- **After A41, more traffic took the A43 branch.** Of A41's outgoing transitions, the share to A43 rose from about 45 to 51 in 100 while the share to A42 fell from about 52 to 47 (about 325 vs 341 transitions; strength about 0.86). A43 and A44 mean occurrences rose from about 0.76 to 0.90 per trace (participation about 60 → 62 in 100; strengths about 0.89), while A42 slipped from 0.87 to 0.82 (participation about 66 → 62 in 100). [DIR041, OCC035, OCC036, OCC037, OCC038]

- **A08 became more frequent.** It appeared in about 17 in 100 traces in the later window versus about 7 in 100 earlier (33 vs 14 of 200 traces; strength about 0.99), showing up as a rare extra successor after activities such as A43, A45 and A15. [OCC007, DIR043, DIR045, DIR015]

- **Smaller shifts.** A24 participation rose from about 30 to 37 in 100 traces; A19 fell from about 34 to 30; A02 fell from about 8.5 to 5 in 100 with a visibly reshuffled outgoing mix, but that profile rests on only about 16 vs 10 outgoing transitions. Entry routing at A01 moved from A09 (about 34 → 24 in 100) toward A07 (about 27 → 37), and at A11 from A12 (about 34 → 27) toward A14 (about 25 → 34); A34 and A35 also swapped their typical visiting order, though more weakly. [OCC022, OCC018, OCC002, DIR002, DIR001, DIR011, DIR034, DIR035]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the typical traversal order of the A20/A22/A23 segment changed: in the earlier window traces usually progressed A20 → A23 → A22, whereas in the later window they usually progressed A20 → A22 → A23 and then exited via A30, with entry from A29 occurring via A20 more often. This rests on direct successor evidence at all four branch points of the segment, each based on about 200 outgoing transitions with empirical strengths of about 0.80–0.99, and on the occurrence rates of A20, A22 and A23 staying at about one per trace, so only the shares between existing paths shifted rather than activities being added, removed or repeated differently. A competing explanation the evidence cannot fully rule out is a changed mix of complete trace variants that traverse the segment in different orders; it would look identical in these transition shares, although the alignment of all four branch points makes pure sampling noise unlikely. [DIR020, DIR022, DIR023, DIR029, OCC020, OCC021]

Other differences: the A15–A16 loop being repeated less often, the shift of the post-A41 branch towards A43/A44, and the increased presence of A08 are separate behavioural differences, as are the smaller participation and entry-routing shifts listed above.
