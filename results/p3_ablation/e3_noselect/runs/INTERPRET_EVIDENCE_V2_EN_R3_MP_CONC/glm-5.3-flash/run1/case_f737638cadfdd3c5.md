### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

- Main hypothesis: in the later window, a new step was inserted between A41 and A26, filled by one of three alternative activities — about 37 in 100 cases take A19, about 34 take A25 and about 29 take A20 — none of which was observed in the earlier window (0 of 200 traces each). A41 and A26 themselves continued to occur in essentially every trace in both windows. [OCC017, OCC018, OCC023, OCC024, DIR041]
- The change is shown directly where it happens: A41's successor profile flipped from A26 to the three new activities [DIR041], each new activity leads on to A26 in about 96–98 in 100 of its transitions [DIR019, DIR020, DIR025], and the A41-to-A26 distance moved from one step (about 94 in 100) to two steps (about 90 in 100) [LAG002].
- Ordering and position corroborate the placement: A41 precedes the new activity, which precedes A26, and the new activities sit positionally between A41 and A26 while A26 moved later in the trace. [REL003, REL006, REL008, REL011, REL012, REL015, POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004]
- The volumes involved — three activities appearing in 57–74 of 200 traces after being absent in 200 — far exceed what sampling variation or a rare variant surfacing in one window would produce, and the mutual exclusivity of the three (co-occurrence in at most about 1 in 200 traces) shows a per-case choice among alternatives rather than a fixed new sequence. [OCC017, OCC018, OCC023, REL001, REL002, REL007]
- Limitations: the evidence cannot show what determines the choice among A19, A20 and A25, and a small direct A41-to-A26 flow of about 1 in 100 remains, so the insertion need not apply to every case. [LAG002, DIR041]
- An important competing explanation — that the three activities existed before but were missed by the earlier 200-trace sample — is implausible given their later frequency, but with two finite samples it cannot be formally excluded. [OCC017, OCC018, OCC023]
