### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that, in the later window, the activities A31 and A32 — both of which can recur within a trace — are executed in fewer traces and repeated less often, with the affected flow routed around parts of this segment: traces that previously moved on from A11 into A32 now go to A12 or A13 instead, and A32 hands over to A33 more often and to A31 less often. This rests on direct participation and recurrence drops on both activities, each exceeded by fewer than about 1 in 100 random re-splits [OCC021, OCC022], on matching shifts in the outgoing-transition shares of A11 and A32 [DIR009, DIR020], and on increases in A12 and A13 that together (about 12 in 100 traces) match the size of A32's participation drop [OCC011, OCC012]. Limitations: the evidence contains no relational or positional items, so the ordering inside the segment cannot be verified and the rerouting is established only through outgoing-share changes; no screening baseline is provided, so the number of flagged items cannot be compared against a chance expectation. The main competing explanation — ordinary sampling variability between the two 200-trace windows — is made unlikely by the size and one-directional coherence of these shifts, but it cannot be excluded entirely. [OCC021, OCC022, DIR020, DIR009]
