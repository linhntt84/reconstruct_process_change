### Observed differences

- In the later window, the repeated execution of A15 and A16 became less frequent. Both activities still appear in every trace in both windows, but mean occurrences per trace fell from about 1.7 to about 1.55 for each, and the share of traces containing a repeat fell from about 40 in 100 to about 33 in 100. The transition profiles match: A15's transitions to A16 fell from about 87% to 83% while its exit to A04 rose from about 13% to 17%, and A16's return to A15 fell from about 42% to 35% while its exits to A41 and A18 rose to about 35% and 15%. [OCC014, OCC015, DIR015, DIR016]

- In the later window, routing after A43 shifted toward A44: A44 appears in about 65 of 100 traces, up from about 58 (mean occurrences about 0.9 versus 0.8), while A45 and A46 each slip to about 62 from about 66 and 65. Of A43's outgoing transitions, the share leading to A44 rose from about 44% to 50% and the share to A45 fell from about 54% to 49%; A44 returned to A43 more often (about 35% to 41% of its transitions) and A46 exited to A47 more often (about 51% to 55%). [OCC042, OCC043, OCC044, DIR043, DIR044, DIR046]

- Smaller, diffuse shifts appear among optional activities: those near 45% participation (for example A01, A05, A07, A21) each rose by 3–5 points while those near 55% (for example A03, A11, A12, A13) each fell by 4–5 points, and within the A11/A12/A13 block the order mix changed — after A03, transitions to A11 rose from about 26% to 37% and to A12 fell from about 36% to 27%; after A12, transitions to A13 rose from about 22% to 33% and returns to A11 fell from about 41% to 30%. [OCC001, OCC005, OCC006, OCC020, OCC003, OCC010, OCC011, OCC012, DIR003, DIR012]

- Profiles of rarely executed activities are unstable: A02 appears in about 6 of 100 later traces versus 2 of 100 earlier (4 versus 12 traces) with a completely different successor set, but that profile rests on only 4 versus 13 outgoing transitions; A06, A08, A10 and A48 likewise show near-total successor turnover on about 2–9 outgoing transitions per window, and for A08 and A10 the dissimilarity is no larger than in any random re-split, marking this as typical sampling noise rather than change. [OCC002, DIR002, DIR006, DIR008, DIR010, DIR048]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that, in the later window, the repetition of the pass through A15 and A16 was scaled back: every case still runs A14 and then A15, but cases repeat the A15–A16 pass less often (mean occurrences per trace down from about 1.7 to about 1.55; traces with a repeat down from about 40 to about 33 in 100). The change is measured directly on all 200 traces of each window and is unusual for random re-splits (empirical strengths about 0.86–0.88), and the successor shares shift exactly as fewer loop iterations would predict — loop transitions (A15→A16, A16→A15) fall while exits to A04, A41 and A18 hold or rise. Because participation stays at 1.0 in both windows and A14 still leads to A15 in about 98–100% of its transitions, competing readings such as the activities becoming optional or being bypassed are ruled out; the main competing explanation that remains is ordinary sampling variability between the two finite windows, and without relational items the loop structure itself is inferred from recurrence and transition shares rather than observed directly. [OCC013, OCC014, OCC015, DIR014, DIR015, DIR016]

Other differences: a routing shift in the A43–A47 region, where the A44 branch after A43 was taken more often (about 44% to 50% of A43's transitions) at the expense of the A45 route, with A44 up (about 58 to 65 in 100 traces) and A45/A46 down (about 66/65 to 62); all involved activities are well observed in both windows, so this behaves like existing paths being taken with different frequencies, though it rests on a handful of agreeing items and sampling cannot be excluded. A smaller change appears in the ordering mix among A11, A12 and A13 (more A03→A11 and A12→A13 sequences, fewer A03→A12 and A12→A11). The near-total successor turnover of rare activities (A02, A06, A08, A10, A48) is treated as sampling noise, not as change.
