### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that an existing choice between two alternative branches shifted in frequency: in the later window the branch containing A01, A05, A07 and A09 is executed in about 59 in 100 traces (previously about 49), while the alternative branch containing A03, A11, A12 and A13 is executed in about 42 in 100 (previously about 51). The support is direct and consistent: all eight activities are observed in a substantial share of traces in both windows, the branches' shares remain complementary at about one execution per trace and both still converge on A14, the four increases and four decreases mirror each other in size (about 9–10 points in 100), and each difference is larger than about 90–95 in 100 of the random re-split references, with roughly 80–120 traces behind each activity per window. This matches a change in how often existing paths are taken, not a replacement of one path by another. The evidence cannot show which point in the process governs the choice or why it moved, and it cannot fully rule out that the two windows simply captured a different mix of cases; as no expected-by-chance item count is provided, the judgement rests on the directness and coordination of the pattern rather than on a chance-expectation comparison.
