### Observed differences

- In the later window, A02, A09 and A06 all ran more often: A02 and A09 each appeared in about 21 in 100 traces before versus 27 in 100 after (mean occurrences per trace about 0.34 → 0.59), and A06 in about 17 in 100 versus 23 (mean occurrences about 0.36 → 0.66). Repeated executions also became more common, e.g. traces with A02 occurring more than once rose from about 10% to 16% of all traces. [OCC002, OCC005, OCC008]
- The extra executions concentrated on the loop between these three activities: from A02, the share of outgoing transitions to A09 rose from about 45% to 57% while the share to A07 fell from about 36% to 21%; from A09, the share to A06 rose from about 19% to 32% while returns to A02 fell from about 55% to 42%; from A06, the share to A02 rose from about 17% to 27% while the share to A08 fell from about 58% to 51%. [DIR002, DIR007, DIR005]
- The volumes behind these routing shares grew accordingly: outgoing transitions from A02 and A09 rose from 67 to 118 each, and from A06 from 71 to 131, matching the occurrence increases. [DIR002, DIR007, DIR005]
- Smaller movements elsewhere: A07, A08, A10 and A11 each appeared slightly more often (e.g. A08 mean occurrences about 0.46 → 0.62); from A11, transitions to A06 rose from about 45% to 58% while transitions to A07 fell from about 52% to 42%; from A04, transitions to A02 rose from about 10% to 16%. A01, A04, A05 and the outgoing routing of A08 and A10 were essentially unchanged. [OCC006, OCC007, OCC009, OCC010, DIR009, DIR003, DIR001, DIR004, DIR006, DIR008]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that in the later window the loop among A02, A09 and A06 is executed more often. In the earlier window each of the three appeared in roughly a fifth of traces with about 1.6–2.0 executions per containing trace; in the later window participation rose to about 23–27 in 100 traces, executions per containing trace rose to about 2.2–2.9, and the additional traversals ran along the loop edges (A02→A09, A09→A06 and A06→A02 all gained share of outgoing transitions) rather than exiting towards A07 or A08. [OCC002, OCC005, OCC008, DIR002, DIR005, DIR007] The signs are direct and consistent: the three occurrence profiles and the three loop edges all moved in the same direction with empirical strength between about 0.91 and 0.97, and the outgoing-transition counts independently match the occurrence increases. [OCC002, OCC005, OCC008, DIR002, DIR005, DIR007] The main limitations are sample size — the participation shifts rest on roughly 35–55 traces per activity per window — and that A02 and A09 show identical profiles, so they largely reflect the same traces rather than two independent confirmations. [OCC002, OCC008]

Other differences: the slight occurrence increases for A07, A08, A10 and A11, the shift of A11's outgoing transitions from A07 towards A06, and the rise of A04→A02 from about 10% to 16% of A04's outgoing transitions are not part of the main hypothesis; they may be secondary effects of the same behaviour or sampling noise. [OCC006, OCC007, OCC009, OCC010, DIR009, DIR003]

An important competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is ordinary variation between the two 200-trace windows: no screening baseline is included, so the number of findings expected by chance alone cannot be compared with the number observed. [OCC002, OCC005, OCC008, DIR002, DIR005, DIR007]
