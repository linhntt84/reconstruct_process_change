- The A36–A37 cycle was used much less in the later window. In the earlier window A37 appeared in about 60 in 100 traces (about 1.1 occurrences per trace) and A36 in about 31 in 100; in the later window A37 appears in about 48 in 100 traces (about 0.8 occurrences per trace) and A36 in about 22 in 100, with fewer repeat passes (A37 recurrence about 31 vs 21 in 100 traces). ### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the amount of repetition in the late stages of the process changed. In the earlier window, traces frequently cycled through A36 and A37, while in the later window fewer traces enter this cycle (A16 routes about 6 in 100 fewer of its exits to A37 and more via A17/A18 directly to A38) and fewer passes are made once inside, so both A36 and A37 are executed less. In the same late stage but in the opposite direction, the cycle around A39, A40, A41 and A42 was traversed more often per trace, with more returns to A39 from A40 and A42 before exiting to A43. The occurrence and direction indicators agree for each cycle, the lead differences exceed those of about 83–98 in 100 random re-splits of the pooled data, and every involved activity has substantial support in both windows (for example A37 in 119 vs 95 traces, A39 in 200 vs 200), which makes a rare-variant artefact unlikely. [OCC033, OCC034, OCC035, OCC036, DIR016, DIR037, DIR040, DIR042]

Limitations: the A41 and A42 items are weaker (their differences rank above only about 44–48 in 100 random re-splits), both windows are finite samples of 200 traces, and the evidence cannot rule out that a different mix of cases in the later window, rather than a modification of the process, produced the changed loop frequencies. [OCC037, OCC038]
