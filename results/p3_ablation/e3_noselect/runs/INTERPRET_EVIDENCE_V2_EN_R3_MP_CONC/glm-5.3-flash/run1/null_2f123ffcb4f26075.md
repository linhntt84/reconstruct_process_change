### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the dominant traversal order through A15, A17 and A18 reversed, together with the entry into the group from A24: in the earlier window the dominant chain ran A24 → A18 → A17 → A15 (with A15 most often returning to A18), while in the later window it runs A24 → A15 → A17 → A18, with A18 now most often continuing to A25. Supporting signs: four successor-distribution items, each anchored on a different source activity, change in mutually consistent directions (each successor share that rose in the group is mirrored by one that fell), the changes are unusual for random re-splits (for A15 and A17 only about 1–3 of 100 reference comparisons are larger), and each source shows about 200 outgoing transitions in both windows with no qualifying occurrence change, so the shift concerns routing rather than the presence of these activities. The main limitation is that the evidence aggregates successor shares: it cannot distinguish a genuine reordering from a changed mix of traversal orders that already existed in both windows, and the evidence set provides no chance-expectation baseline for the item count.
