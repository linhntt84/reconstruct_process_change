### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the ordering constraints around A02 and A04 were relaxed and new shortcuts were introduced: the previously fixed chain A02 → A04 → A03 became a mixed order in which A04 often precedes A02 (about 46 in 100 traces), A02 can pass directly to A03 (skipping A04), A01 can pass directly to A04 (skipping A02), and A04 can pass back to A02. This is shown directly by the successor profiles of A01, A02 and A04, by the fixed-to-mixed order change between A02 and A04, and by matching lag shifts on the same activity pairs; the key items exceed 199 of 200 random re-splits in strength, rest on about 200 traces per window, and the opposite position shifts of A02 (later) and A04 (earlier) point the same way. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR004, REL004, LAG001, LAG003, LAG005, LAG009, POS002, POS004]

Limitations: the indicators cannot distinguish a reordering in which A04 now runs before A02 from a new repetition loop in which A02 and A04 alternate one or more times — the two-way transitions between them are consistent with both. Also, the later window still contains the old pattern in roughly half of the cases, so a coexistence of an old and a new variant (for example during a gradual transition) cannot be ruled out. [DIR002, DIR004, REL004]

Other differences: returns to A01 became somewhat more frequent and shorter — A04→A01 rises from about 4 to about 10 in 100 of A04's outgoing transitions and its distance to A01 shrinks — but this rests on only 14 versus 27 observations, and the A02→A01 and A03→A01 lags are based on fewer than 30 observations each, so these are weak signals rather than part of the main hypothesis.
