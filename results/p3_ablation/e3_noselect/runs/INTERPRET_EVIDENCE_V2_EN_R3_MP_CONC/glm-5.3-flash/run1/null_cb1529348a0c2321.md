### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the execution order of A11, A12 and A13 became less fixed: in the earlier window they usually ran as the sequence A11→A13→A12 (returning to A11), whereas in the later window the direct successions between them occur in both directions in similar proportions, with A11→A13 falling from about 51 to 26 of 100 transitions out of A11 while A13→A11 rose from about 20 to 33. This is a shift in how often existing orders are taken, not the appearance or removal of a path: all three activities ran in about 45–48 of 100 traces in both windows and every succession involved was observed in both windows. The change shows directly and consistently in the successor distributions after each of the three activities, the largest difference (after A11) being larger than in about 199 of 200 random re-splits, with each profile backed by roughly 90–100 outgoing transitions per window [DIR011, DIR012, DIR013, OCC011, OCC012, OCC013].

Limitations: the evidence carries no screening baseline, so the number of qualifying items cannot be checked against a chance expectation; the accompanying rise in participation of the three activities is weak on its own (empirical strengths about 0.33–0.46) [OCC011, OCC012, OCC013]; and the evidence cannot rule out that the reshuffle is a downstream effect of a changed mix of upstream paths rather than a local change in ordering — share shifts at the entry to the group (after A03) and at several other decision points suggest a broader redistribution of existing paths of which this may be one facet [DIR003, DIR043].
