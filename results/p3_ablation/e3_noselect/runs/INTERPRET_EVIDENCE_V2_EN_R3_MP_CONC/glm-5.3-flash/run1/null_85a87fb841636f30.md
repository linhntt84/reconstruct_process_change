### Observed differences

- The clearest difference concerns how A11, A12 and A13 follow one another. In the earlier window there was a strong preferred rotation: A11 was followed by A13 in about 52 of 100 outgoing steps (A14 about 24, A12 about 22), A13 was followed by A12 in about 44 of 100, and A12 by A11 or A14 in about 37 each. In the later window these preferences largely flattened: A11 now passes most often to A12 (about 41 of 100), and after A12 and A13 the three activities follow one another in nearly equal shares (about 3 in 10 each). [DIR011, DIR012, DIR013]
- All three of these activities also occur slightly more often, in about 48–49 of 100 traces in the later window versus about 45–46 before. [OCC011, OCC012, OCC013]
- After A44, the branch to A19 is taken more often (about 49 of 100 outgoing steps, up from 44) and the branch to A20 less often (about 42, down from 52); matching this, A19 appears in about 54 of 100 traces (up from 47) and A20 in about 48 (down from 55). [DIR044, OCC017, OCC018]
- Several other choice points show shifted branch shares: after A25 the share continuing to A35 rose to about 38 of 100 (from 30) while A41 fell; after A24 the share going to A21 rose to about 32 (from 18) while A31 fell; after A21 the share going to A31 rose to about 58 (from 49). [DIR025, DIR024, DIR021]
- Some infrequent activities occur more often in the later window: A43 in about 51 of 100 traces (up from 44, with more repeats), A42 in about 23 (up from 16), A08 in about 10 (up from 4; 8 versus 21 traces), A04 in about 10 (up from 7). A18 occurs less often, about 27 of 100 traces versus 33. [OCC036, OCC035, OCC008, OCC004, OCC016]
- A few always-present activities now occasionally repeat: A44 and A50, previously exactly once per trace, recur in about 3 of 100 traces. [OCC037, OCC042]
- The successor profiles of rare activities (A08, A50, A04, A10) changed markedly, but each rests on fewer than about 25 observed outgoing transitions, so these are weakly grounded. [DIR008, DIR050, DIR004, DIR010]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the routing among A11, A12 and A13 changed: in the earlier window these activities were typically traversed in the rotation A11 → A13 → A12 (each preferred step taken in roughly half of the cases, with exits to A14), whereas in the later window the successor choice after each of the three is nearly even and A11 → A12 has become the most frequent step. This is a change in ordering constraints within an existing group, not a replacement of paths: all three activities and all the successor edges involved are observed in both windows, with each activity present in about 45–49 of 100 traces in both windows. The change is visible directly and consistently in the outgoing-transition profiles of all three activities, each backed by roughly 90–100 transitions per window, and the A11 shift is larger than all but about 1 in 200 random re-splits of the pooled data. [DIR011, DIR012, DIR013, OCC011, OCC012, OCC013]

Limitations: the evidence carries no screening baseline, so the total number of qualifying items cannot be checked against the number an unchanged process would produce; and the trio's shift is one of several simultaneous branch-share changes, so it may be part of a broader re-routing rather than an isolated modification. [DIR011, DIR024, DIR025, DIR044]

Other differences: the branch after A44 shifted from A20 toward A19, with A19 occurring more and A20 less often overall [DIR044, OCC017, OCC018]; branch shares after A25/A35/A36 and after A21–A24 shifted as described above [DIR025, DIR035, DIR024, DIR021]; A43, A42, A08 and A04 occur more often and A18 less often [OCC036, OCC035, OCC008, OCC004, OCC016]; A44 and A50 occasionally repeat [OCC037, OCC042].

An important competing explanation that cannot be ruled out: for the weaker items — especially the successor changes of rare activities such as A08 or A50, observed on fewer than about 25 transitions — the differences could be sampling variation between the two 200-trace windows. [DIR008, DIR050]
