### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that, in the later window, A02 executes as a new step in the repeating section, directly between A15 and its successors A07, A08 and A12. In the earlier window, a trace reaching A15 continued straight to A07 (about 52 in 100 of A15's outgoing transitions), A08 (about 25) or A12 (about 23); in the later window A15 leads only to A02, whose outgoing transitions reproduce that successor mix almost exactly, the gap from A15 to each successor grew from one event to two, and every measured distance inside the section grew from four to five events. A02 went from not being observed at all to running in about 58 in 100 traces, exactly as often as A15 (about 2.4 occurrences per trace each, never one without the other), fitting one A02 execution per pass of the section. The relational items show this directly — A15's successor set changed to A02 alone and the A15→successor distances shifted from 1 to 2 — and every key item stands at empirical strength about 0.995, so sampling variation cannot account for the pattern. [OCC001, OCC013, DIR002, DIR011, LAG001, LAG005, LAG006, LAG007, LAG008, REL004]

Limitations: the evidence carries no screening baseline, so the number of qualifying items cannot be compared with a chance expectation; some lag profiles rest on few pair observations (as few as 8–31); and nothing shows how A02 relates to A11 or A14. The main competing explanation the evidence cannot exclude is that the repeating section is entered in more traces in the later window — participation of its activities all rose in the same direction — but each of those rises lies within the range random re-splits produce, so a real second change cannot be separated from sampling noise.
