### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the recurring group in the middle of the process was restructured and its membership changed. In the earlier window, A01 belonged to a group that always also contained A09, A04, A05 and A13, repeatedly handed over directly to A09, and received the flow back from A05; in the later window A01 has left this group — it occurs once per trace in about a third of traces, never together with any former partner, at one fixed position and with no observed outgoing transitions — while A17, previously unobserved, has taken over the role of directly preceding A09 inside the group. The internal edges were rewired: A08 now leads to A16 and A17 instead of A13 and A04, A16 leads to A08 instead of A05, A05 leads to A04 and A13 instead of A01 and A16, and A09 leads to A05 instead of A08; the group appears in fewer traces (about 21 to about 13 in 100) and A08 now repeats about four times per trace where it occurs. [OCC001, OCC005, OCC008, OCC017, DIR001, DIR003, DIR006, DIR011, DIR012, REL001, REL004, REL025]

The signs are direct and consistent: four source activities swapped essentially their entire outgoing profiles, A01's co-occurrence with its former partners flipped from every shared trace to none, A17's arrival shows in occurrence, position, relation and successor data, and the lag shifts line up with the lost and new adjacencies — nearly all at the top of the reference distribution (empirical strength about 0.995). Limitations: these indicator families partly describe the same traces, so their agreement is consistency rather than independent confirmation; an empty outgoing profile does not prove A01's edge to A09 is gone, only that it was not observed; the evidence cannot distinguish a rewiring of one group from a shift in the mixture of complete trace variants that would produce the same aggregates, and no screening baseline is included to compare item counts against chance. [DIR001, DIR006, DIR011, POS001, REL001, LAG018]
