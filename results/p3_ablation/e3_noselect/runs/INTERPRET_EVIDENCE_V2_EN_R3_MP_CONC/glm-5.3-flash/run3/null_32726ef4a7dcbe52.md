### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the split between two existing alternative routes moved: the route through A01, A05, A07 and A09 — whose activities feed one another and then hand over to A14 — is taken in about 53 in 100 traces in the later window, up from about 43, while the alternative route through A03, A11, A12 and A13, which likewise converges on A14, falls from about 58 to about 48 in 100. [OCC001, OCC003, OCC005, OCC007, OCC009, OCC011, OCC012, OCC013]

Supporting signs: the participation moves are mirrored and of similar size across all eight activities, each difference exceeding roughly 93 in 100 random re-splits of the same data; the successor profiles show each group internally connected with no flow between the groups; and the combined shares of the two groups remain near one per trace, as expected for two alternatives whose split moved. Limitations: no relational item compares the two groups directly, so their mutual exclusivity and the single-rerouting reading are inferred from the mirrored pattern, and the internal routing changes (A07 exiting to A14 sooner, A12 and A13 exchanging successor shares) could be partly independent of the shift. An important competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that these are several independent usage shifts rather than one decision between two paths. [DIR001, DIR003, DIR007, DIR009, DIR012, DIR013]

Other differences: the predominant order of A36 and A37 reversed (typically A32 → A37 → A36 → A33 before, A32 → A36 → A37 → A33 afterwards), a directly and consistently observed ordering change across successor, co-occurrence and lag evidence, with both activities' frequency unchanged.
