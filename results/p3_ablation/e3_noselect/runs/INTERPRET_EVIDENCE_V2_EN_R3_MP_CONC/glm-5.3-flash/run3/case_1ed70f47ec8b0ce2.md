### Observed differences

- The dominant difference is a reversal of sequence order between two groups of activities. In the earlier window, exactly one of A14, A16 or A19 ran per case (about 30, 35 and 36 in 100 cases respectively, never two of them together), positioned about a third of the way through the trace (mean position ~0.34), while A15, A17 and A18 all ran in every case at about two thirds through (~0.64). In the later window the groups have exchanged places: A15/A17/A18 sit at ~0.38 and A14/A16/A19 at ~0.67. [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006, OCC013, OCC014, OCC015, REL002]

- Pairwise order flipped completely rather than partially: A20 preceded A15 in 100 in 100 earlier cases and followed it in 100 in 100 later cases; A24 likewise moved from always before A15 to always after it; A14 moved from always before A15, A17, A18, A20 and A24 to always after them. [REL014, REL015, REL001, REL006, REL007, REL025, REL028, REL029]

- Directly-follows profiles were rewired accordingly. Earlier, A14, A16 and A19 always led to A20, and A15/A17/A18 led to A25 in roughly 30–38 in 100 of their outgoing transitions; later, A14/A16/A19 always lead to A25 and A15/A17/A18 lead to A20. A24's outgoing transitions moved from the A15/A17/A18 group to the A14/A16/A19 group, and A35's moved the opposite way. [DIR010, DIR011, DIR012, DIR015, DIR017, DIR026]

- Step distances changed consistently with a three-activity block changing sides: the gap from A24 to A25 was always 4 steps in the earlier window and is always 2 steps in the later one; the gap from A35 to A20 was always 2 and is now always 4. [LAG007, LAG008]

- Participation was essentially preserved: A15/A17/A18 still occur in every case, still exactly one of A14/A16/A19 per case (shares shifted mildly: A16 from about 35 to 29 in 100, A19 from 36 to 39), and the anchors kept their roles — A35 and A25 stayed put, while A20 and A24 sit slightly later (mean position ~0.38 to ~0.44 and ~0.57 to ~0.64), consistent with a longer block now preceding them. [OCC013, OCC014, OCC015, POS007, POS008, POS009, POS010]

- Outside this cluster there are only small, scattered fluctuations, e.g. A01's outgoing share to A05 rose from about 26 to 37 in 100, A29's share to A30 from 46 to 57 in 100, A36's split moved from about 48/52 to 54/46 toward A37, A34's mean occurrences per case rose from 0.78 to 0.89, and A38/A39 fell from 0.89 to 0.77. [DIR001, DIR021, DIR027, OCC021, OCC024, OCC025]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that two blocks of activities exchanged their placement in the sequence while their participation stayed the same. In the earlier window, each case ran one of A14, A16 or A19 between A35 and A20, and ran the trio A15, A17 and A18 (all three, in no fixed internal order) between A24 and A25; in the later window the trio runs between A35 and A20 and the single-choice step runs between A24 and A25, with A35 still upstream and A25 still downstream. [REL014, REL015, DIR017, DIR026, LAG007, LAG008]

The placement change is shown directly and consistently by all four relational or positional views: order splits flip all-or-nothing (A20 before A15 in 100 in 100 earlier cases, after it in 100 in 100 later cases), the six activities' positions shift by about a third of the trace, the successors of the single-choice activities change from always A20 to always A25, and the A24→A25 and A35→A20 distances change from 4 to 2 and 2 to 4 steps. Each of these items is larger than in about 199 of 200 random re-splits of the pooled data, and participation is essentially unchanged, so this is a change in placement rather than in how often the activities run. [REL014, REL015, POS001, POS002, DIR010, DIR011, LAG007, LAG008]

An important competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that the two windows sample different case populations — for example two pre-existing variants with opposite block order, each dominating one window — rather than a reordering within one process; the completely one-sided order splits would require the variant mix to flip entirely, and only traces spanning the change point could distinguish these possibilities. [REL014, REL015]

Other differences: small, scattered shifts outside this cluster — A01's outgoing share to A05, A29's share to A30 and A36's split toward A37 all moved by a few percentage points, A34 became slightly more frequent (mean 0.78 to 0.89 per case) and A38/A39 slightly less frequent (0.89 to 0.77) — are not part of the main hypothesis; with no chance-expectation baseline provided, they cannot be separated from ordinary sampling variation. [DIR001, DIR021, DIR027, OCC021, OCC024, OCC025]
