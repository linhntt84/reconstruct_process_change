### Observed differences

- In the later window the usual order between A36 and A37 reversed. Earlier, among the roughly 45 in 100 traces containing both, A37 ran before A36 in about two thirds of them (29 vs 16 in 100 traces overall); afterwards A36 ran before A37 in about three fifths (28 vs 18 in 100). The two stayed immediately adjacent in both windows — a one-event gap in essentially all co-occurrences later and about 97 in 100 earlier. [REL006, LAG002]
- The handovers around the pair flipped with the order: from A36, continuation to A37 rose from about 34% to 60% of outgoing transitions while continuation to A33 fell from about 64% to 37%; from A37, continuation to A33 rose from about 35% to 58% while continuation to A36 fell from about 64% to 38%; and from A32, entry into the pair shifted from A37 (29% to 17%) toward A36 (16% to 28%). Their typical positions reversed accordingly: A37 previously sat slightly earlier than A36 (mean position about 0.76 vs 0.78) and afterwards slightly later (about 0.79 vs 0.77), with A37's spread narrowing. [DIR036, DIR037, DIR032, POS004, POS003]
- Participation moved in opposite directions for two groups of activities: A03, A11, A12 and A13 each appear in about 9–10 fewer of 100 traces (from roughly 57–58 down to 47–49), while A01, A05, A07 and A09 each appear in about 10 more (from roughly 42–44 up to 52–54) — a net switch of about 20 in 200 traces between the groups. [OCC003, OCC010, OCC011, OCC012, OCC001, OCC005, OCC007, OCC009]
- Routing around A07, A09 and A14 shifted: from A01 the share continuing to A07 fell from about 41% to 22% while A09 rose from about 26% to 37%; from A09 continuation to A14 fell from about 35% to 21% while A07 rose to about 41%; from A07 continuation to A14 rose from about 28% to 44%. A07 and A14 now occur together in about 53 of 100 traces (previously 43), almost always with A07 first, and the gap between them shortened (a one-event gap is now the most common, previously three events); A07 also occurs slightly later within traces and more consistently. [DIR001, DIR009, DIR007, REL001, LAG001, POS001]
- A40 and A41 run and repeat more: A40's participation rose from about 18 to 25 in 100 traces (occurrences per trace 0.31 to 0.43, on a modest base of 36→51 traces), A41's repeat share rose from about 17 to 26 in 100 while its participation stayed near 51 in 100, and from A41 the share continuing to A40 rose from about 37% to 45% while continuation to A42 fell from about 61% to 53%. [OCC034, OCC035, DIR041]
- Several marked redirections at rare activities (A02, A10, A48) rest on very few observed outgoing transitions (about 8–9, 6, and 2–6 respectively) and are consistent with sampling noise rather than a behavioural change. [DIR002, DIR010, DIR048, OCC002]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports one specific possibility directly: in the later window, A36 and A37 exchanged their usual order in the late segment of the process. Before, the pair was typically entered from A32 into A37 and exited from A36 to A33; afterwards it is entered into A36 and exited from A37 to A33. The order relation itself flipped (A36-before-A37 up from 16 to 28 in 100 traces, A37-before-A36 down from 29 to 18), the pair stayed immediately adjacent, the surrounding handovers flipped consistently, the two activities' typical positions reversed, and both activities' overall frequencies stayed stable at about 45–46 in 100 traces — which rules out a mere change in how often they run. [REL006, LAG002, DIR036, DIR037, DIR032, POS004, POS003, OCC031, OCC032]

An important competing explanation remains: both orders already occurred in the earlier window (roughly one third vs two thirds), so the evidence cannot distinguish a re-ordered constraint from a shift in the mix of two pre-existing order variants; the adjacency comparison also rests on only 32 pairs in the earlier window. [REL006, LAG002]

Other differences:
- A change in how often two alternative groups of activities run: A03, A11, A12 and A13 each appear in about 10 fewer of 100 traces while A01, A05, A07 and A09 each appear in about 10 more, together with rerouting around A07, A09 and A14 and a tighter, more frequent A07→A14 succession. This is consistent with roughly 20 in 200 cases switching routes, though the internal routing changes mean it is not a pure shift between unchanged paths. [OCC001, OCC003, OCC005, OCC007, OCC009, OCC010, OCC011, OCC012, DIR001, DIR007, DIR009, REL001, LAG001, POS001]
- A40 and A41 occur and repeat more often, with A41's handover shifting toward A40. [OCC034, OCC035, DIR041]
- Apparent redirections at rare activities (A02, A10, A48) rest on fewer than ten outgoing transitions each and may be sampling noise. [DIR002, DIR010, DIR048]
