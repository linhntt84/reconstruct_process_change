### Observed differences

- The clearest difference concerns A14, A15 and A20. In the earlier window each of them appeared in 94 of 200 traces (about 47 in 100), and the three always appeared together as a block or not at all; in the later window each appeared in 199 of 200 traces (about 995 in 1000). Their typical event position within the trace stayed the same (medians unchanged), so the difference is how often they run, not where they sit [OCC013, OCC014, OCC019, REL001, POS001, POS002, POS003].

- Routing out of A36 changed accordingly. Earlier, A36 was followed by A21 in about 53 in 100 of its outgoing transitions and by A14 in about 47; in the later window A36 is followed by A14 in about 995 in 1000 transitions and almost never by A21. The step distance from A36 to A21 changed from usually one step (53 in 100) to almost always four steps (995 in 1000) [DIR024, LAG001].

- The block now always sits between A36 and A21 in a fixed order: A36 before A14, A14 before A20, A20 before A15, and all three before A21, each pair co-occurring in about 995 in 100 later-window traces. Earlier, the block members and A21 were mutually exclusive — A20 and A21 never co-occurred, and A14 and A36 co-occurred only within the 47-in-100 block cases. Consistently, A21's average event position moved later (0.41 to 0.43) and A36's moved earlier (0.33 to 0.30) [REL002, REL003, REL004, REL007, REL012, REL013, POS004, POS005].

- A37, A39 and A40 repeat more often in the later window: A37 averaged 1.7 occurrences per trace versus 1.6, with repeats in about 45 vs 36 in 100 traces; A39 and A40 averaged 0.89 versus 0.76, with repeats in about 20 vs 12 in 100 traces; A40 also led back to A37 more often (46 vs 36 in 100) and on to A41 less often (54 vs 64 in 100) [OCC026, OCC028, OCC029, DIR027, LAG002, REL016, REL017].

- Smaller shifts appear elsewhere: A16 led to A26 more often (55 vs 37 in 100) and to A19 less often (21 vs 38), while A19 led back to A16 more often (29 vs 16); participation rose for A01, A03, A04 and A05 (about 49 to 55 in 100 traces) and fell for A02, A06, A07 and A08 (about 51 to 45) and A13 (30 to 23), with A02, A07 and A08 rerouting toward A08 and A09; A35 led to A36 more often (67 vs 59 in 100) [DIR010, DIR012, DIR013, OCC001, OCC002, OCC006, OCC007, OCC008, OCC012, DIR002, DIR007, DIR008, DIR023].

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the block A14 → A20 → A15 between A36 and A21 changed from optional to mandatory. In the earlier window about 47 in 100 cases executed the block — always in the fixed order A36, A14, A20, A15, A21 — while the rest went from A36 directly to A21, making A14/A15/A20 and A21 alternatives; in the later window the block runs in essentially every case, with A21 following four steps after A36. The change is shown directly by relational and positional evidence (A36's successor profile, the A36-to-A21 step distance, the pair relations), it is consistent across the occurrence, direction, position and relation indicators, and its size (94 vs 199 of 200 traces per activity, empirical strength 0.995 on the key items) is far outside what random re-splits of a stable process produce. Limitations: most items describe one underlying trace behaviour and are not independent confirmations, no screening baseline for chance expectations was supplied, and the evidence cannot rule out that the two windows simply contain different case mixes rather than a changed process [OCC013, OCC014, OCC019, DIR024, LAG001, REL003, REL012, POS004, POS005].

Other differences: A37, A39 and A40 repeat more often in the later window, with A40 routing back to A37 more often; smaller routing and participation shifts involve A16, A19 and A26, the group A01–A08, and A13 — these have moderate empirical strength and could partly reflect sampling variation, and they are not part of the main hypothesis [OCC026, OCC028, OCC029, DIR027, DIR010, DIR013, OCC001, OCC002, OCC012].
