### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that routing between existing alternatives changed: in the later window, several preferred successions between activities that already executed together were reversed. In the group reached from A01, the typical chain A03→A04→A05 (exiting to A09) ran in the opposite direction, A05→A04→A03; in the group reached from A02, A06 and A08 exchanged places (A06→A08→A07 became A08→A06→A07); and after A26 the pairs (A32, A33) and (A34, A35) swapped their internal order while A26's entry shifted from A32 toward A34. This rests on direct successor-distribution changes: the branch sources were observed throughout (about 200 outgoing transitions each), the group members ran in roughly 45–55 in 100 traces in both windows, and the same successor sets appear in both windows, so no path was added, removed or bypassed — only the shares between existing alternatives moved. The shifts are complementary across related activities, and the largest differences were almost never approached by random re-splits of the pooled traces (empirical strengths up to about 0.99). An important competing explanation that cannot be fully excluded is sampling variation between the two 200-trace windows — the evidence provides no screening baseline against which the number of flagged items could be compared — and with successor shares alone a strict reversal cannot be distinguished from a re-weighting of parallel edges, although the size of the largest shifts (for example A06→A08 halving from about 52% to 26%) makes pure chance unlikely.
