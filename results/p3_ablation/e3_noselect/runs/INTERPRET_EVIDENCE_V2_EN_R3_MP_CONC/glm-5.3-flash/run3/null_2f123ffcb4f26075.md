### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that, in the later window, the existing alternative paths around A15, A17 and A18 are taken with different frequencies: A15 now leads to A17 rather than to A18, A17 now leads to A18 rather than back to A15, A18 now leads to A25 rather than to A17, and A24 more often continues into A15. This is a redistribution among paths that all remain in use — each source shows about 200 outgoing transitions in both windows and every edge keeps a share of roughly 23–40 in 100 — not an added, removed or bypassed path. The sign is direct (directly-follows shares) and consistent across four edges, each difference larger than in about 78–99 of 100 random re-splits of the same data. The evidence cannot show whether the four shifts have one shared driver (for example a change in how often the A15–A17–A18 cycle runs) or are independent routing changes, and with 200 traces per window a pure sampling difference cannot be fully excluded; no expected-by-chance baseline for the total number of flagged items is provided.
