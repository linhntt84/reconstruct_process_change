### Observed differences

- Across all six profiled activities, the outgoing-transition mixes differ only by a few percentage points between the windows, and the two busiest sources were essentially stable: in the earlier window about 81 in 100 of A01's outgoing transitions went to A06 (about 82 in 100 in the later window), and about 85 in 100 of A06's went to A05 (about 86 in 100 later); both differences sit well inside the range produced by random re-splits of the pooled data (strength about 0.17 and 0.03) [DIR001, DIR006].
- The largest shifts all favour A01 as a destination: in the later window A01 took about 10 in 100 of A02's outgoing transitions versus about 16 in 100 earlier, about 45 in 100 of A03's versus about 39, and about 52 in 100 of A04's versus about 46; these are the most unusual differences in the set, but even the strongest of them (from A02) is exceeded by roughly one in five random re-splits [DIR002, DIR003, DIR004].
- Secondary shifts point in mixed directions: A04 gained share as successor of A02 (about 67 to 70 in 100) while losing share as successor of A03 (about 12 to 8 in 100), and A06 lost share as successor of A03 (about 8 to 5 in 100) [DIR002, DIR003].
- From A05, the successor mix tilted away from A04 (about 44 in 100 earlier to about 36 later) toward A02 (about 39 to 49 in 100), but this profile rests on only 23 outgoing transitions before and 39 after, making it weakly supported [DIR005].

### Inference

Support level: Insufficient evidence

The evidence is insufficient to infer a change in the process. All observed differences are small redistributions of a few percentage points in outgoing-transition shares with mostly low-to-moderate empirical strengths, and even the strongest item is one that about one in five random re-splits exceeds, so sampling variation between the two 200-trace windows remains a plausible explanation [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006].
