### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that A01 appears in the later window as a new, at-most-once-per-case step executed after A18 and before the activities A18 previously fed directly. In the earlier window A01 was never observed and A18 distributed its outgoing transitions across many activities; in the later window A01 runs once in about 48 in 100 cases at a late position, every case containing A01 also contains A18 before it, A18 leads to A01 in about 65 in 100 of its outgoing transitions, and A01's outgoing mix closely mirrors what A18 itself fed in the earlier window. The occurrence, position, direction and relation items describe facets of this one difference rather than independent confirmations, and each is individually unusual under random re-splits of unchanged windows (empirical strength about 0.995); 0 of 200 versus 95 of 200 cases makes a rare-variant explanation implausible. Limitations: the successor shares out of A01 rest on about 59 observed transitions, and strictly speaking absence in the earlier window means only that A01 was not observed there; A18's slightly earlier position is consistent with, but not specific to, this change. [OCC001, POS001, DIR001, DIR017, REL001, OCC018, POS002]

Other differences: repetition shifted from one group of activities to another — A02 and A09 began repeating while A08 and A14 stopped — together with routing shifts such as A09 now being followed by A02 in about 65 in 100 of its transitions and A14's successors spreading from mostly A08 to a mix including A06 and A15; these shifts are not explained by the A01 change. [OCC002, OCC008, OCC014, OCC009, DIR008, DIR013]

An important competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that the appearance of A01 and these repetition and routing shifts are parts of one broader restructuring rather than a single isolated change; the comparison shows they coincide in the same windows, not how they are related. [OCC002, OCC008, DIR008, DIR017]
