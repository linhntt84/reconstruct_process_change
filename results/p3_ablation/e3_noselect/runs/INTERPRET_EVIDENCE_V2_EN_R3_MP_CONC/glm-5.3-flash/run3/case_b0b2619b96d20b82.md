### Observed differences

- **Succession around A01, A04 and A06 flipped.** In the earlier window, every observed outgoing transition of A01 went to A06 (192 transitions) and every outgoing transition of A04 went to A01 (89 transitions), while A06 led to A05 in about 54 in 100 of its transitions and to A04 in about 46 in 100. In the later window, A01 leads to A04 (77 transitions), A04 leads to A06 (181 transitions), and A06 leads to A05 in about 57 in 100 and to A01 in about 43 in 100; transitions from A06 to A04 are no longer observed. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003]
- **Adjacent pairs swapped.** Before, the pairs A01→A06, A06→A04 and A04→A01 typically occurred one step apart, with the opposite pairs two steps apart; in the later window this is reversed — A01→A04, A04→A06 and A06→A01 are the one-step pairs, and the former pairs now sit two steps apart. [LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006]
- **Relations changed accordingly.** A01 and A06 appeared together in about 52 in 100 traces before versus 20 in 100 after, with A06 now appearing without A01 in about 32 in 100 traces (essentially never before) and the earlier "A01 before A06" ordering (about 27 in 100 traces) gone. A04 and A06 appeared together in about 24 in 100 traces before versus 52 in 100 after, with A04 preceding A06 in about 32 in 100 traces after (never before). A01 also no longer appears without A04, whereas before it did so in about 28 in 100 traces. [REL001, REL002, REL003]
- **Participation and repetition shifted.** A01 takes part in about 20 in 100 traces in the later window (about 52 in 100 before), with mean occurrences per trace down from about 0.96 to 0.39 and within-trace repetition down from about 24 in 100 to 8 in 100 traces. A04 takes part in about 52 in 100 traces (about 24 in 100 before), with mean occurrences up from about 0.45 to 0.91 and repetition up from about 10 to 20 in 100. A06 and A05 are essentially stable (about 52 in 100 traces in both windows), as is A03 (about 48 in 100). [OCC001, OCC003, OCC005, OCC004, OCC002]
- **Event positions (not clock time).** When it occurs, A01 now sits later in the trace (median position 0 versus about one-third in before) and A04 earlier (about one-third versus 0 before); A06's position is unchanged (median about one-third in both windows). [POS001, POS002, POS003]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the repeating cycle through A01, A04 and A06 runs in the opposite order in the later window: in the earlier window traces moved A01 → A06 → A04 and back to A01 before exiting to A05, whereas in the later window they move A01 → A04 → A06 and back to A01, still exiting to A05 from A06 — in effect, A04 and A06 have exchanged places in the cycle. Along with this, the cycle's footprint changed: A01 now takes part in far fewer traces (about 20 in 100 versus 52 in 100) and repeats less often, while A04 takes part in more traces (about 52 in 100 versus 24 in 100) and repeats more often. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, LAG001, LAG004, REL002, REL003, OCC001, OCC003]

The hypothesis rests on direct successor changes, each observed on roughly 80–190 transitions per window, together with matching one-step lag changes and relation changes; the occurrence and position shifts are consistent with the new order, and A03, A05 and A06's own frequency and position remained stable, so the difference is confined to this cycle. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006, OCC002, OCC004, OCC005, POS003]

What the evidence cannot rule out: the reversal itself is observed directly, but the evidence does not show where the repetition starts, so the participation shift (fewer traces with A01, more with A04) could reflect a changed entry point into the same cycle rather than the reordering alone; and since no screening baseline is included, the number of qualifying items cannot be compared against a chance expectation, leaving a chance origin of the whole pattern unlikely but not formally excluded given the finite 200-trace windows. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003]
