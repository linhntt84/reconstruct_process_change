### Observed differences

- The cycle between A15 and A16 repeated less often in the later window. In the earlier window, A15 and A16 each occurred about 1.7 times per trace (both in every trace), about 40 in 100 traces repeated the cycle, and the return edge A16→A15 accounted for about 42 in 100 of A16's outgoing transitions. In the later window, both means fell to about 1.55 per trace, only about 33 in 100 traces repeated the cycle, the A16→A15 return fell to about 35 in 100, and exits grew: A15→A04 rose from about 13 to 17 in 100, and A16→A18 and A16→A41 from about 12 and 31 to about 15 and 35 in 100. [OCC014, OCC015, DIR015, DIR016]
- Consistent with earlier exits from that cycle, A04 appeared in about 25 of 100 traces before versus about 30 of 100 after, and A18 in about 21 versus 25. [OCC004, OCC017]
- After A43, the choice between two downstream branches shifted: A43→A44 rose from about 44 to 50 in 100 of A43's outgoing transitions while A43→A45 fell from about 54 to 49; A44 also returned to A43 more often (about 35 to 41 in 100) at the expense of leaving to A47 (about 64 to 58). A44 appeared in about 58 of 100 traces before and about 65 of 100 after, while A45 and A46 each appeared in about 66 of 100 traces before and about 62 after, with their mean occurrences falling from about 0.97 and 0.96 to about 0.86 and 0.87. [DIR043, DIR044, OCC042, OCC043, OCC044]
- Several other well-observed activities show small routing shifts of roughly 5–13 percentage points (for example A01→A05 up from about 33 to 44 in 100 against A01→A07, A03→A11 up against A03→A12 down, A12→A13 up against A12→A11 down, A23→A21 up against A23→A22 down, A38→A37 up against A38→A39 down), and participation of mid-frequency activities shifted by about 2–5 in 100 traces in both directions (for example A11, A12 and A13 down from about 55 to 51, A01, A05 and A07 up from about 45 to 49–50). [DIR001, DIR003, DIR012, DIR023, DIR038, OCC001, OCC005, OCC006, OCC010, OCC011, OCC012]
- The rarely observed activities A02, A06, A08, A10 and A48 show completely reshuffled outgoing-transition profiles between the windows, but these rest on only about 2–13 observed transitions per window, and for A08 and A10 random re-splits look at least as different, so these profiles carry little weight. [DIR002, DIR006, DIR008, DIR010, DIR048]

### Inference

Support level: Weak hypothesis

The evidence suggests one specific hypothesis: in the later window, the cycle between A15 and A16 was iterated fewer times per case, with cases leaving the cycle earlier instead of looping back from A16 to A15 — before, the cycle repeated in about 40 of 100 traces with A16 returning to A15 in about 42 of 100 of its transitions; after, about 33 of 100 and 35 of 100, with average occurrences of both activities falling from about 1.7 to about 1.55 while both remained in every trace. [OCC014, OCC015, DIR015, DIR016]

Supporting signs: the shift is visible directly in the successor shares of A15 and A16 (about 310–340 outgoing transitions per window), the occurrence indicators agree on units observed in all 200 traces per window, and the presumed exit targets A04 and A18 participate more often afterwards. What is missing: the supporting items describe one underlying behaviour rather than independent confirmations, their empirical strengths of about 0.79–0.88 mean that between roughly one in ten and one in five random re-splits produces a difference at least as large, and the evidence provides no screening baseline showing that the number of qualifying items exceeds what an unchanged process yields — so sampling variability across the many tested activities remains a competing explanation that cannot be ruled out. [OCC014, OCC015, DIR015, DIR016, OCC004, OCC017]

Other differences:
- A second, separate pattern suggests a shift between two existing alternative paths after A43 — more traffic on the A44 branch (including A44→A43 returns) and less on the A45→A46 branch; all involved activities are well observed in both windows, so this would qualify as a change in how often existing paths are taken, but it rests on similarly moderate strength values and is not part of the main hypothesis. [DIR043, DIR044, OCC042, OCC043, OCC044]
- The small routing and participation shifts at other activities are of a size that finite samples of 200 traces per window also produce without a change. [DIR001, DIR003, DIR012, DIR023, DIR038, OCC001, OCC005, OCC006, OCC010, OCC011, OCC012]
- The completely reshuffled successor profiles of the rare activities A02, A06, A08, A10 and A48 are typical of random re-splits for units with so few observations and should not be read as structural change. [DIR002, DIR006, DIR008, DIR010, DIR048]
