### Observed differences

- For all five observed source activities (A01, A03, A04, A05, A06), the mix of outgoing transitions is broadly the same in both windows: every successor seen in the earlier window is still seen in the later window and no new successors appear, so all differences are small redistributions among the same edges [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005].
- The largest shift concerns A01: in the later window, about 13–14 in 100 of its outgoing transitions go to A06, down from about 20 in 100 earlier, while transitions to A04 rose from about 38 to 40 in 100 and to A05 from about 9 to 12 in 100; the share to A03 stayed at about 28 in 100 [DIR001].
- From A04, transitions to A03 rose from about 8–9 in 100 earlier to 14 in 100 in the later window, while transitions to A05 fell from about 69 to 64 in 100 [DIR003].
- From A03, transitions to A01 fell from about 21 in 100 earlier to about 16 in 100 in the later window, while transitions to A06 rose slightly from about 60 to 62–63 in 100 [DIR002].
- From A05 and A06, all per-successor shifts are about 4 in 100 or smaller [DIR004, DIR005].
- None of these differences stands out against random variation: the highest empirical strength is about 66 in 100, meaning roughly one in three random re-splits of the pooled traces produces a difference at least this large, and the remaining items range from about 12 to 49 in 100 [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005].

### Inference

Support level: Insufficient evidence

The evidence is insufficient to infer a change in the process. The observed shifts in transition shares are small (at most about 6–7 in 100 on any single edge) and none is unusual for random re-splits of unchanged traces, so ordinary sampling variation between the two 200-trace windows remains a competing explanation [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005].
