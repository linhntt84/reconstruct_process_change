### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that A37 and A42 exchanged their places in the sequence after A36: in the earlier window A36 typically led directly to A42, with A37 following A42, whereas in the later window A36 typically continues to A37, which then leads to A42, and A42 precedes A36 in a small majority of traces. Since A36, A37 and A42 each still occur once in essentially every trace in both windows, this is a change in ordering and routing between existing activities, not a change in participation. [DIR036, DIR037, DIR042, REL001, LAG001, LAG002]

Supporting signs: the successor-share shifts at all three activities are coordinated (each activity's modal successor flipped), the order mix for the A36/A42 pair shifted in the same direction, and the adjacency changes match (A36→A42 less often immediate and more often two steps apart; A42→A36 more often immediate). The key items score high against random re-splits (roughly the 80th–99th percentile). These items likely reflect one underlying difference rather than independent confirmations. [DIR036, DIR037, DIR042, REL001, LAG001, LAG002]

Limitations: the order-mix shift itself is moderate (about 6–7 in 100 traces), and with 200 traces per window, sampling variability remains a competing explanation for the size of each individual shift; the consistency across successor, order and adjacency evidence makes a pure-sampling account unlikely but cannot rule it out. No screening baseline was provided to compare the number of qualifying items against a chance expectation. [REL001, LAG001]

Other differences: A08 occurs in more traces and reroutes to A49, while A15 and A16 receive fewer of its transitions and themselves repeat less often; A44 is executed in fewer traces and A43→A44 is less dominant; A01's successor mix shifted from A09 to A07; A51's outgoing profile changed on very few observations.
