### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that, in the later window, A10 has taken over the position and connections A05 held in the earlier window, and A05 those A10 held. A10 became the mandatory, about 4-times repeated hub reached from A14 (directly or via A15/A11), looping with A06 and leading to A16 or A09; A05 became an optional, early-placed activity (about 50 in 100 traces, about 2 occurrences) reached directly from A14 and leading to A17 or A08. As consequences, A16 — still fed by A10 — rose to about 68 in 100 traces at a later position, A08 — still fed by A05 — fell to about 34 in 100 at an earlier position, and A09 remained mandatory but is now reached from A10 instead of A05. [OCC004, OCC006, OCC008, OCC012, POS001, POS003, POS005, POS009, DIR003, DIR004, DIR007, DIR008, DIR010, DIR011, REL025]

This is shown directly rather than inferred from frequency shifts alone: the successor changes at A06, A11, A14, A15, A05 and A10, the swapped position distributions, and the flipped co-occurrence and distance patterns all describe the same restructuring, and every activity involved is well observed in both windows (each profile rests on roughly 59–200 traces, most on 93–200). [DIR004, DIR007, DIR008, DIR010, DIR011, POS001, POS005, REL003, REL004, LAG013, LAG015]

An important competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is a complete replacement of one case variant by another (earlier every trace contained A05 and about half also A10; later every trace contained A10 and about half also A05) rather than a modification within a single process; positions are also measured in event order, not clock time. [REL004, POS001, POS005]

Other differences: only small movements elsewhere (A01/A13/A14 loop shares, A07's branch shares to A02/A03/A12, A09's position), each within the range random re-splits produce. [DIR001, DIR005, POS004]
