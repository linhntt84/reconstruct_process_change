### Observed differences

- The clearest difference is a re-routing inside the A19/A20/A21 group, which runs in a minority of cases (about 43 of 200 traces in the earlier window, 50 in the later) and whose activities are followed either by another group member or by A25. In the earlier window the dominant successors were A21→A19 (about 51 in 100 of A21's outgoing transitions), A20→A21 (about 44 in 100) and A19→A25 (about 40 in 100), so the group most often ran A20 → A21 → A19 and then continued to A25. In the later window the dominant successors are A21→A20 (about 46 in 100), A20→A25 (about 52 in 100) and A19→A21 (about 42 in 100), so it most often runs A19 → A21 → A20 and then continues to A25, while the earlier majorities A21→A19 (down to about 26 in 100) and A19→A25 (down to about 20 in 100) receded; these redirections are unusual for random re-splits (empirical strengths about 0.90, 0.95 and 1.00). [DIR016, DIR017, DIR018]
- The entry into this group moved in the same direction: in the earlier window A15 led to A20 in about 10 in 100 of its outgoing transitions and to A19 in about 5 in 100; in the later window A15→A19 rose to about 13 in 100 while A15→A20 fell to about 4 in 100 (strength about 0.83). [DIR012]
- How often the group runs at all changed only marginally: A19, A20 and A21 each took part in about 21.5 in 100 traces earlier and 25 in 100 later, while A16, A17 and A18 each fell from about 78.5 to 75 in 100; differences of this size arise in about half of random re-splits, so they are unremarkable. - Smaller scattered shifts appeared elsewhere: A25 repeated slightly more often (mean about 1.6 → 1.7 occurrences per trace, strength about 0.65), A27 and A28 participated a little more (about 60.5 → 64 in 100 traces, strength about 0.63), A05 routed relatively more often to A09 (about 32 → 41 in 100, strength about 0.72), A18 routed less often to A16 (about 37 → 27 in 100, strength about 0.83), and A26 routed more often back to A25 than to A29 (about 37 → 43 in 100, strength about 0.66). [OCC021, OCC023, OCC024, DIR005, DIR015, DIR021]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the execution order within the A19/A20/A21 group changed. In the earlier window the group's most frequent sequence was A20 → A21 → A19, continuing to A25; in the later window it is A19 → A21 → A20, continuing to A25 — A19 and A20 effectively exchanged their places around A21, and the continuation to A25 now starts from A20 rather than from A19. The entry from A15 moved accordingly, from mostly A20 to mostly A19. [DIR016, DIR017, DIR018, DIR012]

The change is shown directly by the successor shares at all three group activities, each with an empirical strength of about 0.90 or higher, and every transition involved was observed in both windows, so this is a re-routing of existing paths rather than activities appearing or disappearing. The main limitation is sample size: the group runs in only about a quarter of traces, so each share rests on roughly 43–50 outgoing transitions per activity and window, with counts as low as about 8 versus 23 transitions for A21→A20; both windows also still show mixed orders inside the group, so the change concerns the most frequent sequence rather than a wholesale replacement of the ordering. [DIR016, DIR017, DIR018]

An important competing explanation that cannot be fully ruled out is sampling coincidence: with roughly two dozen routing comparisons, a few strong-looking items can arise without any change, although three aligned redirections plus the matching entry shift make a pure coincidence unlikely. [DIR012, DIR016, DIR017, DIR018]

Other differences: the choice between the two alternative trios after A15 shifted only slightly (A19–A21 up from about 43 to 50 of 200 traces; A16–A18 down from about 157 to 150), which random re-splits produce about half the time, and the small scattered shifts at A25, A27/A28, A05→A09, A18→A16 and A26→A25 noted above are not part of the main hypothesis.
