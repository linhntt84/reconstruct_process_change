### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the late block between A30 and A31 was restructured from a choice into a mandatory sequence: in the earlier window, each trace executed exactly one of two alternative trios — either A33 with A35 and A36 (about 49 in 100 traces) or A37 with A38 and A39 (about 51 in 100) — in a mixed internal order; in the later window, all six activities run in almost every trace (about 98–99 in 100), with A33, A35 and A36 preceding A37, A38 and A39 in about 95–98 of 100 traces. In other words, six activities that were optional alternatives appear to have become mandatory and sequentially ordered, while A30 and A31 remain the anchors before and after them. [REL049, REL051, REL057, REL062, OCC029, OCC033, DIR030, DIR033]

The hypothesis rests on direct relational evidence — cross-trio co-occurrence rising from about 2 to about 98 in 100 traces with a stable cross-trio order — corroborated by the successor changes at A30 and the first trio, by the growing A30-to-A31 distance, and by consistent position shifts; the key items show differences larger than in about 199 of 200 random re-splits. The agreeing indicators largely describe one underlying trace behaviour, so they should be read as one consistent picture rather than independent confirmations, and the many lag items based on only a handful of observations were not needed for it. A competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is a shift in the mix of case types between the windows rather than a modified routing rule; the near-absence of both-trios traces earlier (about 2 in 100) makes pure sampling noise implausible, but the aggregates cannot show what individual cases did. [REL051, DIR030, LAG020, POS006, POS009]
