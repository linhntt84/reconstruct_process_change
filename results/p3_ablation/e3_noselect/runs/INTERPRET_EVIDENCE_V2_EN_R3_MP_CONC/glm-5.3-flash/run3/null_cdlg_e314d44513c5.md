### Observed differences

- The clearest difference is in how three activities choose between the alternatives A03 and A15. In the earlier window, A13 sent about 46 of 100 of its outgoing transitions to A03 and about 54 to A15; in the later window about 67 go to A03 and 33 to A15. A10 moved from about 38 to 52 of 100 toward A03 (A15 falling from 58 to 43), and A11 from about 37 to 54 toward A03 (A15 from 63 to 43). [DIR013, DIR010, DIR011]
- In aggregate, A03 appeared in about 48 of 100 traces in the earlier window and about 58 of 100 in the later one (95 vs 116 of 200 traces), while A15 fell from about 53 to 42 of 100 (105 vs 84); both differences are larger than in roughly 96 of 100 random re-splits. [OCC002, OCC013]
- Within the A04/A05/A08 cluster the outgoing shares rotated: from A05, transitions to A08 rose from about 48% to 58% while those to A04 fell from 51% to 39%; from A04, transitions to A05 rose from 17% to 24% while those to A08 fell from 49% to 43%; from A08, transitions to A04 rose from 47% to 51% while those to A05 fell from 23% to 20% (this last profile change is weak). - A02 and A10 each occurred in about 23 of 100 traces before and 28 of 100 after (45 vs 56 of 200 traces), and A02's outgoing traffic concentrated further on A10 (from 87% to 95% of its transitions). [OCC001, OCC009, DIR002]
- A06, A07 and A13 occurred slightly less often in the later window (50 to 43, 56 to 48 and 50 to 43 of 200 traces), differences of modest strength. [OCC005, OCC006, OCC011]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that existing alternative paths are now taken with different frequencies: in the later window, A10, A11 and A13 route to A03 more often and to A15 less often than in the earlier window (for example, A13 sends about 67 of 100 transitions to A03 versus about 46 before), and correspondingly A03 appears in more traces (about 58 vs 48 of 100) while A15 appears in fewer (about 42 vs 53). The routing shares are observed directly at each source and are unusual for random re-splits (strengths about 0.80–0.95), the aggregate participation of A03 and A15 moves by matching amounts, and the shift is visible at each source individually, so it is not just a changed mix of source activities. Both alternatives and all three sources remain well observed in both windows and only the shares between them move, which fits a change in path frequencies rather than a path being replaced or an activity added or removed. [DIR010, DIR011, DIR013, OCC002, OCC013]

Limitations: the supporting items partly describe the same underlying behaviour and are not independent confirmations, and no screening expectation is provided to discount the number of indicators tested, so chance differences between two 200-trace samples cannot be fully excluded. The rare sources A09 and A14 even hint at a shift in the opposite direction, but with about 10–15 observations each they carry little weight. [DIR009, DIR014]

Other differences: the A04/A05/A08 cluster shows a rotation of routing shares toward the cycle A04→A05→A08→A04, clear from A05 and A04 and weak from A08; A02 and A10 occur in somewhat more traces than before, and A06, A07 and A13 slightly fewer, all of modest strength.
