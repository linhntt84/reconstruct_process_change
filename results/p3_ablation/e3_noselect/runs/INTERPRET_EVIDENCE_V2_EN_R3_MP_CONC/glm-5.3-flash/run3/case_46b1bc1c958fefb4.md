### Observed differences

- In the later window, A01 appears for the first time: it is not observed in any of the 200 earlier traces, but runs in about 101 of 200 traces (about half), roughly 0.5 times per trace, never twice in the same trace, and early within the case (median relative position about 0.14). [OCC001, POS001]
- In the later window, A02 is no longer observed: in the earlier window it ran in about 92 of 200 traces (about 46%), also early within the case (median relative position about 0.11), and in 0 of 200 traces afterwards. [OCC002, POS002]
- The two activities never share a trace in either window: earlier, about 46% of traces contained A02 alone and 54% contained neither; later, about 51% contain A01 alone and 49% neither. [REL001]
- The routing fan-out transfers from A02 to A01. Earlier, A02's outgoing transitions went mainly to A03 (about 32%), A06 (about 22%), A11 (about 14%), A13 (about 11%), A04 (about 9%) and A10 (about 7%); later, A01's outgoing transitions go mainly to A06 (about 24%), A03 (about 19%), A10 (about 15%), A11 (about 15%), A13 (about 11%), A05 (about 8%) and A04 (about 7%). ### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that A01 has taken over the role previously played by A02: an early, optional activity, present in about half of the cases, whose outgoing transitions fan out broadly to A03, A04, A05, A06, A09, A10, A11 and A13. In the earlier window A02 held this role, running in about 46% of traces at a similar early position with a nearly identical fan-out; in the later window A02 is not observed in any of 200 traces, while A01 runs in about 51% of them. [OCC001, OCC002, POS001, POS002, DIR001, DIR002]

The hypothesis is direct and consistent rather than a matter of counting signs: the two activities are strict alternatives in both windows, with the one-of-the-two share switching from A02 (about 46%) to A01 (about 51%) and no co-occurrence in either window; predecessors such as A03 and A11 rewire from A02 to A01 at comparable shares; and A01 builds the co-occurrence relations with A03 (about 51% of traces) and A06 (about 26%) that A02 used to have (about 46% and 26%). All supporting items sit at the top of the reference scale (empirical strength about 0.995) and rest on large samples (92 versus 0 traces for A02, 0 versus 101 for A01). [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, DIR003, DIR011]

An important competing explanation cannot be ruled out: a relabelling of the same step under a new activity code would look the same here. The fan-out is also not perfectly identical — A02 earlier sent about 1% of its outgoing transitions to A08, for which A01 shows no counterpart — and A01's participation (about 51%) is slightly higher than A02's (about 46%), which could be sampling variation or a real difference in how often the branch is taken. [DIR002, DIR001, OCC001, OCC002]

Other differences: A03 and A10 are executed more often in the later window (A03 from about 1.9 to about 2.2 times per trace, repeating in about 53% versus 45% of traces; A10 from about 0.9 to about 1.2 times per trace, appearing in about 53% versus 45% of traces). A01's fan-out includes A03 and A10, so this could be downstream of the new path, but the evidence does not establish that link. Small, weakly supported shifts also appear in the step distances around A03 and A06 and in A06's position within the case. [OCC003, OCC010, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, POS004]
