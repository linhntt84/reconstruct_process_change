### Observed differences

- The repeat loop between A36 and A37 was traversed less in the later window: A37 appeared in about 48 in 100 traces (about 0.8 occurrences per trace, with repeats in about 21 in 100) versus about 60 in 100 traces earlier (about 1.1 occurrences, repeats in about 31 in 100), and A36 appeared in about 22 in 100 traces versus about 31 earlier; observed outgoing transitions from A36 fell from 108 to 64. These are the largest activity-level differences in the evidence, each larger than in about 96–98 of 100 random re-splits. [OCC033, OCC034, DIR036]

- Routing around this loop shifted in the same direction. From A37, the share of outgoing transitions exiting to A38 rose from about 52% to about 60%, while returns to A36 fell from about 48% to about 40%. From A16, the share leading into A37 fell from about 35% to about 29%, while transitions to A17 and A18 rose by about 4 percentage points each; A17 and A18, which lead on to A38, appeared in about 24 and 29 in 100 traces versus 18 and 23 earlier. [DIR037, DIR016, OCC017, OCC018]

- The loop region around A39–A42 was executed more in the later window: A39 occurred about 1.7 times per trace versus about 1.5, with repeats in about 42 in 100 traces versus 31 (present in every trace in both windows), and A40 appeared in about 64 in 100 traces versus 60. From A40, transitions returning to A39 rose from about 33% to about 40%, while exits to A43 fell from about 64% to about 57%. [OCC035, OCC036, DIR040, DIR042]

- Several smaller participation shifts of roughly 4–7 in 100 traces occurred: A23 (about 49 vs 55) and A32 and A33 (about 49 vs 53–54) decreased, while A24 (about 52 vs 45), A34 and A35 (about 52 vs 46–47) increased; A05's outgoing split moved from A09 (about 27% vs 37%) towards A14 (about 36% vs 27%), and A21's split moved from A23 (about 22% vs 32%) towards A24 (about 26% vs 16%). [OCC022, OCC023, OCC029, OCC030, OCC031, OCC032, DIR005, DIR021]

- A44 occurs in every trace in both windows, but an outgoing transition from it was observed only once earlier (to A43) and five times in the later window (once each to A36, A37, A26, A06 and A34); with 1 versus 5 observations this difference cannot be judged. [DIR044, OCC039]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that, in the later window, the repeat loop between A36 and A37 is executed less often and repeated less: fewer traces contain A37 (about 48 vs 60 in 100) and A36 (about 22 vs 31), entry into the loop from A16 decreased (about 29% vs 35% of A16's outgoing transitions), and at A37 the exit to A38 is chosen more often (about 60% vs 52%) instead of returning to A36, with the alternative route from A16 via A17 and A18 to A38 used more. The same activities and successors are observed in both windows, so this is a shift in how often existing paths are taken and how often the loop repeats, not a replacement or relocation of activities; the occurrence drops are the largest and most unusual activity-level differences in the evidence, and the routing shifts rest on 157–333 observed outgoing transitions. [OCC033, OCC034, DIR037, DIR016, OCC017, OCC018]

Limitations: the occurrence and routing items summarise the same traces, so they are consistent facets of one underlying difference rather than independent confirmations, and no screening baseline is provided, so the number of qualifying items cannot be compared with what an unchanged process would produce. [OCC033, OCC034, DIR037, DIR016]

An important competing explanation that cannot be ruled out is sampling variation between the two 200-trace windows: several of the smaller routing and participation differences fall within the range random re-splits produce, and the rare transitions out of A44 rest on too few observations to interpret. [DIR010, DIR044]
