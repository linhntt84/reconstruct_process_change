### Observed differences

- The routing out of A21 changed markedly. In the earlier window, A21's outgoing transitions split between two routes: about 52 in 100 went to A22, and about 48 in 100 went to one of three other activities (A28 about 17%, A33 about 18%, A27 about 14% of those transitions). In the later window, about 99.5 in 100 of A21's outgoing transitions go to A22, with A28 retaining only about 0.5% [DIR014].
- Participation shifted accordingly: A22 appears in about 52 in 100 traces before and in essentially every trace afterwards (0.515 to 0.995), while A27, A28 and A33 each fall from about 48–49 in 100 traces to about 1 in 200. A21 and A23 still occur in every trace in both windows [OCC019, OCC020, OCC021, OCC026, POS001, POS003].
- Before, A27, A28 and A33 ran together as a group in about 48–49 in 100 traces, without a fixed internal order (A27 before A28 in about 24 in 100, A28 before A27 in about 25 in 100). In the later window they co-occur in only about 1 in 200 traces, and in about 99.5 in 100 traces neither appears; their co-occurrence with A21, A22 and A23 collapses the same way (for example A21–A27 from about 48 to about 0.5 in 100) [REL012, REL013, REL014, REL002, REL006, REL009].
- The distance from A21 to A23 was two steps in about 52 in 100 traces and four steps in about 48 in 100 before; in the later window it is two steps in about 99.5 in 100. A21's mean relative position moves slightly later and A23's slightly earlier, which is consistent with the shorter segment rather than a relocation of either activity [LAG001, POS001, POS003].

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the alternative route between A21 and A23 — the block of activities A27, A28 and A33, previously executed in roughly half of all cases in varying order — is effectively no longer executed, so that in the later window A21 → A22 → A23 is the near-universal path and A22 has become mandatory on this segment. In the earlier window the two routes were roughly balanced (about 52 versus 48 in 100 cases); in the later window about 199 in 200 traces take the A22 route and the three block activities appear in only about 1 in 200 traces. Because A27, A28 and A33 are nearly absent afterwards, this is a change in participation (the branch is skipped or has disappeared), not a re-weighting between two stable alternatives [DIR014, OCC019, OCC020, OCC021, OCC026, REL012, LAG001].

The change is shown directly by relational and directional evidence rather than inferred from marginals: A21's successor profile collapses onto A22, and the block activities' co-occurrence with A21, A22 and A23 falls from about 48–49 to about 0–1 in 100 traces. The core items sit at the top of the reference range (empirical strength about 0.995, meaning larger differences arose in fewer than 1 of 200 random re-splits), and the branch was frequent before (97 of 200 traces), which makes a rare-variant or sampling explanation implausible. Occurrence, direction, position and lag indicators all describe this one segment consistently [DIR014, REL002, REL009, OCC020, LAG001].

Limitations: the evidence cannot distinguish whether the block was removed from the process or became a conditional branch that is now almost never chosen, and it cannot rule out that cases which previously needed the block are now routed elsewhere before reaching A21 (a case-mix change rather than a change at the branch itself). The later-window internal ordering of A27, A28 and A33 rests on a single trace, and no chance-expectation baseline is provided, so the judgement rests on the directness and consistency of the items [DIR017, LAG004].
