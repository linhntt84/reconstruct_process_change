### Observed differences

- The clearest difference concerns A25, A31 and A26, which appear in every case in both windows. In the earlier window each was executed about 3.4 times per case on average and repeated within a case in about 75 in 100 cases; in the later window each is executed about 1.0 times per case and repeats in essentially no case (about 1 in 200). [OCC019, OCC020, OCC025]
- Routing out of A31 changed accordingly: earlier, about 71 in 100 of A31's outgoing transitions went back to A25 and about 29 in 100 went to A20; in the later window, about 96 in 100 go to A20 and only about 4 in 100 to A25. [DIR020]
- The relationship between A25 and A31 changed from interleaved repetition to a fixed sequence. The two activities still occur together in every case, but earlier they were interleaved or repeated in about 75 in 100 cases (A25 strictly first in about 25 in 100), whereas in the later window A25 precedes A31 in about 199 of 200 cases. [REL001]
- Succession timing changed in step: A31 follows A25 exactly two event positions later in about 92 in 100 cases in the later window, versus about 12 in 100 earlier; conversely, A31 followed by A25 within five positions, seen in 150 of 200 cases earlier, is seen in only 1 of 200 cases later. [LAG001, LAG002]
- The event positions of A25 and A31 became much more tightly concentrated (spread, as standard deviation, fell from about 0.12 to about 0.06 for both), with the late positions disappearing. [POS001, POS002]
- Smaller shifts occurred elsewhere: A13 (24 to 29 in 100 cases) and A14 (49 to 55) occur more often, A15 (52 to 46) and A18 (50 to 42) less often, A19 more often (51 to 58); A32, A33, A35 and A36 also occur somewhat less often. Routing shares moved by up to roughly 12 in 100: from A22 to A19 (26 to 35), from A19 to A16 (29 to 35) and away from A23 (50 to 40), from A33 to A34 (53 to 58), and from A38 to A39 (55 to 67). [OCC012, OCC013, OCC014, OCC017, OCC018, OCC026, OCC027, OCC028, OCC029, DIR013, DIR014, DIR021, DIR025]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the repeated back-and-forth cycle between A25 and A31 was removed: in the earlier window these two activities (together with A26, which shows the same counts) alternated and were re-executed about three to four times per case, whereas in the later window each runs once per case in the fixed order A25 then A31, after which A31 continues to A20 instead of returning to A25. [OCC019, OCC020, OCC025, DIR020, REL001]

The structural signs are direct and mutually consistent: the return from A31 to A25 collapsed (from about 71 to about 4 in 100 of A31's outgoing transitions, and from 150 to 1 of 200 cases within five positions), the pair's order became fixed (interleaved-or-repeated fell from 75 in 100 cases to essentially none, A25-before-A31 rose to about 199 of 200), close lag-2 succession became the norm (about 12 to 92 in 100), and the position spread of both activities halved — while participation stayed at 100 in 100 cases in both windows, indicating the activities stopped repeating rather than becoming optional. [DIR020, LAG002, REL001, LAG001, POS001, POS002, OCC019]

Limitations: no screening baseline is included, so the number of qualifying items cannot be compared against a chance expectation; the judgement rests on the size of the differences (about 2.4 fewer executions per case) and their agreement across occurrence, routing, relation, lag and position indicators. A26 shows the same occurrence collapse but no relational item covers it, so its place in the removed cycle is inferred from counts alone. [OCC020]

A competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that the dropped repetitions were partly displaced into slightly more frequent executions elsewhere, such as A10 and A11 rising from about 1.6 to 1.7 executions per case; that shift is small and only moderately strong. [OCC009, OCC010]

Other differences: the smaller participation and routing shifts listed above (around A13–A19, A22, A32–A36 and A38–A39) are not part of this hypothesis; they are changes of a few percentage points and could partly reflect sampling variation between the two 200-case windows.
