### Observed differences

- Activity A01 was not observed at all in the earlier window (0 of 200 traces); in the later window it runs in about 46 in 100 traces (92 of 200), roughly once per trace on average, typically around 40% of the way through the trace (median relative position about 0.38). [OCC001, POS001]
- In the earlier window, all of A05's outgoing transitions went to A10 and all of A06's went to A05. In the later window, about 47 in 100 of A05's and about 48 in 100 of A06's outgoing transitions go to A01 instead, and A01's outgoing transitions go to A05 (about 53 in 100) or A10 (about 47 in 100). Exits from the A03/A11 loop also changed: about 35 in 100 of A03's and about 24 in 100 of A11's outgoing transitions now go to A01, while A03's direct exits to A05 fell from about 51 to 16 in 100. [DIR001, DIR003, DIR005, DIR006, DIR007]
- Before, A01 never occurred together with A03, A05, A06 or A10, and A05 and A10 co-occurred in about 53 in 100 traces. In the later window, A01 co-occurs with A05 in 46 in 100 traces, with A10 in 46 in 100, with A06 in 33 in 100 and with A03 in 28 in 100 — and A05, A10, A06 and A03 were never observed without A01 in that window. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL009]
- Step distances between the neighbouring activities grew by about one step: A05→A10 moved from one step in about 56 in 100 pairs to one or two steps; A06→A05 from one step (about 76 in 100) to one or two; A06→A10 from two steps (about 76 in 100) to three (about 61 in 100); A03→A10 from two–three to three–four steps. [LAG001, LAG002, LAG005, LAG006, LAG007]
- Smaller shifts: participation in A05, A09 and A10 fell from about 53 to 46 in 100 traces and in A03/A11 from about 32 to 28; A06's median relative position moved later (about 0.14 to 0.26) and A10's slightly later (about 0.43 to 0.50). The participation drops are of a size that random re-splits of an unchanged process commonly produce. [OCC003, OCC004, OCC008, OCC009, OCC010, POS004, POS005]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that activity A01 became part of the flow as an intermediate step between A06/A05 and A05/A10. In the earlier window the segment ran A06 → A05 → A10, with the A03/A11 loop exiting directly to A05; in the later window A01 executes in about 46 in 100 traces, receives about half of the outgoing transitions of A05 and A06 and part of the exits from A03/A11, and itself leads to A05 or A10. [OCC001, DIR001, DIR003, DIR005, DIR006, DIR007]

The hypothesis is shown directly and consistently: A01's occurrence rises from zero to 92 of 200 traces; the rerouting of A05's and A06's outgoing transitions is observed on the transitions themselves; A01 now co-occurs with A05, A06, A10 and A03 whenever those appear; and the A05→A10, A06→A05, A06→A10 and A03→A10 distances each grow by about one step, which is what an added intermediate activity produces. Because A01 was absent from the earlier window, this is an activity becoming part of the segment rather than a shift in how often existing alternative paths are taken; the key differences are larger than in about 199 of 200 random re-splits. [OCC001, DIR005, DIR006, REL002, REL003, REL004, LAG005, LAG006, LAG007]

An important competing explanation cannot be fully ruled out: zero participation in the earlier window strictly shows only that A01 was not observed there, so a finite sample could in principle have missed a rare variant — although a behaviour now present in nearly half of the traces is unlikely to be missed across 200 earlier traces. The small participation drops for A05/A10 (about 53 to 46 in 100) are within the range random re-splits produce and are not claimed as part of the change. [OCC001, OCC004]

Other differences: A03 and A11 participation fell slightly (about 32 to 28 in 100 traces), A06's median relative position moved later (about 0.14 to 0.26), and the A05→A06 step distance increased by one in about a third of the observed pairs; these are small and the evidence does not establish them as changes beyond the added activity. [OCC003, OCC010, POS004, LAG004]
