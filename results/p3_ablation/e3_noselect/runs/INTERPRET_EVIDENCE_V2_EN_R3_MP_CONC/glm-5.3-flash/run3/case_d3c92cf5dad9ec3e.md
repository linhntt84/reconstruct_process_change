### Observed differences

- The dominant difference is a reordering in the middle of the process. In the earlier window, a trace ran A40, then one (effectively exactly one) of the alternatives A19/A21/A24, then A25, then A29, then the trio A20/A22/A23 (all three, in varying order), then A30. In the later window, traces run A40, then the trio A20/A22/A23, then A25, then A29, then one of A19/A21/A24, then A30 — the two middle blocks have exchanged places around the A25→…→A29 segment. - The reversal is near-deterministic on both sides: A25 preceded A20 in about 995 of 1,000 co-occurring cases before, and A20 precedes A25 in about 995 of 1,000 after; A29 preceded the trio members in about 99 in 100 cases before, and the trio members precede A29 in about 99 in 100 after. ### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that, in the later window, the always-executed trio A20/A22/A23 runs between A40 and A25 — where the one-per-case branch previously ran — while the branch (exactly one of A19, A21, A24 per case) runs between A29 and A30, directly before A30; the A25→…→A29 segment, A40's leading role and A30's trailing role are preserved, and no activity in this segment became more or less frequent, so the change is in ordering rather than in participation or path frequency. [REL014, REL015, REL027, REL028, REL032, REL033, DIR040, DIR029, DIR019, DIR021, DIR024, POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006, OCC018, OCC019, OCC020, OCC021, OCC022, OCC023]

- The order reversals are shown directly and flip from about 99 in 100 one way to about 99 in 100 the other, with empirical strength 0.995 on nearly every affected pair. [REL001, REL003, REL004, REL006, REL007, REL010, REL013, REL014, REL015, REL018, REL019, REL021, REL022, REL026, REL027, REL031, REL032, REL033, REL036, REL037]
- Directly-follows profiles, positions and gaps all change in the directions and magnitudes the block swap predicts, so the hypothesis is consistent across all five indicator families rather than resting on one indicator. [DIR040, DIR029, DIR020, DIR022, DIR023, POS001, POS002, POS004, POS005, LAG013, LAG019, LAG008, LAG015]
- Participation and co-occurrence are preserved (the trio co-occurs in all traces in both windows; the branch activities remain mutually exclusive alternatives), which rules out readings in which activities were added, removed, made optional or taken more or less often. [REL011, REL012, REL025, REL002, REL005, REL020, OCC019, OCC021, OCC022, OCC024, OCC027, OCC029, OCC037]

Limitations: the many agreeing items describe one underlying block swap and are not independent confirmations; the internal order of A20/A22/A23 remains unordered in both windows, so only block placement, not the trio's internal sequence, is established; no chance-expectation baseline was provided, so the judgement rests on the directness and consistency of the relational evidence; and the evidence cannot rule out that both orderings existed as pre-existing variants whose mix flipped between the windows rather than a re-routing of a single process. [REL011, REL012, REL025]

Other differences: A08's participation fell from about 17 to about 7 in 100 traces, the clearest participation change, though its after-window profile rests on only 14 traces [OCC007, DIR008]. Scattered small participation shifts and transition-share tilts occurred elsewhere (for example A41's split between A42 and A43 tilted toward A42, about 47 → 54 in 100); with roughly 46 activities tested, several such small shifts are also what an unchanged process produces [OCC003, OCC010, OCC011, OCC012, OCC016, OCC036, OCC039, OCC040, OCC041, DIR041, DIR034, DIR035, DIR012]. A few items rest on very few observations (for instance DIR046 with 7 vs 5 outgoing transitions, and LAG010–LAG012/LAG014 with 1–3 observations) and carry little weight on their own.
