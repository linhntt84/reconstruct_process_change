### Observed differences

- In the earlier window the three activities formed one fixed chain in every case: A02 was always followed by A04, A04 always by A03, and each occurred exactly once per trace (participation 100% for A02 and A04, no within-trace repetition, A02 always at the same mid position and A04 at a fixed later position). [OCC001, OCC002, DIR001, DIR002, POS001, POS002, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003]
- In the later window, A02 still appears in every trace but occurs about 2.1 times per trace on average and repeats within the trace in about 50 in 100 cases (previously never). [OCC001]
- A04 has become conditional: it appears in only about 99 of 200 traces (before: all 200), while its total volume is roughly unchanged (about 1.1 occurrences per trace, i.e., about 2.2 within the traces that contain it, repeating in about 28 in 100 traces). [OCC002, REL002, REL003]
- Routing changed at both activities: in the later window about 53 in 100 of A02's outgoing transitions lead to A04 and about 47 in 100 lead directly to A03 (before: all led to A04), and A04 now always leads back to A02 (before: always to A03). [DIR001, DIR002]
- The pairwise relations changed accordingly: A02–A04 and A03–A04 co-occur in only about half the traces (before: all), with no fixed order between A02 and A04 when they do; A04 precedes A03 in only about 22 in 100 traces (before: all); A02 and A03 still co-occur everywhere, but the strict A02-before-A03 order holds in only about half the traces, the rest showing interleaved or repeated occurrences. [REL001, REL002, REL003]
- Position and gap distributions spread out: A02 moved from a single fixed position (std 0) to a broader, on average later one (mean about 0.59, std about 0.21); A04's mean position moved earlier (about 0.75 to about 0.56); the A02→A03 gap changed from always two positions to most often one (about 40 in 100 observed pairs, the rest at three or five); the A04→A03 gap changed from always one to most often two (about 46 in 100) or four (about 18 in 100); and the A02→A04 pair within five steps now appears in about 99 of 200 traces instead of all 200, no longer exclusively at one step. [POS001, POS002, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

In the later window, the fixed chain A02 → A04 → A03 appears to have been restructured into an optional repetition around A02: A02 can either run directly to A03, or enter a cycle in which A04 always returns to A02, so that A02 → A04 → A02 runs one or more times before the case proceeds to A03. In about half the traces A04 is skipped entirely; among the about 99 traces that do contain it, it runs two or more times in about 56. [DIR001, DIR002, OCC001, OCC002, REL002, REL003]

This is shown directly rather than inferred from shifting path frequencies alone: A04's successor flipped completely from A03 to A02 while A02's successors split between A04 and A03; A02 gained within-trace recurrence it previously did not have; A04's participation halved while its per-case volume stayed similar; and the A04→A03 gap widened from one to typically two positions, exactly what an intervening A02 produces. Eleven of the twelve items show differences larger than those arising in about 995 of 1000 random re-splits of the pooled data, and all items tell one consistent story. [DIR001, DIR002, OCC001, OCC002, REL002, REL003, LAG001, LAG003]

Limitations: the evidence covers only A02, A03 and A04, so any further activities that might participate in the repeated cycle are invisible, and the aggregate shares cannot show whether every case follows one routing rule or the window mixes case populations with different behaviour. POS and LAG are based on event positions, not clock time, so no conclusion about timing is implied. [DIR001, POS001, LAG001]

Other differences: none — the position and gap shifts reported above are consequences of the same restructuring rather than separate changes. [POS001, POS002, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003]
