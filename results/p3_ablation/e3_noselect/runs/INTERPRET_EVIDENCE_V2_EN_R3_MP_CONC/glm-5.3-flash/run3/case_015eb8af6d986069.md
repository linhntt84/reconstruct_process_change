- The clearest difference is in the ordering of six activities — A20, A22, A23, A24, A25 and A26 — that run between A31 and A32 and occur in essentially every trace in both windows. ### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the ordering constraints inside the segment bounded by A31 and A32 were tightened into a single fixed sequence: in the later window A20, A22, A23, A24, A25 and A26 always run as A31 → A22 → A20 → A23 → A24 → A26 → A25 → A32, whereas in the earlier window the same activities — each present in essentially every trace in both windows — appeared in varying orders (A24 and A26 often before A23, A24 often before A20, A20 and A26 in both orders) and at varying distances, with A23 frequently passing straight to A32. This is a change in ordering constraints, not an exchange of positions or a shift in how often existing paths are taken. The order change is shown directly by relational items on pairs that co-occur in all 200 traces of both windows, with order shares moving by 0.43–0.87, and it is corroborated by the successor profiles, the fixed step distances and the consistent position shifts; nearly all of these items show differences larger than in essentially all of the roughly 200 random re-splits of the pooled data. The evidence cannot rule out that the two windows sample different mixes of case types that route through this segment differently, and it says nothing about why the order changed; chance expectations per indicator family were also not reported, so the screening check rests on these qualitative strengths.
