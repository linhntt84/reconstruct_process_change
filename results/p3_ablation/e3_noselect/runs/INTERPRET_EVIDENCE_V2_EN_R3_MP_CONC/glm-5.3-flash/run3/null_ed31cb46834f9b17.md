### Observed differences

- The clearest difference lies in what follows A24, A25 and A26. In the earlier window, A24's outgoing transitions went mainly to A30 (about 39%) and A25 (about 34%); in the later window they go mainly to A26 (about 41%) and A25 (about 37%), with A30 down to about 20%. A25 moved from leading mostly to A26 (about 43%) to leading mostly to A30 (about 49%), and A26 moved from leading mostly back to A24 (about 50%) to leading mostly to A25 (about 44%). Each of these three successor-profile changes is more extreme than about 89–99 in 100 random re-splits, on bases of roughly 44–53 outgoing transitions per activity and window [DIR024, DIR025, DIR026].

- A10 occurs in roughly twice as many traces (about 9 in 100 earlier, about 16 in 100 later; 18 versus 31 of 200), the largest single participation change (more extreme than about 96 in 100 random re-splits), while A10's own successor mix is essentially unchanged [OCC010, DIR010].

- A35's maximal direction score arises only because no outgoing transitions from it were observed in the earlier window (two in the later); an unobserved edge does not show that an activity or edge is absent from the process [DIR035].

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the flow among A24, A25 and A26 was rerouted in the later window. Earlier, A25 led mostly to A26, A26 led mostly back to A24, and A24 split mainly between A30 and A25; in the later window, A24 leads mainly to A26, A26 mainly to A25, and A25 mainly to A30, so the main transition into A30 now occurs from A25 rather than from A24 or A26. Alongside this, all three activities take part in more traces (about 22 versus 26 in 100), A30 is executed somewhat more often per trace (about 1.60 versus 1.72), and A20 reaches the group more often via A24 (about 5% versus 13% of its outgoing transitions) and less often via A25 (about 10% versus 4%) [DIR024, DIR025, DIR026, OCC022, OCC023, OCC024, OCC028, DIR020].

The supporting signs are direct and consistent: the three successor changes are observed at the activities concerned themselves, fit a single rerouting, and are each more extreme than about 89–99 in 100 random re-splits, making chance an unlikely joint explanation [DIR024, DIR025, DIR026]. Limitations: each successor share rests on only about 44–53 outgoing transitions per activity and window, and the profiles show changed shares rather than an unambiguous new sequence — A25→A24 did not become dominant, so any sharper claim about the exact new ordering goes beyond the evidence [DIR025]. An important competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that the succession shift is a downstream consequence of the changed entry into the group via A20 rather than a change in the group's internal flow [DIR020].
