In the earlier window, A23's outgoing transitions went most often to A31 (about 53 in 100, versus about 46 in 100 to A30), A31 led mostly back to A30 (about 53 in 100, versus about 47 in 100 to A24), and A30 led mostly to A24 (about 52 in 100, versus about 46 in 100 to A31) — a typical chain A23→A31→A30→A24. ### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the typical order of A30 and A31 between A23 and A24 reversed: in the earlier window the segment usually ran A23→A31→A30→A24, and in the later window it usually runs A23→A30→A31→A24. Both orders were already in use in both windows and only their shares moved (each edge of the earlier chain lost roughly 10–12 in 100, each edge of the later chain gained roughly 9–10 in 100), so this is a change in how often existing orders are followed, not in whether the activities run: A23, A24 and A30 still occur in every trace in both windows and A31 about once per trace. The three successor-profile changes agree in direction, each is based on about 200 outgoing transitions per window and each is larger than in about 96 in 100 reference comparisons, which makes a pure sampling fluctuation or a one-window rare variant unlikely. [DIR023, DIR029, DIR030, OCC022, OCC023, OCC026]

Limitations: the three items are three views of one underlying difference rather than independent confirmations, and successor shares alone cannot distinguish a uniform reordering of the segment from a changed mix of case variants in which the A23→A30→A31→A24 order is simply taken by more cases — the evidence cannot separate these. The evidence set also contains no screening summary, so the expected number of chance findings across all tested units cannot be verified and the judgement rests on the strength and coherence of the individual items. [DIR023, DIR029, DIR030]

Other differences: Comparable reversals occur elsewhere and are not part of this hypothesis: A07 and A09 exchanged their typical order around A05 in the loop entered from A01 [DIR001, DIR005, DIR007, DIR009]; the A11/A12/A13 block between A03 and A14 changed its typical traversal from A11→A13→A12 towards A12→A11→A13 [DIR003, DIR013, DIR012]; and A20 and A22 exchanged their typical order before exiting to A28 [DIR020, DIR022]. Participation also shifted for some rare activities — A04 was observed in about half as many traces later, and A08 and A10 declined slightly — while A15 and A16 repeated slightly more often [OCC003, OCC007, OCC009, OCC014, OCC015]. Whether these parallel shifts are independent local changes or one broader change in the case mix cannot be decided from this evidence. [DIR001, DIR020, OCC003]
