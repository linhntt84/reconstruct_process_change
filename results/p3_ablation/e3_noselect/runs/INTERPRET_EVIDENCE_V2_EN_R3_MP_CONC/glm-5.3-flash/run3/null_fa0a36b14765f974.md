### Inference

Support level: Weak hypothesis

The evidence suggests the following possibility: in the later window, existing alternative paths in the segment between A28 and A29 are taken with different frequencies. A32 and A33 are executed in about 55 in 100 traces (previously about 48) and A34 and A35 in about 46 (previously about 53); A28 routes more of its outgoing transitions to A32 (about 28 vs 19 in 100) and fewer to A35 (23 vs 31); and within the block the direct shortcuts toward A29 (A32→A29, A33→A32, A35→A34) lost share to the sequential transitions (A32→A33, A33→A29, A34→A35, A35→A29). Every activity and transition involved is well observed in both windows, so this reads as a redistribution of shares over existing paths, not an addition, removal or bypass. [OCC029, OCC030, OCC031, OCC032, DIR028, DIR032, DIR033, DIR034, DIR035]

Supporting signs: the participation and routing items agree in direction, the routing items rest on roughly 90–200 outgoing transitions per window, and the outgoing-profile differences at A28, A32 and A33 are larger than about 90 in 100 random re-splits. [DIR028, DIR032, DIR033] What is missing: no screening baseline is reported, so the number of qualifying items cannot be compared with what an unchanged process would produce; the participation shifts alone are moderate (about 76–81 in 100 reference comparisons smaller), the magnitudes are small (5–15 percentage points), and several items re-describe the same traces rather than confirming independently. [OCC029, OCC030, OCC031, OCC032, DIR034, DIR035] An important competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is therefore a coordinated sampling fluctuation between the two 200-trace windows.
