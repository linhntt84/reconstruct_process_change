In the earlier window, A24, A25 and A26 each ran in about 82 in 100 traces while A21, A22 and A23 each ran in about 19 in 100; in the later window, A21–A23 each run in about 79–80 in 100 traces and A24–A26 in about 21 in 100. ### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the two alternative paths available after A20 swapped dominance: in the earlier window roughly 4 of 5 traces continued from A20 into the block A24–A25–A26 — the three activities running together in varying order, typically alongside A30 — and about 1 in 5 took the block A21–A22–A23, while in the later window roughly 4 of 5 traces take A21–A22–A23 and about 1 in 5 take A24–A25–A26. The reversal is shown directly by A20's outgoing-transition shares and by the co-occurrence flips of all six activities, it is consistent with the unchanged internal mixed ordering of both blocks, with A30's switched company and with the blocks' stable event positions, and both paths are well observed in both windows (supports of 37–38 versus 158–163 traces, empirical strengths at about 0.99), which makes a rare-variant or sampling explanation unlikely. Because the blocks practically never share a trace and their structure is unchanged, this reads as a shift in how often two existing alternative paths are taken, not as activities being added, removed or reordered; an important competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that the two windows capture a different case mix rather than a modified process.
