### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that, in the later window, cases repeat the sequence between A42 and A43 more often before leaving it: A43 is executed in about 70 in 100 cases (previously about 58) and about 1.0 times per case (previously about 0.8), A42 about 1.8 times per case (previously about 1.6), and within the pair both directions gain share of the source activity's outgoing transitions (A42→A43 about 48→55 in 100; A43→A42 about 38→44) while the exits to A44 and A46 lose it. Supporting signs: the A43 change is the largest occurrence change in the sample and is exceeded in only about 3 in 100 random re-splits, the direction shares on every edge of the pair move the same way, and the extra A43 executions (about 44 per 200 cases) match the extra A42→A43 transitions almost one-for-one, which argues against the competing reading that A43 simply gained a new predecessor. Limitations: each window holds only 200 traces, so a chance concentration of loop-heavy cases in the later window cannot be ruled out, and the accompanying participation dips of A44 and A45 (about 65→57 in 100 cases) are weak — their differences are larger than those of only about a third of random re-splits — so the claim that exits became rarer rests on the direction shares alone.
