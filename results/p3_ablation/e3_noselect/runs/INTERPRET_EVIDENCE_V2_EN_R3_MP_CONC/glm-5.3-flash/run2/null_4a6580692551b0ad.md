### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that, in the later window, the block of activities running between A31 and A32 changed: the pair A37/A38 was executed in about 56 in 100 traces (up from about 49) while the pair A35/A36 was executed in about 44 in 100 (down from about 52), and within the A35/A36 pair the dominant execution order reversed — A36 immediately before A35 was the majority order in the earlier window (about 62 vs 41 traces), A35 immediately before A36 is the majority order in the later window (about 54 vs 34). A31 and A32 themselves kept running in every trace with unchanged typical spacing, so the change is confined to which pair runs and in which internal order. [REL001, LAG001, REL006, LAG005, LAG007, DIR031, OCC031, OCC032, OCC033, OCC034]

This rests on direct relational and positional evidence rather than on frequencies alone: the order shares flipped in REL006, the dominant step distances between A31/A35, A31/A36, A35/A32 and A36/A32 all swapped, and the outgoing shares of A31, A35 and A36 moved consistently; the direct items show differences larger than in about 99 of 100 random re-splits. Because the order flip is measured within traces that contain the pair, it cannot be explained by the pair simply running in fewer traces. [REL006, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG006, DIR031, DIR035, DIR036]

Limitations: the evidence set carries no screening baseline, so the number of items an unchanged process would produce cannot be compared; the frequency facet rests on occurrence differences that are large but not maximal (about 80–85 of 100 reference comparisons smaller), and both orders and both pairs remain observable in both windows, so this is a shift in the frequencies of existing alternatives rather than a replacement of paths. Random re-sampling of the two 200-trace windows remains a competing explanation, and the evidence cannot tell whether the order reversal and the pair-frequency shift are one underlying change or two. [OCC031, OCC032, OCC033, OCC034]
