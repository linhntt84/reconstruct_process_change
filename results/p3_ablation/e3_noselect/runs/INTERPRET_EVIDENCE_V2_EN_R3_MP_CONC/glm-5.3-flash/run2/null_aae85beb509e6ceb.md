### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the preferred visiting order of A30 and A31 between A23 and A24 flipped: in the earlier window the more frequent sequence was A23→A31→A30→A24, and in the later window it is A23→A30→A31→A24, with both orders still taken. The change is shown directly at all three decision points (after A23, A30 and A31), each backed by about 200 outgoing transitions, and each difference is larger than in roughly 90–95 of 100 random re-splits of the same traces; A23, A30, A31 and A24 all still run in essentially every trace with stable occurrence counts, so the change lies in the choice of order rather than in activities becoming optional, repeated or removed. The main limitation is that both orders remain frequent in both windows — this is a shift in preferred routing, not a replacement of one path by another — and, as no chance-expectation baseline is reported, an extreme sampling fluctuation cannot be excluded outright, though three mutually consistent views of the same flip make chance unlikely. [DIR023, DIR030, DIR031, OCC023, OCC027]

Other differences:
- The same kind of preferred-order flip appears between A07 and A09 around A05 in the branch reaching A14. [DIR001, DIR005, DIR007, DIR009]
- The exit to A28 in the A19–A22 sub-loop moved from A20 to A22. [DIR020, DIR022]
- In the A03–A13 cluster, the entry and exit preferences between A11 and A12 reversed. [DIR003, DIR013]
- Participation shifted slightly for several activities (A10 and A40 up; A35 and A18 down). [OCC010, OCC036, OCC031, OCC018]

An important competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is sampling variability in the two 200-trace windows: the secondary flips rest on smaller differences, and some changed outgoing profiles (for example A02 and A10) have only about 25–40 outgoing transitions behind them, making them unreliable; items describing the same rerouting also share the same traces and are not independent confirmations.
