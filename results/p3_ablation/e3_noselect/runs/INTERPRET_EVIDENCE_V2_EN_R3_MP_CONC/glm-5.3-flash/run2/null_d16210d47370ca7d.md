### Observed differences

- In the later window, routing around A31, A32 and A37 shifted. Earlier, about 39 in 100 of A31's outgoing transitions went to A37 and about 28 in 100 to A32; later, only about 27 in 100 went to A37 while about 38 in 100 went to A32. From A32, transitions to A37 rose from about 30 to about 39 in 100, and from A37, transitions to A31 rose from about 30 to about 37 in 100 while transitions to A32 fell from about 37 to about 31 in 100. [DIR017, DIR018, DIR023]
- The dominant order of A31 and A37 flipped. The two activities still occur together in every trace in both windows; earlier, A31 ran before A37 in about 53 in 100 traces, later A37 ran before A31 in about 54 in 100 traces. Both orders occur in both windows, so the pair had no fixed order at either time — only the majority direction changed. [REL001]
- A31 and A37 became less often immediate neighbours. Direct A31→A37 hand-offs one step apart fell from about 74 to about 57 in 100 observed pairs, while pairs two steps apart rose from about 26 to about 43; in the reverse direction, A37→A31 one step apart rose from about 63 to about 69 in 100. [LAG001, LAG002]
- Both A31 and A37 appear marginally earlier in the trace (mean event position from about 0.40 to about 0.395 of the trace length); this shift is small and by itself does not show that either activity was moved. [POS001, POS002]
- Away from this cluster, several activities show smaller shifts: from A01, traffic moved from A05 (about 43 to 30 in 100 of its outgoing transitions) to A04 (about 29 to 42 in 100); A16 participation rose (about 93 to 110 of 200 traces) while A17 fell (about 107 to 90); A10 and A11 still occur in every trace but repeat less often (about 1.7 to about 1.5 occurrences per trace); A39 participation fell (about 110 to 96 of 200 traces) with fewer occurrences per trace (about 0.98 to about 0.84). [DIR001, OCC015, OCC016, OCC009, OCC010, OCC024]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that, in the later window, the flow among A31, A32 and A37 was re-routed: A31 handed over directly to A37 less often and more often continued to A32, A32 in turn led to A37 more often, and A37 led to A31 more often. Together with this, the dominant execution order of A31 and A37 flipped from A31-first to A37-first, and the two activities became less often directly adjacent (one-step A31→A37 pairs down from about 74 to about 57 in 100, two-step pairs up from about 26 to about 43). [DIR017, DIR018, DIR023, REL001, LAG001, LAG002]

The supporting signs are direct and mutually consistent: the successor-share changes, the order flip and the adjacency shift all describe the same rerouting, and the strongest items (LAG001, DIR017) exceed about 98–99 in 100 random re-splits. All involved activities and paths are observed substantially in both windows — A31, A32 and A37 each have outgoing transitions in 200 of 200 traces in both windows, and A23 remains a successor throughout — so this is a shift in how often existing paths are taken, not a replacement, addition or removal of paths. The main competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is sampling variability between the two 200-trace windows: several unrelated activities show moderate shifts of a similar kind, so parts of the overall picture may be noise, although the concentration and mutual consistency of the A31/A32/A37 items make a pure chance origin unlikely. [DIR017, DIR018, DIR023, REL001, LAG001, LAG002]
