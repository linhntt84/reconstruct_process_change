### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that, in the later window, existing alternative paths are taken with different frequencies, while the activities and connections involved stayed in place. The clearest case concerns A23, A30, A31 and A24, all present in essentially every trace: about 10 percentage points of flow per edge moved from the ordering A23→A31→A30 (continuing to A24) to the ordering A23→A30→A31 (continuing to A24), with every involved edge still observed in both windows. The same coordinated share changes appear around A01, A05, A07, A09 and A14, where the two alternative orderings of A05, A07 and A09 exchanged about 10 points of share; around A20, A22 and A28, where the direct continuation from A20 to A28 lost share to the route through A22; and after A03 and A13, where A03 now leads more often to A12 than to A11 and A13 exits more often to A14. Because participation of every involved activity stayed within about ±5 in 100 traces, this reads as a change in how often existing paths are taken rather than as activities or paths being added, removed or made optional. The evidence carries no screening baseline, so the number of flagged items cannot be compared with what an unchanged process would produce; the judgement rests on the directness and coordination of the routing evidence. Ordinary sampling variation between two 200-trace windows remains a competing explanation — weakened by the largest shifts being larger than in about 92–97 of 100 random re-splits and by several edges of the same route moving together, but not fully excluded — and the evidence cannot show whether the shifts in the different regions are one change or several independent ones.
