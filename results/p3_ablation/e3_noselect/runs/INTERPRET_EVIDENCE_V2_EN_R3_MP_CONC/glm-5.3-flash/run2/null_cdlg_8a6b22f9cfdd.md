### Inference

Support level: Weak hypothesis

The evidence suggests that in the later window a segment built around A01, A13 and A14 is executed more often and, importantly, repeats more often within traces (occurrences per trace rose faster than trace participation for all three), with A09 and A10 appearing in more traces, while A03 is executed in fewer traces. Since every activity involved is observed in both windows, this reads as a shift in how often existing, partly repeating behaviour is exercised rather than activities being added or removed. Supporting signs: the joint rise of A01, A13 and A14 across all occurrence components [OCC001, OCC010, OCC011]; the routing shifts from A08 to A01 and from A14 to A09 running between these same activities [DIR004, DIR008]; and A03's decline as the strongest single occurrence difference [OCC002].

What is missing: no screening baseline is provided, so the number of qualifying items cannot be compared with the number expected for an unchanged process; the empirical strengths (0.68–0.85) mean random re-splits of unchanged data produce differences this large in roughly 1 of 6 to 1 of 3 cases; the agreeing items may reflect one underlying trace behaviour (for example a single repeating variant occurring more often in the later sample) rather than independent confirmations; and there is no relational or positional evidence (REL, POS, LAG) to establish the order of these activities, so the "repeating segment" reading is an interpretation of successor shares, not an observed sequence change. An important competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is chance sampling variation between the two finite windows, possibly driven by a rare variant appearing more often in the later sample. [OCC001, OCC010, OCC011, DIR004, DIR008]
