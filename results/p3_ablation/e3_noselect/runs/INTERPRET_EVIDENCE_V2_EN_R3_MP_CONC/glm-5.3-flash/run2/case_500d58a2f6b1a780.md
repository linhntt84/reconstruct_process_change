### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the loop repeating the block of A25, A26 and A31 was largely switched off. In the earlier window the block ran about three to four times per trace in roughly three of four traces, sustained by a loop-back from A31 to A25 that carried about seven of ten of A31's outgoing transitions, with A25 and A31 interleaved across repetitions; in the later window the block runs once per trace, A31 proceeds to A20 in about 96 of 100 transitions, and A25 and A31 each appear once in a fixed order, with A31 two positions after A25 in about nine of ten cases. A26 shows the same occurrence profile as A25 and A31, suggesting it belongs to the same repeated block, although the relational evidence directly covers only the A25–A31 pair. The occurrence, direction, relation, lag and position items all describe this one mechanism consistently, each with empirical strength about 0.995, and the direction item shows the specific edge whose near-disappearance explains the repetition change. Limitations: these items describe the same traces rather than independent confirmations; the evidence shows the loop-back became rare (about 4 in 100 transitions) rather than proving it was removed; and no screening baseline is included, so the number of qualifying items cannot be compared against a chance expectation — the size of the differences (about 2.4 fewer occurrences per trace) makes a pure sampling artefact implausible but not impossible. [OCC019, OCC020, OCC025, DIR020, REL001, LAG001, LAG002, POS001, POS002]
