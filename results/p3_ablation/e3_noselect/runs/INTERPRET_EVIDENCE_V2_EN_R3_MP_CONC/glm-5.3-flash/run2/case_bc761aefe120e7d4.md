### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, A30, A31 and A36 each occurred about 3.4 times per trace and appeared in every trace, with repeats of each activity in about 75 in 100 traces; in the later window each occurred about once per trace, still in every trace, with repeats in only about 1–2 in 100 traces [OCC027, OCC028, OCC033].
- The relation between A30 and A36 changed accordingly: earlier they were interleaved or repeated in about 75 in 100 traces (a fixed A30-before-A36 order in about 25 in 100); in the later window about 98 in 100 traces show A30 before A36, with interleaving almost gone [REL001]. Among A30→A36 pairs seen within five steps, the share at a gap of two steps rose from about 12 in 100 to about 89 in 100 [LAG002], while the reverse order A36-before-A30, present in about 150 earlier traces, appears in only 1 later trace [LAG003]. Self-repetitions of A30 and of A36 fell from about 150 traces each to about 2 [LAG001, LAG004].
- Successor behaviour changed consistently: earlier about 70 in 100 outgoing transitions of A36 led back to A30 and about 29 in 100 to A25; in the later window about 93 in 100 lead to A25 and only about 4 in 100 return to A30 [DIR036]. A30 still leads almost exclusively to A31, and A31 to A36, in both windows, but the volume of these transitions fell from about 690 to about 210, matching the occurrence decline [DIR030, DIR031].
- The positions of A30 and A36 within the trace became much more concentrated: A30's positional spread fell from about 0.12 to about 0.07 (upper quartile 0.52 to 0.44) and A36's from about 0.12 to about 0.06 (lower quartile 0.40 to 0.46) [POS001, POS002].
- Other, smaller shifts: participation fell for A10 (about 14.5 to 8 in 100 traces), A20 (about 51.5 to 46) and A23 (about 49.5 to 43), and rose for A18 (about 24 to 29), A19 (about 49 to 54.5) and A24 (about 50.5 to 58) [OCC010, OCC020, OCC023, OCC018, OCC019, OCC024]. Mean occurrences per trace decreased for A37 (0.47 to 0.32), A38 (1.0 to 0.75), A40 (1.76 to 1.56, repeats down from about 42 to 34 in 100 traces) and A41 (0.89 to 0.78), and rose mildly for A15 and A16 (about 1.6 to 1.7) [OCC034, OCC035, OCC036, OCC037, OCC015, OCC016]. Several routing redistributions (out of A02, A06, A08, A10, A45) rest on only about 3–27 observed transitions and are weakly based [DIR002, DIR006, DIR008, DIR010, DIR045].

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the repeating cycle A30 → A31 → A36 stopped repeating. In the earlier window this cycle ran about three times per trace — A36 returned to A30 in about 70 in 100 of its outgoing transitions, and A30/A36 were interleaved or repeated in about 75 in 100 traces — whereas in the later window A30, A31 and A36 each execute once in the fixed order A30 → A31 → A36, after which A36 proceeds to A25 instead of looping back [OCC027, OCC028, OCC033, REL001, LAG002, LAG003, DIR036]. Supporting signs: participation remains at 100 in 100 traces for all three activities in both windows, so they did not become optional or disappear — only their repetition changed; the occurrence drop (about 3.4 to about 1 per trace) exceeds about 995 in 1000 random re-split reference differences; and the occurrence, relation, lag and successor evidence all point to the same structural difference [OCC027, OCC028, OCC033, REL001, LAG002, DIR030, DIR031, DIR036]. Limitations: most items describe one underlying trace behaviour rather than independent confirmations, and the evidence cannot rule out that the later window simply captures a case mix in which the cycle runs once, nor explain why the repetition stopped [OCC027, REL001, LAG002].

Other differences: the participation and occurrence shifts around A10, A15–A16, A18–A19, A20, A23–A24 and A37–A43 are not part of this hypothesis; they could be downstream consequences of the loop removal or separate changes, which the evidence cannot distinguish [OCC010, OCC015, OCC016, OCC018, OCC019, OCC020, OCC023, OCC024, OCC034, OCC035, OCC036, OCC037, OCC038, OCC039].
