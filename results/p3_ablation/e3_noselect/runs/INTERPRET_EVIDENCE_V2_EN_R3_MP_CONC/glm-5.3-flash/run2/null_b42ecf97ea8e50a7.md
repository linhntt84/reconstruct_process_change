### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The main hypothesis supported by the evidence is a shift in how often the two existing paths after A39 are taken: in the earlier window the branch to A15 was the more common continuation of A39 (about 54 in 100, versus 46 to A14), while in the later window the branch to A14 is taken more often (about 53 in 100, versus 47 to A15). The change is observed directly in the successor shares at A39 and matches the participation counts exactly — A14 in 92 of 200 traces before and 106 after, A15 in 108 before and 94 after, complementary in both windows — and both alternatives remain common throughout, so this is a re-weighting of existing paths rather than an activity or path being added, removed or bypassed. The main limitation is that the directional and occurrence items describe one underlying behaviour rather than independent confirmations, and with 200 traces per window a swing of about 7 percentage points could in principle be a large sampling fluctuation; that competing explanation cannot be fully ruled out, although the agreement in direction and size across the two views makes the routing shift the best-supported reading. [DIR025, OCC013, OCC014]

Other differences: the block around A37 and A38 is executed more often in the later window — more traces contain it, it repeats within traces more often, and A11 routes to A38 more often and to A13 less often, with A13's participation falling to about 27 in 100 traces; smaller routing rebalances occur inside the A16–A19 group (toward A16 and A18), after A26 and at the A32/A34 splits (toward A33/A34 and toward A27/A35), after A41 (toward A44), around A20/A30/A31, and between the A01–A05 and A02–A08 alternative groups (about 55/45 moving to 52/48). For these smaller shifts, ordinary sampling variation between the two windows remains a competing explanation that the evidence cannot exclude, since several of them carry only moderate empirical strength.
