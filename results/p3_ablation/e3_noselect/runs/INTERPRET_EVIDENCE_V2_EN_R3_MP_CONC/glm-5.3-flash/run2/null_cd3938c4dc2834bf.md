### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the preferred order of A25 and A26, the two activities executed between A18 and A19, shifted: in the earlier window the majority of passes ran A18 → A26 → A25 → A19 (about 53 in 100 at each step), while in the later window the majority ran A18 → A25 → A26 → A19 (about 57 in 100 at each step). The change is shown directly by the outgoing-transition shares of A18, A25 and A26, which each moved by 10 points in exactly the pattern a single order shift produces, and each difference is larger than in about 90 in 100 random re-splits of an unchanged process. Participation and repetition are ruled out as explanations: each of the three activities shows 200 outgoing transitions in both windows (one per trace on average), no occurrence change for them was reported, and A25→A26 rising while A26→A25 falls cannot be produced by the block repeating more or less often. Limitations: the three transition shifts are three views of the same traces rather than independent confirmations, the evidence set provides no chance-expectation baseline against which to compare the number of qualifying items, and the previously preferred order remains common (about 43 in 100 passes still run A26 first), so a flexible-order segment producing this difference through sampling alone cannot be fully excluded.
