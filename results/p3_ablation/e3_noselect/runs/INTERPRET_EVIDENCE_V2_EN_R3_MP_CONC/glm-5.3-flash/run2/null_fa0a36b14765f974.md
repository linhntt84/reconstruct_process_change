### Inference

Support level: Weak hypothesis

The evidence suggests that existing alternative paths are now taken with different frequencies. The clearest instance is at the process entry: in the later window, traces more often ran the path via A01 and then A05, A07 and A09 (each from about 48–49 to about 55 in 100 traces) and less often the path via A03 and then A11, A12 and A13 (each from about 52 to about 46 in 100). A01 and A03 together covered essentially every trace in both windows and all involved activities stayed common, so this reads as a re-splitting of existing traffic between two alternatives rather than a new, removed or bypassed path. Shifts in the same direction at downstream choice points — after A28 toward A32/A33 and away from A34/A35, after A39 toward A20 and away from A19, and around A40 toward A42/A43 against A41's direct exit — would be consistent with the same underlying shift in path frequencies, though they could also be several independent changes. [OCC001, OCC003, OCC011, OCC013, DIR028, DIR032, DIR039, DIR041]

Supporting signs: occurrence and directly-follows evidence point the same way in every affected subsystem, only shares between existing alternatives moved (all affected activities appeared in roughly 45–66 in 100 traces in both windows), and the entry split is nearly complementary in both windows. What is missing: no screening baseline is available to check whether the number of qualifying items exceeds what an unchanged process would produce; the individual differences are only moderately unusual (about one in three random re-splits would show at least as large an entry-split difference, and the strongest single items reach strengths of about 0.93–0.95 among the 45 tested directly-follows relations); and the activities inside each block move together with their anchor, so they do not confirm one another independently. An important competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is ordinary sampling variability between two finite 200-trace windows of an unchanged process. [OCC001, OCC005, DIR032, DIR041]

Other differences: the preferred order within the entry blocks changed (a change in ordering behaviour on top of the frequency shift); the A37/A38 pair repeated more often; and the rare activities A10 and A06 changed their participation and successor mixes, while A45's changed profile rests on very few observations.
