### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that flow was redistributed between existing alternative paths at several branch points, with every involved activity and path still observed in substantial shares of traces in both windows: in the later window, traces more often continued after A25 with A22 instead of A23, after A31 with A35/A36 instead of A37/A38, and after A42 with A43 instead of A44/A45, and the A05→A07 sequence became more frequent relative to A07→A05, with A07 passing flow on to A09 rather than back to A05. The occurrence shifts (about 8–11 participation points per 100 traces, mirrored in size between the rising and falling activities of each branch) and the successor-share shifts at the shared predecessors agree in direction at every named branch point, the key differences rank high against random re-splits of the pooled data (empirical strengths of about 0.7–0.99), and the A05/A07 change is corroborated by relational and lag evidence in addition to occurrence counts. A competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that some of the smaller shifts are finite-sample noise: no expected-by-chance baseline for the number of qualifying items is provided, and whether the several branch-point shifts reflect one coordinated redistribution or independent local changes cannot be determined from this evidence. Other differences: A15 and A16 (and slightly A39) repeated less often per trace in the later window; A04 occurred somewhat more often and gained A45 as a frequent successor on few observations; A08's outgoing share shifted toward A16. These are not part of the main hypothesis. [OCC015, OCC016, OCC035, OCC004, DIR004, DIR008]
