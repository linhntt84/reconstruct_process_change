### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the point in the sequence where the optional choice among A19, A20 and A25 executes moved: in the earlier window exactly one of the three ran per case, inserted after A27 in one of two slots between A27 and A32, while in the later window the same choice runs between A45 and A26, ahead of the A26→A27→A28→A32 segment, whose internal order and presence in every case are unchanged. Because the block still runs in essentially every case with similar member shares, this is a change in where the choice executes, not in how often it runs. [OCC019, OCC020, OCC025, REL001, REL002, REL008, DIR019, DIR020, DIR025, DIR045, DIR027, DIR028, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL009, REL010, REL011, REL014, REL015, REL016]

The supporting signs are direct rather than marginal: the order relations between the choice activities and A26/A27/A28 reversed with empirical strength near 0.995, the successor roles moved consistently (A45 now feeds the choice, the choice now feeds A26, A27 and A28 became tightly chained), event positions of A19/A20/A25 moved early while A26–A28 shifted slightly later, and step distances changed in the matching direction. These items span four indicator families but describe one underlying difference, so they should be read as one coherent body of evidence rather than independent confirmations. [REL003, REL009, REL014, DIR019, DIR020, DIR025, DIR045, DIR027, DIR028, POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006, LAG007, LAG008, LAG010, LAG011]

Limitations: the evidence contains no screening baseline for how many items an unchanged process would produce, so the judgement rests on directness and consistency rather than a chance comparison. An important competing explanation that cannot be ruled out is a small residual use of the old placement: A27 and A28 still occasionally lead to A19/A20/A25 (~2–5 in 100 of their outgoing transitions), and with 200 cases per window the evidence cannot determine how much of the old placement, if any, remains in use.
