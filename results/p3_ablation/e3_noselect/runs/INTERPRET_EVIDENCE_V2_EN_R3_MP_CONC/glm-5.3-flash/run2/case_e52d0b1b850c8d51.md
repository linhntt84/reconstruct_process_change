### Observed differences

- The pass-through A06 between A14 and A15 largely disappeared from the main route. In the earlier window A06 ran in about 71 in 100 traces, early in the trace (median position about 0.16 of the trace); A14 sent about 69 in 100 of its outgoing transitions to A06, and A06 passed on to A15 in about 92 in 100 of its outgoing transitions. In the later window A06 runs in only about 26 in 100 traces, late and at much more variable positions (median about 0.30, upper quartile about 0.83); A14 sends only about 12 in 100 of its outgoing transitions to A06 and about 78 in 100 directly to A15 (about 31 in 100 before), while A06's own outflow now spreads over many targets (about 37 in 100 to A15). A14 now occurs without A06 in about 74 in 100 traces (about 29 in 100 before), A06 precedes A15 in only about 3 in 100 traces (about 38 in 100 before), and among observed A14→A06 pairs the share at distance one fell from about 98 to 75 in 100. [OCC006, POS001, DIR014, DIR006, REL001, REL002, LAG009]

- The previously mandatory sequence A19 → A25 → A20 became skippable. In the earlier window each of the three ran in every trace; in the later window each runs in only about 53–54 in 100 traces. The three still run together and in the same order when they run (A19 before A25 and A25 before A20 in essentially all joint occurrences), and in about 46 in 100 later traces all three are absent together while the surrounding flow (e.g. A14) still executes. [OCC018, OCC019, OCC024, REL024, REL025, REL030, REL011]

A41 also now follows A14 most often about four steps later rather than five. [DIR019, DIR025, DIR020, OCC014, OCC015, POS002, POS007, OCC037, POS009, POS010, DIR041, REL026, REL031, REL037, LAG011]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that, in the later window, two segments that previously ran in most or all traces are frequently skipped. First, the pass-through A06 between A14 and A15 is usually bypassed: A14 now leads directly to A15 in about 78 in 100 of its outgoing transitions (about 31 in 100 before), and A06 takes part in only about 26 in 100 traces (about 71 in 100 before), late in the trace and with widely spread successors instead of early with outflow concentrated on A15. Second, the block A19 → A25 → A20 is omitted in about 46 in 100 traces; when it runs, its internal order and its entry from A41 and exit to A26 are unchanged, and A41 now splits between entering the block (about 47 in 100 of its outgoing transitions, down from about 96) and going straight to A26 (about 39 in 100, previously unobserved). [OCC006, POS001, DIR014, DIR006, REL001, REL002, OCC018, OCC019, OCC024, REL024, REL025, REL030, DIR041]

This reading is direct and consistent: it rests on co-occurrence and ordering measurements for A06 with A14/A15 and for the block activities with their neighbours, on successor shares at A14 and A41 computed from roughly 200 outgoing transitions per window, and on participation drops from 100 to about 53 in 100 traces over 200-trace windows — magnitudes that random re-splits of these data essentially never produce. What the evidence cannot rule out is that the later window mixes two coexisting trace variants, one with and one without these segments, rather than a single process in which the segments became optional; the two descriptions predict the same observed behaviour. [REL024, REL030, DIR014, DIR041, OCC018]
