### Observed differences

- Six activities became near-mandatory: A28, A30 and A31 ran in 97 of 200 traces in the earlier window versus 197 of 200 in the later window, and A32, A33 and A34 in 103 of 200 versus 199 of 200. [OCC019, OCC020, OCC021, OCC022, OCC023, OCC024]
- In the earlier window the two trios were mutually exclusive alternatives — A28 and A32 never shared a trace (co-occurrence 0), each trace containing exactly one trio — whereas in the later window they co-occur in about 98 in 100 traces, always with the A28/A30/A31 trio first (about 98 in 100); within each trio the order stays flexible (A28 precedes A30 in about 49 in 100 later traces and follows it in about 50 in 100). [REL015, REL019, REL022, REL013, REL018, REL025]
- Routing at the entry changed: A25 previously fanned out roughly evenly to all six trio activities (about 15–19% of its outgoing transitions each), while in the later window it leads almost only to A28, A30 and A31 (about 30–38% each) and practically never to A32 or A34. [DIR016]
- The first trio no longer exits to A26: A28→A26 fell from about 36% of A28's outgoing transitions to under 1%, and A30→A26 and A31→A26 are no longer observed, while the second trio still leads to A26 in about 29–39% of its outgoing transitions. [DIR018, DIR020, DIR021, DIR022, DIR023, DIR024]
- Positions and distances moved accordingly: A25 and the A28/A30/A31 trio now occur earlier (mean relative position about 0.67 → 0.62 and 0.74 → 0.68), A32/A33/A34 and A26 later (about 0.73 → 0.77 and 0.80 → 0.83), and the distance from A25 to A32/A33/A34 and from the first trio to A26 grew from 1–3 steps to 4–5 steps. - Away from this block the differences are small: A38 repeats slightly less often (about 1.76 → 1.60 times per trace), A10 and A11 slightly more often (about 1.55 → 1.66), and a few routing shares moved by roughly 5 percentage points (e.g. after A37, A15 now narrowly overtakes A14). [OCC027, OCC009, OCC010, DIR026, OCC013, OCC014]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that two previously alternative blocks were joined into one mandatory sequence. In the earlier window, each trace executed either the block A28–A30–A31 (97 of 200 traces) or the block A32–A33–A34 (103 of 200), entered from A25 and exiting directly to A26; in the later window nearly every trace runs both blocks in sequence — A25 enters the A28/A30/A31 block, which hands over to the A32/A33/A34 block, which then leads to A26 — so the six activities changed from optional, mutually exclusive alternatives to near-universal steps, and the direct paths from the first block to A26 disappeared. [OCC019, OCC022, REL015, REL007, DIR016, DIR018, DIR020, DIR021]

The change is shown directly by the relational items (cross-block co-occurrence rising from 0 to about 98 in 100 with a fixed inter-block order) and is consistent across the participation, successor, position and distance items, all with differences larger than in about 199 of 200 random re-splits of the pooled data. A competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is a shift in the mix of cases between the windows (the later window being dominated by cases that always ran both blocks) rather than a routing change, and no chance baseline for the number of flagged items is provided to check the item count against. [REL015, REL019, REL022, OCC019, OCC022, DIR016, POS003, POS006, LAG004]

Other differences: A38 repeats less often per trace (about 1.76 → 1.60, recurrence support down from about 0.44 to 0.36), A10 and A11 repeat slightly more often (about 1.55 → 1.66), and A39–A41 participate slightly less; these modest shifts lie outside the affected block and are not covered by the main hypothesis. [OCC027, OCC009, OCC010, OCC028, OCC029, OCC030]
