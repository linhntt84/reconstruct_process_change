### Observed differences

- The successor profile of **A02** changed: in the earlier window its outgoing transitions went to A09 in about 50 of 100 cases and directly to A05 in about 46, while in the later window A05 became the dominant successor (about 58 in 100) and A09 fell to about 38. This is the most unusual single item in the evidence: only about 7 of 100 random re-splits produce a larger change. [DIR002]
- The successor profile of **A09** moved in the mirror direction: earlier it led to A05 in about 53 of 100 transitions and back to A02 in about 44; later, A02 was the most common successor (about 52 in 100) and A05 dropped to about 41. About 13 of 100 random re-splits produce a larger change. [DIR009]
- Read together, the preferred relative order of the A02/A09 pair tipped: before, A02→A09 (about 50%) slightly outweighed A09→A02 (about 44%); in the later window A09→A02 (about 52%) clearly outweighed A02→A09 (about 38%), with A05 following the pair in both windows. Both orders still occur in both windows, so the mix between two existing orders shifted rather than one path replacing the other. [DIR002, DIR009]
- Participation shifted slightly and in two coherent groups: A02, A05, A08 and A09 each appeared in 49% of traces before and 45% after (98 vs 90 of 200 traces), while A04, A06, A07 and A10 each rose from 51% to 55% (102 vs 110). Each of these differences is unremarkable on its own — about half of random re-splits show a larger one. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, OCC007, OCC008]
- Minor routing fluctuations: from A07, transitions to A10 rose from about 94% to 97% [DIR007]; a single after-window trace shows A08 followed by A03, not observed before, and A10's split between A06 and A03 moved from about 67/33 to 83/17 — both rest on very few observations (1 trace; 3 vs 6 transitions) [DIR008, DIR010]. The remaining successor profiles (from A01, A03, A04, A05, A06) moved only slightly, within normal variation. [DIR001, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006]

### Inference

Support level: Weak hypothesis

The evidence suggests that the handover between **A02** and **A09** changed: in the earlier window A02 typically passed to A09 (about 50% of its transitions) and A09 typically passed on to A05 (about 53%), consistent with A02 usually preceding A09 before the pair reaches A05; in the later window this tipped over, with A09 passing back to A02 in about 52% of its transitions and A02 passing directly to A05 in about 58%, consistent with A09 now usually preceding A02. Both orders remain present in both windows, so this is a shift in how often the two existing orders are taken, not a replacement of one path by another. [DIR002, DIR009]

Supporting signs: the change appears directly in the successor distributions of both source activities and in mirror-image directions; all four involved transitions were observed on roughly 90–100 transitions per window, so the shares are not based on isolated cases; and both profile changes are unusual for random re-splits (exceeded in only about 7 and 13 of 100 reference comparisons). [DIR002, DIR009]

What is missing: the two items describe the same trio of activities and likely the same traces, so they are not independent confirmations; no screening baseline is included, so the number of qualifying items cannot be compared with what an unchanged process typically produces; and shifts of roughly 12 percentage points can arise from sampling variation between two 200-trace windows. The competing explanation that cannot be ruled out is therefore ordinary window-to-window variability. [DIR002, DIR009]

Other differences: A02, A05, A08 and A09 each appeared in about 4 in 100 fewer traces in the later window, and A04, A06, A07 and A10 in about 4 in 100 more; every one of these differences lies within the range of random re-splits and is not part of the main hypothesis [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, OCC007, OCC008]. Small successor fluctuations from A07, A08 and A10 were also observed but rest on very few observations. [DIR007, DIR008, DIR010]
