### Observed differences

- A01 and A07 exchanged their activity levels. Earlier, A01 appeared in about 28 of 100 traces (about 0.53 occurrences per trace, repeating in about 11 of 100 traces), while A07 appeared in about 51 of 100 traces (about 1.03 occurrences per trace, repeating in about 28 of 100). In the later window A01 appears in about 53 of 100 traces (about 1.1 per trace, repeating in about 29 of 100) and A07 in about 29 of 100 traces (about 0.57 per trace, repeating in about 14 of 100). A02, A03, A04 and A06 stay near 50 of 100 traces in both windows (shifts of at most 2–3 points), and A05 stays near 28–29. [OCC001, OCC007, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006]

- The successors of A01, A05 and A07 rewired (shares below are over each source activity's observed outgoing transitions). Earlier, about 94 in 100 of A01's outgoing transitions went to A07 and about 96 in 100 of A05's went to A01, while A07 split between A05 (43 in 100) and A06 (41 in 100). In the later window A01 goes mostly to A05 (57 in 100, plus A06 18, A04 14, A03 11), A05 goes to A07 (91 in 100), and A07 goes almost only to A01 (94 in 100): the small cycle A01→A07→A05→A01 now runs in the opposite order, A01→A05→A07→A01. [DIR001, DIR004, DIR006]

- The link between this cycle and the A03/A04/A06 group moved from A07 to A01. Earlier, A03 and A04 led to A07 in about half of their outgoing transitions (51 and 41 in 100) and A07 led to A06 (41 in 100); in the later window A03, A04 and A06 lead to A01 (about 34, 39 and 31 in 100) and A01 leads back to A06, A04 and A03 (together about 43 in 100), while A07's edges to that group nearly vanish (A03→A07 about 51→2 in 100, A04→A07 41→1, and A07→A06 pair observations fall from about 100 to 8). [DIR002, DIR003, DIR005, DIR006, LAG011, LAG016, LAG029]

- A06 moved from the end to the middle of the trace: its mean relative position falls from about 0.96 to 0.48, and it now often directly follows A01 (lag-1 share of its pair observations rises from 0 to about 41 in 100). A07 now occurs later in the trace (mean 0.51→0.69) and A05 somewhat later (0.47→0.57). [POS005, POS006, POS004, LAG005]

- Co-occurrence patterns flipped accordingly. Earlier, A03 and A04 each occurred without A01 in about 23 of 100 traces; later, essentially never, and both co-occur with A01 in about 53 of 100 traces. Conversely, A01 now occurs without A07 in about 24 of 100 traces (earlier it was A07 that occurred without A01, in about 23 of 100), and A05 and A07, earlier often apart, later always appear together when either does. [REL001, REL002, REL005, REL014, REL009, REL012, REL015]

- Edge-level lags confirm the new adjacencies: A01→A07 at lag 1 (about 28 of 100 pair observations earlier) and A05→A01 at lag 1 (about 29 in 100) disappear, while A07→A01 (about 25 in 100), A01→A05 (about 20 in 100) and A05→A07 (about 23 in 100) appear at lag 1. [LAG006, LAG017, LAG025, LAG004, LAG022]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a structural change in which A01 and A07 exchanged their roles. In the earlier window, A07 was the high-frequency activity (about 51 of 100 traces, about one occurrence per trace) shared between the small A01–A05 cycle (A01→A07→A05→A01) and the A03/A04/A06 group (A03/A04→A07→A06); in the later window A01 holds that position (about 53 of 100 traces, about 1.1 occurrences per trace): A03, A04 and A06 lead to A01, A01 leads back to them and to A05, and the small cycle runs as A01→A05→A07→A01. A05 keeps its low frequency (about 28–29 of 100 traces) but its successor changes from A01 to A07. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006, OCC001, OCC005, OCC007]

The change is shown directly and consistently: the successor distributions of A01, A05 and A07 reverse, the same edges appear and disappear at lag 1, the occurrence and recurrence profiles of A01 and A07 are near mirror images across the windows, and the co-occurrence and position shifts match the rerouting; nearly all qualifying items sit at the top of the reference scale (differences larger than in about 99 of 100 random re-splits). No screening baseline for chance expectations is present in the evidence, so this judgement rests on directness and cross-family consistency rather than on a count-versus-chance comparison. [DIR001, DIR004, DIR006, LAG004, LAG006, LAG017, LAG022, LAG025, OCC001, OCC007, REL005, REL014, POS005]

An important competing explanation cannot be ruled out: the evidence shows resulting edges, positions and frequencies, not the underlying model, so it cannot distinguish a full exchange of the two activities' roles from a reversal of the small cycle combined with re-attaching the A03/A04/A06 group to A01. Fine details of that group's internal routing also rest on few observations (A06 had only about 11 observed outgoing transitions earlier, and the A07→A04 pair appears once in the later window). [DIR005, LAG027]

Other differences: the previously fixed orders inside the A03/A04/A06 group loosened — A03 before A06 in about 47 of 100 traces earlier versus 36 later, with A06 before A03 rising from 4 to 17, and similarly for A04–A06 (about 49→38 and 2→15) — and A01's repeats now occur at longer lags. [REL008, REL011, LAG001]
