### Observed differences

- The clearest difference is in the routing immediately after **A19, A20 and A21** — three activities that each occur in about 1 in 5 cases in both windows (43 of 200 cases earlier, 50 of 200 later). Earlier, the most frequent successor of A19 was A25 (about 40 in 100 of its outgoing transitions), of A20 it was A21 (about 44 in 100) and of A21 it was A19 (about 51 in 100). In the later window each of these preferences has flipped: A19 is now most often followed by A21 (about 42 in 100), A20 by A25 (about 52 in 100) and A21 by A20 (about 46 in 100). These are the largest direction differences in the sample and arise in at most about 1 in 10 random re-splits of the pooled data — for A21, in only about 1 in 200. [DIR016, DIR017, DIR018]
- Read together, a typical pass through this branch looks like A19 → A20 → A21 returning to A19 in the earlier window, versus A19 → A21 → A20 in the later window, with the exit to A25 now happening mainly after A20 (about 30 in 100 of A20's outgoing transitions earlier, about 52 in 100 later). All involved edges are observed in both windows, so this is a shift between existing alternatives, not a replaced path. [DIR016, DIR017, DIR018]
- **A15**, which occurs in every case, feeds this branch differently in the later window: it leads to A19 about twice as often (about 5 in 100 of its outgoing transitions earlier, about 14 in 100 later) and to A20 clearly less often (about 10 in 100 earlier, about 4 in 100 later). [DIR012]
- Participation itself barely moved: A16, A17 and A18 appear in about 79 in 100 cases earlier and 75 in 100 later; A19, A20 and A21 in about 22 in 100 earlier and 25 in 100 later; similar 3–4 point swings appear elsewhere (e.g., A01, A03–A05 down from about 56 to 52 in 100; A02, A06–A08 up from about 45 to 48 in 100). Differences of this size occur in roughly half of random re-splits, so they look like ordinary sampling variation. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC013, OCC016]
- Weaker, isolated shifts: A18 leads less often to A16 (about 37 vs 27 in 100) and more often to A17 and A25; A05 leads more often to A09 (about 32 vs 41 in 100); A26 leads more often to A25 (about 37 vs 43 in 100); A25 occurs slightly more often per case (1.59 vs 1.71 times, with repeats in about 36 vs 40 in 100 cases); A27 and A28 occur slightly more often (mean 0.76 vs 0.84). All of these sit at levels random re-splits reach regularly. [DIR015, DIR005, DIR021, OCC021, OCC023]

### Inference

Support level: Weak hypothesis

The evidence suggests that the routing among **A19, A20 and A21** changed: in the later window the preferred next step after each of the three is different (A19 → A21 instead of A25; A20 → A25 instead of A21; A21 → A20 instead of A19), so the typical pass through this branch effectively runs in the reverse order and now exits to A25 mainly after A20, while A15 steers into the branch towards A19 rather than A20. Supporting signs: the three direction profiles changed coherently as one pattern, each is individually very unusual under random re-splits (strengths about 0.90–0.99), and the A15 shift points the same way with full support in both windows. [DIR016, DIR017, DIR018, DIR012]

What is missing: each profile rests on only about 43–50 outgoing transitions per window, and the three items describe the same small set of cases, so they are not independent confirmations; no relational or positional evidence (REL/LAG/POS) is available to confirm a true reordering rather than a change in branch shares; and no screening baseline is provided to check whether one or two items this extreme among the 22 direction tests are what a stable process produces. An important competing explanation is therefore sampling variability in this low-participation branch (about 1 in 5 of cases), which could flip the modal successors without any real change. [DIR016, DIR017, DIR018]

Other differences: A18's outgoing mix shifted away from A16 towards A17 and A25 [DIR015]; A05 leads to A09 more often [DIR005]; A26 leads to A25 more often [DIR021]; A25 repeats slightly more often [OCC021]; A27 and A28 occur slightly more often [OCC023, OCC024]; and the small participation swings across many activities are within normal variation [OCC001, OCC002, OCC013, OCC016]. These are not part of the main hypothesis.
