### Observed differences

- The largest difference concerns the relative order of eight activities that occur in every trace in both windows: A26, A17, A15, A18, A19, A21, A20 and A27. In the earlier window their order was mixed — A15 preceded A19 in about 26 in 100 traces (and followed it in about 74 in 100), A18 preceded A19 in about 12 in 100, and A15 preceded A21 in about 50 in 100 — whereas in the later window every tested pair follows one single order, at or near 100 in 100, giving the fixed sequence A26 → A17 → A15 → A18 → A19 → A21 → A20 → A27. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, REL007, REL008, REL009]
- Successor choices collapsed accordingly. A15 led to A18 in about 62 in 100 of its outgoing transitions before, with the remainder split over A19, A20 and A21; in the later window about 99.5 in 100 lead to A18. A18 moved from mostly A27 (about 53 in 100) to almost only A19; A19 from mostly A21 (about 56 in 100) to about 99.5 in 100 A21; A20 to A27 rose from about 47 to 99.5 in 100; A21 to A20 from about 66 to 100 in 100; A17 to A15 from about 56 to 99.5 in 100; and A26 went from an even split between A19 and A17 to A17 alone. [DIR012, DIR013, DIR014, DIR015, DIR016, DIR017, DIR018]
- Spacing became fixed as well: A15 reaches A18 within one step in about 99.5 in 100 later traces (about 62 in 100 before), A15 reaches A27 at distance five in 99.5 in 100 (previously spread over distances two to five), and A26 reaches A19 at distance four in 99.5 in 100 (previously at distance one in about 50 in 100); the reverse orders, such as A19 before A15, remain in only about 0.5 in 100 of later traces. [LAG001, LAG005, LAG027, REL001]
- Relative positions moved coherently with this: A18's mean position moved earlier (from about 0.68 to 0.62 of the trace) and A19's moved later (from about 0.59 to 0.65), A15 and A17 slightly earlier and A20 and A21 slightly later, while A26 and A27 stayed in place; in the later window the eight mean positions line up exactly in the order of the chain above. [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006, POS007, POS008]
- Outside this block, A10 and A11 were executed more often per trace: the mean rose from about 1.6 to about 1.8 occurrences, the share of traces with repeats rose from about 39 in 100 to 46 in 100, and A11's transitions increasingly went to A10 (about 38 in 100 to 45 in 100). [OCC009, OCC010, DIR009]
- Smaller shifts appear around A03–A05 (A05's transitions to A04 fell from about 42 in 100 to 24 in 100 while those to A03 and A09 rose, and A01's transitions to A04 rose from about 24 to 38 in 100) and around A35–A39 (A35 appeared in about 26 in 100 traces before versus 22 in 100 after; A36's mean occurrences fell from 0.93 to 0.85 with more transitions going to A37). These rest on roughly 50–100 observations per window and are small enough to reflect sampling variation. [DIR001, DIR004, DIR005, OCC017, OCC018, DIR024]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis of a change in ordering constraints involving A26, A17, A15, A18, A19, A21, A20 and A27: in the earlier window these activities, which always occurred together, followed no fixed order (each pair appeared in both orders in substantial shares), while in the later window they run as one fixed sequence, A26 → A17 → A15 → A18 → A19 → A21 → A20 → A27, each activity immediately followed by the next. This is a tightening of ordering constraints rather than activities being added, removed or made optional, since participation and co-occurrence stayed at 100 in 100 in both windows and only order and spacing changed. The support is direct and internally consistent: nine pairwise relations all moved to a single order, seven successor profiles became near-deterministic, the lag distributions collapsed to single distances that agree with one chain, and the positional shifts match (A18 earlier, A19 later, A26 and A27 unchanged); each of these items shows an empirical strength of about 0.995, a level reached by only about 1 in 200 random re-splits of an unchanged process. [REL001, REL003, REL007, DIR012, DIR014, DIR018, LAG001, LAG005, POS003, POS004]

An important competing explanation remains: the earlier mixed order could reflect either one flexible process or a mixture of several fixed variants, and the evidence cannot distinguish these; what it establishes directly is only that the later window shows a single fixed order. Positions and lags are measured in event positions, not clock time, so no conclusion about timing is implied. [REL001, REL004, DIR015]

Other differences: A10 and A11 were repeated more often per trace in the later window (mean about 1.6 to 1.8; repeats in about 39 to 46 in 100 traces), a repetition change independent of the ordering hypothesis; the small share shifts around A03–A05 and A35–A39 stay within the range expected from sampling. [OCC009, OCC010, DIR009, DIR001, DIR005, OCC017, OCC018]
