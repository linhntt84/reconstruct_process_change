### Observed differences

- The largest difference concerns the three-step sequence A30 → A31 → A36. In the earlier window each of the three activities ran exactly once in every case (mean about 1.0 occurrence per case, participation 100 in 100 cases); in the later window each runs about 3.5 times per case, and repeats occur in about 68 in 100 cases, up from about 1 in 100. [OCC028, OCC029, OCC034]
- The routing that closes this loop changed accordingly: A36 led back to A30 in about 0.5 in 100 of A36's outgoing transitions before (1 of about 200) versus about 71 in 100 after, while the exit A36 → A25 fell from about 98 in 100 to about 28 in 100 of its outgoing transitions; in absolute terms the exit still happens about once per case, so the extra transitions are additional passes through the segment. The internal edges A30 → A31 and A31 → A36 kept their shares (about 97–99 in 100) while their transition counts roughly tripled (about 200 to about 700 each). [DIR036, DIR030, DIR031]
- Order relations agree: A14, A30 and A36 still co-occur in every case in both windows, but the strict one-pass order (A14 before A30/A36, A30 before A36) held in about 99 in 100 cases before and only about 32 in 100 after, with these pairs now interleaved or repeated in about 68 in 100 cases; pairs of A05 with A30/A36 shifted the same way (A05-first from about 54–55 in 100 down to about 17). [REL004, REL005, REL006, REL002, REL003]
- Lags and positions point the same way: A30 followed by A36 within two steps dropped from about 96 in 100 cases to about 12 in 100; the pairs A30→A30 and A36→A30, seen in only 1 case before, appear in about 136 cases after; and the position distributions of A30 and A36 spread out, with standard deviations roughly doubling (about 0.06 to about 0.12). [LAG003, LAG002, LAG004, POS003, POS004]
- Earlier in the process, routing shifted: A01 led to A05 more often (about 29 to 40 in 100 of its outgoing transitions) and to A07 less often (about 36 to 25 in 100); A05 led directly to A14 less often (about 35 to 18 in 100) and to A07 more often (about 19 to 39 in 100), and the immediate follow-on A05 → A14 fell from about 36 to 18 in 100 cases. - Smaller shifts: A06 appears in about 12 in 100 cases versus about 7 before (23 versus 13 traces, so weak); A21 participation fell (about 52 to 46 in 100) while A22 rose (about 48 to 54 in 100); A15 and A16 repeat slightly more often (mean about 1.63 to 1.72 occurrences per case, recurrence about 37 to 44 in 100). [OCC006, OCC021, OCC022, OCC015, OCC016]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that, in the later window, the segment A30 → A31 → A36 is executed several times per case instead of once: each of the three activities runs about 3.5 times per case (up from exactly once), repeats occur in about 68 in 100 cases (up from about 1 in 100), the loop-back A36 → A30 became the dominant continuation (about 71 in 100 of A36's outgoing transitions versus about 0.5 before), and the exit to A25 is still taken about once per case. [OCC028, OCC029, OCC034, DIR036, DIR030, DIR031] The change is shown directly by the occurrence counts, with participation of all three activities at 100 in 100 cases in both windows, which rules out a rare variant appearing in one window; it is corroborated by relational evidence (strict order replaced by interleaved/repeated order while co-occurrence stays at 100 in 100) and by the lag and position-spread items, all with empirical strength around 0.995. [REL004, REL005, REL006, LAG003, POS003, POS004] Limitations: the loop-back edge already existed at low frequency, so this is best read as an existing path now being taken far more often rather than a newly added edge; the indicator families draw on the same traces and are not fully independent confirmations; the evidence cannot distinguish whether every case now iterates the segment about 3.5 times on average or a subset of about 68 in 100 cases iterates more while the rest still run it once (for example through a case-mix change); and no screening baseline is provided, so the judgement rests on the directness and consistency of the items. [OCC028, DIR036, REL004]

Other differences: routing around A01/A05/A07/A09 shifted, with A05 reaching A14 directly in about 18 of 100 of its outgoing transitions versus about 35 before and going via A07 more often; plus minor participation shifts for A06 (up, on few cases), A21 (down) and A22 (up), and slightly more repetition of A15/A16. These are not part of the main hypothesis. [DIR005, DIR001, OCC006, OCC021, OCC022, OCC015, OCC016]
