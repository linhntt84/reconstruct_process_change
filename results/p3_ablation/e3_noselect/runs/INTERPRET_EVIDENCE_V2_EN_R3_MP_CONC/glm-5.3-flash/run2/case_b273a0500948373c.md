### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the branch executed after A15 reversed between two alternative three-activity blocks. In the earlier window, about 82 of 100 cases continued from A15 into the block A19–A20–A21 — the three activities running together in varying order, alongside A25 — while the block A16–A17–A18 ran in about 19 of 100 cases; in the later window this flipped, with about 79 of 100 cases running A16–A17–A18 together (no fixed order among them, typically followed or accompanied by A25) and only about 21 of 100 running A19–A20–A21. The change is shown directly in A15's successor distribution and consistently in the participation of all six activities and in every co-occurrence relation among A15, A16–A18, A19–A21 and A25, with differences larger than in about 99 of 100 random re-splits; both blocks are observed in a substantial share of traces in both windows, so this is a rebalancing of existing alternative paths rather than activities appearing or disappearing. The evidence cannot rule out that the two windows draw a different mix of case types that naturally favour different blocks, rather than a modified branching rule itself. [DIR012, OCC011, OCC014, REL001, REL004, REL008, REL023, REL013, REL025]

Other differences: A10 and A11 repeat somewhat more often per trace in the later window (mean 1.68 vs 1.87, both present in every trace in both windows) [OCC009, OCC010]; A27 and A28 participation rose from about 61 to 69 of 100 traces [OCC021, OCC022]; and successor shares shifted slightly at A23, A25 and A26 (for example, A25's transitions to A27 rose from about 49% to 53%) [DIR019, DIR020, DIR021]. These smaller shifts are partly within the range random window splits produce.
