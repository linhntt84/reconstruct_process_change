### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the cyclic block involving A09, A13 and A15 is repeated substantially more per trace in the later window, centred on A09: A09 rose from about 1.9 to about 4.3 executions per trace while its participation barely moved (about 62 to 68 in 100 traces, i.e. roughly three to roughly six executions per participating trace), the returns A13→A09 and A15→A09 now carry most of those activities' outgoing traffic, A09's self-repeats cluster at shorter gaps, and its relations with A13 and A15 became more interleaved. This is consistent across the occurrence, direction, relation and lag indicator families, each resting on adequate samples (for example 853 observed outgoing transitions from A09 in the later window versus 374 before, and 115 versus 68 self-repeat pairs), while A09's typical position within the trace stayed about the same, fitting more iterations in place rather than a relocation [OCC009, DIR009, DIR013, DIR015, LAG016, REL013, REL015, POS003].

Limitations: all positional and gap evidence uses event positions, not clock time, so no claim about timing is possible; and the evidence cannot distinguish a higher loop probability within each trace from a shift in the mix of trace variants toward a heavily repeating variant, which would look identical at this level [OCC009, LAG016].

Other differences: A03, A14 and A11 show a separate restructuring — A03 and A14 stopped repeating and now run once, earlier, always together with A11, which itself stopped repeating, appears in about 47 of 100 traces, gained new predecessors (A02, A05, A08) and now precedes A14 in about 38 of 100 traces, with A14 leading to A12 more often; additionally A07 appeared in all later traces, A01 runs slightly more often without A15 as successor, and A06 runs somewhat more often. These changes are reported separately and are not part of the main hypothesis [OCC003, OCC014, OCC011, POS002, POS004, POS006, REL008, REL010, REL017, DIR002, DIR005, DIR008, DIR014, OCC001, OCC006, OCC007, DIR001].
