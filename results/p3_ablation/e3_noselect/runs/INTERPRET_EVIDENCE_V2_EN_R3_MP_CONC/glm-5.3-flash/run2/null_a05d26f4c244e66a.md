### Observed differences

- The clearest difference is in how the flow continues through A24, A25 and A26. In the earlier window, A26 led mainly to A24 (about 49 in 100 of its outgoing transitions, against about 18 in 100 to A25), A25 led mainly to A26 (about 43 in 100), and A24 led mainly to A30 (about 39 in 100). In the later window, the dominant links are reversed: A26 leads mainly to A25 (about 46 in 100, against about 26 in 100 to A24), A25 leads mainly to A30 (about 51 in 100), and A24 leads mainly to A26 (about 41 in 100) and A25 (about 37 in 100). [DIR024, DIR025, DIR026]
- The entry into this group changed accordingly: in the earlier window A20 led to A25 in about 10 in 100 of its transitions and to A24 in about 5 in 100, while in the later window it leads to A24 in about 13 in 100 and to A25 in about 4 in 100. Read together, the dominant chain A20 → A25 → A26 → A24 → A30 in the earlier window corresponds to A20 → A24 → A26 → A25 → A30 in the later window. [DIR020, DIR024, DIR025, DIR026]
- All activities involved remain frequently observed in both windows (A24, A25 and A26 in about 22–26 in 100 traces in both; A20 and A30 in essentially all traces), so this is a re-routing among existing, well-attended activities rather than a path appearing or disappearing. [OCC022, OCC023, OCC024, OCC028, DIR020]
- A30 recurs somewhat more in the later window: its mean occurrences per trace rise from about 1.6 to about 1.7 (recurrence support from about 0.36 to about 0.41), and A32 and A33 appear in slightly more traces (about 61 in 100 before, about 64–65 in 100 after). [OCC028, OCC030, OCC031]
- Most other activities shift only by about 3–5 in 100 traces with low to moderate empirical strength (roughly 0.4–0.65) — for example A01, A05, A07 and A09 drop from about 56 to about 52 in 100 traces while A03, A11, A12 and A13 rise from about 45 to about 48 in 100 — a pattern consistent with ordinary sampling variation between two windows. [OCC001, OCC005, OCC009, OCC003, OCC011, OCC012]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that, in the later window, the positions of A24 and A25 in the sequence between A20 and A30 are reversed: the dominant chain A20 → A25 → A26 → A24 → A30 of the earlier window appears as A20 → A24 → A26 → A25 → A30. This is shown directly by the successor profiles of all four involved activities — A26's dominant successor changed from A24 to A25, A25's from A26 to A30, A24's from A30 to A26, and A20's preference flipped from A25 to A24 — and each of these differences is unusual for random re-splits of unchanged data (empirical strengths about 0.82–0.98), while no involved activity was bypassed or dropped. [DIR020, DIR024, DIR025, DIR026]

Limitations: the successor shares rest on about 44–51 outgoing transitions per window for A24, A25 and A26, so individual shares carry several percentage points of uncertainty; both directions between A24 and A25 still occur in both windows, so this is a reversal of dominant routing rather than a strict exchange of a fixed order; and no screening baseline is included, so the expected number of chance findings across the 35 tested units cannot be checked. The main competing explanation — sampling variability in these moderately sized transition samples — is made unlikely by the consistency and strength of the four agreeing items but cannot be fully excluded. [DIR024, DIR025, DIR026, DIR020]

Other differences: A30 is repeated more often per trace and A32 and A33 appear in slightly more traces in the later window (empirical strengths about 0.66–0.70), and A23's outgoing flow shifts away from A21 (about 37 to about 26 in 100) toward A22 and A30 (strength about 0.84); none of these belongs to the main hypothesis. A15 and A16 also occur slightly more often per trace, though with weak empirical strength (about 0.49), and routing out of the rarely observed A06 and A10 differs but rests on only about 11–18 outgoing transitions per window; a few near-fixed edges (for example A19 → A20 and A34 → A35) shift by well under 3 in 100 and are not meaningful despite high strength scores.
