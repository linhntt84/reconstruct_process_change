### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the segment formed by A25, A26 and A31, which previously ran exactly once per case in a fixed arrangement (A25 before A31, two positions apart, with A31 then continuing to A20), became repeatable in the later window: A31 now typically leads back to A25, the single fixed pass survives in only about 32 in 100 cases, and all three activities average about 3.5 executions per case, repeating in roughly 68 in 100 cases. The change is shown directly and consistently across five indicator families — occurrence and recurrence, successor profiles, pairwise order, spacing and position spread — and every key item sits at the top of the reference scale (about 0.995, exceeded by fewer than 1 in 100 random re-splits). Because the step to A20 is still observed in about 29 in 100 of A31's outgoing steps, the segment was not removed or replaced but gained repetitions. [OCC019, OCC020, OCC025, DIR020, REL001, LAG001, POS001, POS002]

Limitations: A26's involvement rests only on its occurrence counts mirroring A25 and A31, as no relational, spacing or positional evidence covers it; no screening baseline was provided, so the item count cannot be checked against the number expected without a change and the judgement rests on the empirical strength and coherence of the items; and the evidence cannot show what distinguishes the roughly 32 in 100 cases that still run the segment once, leaving open that the repetition applies only to a subset of cases rather than uniformly.
