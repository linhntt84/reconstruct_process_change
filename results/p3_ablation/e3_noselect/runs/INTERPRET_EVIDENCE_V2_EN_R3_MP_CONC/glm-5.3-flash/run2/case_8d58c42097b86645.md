### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that, in the later window, A01 acts as an additional step between A08 and A10: traces that previously continued directly from A08 to A10 (about 55% of A08's outgoing transitions) now go from A08 to A01 and from A01 on to A10, while A08's other branch to A09 is unchanged. The signs are direct and mutually consistent: A01 appears only in the later window and only in the traces that contain A08 and A10; A08's direct edge to A10 vanished exactly where an edge to A01 took over the same share; the A08→A10 distance moved from mostly 1 step to mostly 2 steps; and A01's position is mid-trace. [OCC001, DIR001, DIR004, LAG002, REL001, REL002, POS001]

Limitations: the items describe the same traces from different angles, so they are not independent confirmations, and the interleaved or repeated A01–A08 orders in a minority of traces mean the between-A08-and-A10 reading describes the dominant pattern rather than every trace. The package also does not report how many qualifying items an unchanged process would produce, so the guard against chance rests on the individual empirical strengths, all of which exceed 199 of 200 random re-splits. An important competing explanation that cannot be fully excluded is that A01 belongs to a rare variant sampled only in the later window, though this fits poorly given its 42-of-200 frequency, its single consistent successor, and its exact overlap with the A08/A10 traces. [REL001, OCC001, DIR001, REL002, REL003]

Other differences: In the A03–A12 region, the earlier back-and-forth (A12 leading to A03, with about one-third of A03's continuations returning to A12) is gone in the later window: both A03 and A12 now lead to A13, A03 occurs in fewer traces (about 9 vs 13 in 100), and A02 gained a direct transition to A12. This looks like the A03–A12 repetition being bypassed in favour of a direct exit to A13; it is based on modest counts (16–20 outgoing transitions per item) and concerns activities outside the main hypothesis.
