### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the alternative paths after A43 are taken with different frequencies: in the later window the continuation from A43 through A44 is chosen more often, while the path through A45 and its successor A46 is chosen less often. The branch split flipped from about 45/54 in favour of A45 to about 54/45 in favour of A44, and the participation of A44, A45 and A46 shifted by about 9 in 100 traces in opposite directions with almost exactly mirrored case counts. This is seen directly at the branching point itself and consistently in the occurrence profiles of all three downstream activities, each of which remains common in both windows, so it is a re-weighting of existing alternatives rather than a path replacement or a bypass. [DIR043, OCC043, OCC044, OCC045]

Limitations: the windows are finite samples of 200 traces each, and the evidence file provides no chance-expectation baseline against which the number of flagged items can be compared, so the judgement rests on the directness and consistency of the branch-point evidence; an unusually large sampling swing cannot be fully excluded. A competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that the shift partly reflects a changed mix of cases arriving at the branch rather than a changed local choice, since the affected regions lie on one connected flow and no relational (REL or LAG) items are available to separate these.

Other differences: the evidence shows analogous share shifts elsewhere that are not part of this hypothesis — after A32 the choice moved toward A36/A37 and away from A38/A39; in the A22–A25 loop A22 rose while A23 fell, with A22 and A25 redirecting their outgoing transitions; within the A01/A05/A07/A09 block exits to A14 occur later in the block; A10 shifted from A20 toward A21; and the A15→A16 loop repeated slightly less often.
