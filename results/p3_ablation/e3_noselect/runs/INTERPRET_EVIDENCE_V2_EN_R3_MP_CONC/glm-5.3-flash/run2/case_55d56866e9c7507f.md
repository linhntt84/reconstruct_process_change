### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the mutually exclusive choice between A09 and A11 now happens at A06 instead of after A08: in the earlier window the A09-versus-A11 decision followed A08 near the end of each trace and about 42 in 100 traces made no choice at all, whereas in the later window A06 leads directly to A09 or A11 (about half the time within one step), the chosen activity runs earlier in the trace, exactly one of the two executes in every trace, and A08 now runs after that choice — later in the trace, often with no observed successor, and otherwise returning mainly to A02 or A06 rather than leading to A09 or A11. [OCC006, OCC008, REL006, DIR006, LAG002, LAG003, REL002, REL003, POS003, POS004, DIR008, POS002, LAG001]

The change is shown directly by relational and positional evidence, not only by frequencies: A06's successor profile is now dominated by A11 and A09 [DIR006], A06 immediately precedes them in most co-occurrences [LAG002, LAG003], the former immediate A08→A09/A11 successions and co-occurrences collapsed while A08 now feeds A02/A06 [DIR008, LAG005, LAG006, REL004, REL005], and the occurrence and position shifts match [OCC006, OCC008, POS002, POS003, POS004]. Occurrence, direction, position, relation and lag indicators all describe the same underlying difference, with the largest items at empirical strengths near 1.0 and substantial support in both windows.

Limitations: the earlier-window downstream profiles of A09 and A11 rest on only about 7 and 10 outgoing transitions, so what followed them before the change is uncertain [DIR009, DIR011]; no chance-expectation baseline is provided and the two windows are finite samples, so small accompanying shifts cannot be separated from sampling noise. A competing reading — that A09/A11 merely became mandatory in every trace while A08's redirection is an independent shift rather than a relocation of the decision point — cannot be fully ruled out, although the direct relational evidence links the two.
