### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, A14, A15 and A20 formed a three-way choice between A36 and A21: each appeared in about 30–35 in 100 traces (70, 60 and 70 of 200), the three never occurred in the same trace, and each led exclusively to A21. In the later window, none of the three appears in any of the 200 traces. [OCC013, OCC014, OCC019, REL001, REL003, REL008, DIR010, DIR011, DIR016]

- Accordingly, A36's continuations changed: before, about 35 in 100 went to A14, about 30 to A15 and about 35 to A20, while in the later window A36 is followed by A21 after every one of its occurrences (200 of 200 outgoing transitions), and the A36→A21 distance shortened from always two steps to always one step. [DIR027, LAG002]

- The relation and position items agree: before, A14, A15 or A20 preceded A21 in roughly 30–35 in 100 traces and always co-occurred with A36; in the later window A21 occurs in every trace with none of the three. A21's typical position moved slightly earlier (median about 0.38 to 0.36 of the trace) and A36's slightly later (about 0.31 to 0.32). [REL004, REL009, REL016, REL006, REL011, REL018, POS005, POS007]

- Routing shares also shifted in clusters where no activity appeared or disappeared: from A07, continuation to A06 fell from about 41 to 23 in 100 while A09 rose to about 43, and from A02, A06 overtook A07 as the most frequent successor; around A19, continuation to A26 rose from about 41 to 60 in 100 (now immediately adjacent in about 60 in 100); A30 and A31 route to A27 more often (about 49 vs 38 in 100); the A21 split between A28 and A29 flipped from 53/47 to 45/55; A12 now appears in about 29 in 100 traces versus 21 before; and A10/A11 repeat slightly more often (about 1.4 vs 1.5 occurrences per trace). [DIR007, DIR002, DIR015, LAG001, DIR022, DIR017, OCC011, OCC009, OCC010]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the alternative block A14/A15/A20 between A36 and A21 is no longer executed in the later window: earlier, every trace passed from A36 through exactly one of A14, A15 or A20 before reaching A21 two steps later, whereas in the later window A36 leads directly to A21 in all 200 traces and none of the three activities is observed. The change is shown directly by A36's successor profile and the shortened A36→A21 lag, and it is consistent across the occurrence, relation, position and lag indicators; three activities each seen in 60–70 of 200 traces falling to 0 of 200 is far outside ordinary sampling variation. The limitation is that zero participation only establishes non-observation: the evidence cannot distinguish removal from the block becoming restricted to cases absent from the later window. [OCC013, OCC014, OCC019, DIR027, LAG002, REL004, POS005, POS007]

Other differences: the routing shifts listed above — the A02/A06/A07/A08 cluster, A19→A26, A30/A31→A27, the A21→A28/A29 flip, A12 participation and A10/A11 repetition — involve no activity appearing or disappearing and are not part of the main hypothesis; without a screening expectation in the evidence, some of these share movements could reflect window-to-window sampling variation rather than behavioural change. [DIR007, DIR002, DIR015, LAG001, DIR022, DIR017, OCC011, OCC009, OCC010]

An important competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that A14, A15 and A20 were renamed or replaced by other activity codes rather than truly removed, since only their non-observation in the later window is established.
