### Observed differences

- The clearest difference concerns the consecutive group A35, A36, A41. In the earlier window, A25's outgoing transitions led into this group (about 39 in 100 to A35, 32 to A36, 27 to A41) and the group's members exited to A27 (about 29–37 in 100); in the later window, A25 leads almost only to A27 (about 98 in 100), A26 leads to the group (about 34, 32 and 30 in 100 to A35, A36, A41) instead of to A30 (about 99 in 100 before, 2 in 100 after), and the group's exits go to A30 (about 31–35 in 100) while its edges to A27 have nearly disappeared. [DIR025, DIR026, DIR035, DIR036, DIR041]
- Pairwise order reversed: in the earlier window, A35, A36 and A41 each ran before A26 and before A27 in about 98–100 in 100 traces containing both; in the later window, A26 and A27 each run before the three of them in about 97–99 in 100 such traces. [REL032, REL033, REL034, REL036, REL037, REL038]
- Event positions moved in opposite directions: A26's mean position fell from about 0.62 to 0.53 of the trace and A27's from 0.47 to 0.38, while A35, A36 and A41 rose from about 0.41–0.42 to 0.58–0.59. [POS005, POS006, POS008, POS009, POS010]
- Lag profiles agree: the pair A25→A27 occurs in about 200 traces in both windows, but its spacing changed from 4 steps (94 in 100) to 1 step (99 in 100), and A26→A30 stayed at about 200 traces but moved from 1 step (99 in 100) to 4 steps (91 in 100); the pairs from A25 into the group and from the group to A27 fell from about 200 traces each to 2–3, and A36→A30 rose from 1 trace to 198. [LAG019, LAG024, LAG020, LAG021, LAG022, LAG028, LAG033, LAG038, LAG034]
- Participation did not change: A25, A26, A27, A35, A36 and A41 each occur in essentially every trace (100 in 100) about once per trace in both windows, so this is a change of placement, not of added, removed or repeated activities. [OCC025, OCC026, OCC027, OCC034, OCC035, OCC040]
- A separate participation change at A06: it ran in 54 of 200 traces in the earlier window but only 25 of 200 in the later window (27% vs about 12–13%); when present it now sits later in the trace (mean position about 0.25 vs 0.44), its incoming edge from A15 fell from about 15 to 3 in 100 of A15's outgoing transitions, and its co-occurrence with the activities that run in every case fell from 27 to 12–13 in 100 traces. [OCC006, POS001, DIR015, LAG003, REL001, REL003, REL009]
- Several rare activities became more frequent: A02 from about 4.5% to 11.5% of traces (9 to 23), A04 from 4.5% to 7.5% (9 to 15), A08 from 4% to 8.5% (8 to 17) and A10 from 5% to 11% (10 to 22); with so few earlier occurrences, such jumps can occur between samples without any change. [OCC002, OCC004, OCC008, OCC010]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that, in the later window, the consecutive group of activities A35, A36 and A41 is executed at a different place. In the earlier window the dominant flow ran from A25 into the group (internal order mixed) and then through A27, A28, A29, A33, A34 and A26 to A30; in the later window A25 leads directly to A27 and the group runs between A26 and A30. [DIR025, DIR026, DIR035, DIR036, DIR041, REL032, REL036, POS005, POS008, LAG019, LAG024]

The supporting signs are direct and mutually consistent: the successor profiles at A25, A26 and the group's members flip almost completely; the order reversals concern 97–100 in 100 co-occurring traces; the group's positions rise by about 0.17 of the trace while A26 and A27 fall by about 0.09; and the lag profiles change exactly as this relocation predicts (A25→A27 becoming immediate, A26→A30 moving to four steps). Because all six activities keep 100 in 100 participation with about one occurrence each, added, removed, optional or repeated activities cannot explain the difference, and the middle chain A27–A28–A29–A33–A34–A26 as well as A30's own successors are essentially unchanged. [DIR025, DIR026, DIR027, DIR030, DIR034, REL032, REL037, OCC025, OCC026, OCC027, OCC034, OCC035, OCC040, POS005, POS008, LAG019, LAG024, LAG020, LAG034]

Limitations: the evidence establishes a change in relative order but cannot establish which segment "moved" — the alternative reading that the chain A27 through A26 moved ahead of the group fits the same data — and, since positions and lags use event indices, nothing can be said about clock-time timing or about whether the relocation happened abruptly or gradually. [REL032, POS005, POS008, LAG019]

Other differences: A06 was executed in about half as many traces in the later window (25 vs 54 of 200) and appears later within traces when it occurs, a participation change in its own right; the rare activities A02, A04, A08 and A10 roughly doubled their trace share, which may be sampling variation of rare variants. [OCC006, POS001, DIR015, OCC002, OCC004, OCC008, OCC010]
