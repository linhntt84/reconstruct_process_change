### Observed differences

- Before, A01 — which appears in every trace, about twice per trace — sent about 30 in 100 of its outgoing transitions to A05, about 36 in 100 to A03, and about 10 and 11 in 100 to A02 and A11. In the later window, the share to A05 fell to about 20 in 100 while the shares to A02 and A11 rose to about 15 in 100 each (A03 to about 38 in 100); the set of successors is unchanged, only its shares moved. This difference exceeds those of about 99 in 100 random re-splits. [OCC001, DIR001]
- In the later window, A02 and A11 each appeared in about 40 in 100 traces (79 of 200), up from 30 in 100 (60 of 200), with mean occurrences per trace rising from about 0.43 to 0.59. The two activities behave identically and typically follow one another (A02→A11 about 47–49% of A02's outgoing transitions, A11→A02 about 51–53%), consistent with a small loop entered from A01. This difference exceeds about 96 in 100 random re-splits. [OCC002, OCC010, DIR002, DIR007]
- In the later window, A05 appeared in 28 in 100 traces (56 of 200), down from 39 in 100 (78 of 200); its occurrences fell from about 123 to about 79 (mean 0.62 to 0.40 per trace), matching the drop in A01→A05 transitions, so A05 occurs only directly after A01 in both windows. This difference exceeds about 99 in 100 random re-splits. [OCC004, DIR001, DIR005]
- Other behaviour stayed essentially stable: the A03 branch split (A03→A06/A07 about 51/49 before, 49/51 after) and the participation of A06, A07, A04, A08, A09 and A10 (each near 50 in 100 traces in both windows) barely moved. [OCC003, OCC005, OCC006, OCC007, OCC008, OCC009, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006]
- A secondary shift within the A02/A11 branch: after A11, transitions to A10 rose from about 15 to 25 in 100 and transitions to A04 fell from about 32 to 24 in 100 (larger than about 81 in 100 random re-splits), even though A04 and A10 still each appear in about half the traces in both windows. [DIR007, OCC003, OCC009]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that, in the later window, the existing alternative paths after A01 are taken with different frequencies: fewer executions continue from A01 to A05 (about 30% to 20% of A01's outgoing transitions; A05 present in 39% vs 28% of traces), while more executions enter the A02/A11 loop (together about 21% to 30% of A01's outgoing transitions; A02 and A11 each present in 30% vs 40% of traces). Every involved activity occurs in a substantial share of traces in both windows, the successor sets are unchanged, and occurrences per participating trace are roughly stable, so this is a shift between existing alternatives rather than a replacement, addition or removal of a path. [DIR001, OCC002, OCC004, OCC010]

Supporting signs: the change is shown directly at the A01 decision point, and the participation changes of A05, A02 and A11 match it in direction and size; the counts also reconcile, with A05 losing about 44 occurrences while A02 and A11 gained about 33 occurrences each. [DIR001, OCC002, OCC004, OCC010]

Limitations: the qualifying items all describe one underlying trace behaviour and are not independent confirmations, and no screening summary is available to compare their number with a chance expectation; an atypical pair of 200-trace samples therefore cannot be excluded with certainty, although the coherence, size and empirical strength of the differences make pure sampling noise an unlikely explanation. [DIR001, OCC002, OCC004, OCC010]

Other differences: within the A02/A11 branch, the exit after A11 moved from A04 towards A10 (about 32%→24% and 15%→25% of A11's outgoing transitions) while A04 and A10 participation stayed near one half of traces in both windows; this redistribution is not part of the main hypothesis. [DIR007, OCC003, OCC009]
