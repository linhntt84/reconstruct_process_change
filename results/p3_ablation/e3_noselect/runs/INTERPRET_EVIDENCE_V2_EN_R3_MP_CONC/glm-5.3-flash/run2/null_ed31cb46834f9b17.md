### Inference

Support level: Weak hypothesis

The evidence suggests that flow across the alternatives following A20 was redirected. In the later window, the A24–A26 group exits to A30 mainly through A25 (about 49% of A25's outgoing transitions, up from about 30%) instead of through A24 (about 20%, down from about 39%), and its internal transitions favour A25 (A26 to A25 up from about 18% to 44%, A25 to A26 down from about 43% to 26%, A26 to A24 down from 50% to 25%), while entry shifted away from A25, A22 and A23 towards A24 and A21; A24, A25 and A26 also appear slightly more often (about 22 to 26 in 100 traces) and A21, A22 and A23 slightly less (about 79 to 75 in 100). Supporting signs: these are direct successor-distribution changes, they concentrate on one connected branch, and the occurrence figures for the same activities point the same way. [DIR020, DIR023, DIR024, DIR025, DIR026, OCC019, OCC020, OCC021, OCC022, OCC023, OCC024]

What is missing: the evidence contains no screening baseline, so the number of qualifying items cannot be compared with the number expected for an unchanged process; the A24–A26 routing shares rest on only about 44–53 outgoing transitions per window; and outside this branch most items show low-to-moderate empirical strength (for example about 0.07 for A10's and 0.02 for A11's successor mix), so the overall yield of strong items stays within the range an unchanged process can produce. The competing explanation that cannot be ruled out is sampling variability between the two 200-trace windows concentrating by chance in this branch. [DIR010, DIR011]
