### Observed differences

- The route between A26 and A28 collapsed onto a single path. In the earlier window, about 50 in 100 of A26's outgoing transitions led to A27 and about 48 in 100 led to A32, A33 or A38 (roughly 14–18 in 100 each); in the later window, about 98 in 100 lead to A27 [DIR026]. A27's participation rose from about 52 to 199 of 200 traces, while A32, A33 and A38 each fell from about 48–49 in 100 traces to a single trace [OCC025, OCC030, OCC031, OCC036, POS005, POS007, POS008, POS009].
- The relational evidence matches: A27 co-occurred with A26 and with A28 in about half of the earlier traces but in essentially all later traces, in the fixed order A26 before A27 before A28; A32, A33 and A38, which earlier typically ran before A28 and were mutually exclusive with A27, now almost never occur [REL021, REL026, REL027, REL028, REL029, REL030, REL031, REL032, REL033, REL034, REL035]. The gap from A26 to A28 shrank from a roughly even split between 2 and 4 steps to about 95 in 100 at 2 steps [LAG013].
- A04 is now largely skipped after A14. In the earlier window A14 was followed by A04 in about 63 in 100 of its outgoing transitions and by A15 in about 36; in the later window these shares are about 10 and 82 in 100 [DIR014]. A04's participation fell from about 68 to 21.5 in 100 traces, A14 now occurs without A04 in about 79 in 100 traces (previously 32 in 100), and A14→A15 has become more often immediate (within one step: 19% → 47%) [OCC003, REL001, LAG006, LAG008]. When A04 does occur, its event position is later and more spread out (mean relative position about 0.21 → 0.40) [POS001].
- Several rare activities became more frequent: A02 from about 5 to 15 in 100 traces, A06 from 7.5 to 15, A08 from 5 to 20.5 and A10 from 8.5 to 14 [OCC002, OCC005, OCC007, OCC009]. Their outgoing profiles also changed markedly, but with only 8–17 outgoing transitions observed before, that routing detail is weakly supported [DIR002, DIR006, DIR008, DIR010].
- Smaller shifts: A40 repeats less per trace (mean occurrences 0.93 → 0.81 at unchanged participation of about 49.5 in 100), and A39's participation slipped from about 25.5 to 19 in 100 traces [OCC038, OCC037].

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that, in the later window, the route between A26 and A28 runs almost exclusively via A27, while the alternative route through A32, A33 and A38 has been all but abandoned: A26 is followed by A27 in about 98 of 100 outgoing transitions (previously about 50), A27 appears in about 199 of 200 traces (previously about 103), and A32, A33 and A38 each remain in only about 1 of 200 traces (previously about 97–98). The change is shown directly by relational evidence on A26/A27/A28 versus A32/A33/A28, consistently across occurrence, successor, position and gap indicators, and the key differences exceed virtually all random re-splits of the same data (empirical strength about 0.995) [DIR026, OCC025, OCC030, OCC031, OCC036, REL021, REL026, REL027, REL030, REL033, LAG013]. Limitations: the evidence shows near-disuse rather than certain removal, since the three activities still occur in single traces, and it cannot show whether this rerouting and the A04 change below are one coordinated redesign or two independent changes [OCC030, OCC031, OCC036].

Other differences:
- A04 became largely optional after A14: A14 now proceeds directly to A15 in most traces, A04's participation dropped from about 68 to 21.5 in 100 traces, and A04 occurs later and more variably within the trace; this is a participation change, not an exchange of positions between A04 and A15 [DIR014, OCC003, REL001, REL002, POS001].
- The rare activities A02, A06, A08 and A10 appear in roughly twice as many traces as before, though still in at most about 20 in 100 [OCC002, OCC005, OCC007, OCC009].
- A40 repeats somewhat less often per trace, and A39 occurs somewhat less often [OCC038, OCC037].
