### Observed differences

- A01 moved from the start to the late part of the case: in the earlier window its mean relative position was about 0.26 (median about 0.17, with a quarter of occurrences the very first event); in the later window it is about 0.65 (median about 0.67, middle half between roughly 0.5 and 0.83 of the case). A01 occurs in every trace in both windows. [POS001]

- The order between A01 and the other always-present activities reversed. A01 ran before A07 in about 68 in 100 earlier cases but in none of the later 200 (A07 now always first); against A04 the earlier order was mixed (about 49 vs 51 in 100) and A04 now comes first in all 200 later cases; A01 ran before A05 in about 80 in 100 earlier cases but in only 37 in 100 later ones; before A02 in about 55 in 100 earlier, 19 in 100 later. [REL004, REL002, REL003, REL001]

- Immediate succession changed accordingly: A07 now leads to A01 in about 68 in 100 of its observed outgoing transitions (about 17 in 100 before), and A03 is no longer observed directly after A07; A01 now leads to A03 in about 47 in 100 of its outgoing transitions (about 7 in 100 before), while A04 and A07 are no longer observed directly after A01. [DIR006, DIR001]

- Distances are consistent with a dominant sequence A04 → A07 → A01 → A03: A07 precedes A01 within five steps in all 200 later traces (65 of 200 before), usually adjacently (about 68 in 100 at distance one, up from about 54 in 100); A04 precedes A01 in all 200 later traces (103 of 200 before) but typically two steps apart (about 48 in 100 at distance two, adjacent almost never); A04 leads to A07 in about 70 in 100 of its outgoing transitions (about 54 in 100 before); A01 is followed by A03 within five steps in all 200 later traces (143 of 200 before), adjacently in about 47 in 100 (about 10 in 100 before). [LAG019, LAG009, LAG013, DIR003, LAG002]

- A02 now precedes A01 in 162 of 200 later traces (91 of 200 before) but mostly at longer distances (adjacent in about 13 in 100 versus about 57 in 100 before), and A01 precedes A02 within five steps in only 38 of 200 later traces (109 of 200 before). [LAG004, LAG001]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that A01's placement in the sequence changed: in the later window A01 runs late in the case, after A02, A04, A05 and especially A07 — which directly precedes it in about two-thirds of later cases, itself typically after A04 — and A01 is now usually followed by A03; in the earlier window A01 ran near the start, ahead of A05, A07 and A02, with no fixed order against A04. The change is shown directly by a positional shift, four order reversals, and matching successor and distance changes that all point the same way, each larger than in about 995 of 1,000 random re-splits; all five activities still occur together in every trace in both windows, so participation itself did not change. [POS001, REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, DIR001, DIR006, LAG019, LAG009, LAG002]

Limitations: positions are event ranks, not clock time, so this is a change in sequence order, not evidence that A01 happened later in clock time; the items overlap heavily and largely describe one underlying trace behaviour rather than independent confirmations; and the evidence cannot rule out that both windows sample the same set of possible orderings with different variant frequencies (a late-A01 ordering becoming dominant) rather than a modified ordering constraint. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, POS001]

Other differences: A02, A05 and A07 each now occur slightly earlier (mean relative position lower by roughly 0.08–0.10); A02's outgoing transitions shifted away from A01 (about 26 in 100 to 11 in 100) toward A05 (about 27 in 100 to 40 in 100); and A03 tends to come later relative to A05 and A07 (A05 then A03 at distance five rose from about 12 in 100 to 32 in 100, while A07 directly before A03 disappeared). These fit the same reordering but are not part of the main hypothesis above. [POS002, POS004, POS005, DIR002, LAG016, LAG021]
