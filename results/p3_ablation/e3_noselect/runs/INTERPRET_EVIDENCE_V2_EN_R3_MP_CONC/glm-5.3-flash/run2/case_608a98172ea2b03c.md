### Observed differences

- Successors of A01 changed completely: in the earlier window, A01's outgoing transitions went to A04 in about half of the cases (50%), to A03 in about 29% and to A02 in about 22%; in the later window, all observed outgoing transitions (about 380) go to A04 alone. [DIR001]
- Successors of A04 changed in the opposite direction: earlier, A04 led back to A01 in about half of its outgoing transitions (50%), and to A02 (26%) or A03 (23%) otherwise; later, it leads to A03 in about 53% and A02 in about 47% of transitions, and no longer directly back to A01. [DIR002]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility of a structural reordering around A01 and A04: in the later window, A01 hands over directly to A04 and the two run in a fixed sequence A01 then A04, whereas earlier they occurred in both orders with no fixed constraint between them; the branches that previously left A01 (to A02 and A03) now leave A04 instead, and A06 feeds into A01. [REL003, DIR001, DIR002, DIR003]

The change is shown directly by relational evidence (the A01–A04 order moved from mixed to fixed) and by the successor profiles of A01, A04 and A06, and it is corroborated by the position shifts and by step distances that became uniform, consistent with a chain A01 → A04 → A02 or A03, with A06 feeding A01. The items agree across four indicator families (direction, relation, position, step distance), each difference is larger than in about 995 of 1000 random re-splits, and the profiles rest on large supports (about 380 outgoing transitions and 200 traces per window), making sampling noise an unlikely explanation for the rerouting. [REL003, DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, POS001, POS003, LAG002, LAG003, LAG009, LAG010]

Limitations: the evidence establishes that the ordering constraint between A01 and A04 changed from unfixed to fixed, but it cannot distinguish a genuine exchange of the two activities from the removal of the direct A04 → A01 return combined with A01's exits moving to A04; positions and distances are in event steps, so no claim about clock-time timing is possible. [REL003, LAG008, POS001]

Other differences: the share of traces containing A02, A05 or A06 fell by about 5 in 100 (from about 47 to about 42), as did the share of traces in which A01 or A04 repeats; these shifts are small and score low against random re-splits, so they are consistent with sampling variation between the two 200-trace windows rather than a participation change. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005]
