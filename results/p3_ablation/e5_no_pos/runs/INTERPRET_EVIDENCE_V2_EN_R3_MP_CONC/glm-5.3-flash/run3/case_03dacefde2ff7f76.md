### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, A45 handed over to one of three largely mutually exclusive alternatives — about 31% of its outgoing transitions went to A19, 26% to A20 and 28% to A25 — and each of these led almost exclusively to A26 (about 89–98% of outgoing transitions). In the later window, A45 leads almost directly to A26 (about 89% of its outgoing), while A19, A20 and A25 now lead mainly to A28 and A32 (together about 94–97%). [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR006]

- The order between the block A19/A20/A25 and A26 and A27 reversed. Earlier, the block member came before A26 or A27 in about 30–38 of 100 traces and essentially never after; in the later window A26 or A27 comes first in about 31–36 of 100, while the block-first order survives in at most about 1 of 100. [REL001, REL002, REL004, REL005, REL006, REL007]

- Relative to A28, the block changed from consistently earlier to mixed: earlier, A19 and A25 preceded A28 in about 32–37 of 100 traces and never followed it; in the later window they precede it in about 15–16 and follow it in about 15–22 of 100. [REL003, REL008]

- A27 and A28 now route into the block: A27's outgoing share to A28 fell from about 98% to about 51%, with about 47% now going to A19/A20/A25; A28's share to A32 fell from about 94% to about 48%, with about 51% going to the block. [DIR004, DIR005]

- Adjacency moved with the order: A45→A26 became immediate (gap 1 in about 89 of 100 observations, versus gap 2 in about 84 before); the direct A25→A26 step (gap 1 in about 63 observations earlier) is no longer observed; A27→A28 and A28→A32 now often have one event in between (gap 2 in about 48–50 of 100, versus gap 1 in about 96–98 before); A27→A32 shifted from gap 2 to gap 3 (about 97 of 100). [LAG008, LAG003, LAG004, LAG007, LAG005]

- In the later window the block sits directly around A28: A20→A28 is immediate in about 97% of 36 observations, A28→A20 immediate in 30 observations, and A20→A32 lies one to two positions before A32 in about 98% of 66 observations. The earlier-window profiles of these three pairs rest on a single observation each, so only the later adjacencies are informative. [LAG001, LAG006, LAG002]

- Participation stayed broadly stable: A19 appears in about 38% of before-traces versus about 31% after, A20 in about 33% versus 35%, A25 in about 32% versus 39%; A26, A27 and A28 occur in every trace in both windows. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, REL001, REL002, REL003]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the block of alternative activities A19, A20 and A25 — roughly one of which executes per case — was repositioned from before the A26–A27–A28 sequence to after it. Earlier the flow ran A45 → one of A19/A20/A25 → A26, with A27 → A28 → A32 further along the trace; in the later window it runs A45 → A26 → A27 → A28 → one of A19/A20/A25 → A32, with the block inserted sometimes between A27 and A28 and sometimes between A28 and A32. Screening flagged 22 control-flow items from 118 tested units against roughly 0.59 expected without change, so the volume of findings cannot be explained by random re-splits, and every item's difference exceeds about 995 of 1000 random re-splits. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006, REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, REL007, REL008, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006, LAG007, LAG008]

The support is direct and consistent: the order reversal is shown by the relation items themselves, the successor profiles match it at all six profiled sources, and the gap items show the matching adjacency changes (loss of the direct A25→A26 step, new immediate A45→A26 step, one-position shifts around A27/A28). Because every involved activity occurs in a substantial share of traces in both windows and participation is stable, this reads as a relocation of an existing block rather than activities being added, removed or skipped. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006, REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, REL007, REL008, LAG003, LAG004, LAG005, LAG007, LAG008]

Limitations: the outgoing transitions of A26 were not profiled, so the path from A26 onward is inferred from order and gap evidence rather than observed directly; and the evidence cannot rule out that the later ordering existed before as a rare variant whose share rose, rather than a rewiring within a single variant. [LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG006, DIR001, DIR004, DIR005]

Other differences: a few low-frequency successors (each about 1–2% of outgoing transitions, such as A14 and A02 after A19, A06, A12 and A36 after A20, and A15 and A27 after A25) appear in the later window where they were essentially absent before; at these rates they fall within what finite samples typically show. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003]
