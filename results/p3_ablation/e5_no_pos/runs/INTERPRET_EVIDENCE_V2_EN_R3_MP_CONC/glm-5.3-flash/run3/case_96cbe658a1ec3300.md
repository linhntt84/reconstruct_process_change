### Observed differences

- **The order of two groups of activities flipped.** In the earlier window, A26 ran before A35, A36 and A41 in about 98–99 in 100 traces, and A27 ran before them in about 98.5–99.5 in 100 traces. In the later window this inverted: A35, A36 and A41 each ran before A26 and before A27 in about 97–98 in 100 traces. In both windows every one of these pairs still occurred together in essentially all traces (co-occurrence 100%), so presence did not change — only the order did. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006]

- **What follows A25 changed.** Earlier, A27 followed A25 immediately (gap of one event in about 98.5% of traces; about 97.5% of A25's outgoing transitions went to A27). In the later window, A25 is followed by A35, A36 or A41 (roughly one third of its outgoing transitions each) and A27 now follows 4–5 positions later (about 99.5% of traces). [DIR001, LAG001, LAG002]

- **A26's successor changed and A30 moved.** Earlier, A26 led into the A35/A36/A41 group (about 28–38% of its outgoing transitions each) and reached A30 only 4–5 positions later (essentially all traces). In the later window, A26 leads directly to A30 (about 97.5% of outgoing transitions; gap of one event in about 98.5% of traces). [DIR002, LAG003]

- **The group's exit changed from A30 to A27.** Earlier, each of A35, A36 and A41 passed on to A30 in about 28–34% of its outgoing transitions, and A30 followed the group within five positions in about 200 of 200 traces. In the later window they pass on to A27 instead (about 31–33% each, within 1–3 positions in about 199 of 200 traces), and a group-to-A30 succession within five positions survives in only 1–2 traces. [DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, LAG007, LAG008, LAG009, LAG010]

- **Close succession swapped sides accordingly.** Earlier, A26 was followed by A35/A36/A41 within five positions in about 200 traces (gaps 1–3); in the later window such successions survive in only 1–2 traces. Conversely, A25 followed by A35, earlier seen in about 1 trace, now occurs in about 199 traces with gaps of 1–3. [LAG002, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006, LAG007]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that two segments of the process exchanged their execution order. In the earlier window the flow ran from A25 immediately to A27, later reached A26, which led into the group A35/A36/A41 (traversed in varying order), and the group exited to A30. In the later window, A25 is followed directly by the group A35/A36/A41, the group leads to A27, and A26 — now running after the group — is followed immediately by A30. In short, the group A35/A36/A41 moved from after A26/A27 to between A25 and A27, and A30 moved from the group's exit to directly after A26. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, LAG001, LAG003]

Supporting signs: 21 evidence items qualified against fewer than 1 (0.59) expected by chance in the control-flow family, so the set of differences is unlikely for an unchanged process. The order flip is shown directly by six relational items with full support (200 traces per window) and unchanged co-occurrence, and it is corroborated by consistent positional and successor changes: A25→A27 moved from a gap of 1 to 4–5 events, A26→A30 from 4–5 to 1, and the group-to-A30 and A26-to-group successions within five steps dropped from about 200 traces to 1–2, while A25→A35 and A35→A27 rose from about 1 trace to about 199. All items describe one coherent reordering rather than unrelated differences. [DIR001, DIR002, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG007, LAG008]

Limitations: the positional indicators use event positions, not clock time, so this concerns order rather than timing. The relative order of A26 and A27 after the swap, and any activities running between them, are not covered by the relational evidence, which only covers pairs with the group. Also, a shift in the mix of two order variants (one with each sequence) would look identical at the window level; the evidence cannot separate a reordering of one process from such a mix shift, though either way the executed sequence changed. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, LAG001, LAG003]

Other differences: about 1 in 200 outgoing transitions at the edges of several successor profiles differ between windows (for example A06/A08/A22 versus A04/A14/A21 around A25 and A36); this is at the level expected from sampling noise and is not part of the hypothesis. [DIR001, DIR004, DIR005]
