### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that, in the later window, A10 acts as a frequent early step: it appears in about 70 in 100 traces (previously about 19 in 100), typically after A14 and before A15 and the group A20, A22–A26, A31, A32 (with A45–A48 also co-occurring with it far more often), so that the earlier direct A14 → A15 transition now usually runs A14 → A10 → A15. The support is direct and consistent: the participation rise, the shift from rarely co-occurring (about 19 in 100 traces, no fixed order) to usually co-occurring and preceding across fourteen partner activities, the successor flips at A14 and A10, and the new one-event adjacencies all show the same relational change. Chance is an unlikely explanation for the evidence as a whole, since 69 items qualified where roughly 1 would be expected without change, though the many pair items largely mirror this one underlying shift rather than confirming it independently, and every item also ranks above about 99.5 in 100 random re-splits. Limitations: all order evidence uses event positions rather than clock time, and the evidence cannot show whether A10 was added to these paths or an existing occasional step became frequent. [OCC003, REL001, REL002, REL003, REL006, REL011, DIR002, DIR003, LAG001, LAG002]

Other differences: the near-fixed internal order of the A20–A26 block (with A31 and A32) loosened into mixed or reversed orders with more varied successors and spread event distances, as described above; A08 and A04 were executed in far fewer traces. A competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that the later window samples a different mix of case variants — for instance, traces in which A10 runs early became more common — rather than a reordering within one flow; either way, the described behavioural difference is real, and no relational item ties the decline of A08 and A04 to A10's rise.
