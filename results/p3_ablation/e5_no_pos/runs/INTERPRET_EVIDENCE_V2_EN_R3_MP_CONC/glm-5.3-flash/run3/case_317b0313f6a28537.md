### Observed differences

- The outgoing flow of A19, A20 and A25 was redirected. In the earlier window each of the three split its transitions roughly evenly between A28 and A32 (A19 about 47% to A28 and 48% to A32; A20 about 56% and 43%; A25 about 50% and 45%). In the later window each sends about 88–94 in 100 transitions to A26 (A19 92% of 72 transitions, A20 94% of 70, A25 88% of 68), leaving only scattered single-percent edges. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003]
- A27 and A28 now pass straight through. A27's share of transitions to A28 rose from about 47% to about 98%, and A28's share to A32 from about 50% to about 96%. In step terms, A27 reaches A28 one event later in about 98 in 100 later-window traces (before: about half at one step, half at two), A28 reaches A32 immediately in about 96 in 100 (before: about half at one, half at two steps), and A27 reaches A32 typically two steps later instead of three. [DIR004, DIR005, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006]
- The order between the two activity groups flipped. Earlier, A26 and A27 each came before A19, A20 or A25 in about 31–38 in 100 traces and essentially never the reverse; in the later window A19, A20 and A25 each come before A26 and A27 in about 30–36 in 100 traces, with the earlier order nearly gone. How often the activities co-occur barely changed (A19 in about 36–39% of traces in both windows, A20 and A25 about 31–35%), so participation itself was stable. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006]
- A45's hand-off moved one hop upstream. Earlier about 85 in 100 of its transitions went to A26; later about 85 in 100 go to A19, A20 or A25 (roughly 29%, 29% and 27%), and A26 is now reached from A45 two to three steps later rather than immediately. [DIR006, LAG007]
- Three further lag items (A19–A32, A25–A28, A25–A32) point in the same direction but rest on only 1–3 after-window observations, so they carry little weight. [LAG001, LAG002, LAG003]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the segment formed by A19, A20 and A25 changed its place in the flow. In the earlier window these activities executed after A26 and A27 and routed onward to A28 or A32, so the route from A27 to A32 passed through one of them; in the later window they execute before A26 and A27, lead into A26, A27 feeds A28 directly and A28 feeds A32 directly, and A45 now enters via A19, A20 or A25 instead of via A26. The order reversal is shown directly by the relation profiles, the redirection by the successor profiles, and the shortened distances A27→A28, A28→A32 and A27→A32 together with the lengthened A45→A26 corroborate both. As a collection, 20 items qualified against roughly 0.56 expected for an unchanged process (about 35 times the chance expectation), and each item's difference exceeds what was seen in about 199 of 200 random re-splits of the pooled traces. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006, REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006, LAG007]

Limitations: the evidence contains no successor profile for A26 itself, so what A26 leads to after the block in the later window is not directly observed. Also, although the old edges are essentially unobserved later (none of A19's 72 transitions go to A28 or A32) and the new edge unobserved earlier (none of 79 go to A26), a shift in the mix of two coexisting process variants cannot be fully excluded from two finite samples — though such a mix shift would have to be near-total to fit these numbers. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003]

Other differences: A15 still occurs in every trace in both windows, but repeats less often — about 1.58 occurrences per trace versus about 1.89 before, with repeated occurrences in about 38% of traces versus about 47%; the evidence does not connect this to the reordering. [OCC001]
