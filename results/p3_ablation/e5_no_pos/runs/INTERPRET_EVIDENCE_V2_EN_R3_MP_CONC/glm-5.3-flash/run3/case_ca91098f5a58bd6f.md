### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, A14, A15 and A20 each appeared in roughly a third of traces (about 35, 30 and 35 in 100 cases respectively, once per trace when present); in the later window, none of the 200 traces contain any of the three. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003]
- In the earlier window, the observed outgoing transitions of A36 split between A20 (about 35%), A14 (about 35%) and A15 (about 30%); in the later window, all observed outgoing transitions of A36 go to A21. [DIR004]
- In the earlier window, A21 was the only observed successor of each of A14, A15 and A20; in the later window these successor profiles are empty because the source activities were not observed at all. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003]
- The positional gap between A36 and A21 narrowed: in the earlier window A21 followed A36 two positions later in every trace, while in the later window it follows immediately, one position later. [LAG001]
- The relation evidence agrees: before, A14, A15 and A20 never shared a trace (they were mutually exclusive alternatives), and each co-occurred with A21 and with A26 in about a third of traces, always after A36 and before A21/A26; in the later window, A21 and A26 still appear in all traces but never together with any of the three. [REL001, REL003, REL004, REL006, REL009, REL013, REL015]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a structural hypothesis: the alternative block formed by A14, A15 and A20, which previously sat between A36 and A21, is no longer executed in the later window. In the earlier window, each trace ran A36, then exactly one of A14, A15 or A20 (chosen in roughly 35/35/30 proportions), then A21; in the later window, A36 is followed directly by A21, one position earlier than before, while A36, A21 and A26 themselves continue to appear in all traces. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, DIR004, LAG001, REL004]

The change is shown directly by the successor change at A36 and the shortened A36–A21 distance, and it is consistent across the occurrence and relation items, all of which move in the same direction. The screening produced 23 qualifying items where fewer than 1 would be expected if nothing had changed, so the pattern as a whole is not what random re-splitting of a stable process produces. Note this is not a shift in how often existing paths are taken: the three activities are absent from the later window and the predecessor now connects straight to the successor. [DIR004, LAG001, OCC001, REL013]

The main competing explanation that remains is one of degree: zero participation only means the activities were not observed in the later window, so the evidence cannot distinguish a branch removed outright from one that has become very rare and happened to be absent from all 200 later traces, nor can it show whether the choice still exists upstream. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003]

Other differences: A19 appears somewhat more often, in about 56 of 100 later traces versus about 48 of 100 earlier traces; this small shift is not part of the main hypothesis. [REL002, REL007, REL012]
