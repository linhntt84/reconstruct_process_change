### Observed differences

- The activities A25, A26 and A31 still appear in every trace in both windows, but their repetition collapsed: in the earlier window each occurred about 3.4 times per trace and repeated within a trace in about 75 in 100 traces; in the later window each occurs about once per trace, repeating in only about 1 in 200 traces. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003]
- The step from A31 back to A25 largely disappeared: before, about 71 in 100 of A31's observed outgoing transitions led to A25 (about 29 in 100 to A20); in the later window about 96 in 100 lead to A20 and only about 4 in 100 (roughly 8 of 208 transitions) still lead to A25. [DIR001]
- A25 and A31 moved from repeated or interleaved execution to a fixed order: they still occur together in every trace, but before they were interleaved or repeated in about 75 in 100 traces, while in the later window A25 comes before A31 in about 995 in 1000 traces. [REL001]
- The pair spacing changed accordingly: A31 now follows A25 exactly two positions later in about 92 in 100 of the observed close successions, up from about 12 in 100, and the reverse pair A31 then A25 within five steps, seen in 150 of 200 earlier traces, appears in only 1 of 200 later traces. [LAG001, LAG002]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that a repeating cycle over A25, A26 and A31 stopped repeating. In the earlier window these three activities formed a recurring block — about 3.4 executions per trace each, with A31 returning to A25 in most of its transitions and the pair interleaved or repeated in about three quarters of traces — whereas in the later window each activity runs once per trace in a fixed sequence with A25 about two positions before A31, after which A31 passes control to A20 in about 96 in 100 of its transitions. The signs are direct and mutually consistent: the occurrence items show the repetition collapse, while the directional and relational items show the return edge to A25 vanishing and a fixed order emerging; seven qualifying items against roughly 0.29 expected for an unchanged process, each difference larger than about 995 of 1000 random re-splits, also exceeds what sampling alone typically produces. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, DIR001, REL001, LAG001, LAG002]

Limitations: the evidence cannot show whether the cycle was removed entirely or only taken far less often, since a few A31→A25 transitions remain in the later window, and no relational item covers A26, so its place in the former cycle is inferred only from its occurrence profile. A competing explanation that the later window captures a rare single-pass variant cannot be fully excluded, though the screening count and the activities' full participation in both windows make plain sampling fluctuation unlikely.

Other differences: none — all seven qualifying items describe this single behaviour around A25, A26 and A31. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, DIR001, REL001, LAG001, LAG002]
