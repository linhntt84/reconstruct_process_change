### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, A14, A15 and A20 each occurred in about 47 in 100 cases; in the later window each occurs in about 995 in 1000 cases, still at most once per case in both windows [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003].
- The flow out of A36 was split roughly evenly in the earlier window (about 47% of its outgoing transitions to A14, about 53% to A21); in the later window it goes to A14 in about 995 in 1000 observed transitions and to A21 in only about 5 in 1000 [DIR001].
- Earlier, A14, A20 and A15 either all ran together (about 47 in 100 cases, always in the order A14, then A20, then A15) or were all absent together (about 53 in 100 cases); in the later window they run together in that same fixed order in essentially every case [REL001, REL002, REL007].
- A36 precedes the three activities, and the three precede A21, in essentially every case where they co-occur, in both windows [REL004, REL013, REL008, REL012].
- The distance between A36 and A21 changed from one event position (about 53 in 100 cases earlier) to four event positions (about 995 in 1000 cases later) [LAG001].
- A37 was present in every case in both windows, but its ordering against A14 became less fixed: A14 before A37 in about 30 in 100 cases earlier versus about 54 in 100 later, with interleaved-or-repeated cases rising from about 17 to about 46 in 100 [REL005, REL010, REL014].
- A40 remained optional throughout (about 60 in 100 cases earlier, about 62 in 100 later); its relation shifts with A14, A15 and A20 mirror those activities becoming universal rather than an independent change [REL006, REL011, REL015].

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the sequence A14 → A20 → A15 between A36 and A21 changed from optional to essentially mandatory: in the earlier window about half of the cases went directly from A36 to A21 without these three activities, whereas in the later window nearly every case runs A36, then A14, then A20, then A15, then A21, and the direct A36-to-A21 step has all but disappeared [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, DIR001, REL001, REL002, REL007, LAG001].

The support is direct and consistent: participation of all three activities rose from about 47% to about 99.5% [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003]; A36's routing moved from an even split to almost exclusively A14 [DIR001]; the three activities were jointly present or jointly absent earlier and now co-occur in a fixed order in nearly all cases [REL001, REL002, REL007]; and the A36–A21 distance shifted from one to four positions, matching the three activities now sitting between them [LAG001]. Twenty items qualified against roughly 0.38 expected for an unchanged process, so chance sampling cannot explain the pattern as a whole, and the strictly joint presence or absence of the three activities earlier argues against them having merely moved from elsewhere in the process [OCC001, REL001, REL007]. The main competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that the earlier skip share of about 53 in 100 cases was already drifting before the cut, since the comparison rests on one before/after split of finite 200-case windows and a small skip share of about 5 in 1000 may persist [DIR001, LAG001].

Other differences: the ordering between A37 and the A14–A20–A15 chain became less fixed in the later window (interleaved-or-repeated up from about 17 to about 46 in 100 cases) [REL005, REL010, REL014]; A40 stayed optional with essentially unchanged participation, so its relation shifts appear to follow from the three chain activities becoming universal rather than being a separate change [REL006, REL011, REL015].
