### Observed differences

- **Routing out of A40.** In the earlier window, A40 was followed by one of three alternatives — A14 in about 38 of 100 observed outgoing transitions, A15 in about 30, A20 in about 32 — and A21 followed two positions after A40. In the later window, A40 is always followed directly by A21, in the very next position, and none of the three alternatives is observed after A40 any more. [DIR006, LAG004]

- **Successors of A14, A15 and A20.** In the earlier window, each of the three was always followed by A21. In the later window, a successor A21 is no longer observed for them: A14 is followed by A23 in about 52 of 100 observed transitions and by A27 in about 48, A15 by A23 in about 54 and A27 in about 46, and A20 by A27 in about 61 and A23 in about 39. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003]

- **Reversed order against A21 and A22.** In the earlier window, A14, A15 and A20 each ran before A21 in every trace where they co-occurred (about 38, 31 and 32 of 100 traces) and before A22 in every such trace. In the later window this is reversed: A21 now runs before them (about 31, 33 and 37 of 100 co-occurrences), and so does A22. [REL001, REL002, REL004, REL005, REL006, REL007]

- **The A22–A23–A27 segment.** In the earlier window, A22 was always followed by A23, A23 always by A27, and A22 and A27 were always two positions apart. In the later window, A22 leads to A23 in about 52 of 100 observed transitions and to one of A14/A15/A20 in about 48; A23 leads to A27 in about 48 and to one of A14/A15/A20 in about 52; the A22–A23 and A23–A27 distances are now one position in roughly half of the cases and two in the other half, and A22 to A27 is always three positions apart. A14 and A20, which previously always ran before A23, now appear in both orders in similar shares (A14 before A23 about 16 of 100 versus about 15 the other way; A20 about 15 versus about 22). [DIR004, DIR005, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, REL003, REL008]

- **Participation is stable.** Each of A14, A15 and A20 occurs in roughly 30–38 of 100 traces in both windows, and the three shares together sum to about every trace, so one of the three still runs in essentially every case; A21, A22, A23, A27 and A40 are observed throughout. The group did not become more or less frequent — it runs somewhere else. [REL001, REL004, REL006, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006, LAG002]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the alternative group A14/A15/A20 changed its placement along the trace, together with A21. In the earlier window, exactly one of A14, A15 or A20 followed A40 and then always fed into A21, while A22, A23 and A27 ran as an uninterrupted consecutive chain after it. In the later window, A40 feeds directly into A21, and the group activity is now reached from A22 or from A23 and leads on to A23 or A27 — that is, the group runs between A22 and A27, sometimes before A23 and sometimes after it with no fixed order relative to A23, while the choice among the three alternatives itself persists with roughly stable shares. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006, REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, REL007, REL008, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004]

The screening context supports reading this as a real difference rather than sampling noise: 18 control-flow items qualified where about 0.36 would be expected without a change, roughly fifty times the chance expectation, and every item describes the same redirection around the group, each with a difference larger than in about 199 of 200 random re-splits of the pooled data. The unchanged participation of the group fits a placement change rather than activities being added, removed or made optional. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006, REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, REL007, REL008, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004]

Limitations: no item covers the A21–A22 pair, so the later-window order between A21 and A22 is not directly shown, and the group's outgoing shares rest on about 61–76 observed transitions per activity, so the exact A23-versus-A27 proportions carry sampling uncertainty. An important competing description the evidence cannot rule out is that, instead of the group moving later, A21 and A22 moved earlier relative to a stationary group — the observed order reversals are consistent with both readings. [REL001, REL002, REL004, REL005, REL006, REL007, DIR001, DIR002, DIR003]

Other differences: the choice among the three alternatives shifted somewhat — A14 now occurs in about 30 of 100 traces versus about 38 before, and A20 rose from about 32 to about 37 — a small redistribution within the group that is not part of the placement hypothesis and could reflect sampling variation. [REL001, REL004, REL006]
