### Observed differences

- The relative order of two groups of three activities reversed. In the earlier window, A19, A21 and A24 ran before A25 and A29, which in turn ran before A20, A22 and A23; in the later window, A20, A22 and A23 run first, A25 and A29 next, and A19, A21 and A24 last. All 21 observed activity pairs flip in the same direction, usually from essentially always one order to essentially always the other — for example, A25 preceded A20 in 199 of 200 traces earlier and follows it in 199 of 200 traces later. [REL001–REL021]
- The entry points into the two groups exchanged. Earlier, about 95 in 100 of A40's outgoing transitions went to A19, A21 or A24, and about 99 in 100 of A29's went to A20, A22 or A23; in the later window, A40 sends about 94 in 100 to A20, A22 or A23, and A29 about 99 in 100 to A19, A21 or A24. [DIR007, DIR008]
- The groups' exits swapped as well. Earlier, A19, A21 and A24 led almost exclusively to A25 (97–98 in 100 of outgoing transitions) while each of A20, A22 and A23 sent about 29–37 in 100 transitions to A30 (the rest looping within the group); in the later window, A19, A21 and A24 lead almost exclusively to A30 (95–98 in 100) and the A20/A22/A23 group exits to A25 (27–37 in 100). [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006]
- Typical distances changed accordingly: A40 to A25 stretched from 2 event positions in 95 of 100 cases to 4 positions in 92 of 100, while A29 to A30 shortened from 4 positions (93 of 100) to 2 (98 of 100) — consistent with a three-activity block changing sides between these points. [LAG005, LAG009]
- Participation barely moved: A19 occurs in 60 of 200 traces before versus 66 after, A21 in 70 versus 59, A24 in 73 versus 80, and A20, A22, A23, A25, A29 and A40 occur in nearly every trace in both windows. The difference is therefore one of ordering, not of activities appearing or disappearing. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006, DIR007, DIR008, REL008]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that two three-activity blocks reversed their positions around the middle segment of the process. In the earlier window the flow ran A40 → one of A19/A21/A24 → A25 and A29 → the A20/A22/A23 loop → A30; in the later window it runs A40 → the A20/A22/A23 loop → A25 and A29 → one of A19/A21/A24 → A30. With this reversal, the continuation targets exchanged: A19, A21 and A24 now flow into A30 instead of A25, and A20, A22 and A23 now exit into A25 instead of A30. [DIR001–DIR008, REL001–REL021, LAG005, LAG009]

The support is direct and consistent: the change is shown by relational evidence (all 21 observed pairwise orders reversed, each from essentially 100% one order to essentially 100% the other, not to mixed orders), by the exchanged successor profiles of A40 and A29, and by distance shifts that match a three-activity block changing sides; stable participation of every involved activity rules out the blocks becoming optional, mandatory or repeated differently. The 38 qualifying items also far exceed the roughly 0.64 expected for an unchanged process, so the pattern as a whole is not a sampling artefact. [DIR007, DIR008, REL008, LAG005, LAG009]

Limitations: the order between A25 and A29 and the internal order within each block are not covered, so the exact boundaries of the reversal are uncertain, and a few LAG items rest on only one or two observations and add little weight. A competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that, instead of the two blocks exchanging places, the middle segment A25/A29 and the block exits were rewired in a way that produces the same pairwise orders. [LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004]

Other differences: rare transitions (about 1 in 100 of a source's outgoing transitions or fewer) differ between the windows — for instance, A20 earlier showed rare exits to A05, A07, A08 and A31 that are not observed later, and A40 later shows rare exits to A02, A04, A06 and A10; at such low frequencies these can appear between samples of an unchanged process. [DIR002, DIR008]
