### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, the six activities A33, A35, A36, A37, A38 and A39 each ran in every trace (100% participation, about once per trace, never repeated). In the later window, A33, A35 and A36 each run in only about 47 in 100 traces, and A37, A38 and A39 in about 56 in 100 traces, still about once per trace when they run. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006]
- The six split into two blocks of three. In the later window, A33, A35 and A36 always occur together (co-occurring in about 47 in 100 traces, both absent in about 53 in 100), and A37, A38 and A39 likewise always occur together, in about 55–56 in 100 traces. [REL013, REL014, REL018, REL025, REL026, REL027]
- Activities from different blocks, which earlier co-occurred in essentially every trace (for example A33 before A37 in 99 in 100 traces), now co-occur in only about 3 in 100 traces, each appearing without the other in about 43–54 in 100 traces. [REL015, REL016, REL017, REL020, REL022, REL024]
- Ordering is otherwise preserved: A30 and A31 still run in every trace in both windows; A33/A35/A36 still run after A30 and before A31 whenever they run, and the order within each block remains unfixed (A33 and A35 occur in either order in similar shares). [REL001, REL007, REL013, REL025]
- Routing mirrors the split: earlier, A30's outgoing transitions went almost only to A33 (about 34 in 100), A35 (36) and A36 (28); in the later window they spread across all six activities (A39 about 19, A35 18, A38 17, A37 15, A33 14, A36 12 in 100). A33, A35 and A36 now also lead directly to A31 in about 26–34 in 100 of their outgoing transitions, which was not observed earlier. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004]
- Positional gaps shrink accordingly: the distance from A30 to A37/A38/A39 and from A33/A35/A36 to A31 was 4–5 steps for essentially all earlier occurrences, and is 1–3 steps in the later window. [LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG006, LAG009]
- Cross-block distance items (for example A33–A38, A35–A37) rest on only 4–5 later-window observations and carry little weight. [LAG005, LAG007, LAG008, LAG010, LAG011]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the middle segment between A30 and A31 became optional: in the later window a trace typically executes only one of the two three-activity blocks — about 44 in 100 traces run only A33/A35/A36, about 53 in 100 run only A37/A38/A39, about 3 in 100 still run both, and almost none run neither — whereas earlier every trace ran both blocks in full. When a block runs, its internal (unfixed) order and its placement between A30 and A31 are unchanged; the skipped block explains the halved participation of all six activities, the collapse of cross-block co-occurrence, the new direct transitions A30→A37/A38/A39 and A33/A35/A36→A31, and the shortened 1–3-step gaps. [OCC001, OCC004, REL001, REL015, REL025, DIR001, DIR002, LAG004]

The pattern is consistent across participation, co-occurrence, routing and lag indicators, and 48 items qualified against fewer than 1 expected if nothing had changed, so the pattern as a whole is not what random re-splits of unchanged behaviour typically produce. [OCC003, REL013, DIR003, LAG009]

Limitations: the evidence cannot show why one block is skipped, and it cannot fully distinguish an introduced skip or choice within one behaviour from a changed mix of cases (for example two partially overlapping behaviours replacing the single earlier one), since both produce this trace pattern; the roughly 3 in 100 traces containing both blocks and the cross-block lag items rest on only 4–5 later-window observations. [REL015, LAG005, LAG007]

Other differences: a few rare outgoing transitions from A30, A33 and A35 (each about 1 in 200 transitions) appear only in the later window and look like noise-level variation. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003]
