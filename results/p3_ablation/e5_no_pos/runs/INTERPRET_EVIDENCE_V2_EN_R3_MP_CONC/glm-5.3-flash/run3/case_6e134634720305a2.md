### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, A30, A31 and A36 each occurred about 3.5 times per trace and repeated within a trace in about 75 in 100 cases; in the later window, each occurred about once per trace and repeated in only about 1 in 100 cases. All three still appeared in every trace in both windows. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003]
- In the earlier window, about 70 in 100 of A36's outgoing transitions led back to A30 and about 28 in 100 led to A25; in the later window, about 92 in 100 led to A25 and only about 4 in 100 to A30. [DIR002]
- A30's successor choice hardly moved: A30 was followed by A31 in about 99 in 100 of its outgoing transitions earlier and about 97 in 100 later, but the number of such transitions fell from about 690 to about 210, in line with the drop in repetitions. [DIR001]
- The relationship between A30 and A36 changed from interleaved or repeated to a fixed sequence: earlier the two were interleaved or repeated in about 76 in 100 traces (A30 strictly before A36 in about 25 in 100); in the later window, A30 came before A36 in about 98 in 100 traces, with A36 following A30 about two positions later in about 89 in 100 observed pairs. [REL001, LAG001]
- Short-range loop pairs all but disappeared: A36 followed by A30 within five steps fell from 150 before-window observations to 1, and A36 followed by itself within five steps from 150 to 2; these later counts rest on very few observations. [LAG002, LAG003]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the repeating cycle over A30, A31 and A36 was removed. Earlier, these activities ran roughly 3–4 times per trace in a loop: A36 led back to A30 in about 70 in 100 of its transitions, and A30 and A36 were interleaved or repeated in about 76 in 100 traces. In the later window, each of the three runs once per trace in a fixed order A30 → A31 → … → A36, which then continues to A25 (about 92 in 100 transitions) rather than back to A30 (about 4 in 100). The direct signs are the fall in mean occurrences from about 3.5 to about 1 with recurrence support dropping from about 75 in 100 to about 1 in 100 while participation stayed at 100 in 100 traces, together with the routing shift at A36, the fixed A30-before-A36 sequence with a concentrated gap of about two positions, and the near-disappearance of the A36→A30 and A36→A36 short-range repeats. These items largely describe one underlying difference and are not independent confirmations of each other. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, DIR002, REL001, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003]

Nine items qualified in the control-flow screening against roughly 0.5 expected without change across the 92 tested units, so the set as a whole is unlikely under a no-change scenario. Limitations: the A36→A30 and A36→A36 items rest on only 1 and 2 later-window observations, the direction shares are measured over outgoing transitions rather than cases, and the evidence — which covers only these activities — cannot show whether the dropped repetitions were replaced by other behaviour elsewhere in the trace. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, DIR001, DIR002, REL001, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003]

Other differences: only minor redistributions among the rare outgoing edges of A30 and A36 (individual rare targets each moving by well under 2 in 100 transitions, a few disappearing and a few new ones appearing), consistent with the same reduction in cycle runs rather than a separate change. [DIR001, DIR002]
