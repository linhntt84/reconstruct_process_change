- The clearest difference is a reversal of execution order between two groups of activities. In the earlier window, A19, A21 and A24 (each present in roughly a third of traces) ran before A20, A22 and A23: in pairs where both activities ran, the A19/A21/A24 activity came first in about 95 of 100 cases. ### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the relative execution order of two groups of activities was reversed in the later window: A20, A22 and A23, which previously ran after the middle activities A25 and A29, now run before them, while A19, A21 and A24, which previously ran before A25/A29, now run after them. The connecting flow moved with the reversal: A19/A21/A24 now lead to A30 instead of A25, A20/A22/A23 now lead to A25 instead of A30, A40 now feeds A20/A22/A23, and A29 now feeds A19/A21/A24. This rests on direct relational evidence — all 21 tested activity pairs between the groups reversed their dominant order while co-occurrence stayed essentially unchanged, indicating a change in ordering constraints rather than a participation change — and on consistent successor changes across eight activities. The volume of qualifying evidence (85 items versus roughly 1 expected without a change in the control-flow family) rules out sampling chance as an overall explanation, and each item's difference exceeds those seen in about 995 of 1,000 random re-splits. The evidence cannot rule out that the two windows capture different mixes of process variants rather than a single reordering, and some lag-based details rest on very few observations on one side.
