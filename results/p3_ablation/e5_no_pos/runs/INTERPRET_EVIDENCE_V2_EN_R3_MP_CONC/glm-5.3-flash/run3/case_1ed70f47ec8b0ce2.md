### Observed differences

- Participation is essentially unchanged: A15, A17, A18, A20 and A24 occur in every trace in both windows, while A14 occurs in about 30 of 100 earlier vs 33 of 100 later traces, A16 in about 35 vs 29, and A19 in about 36 vs 39 — no activity appeared, disappeared or became optional. [REL001, REL006, REL007, REL008, REL010, REL016, REL020]
- In the earlier window, all observed outgoing transitions of A14, A16 and A19 (present in about 60, 69 and 71 of 200 earlier traces; about 65, 58 and 77 later) went to A20; in the later window, all of them go to A25. [DIR001, DIR003, DIR006]
- In the earlier window, A15, A17 and A18 each sent roughly 30–38% of their outgoing transitions to A25 and none to A20; in the later window they send roughly 28–37% to A20 and none to A25, while their transitions among themselves kept similar shares (for example A15 to A17 about 40% earlier vs about 31% later). [DIR002, DIR004, DIR005]
- A35 and A24 exchanged successor groups: earlier, A35's transitions split across A14, A16 and A19 (about 30–36% each) and A24's across A15, A17 and A18 (about 30–37% each); later, A35 splits across A15, A17 and A18 (about 31–36% each) and A24 across A14, A16 and A19 (about 29–39% each). [DIR007, DIR008]
- Execution order between the two groups reversed in all 21 tested cross-group pairs while co-occurrence barely moved: A20 ran before A15 in 100% of earlier traces but after it in 100% of later traces, A24 ran before A17 earlier and after it later, and A14 preceded A15 in every trace containing both (about 30 in 100) earlier, whereas later A15 preceded A14 in every such trace (about 33 in 100). [REL001, REL008, REL009, REL015, REL016, REL018, REL019]
- Event distances moved accordingly: A25 followed A24 four event positions later in essentially all earlier traces but two positions later, and A20 followed A35 two positions earlier versus four positions later. [LAG001, LAG002]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that two groups of alternative activities exchanged their places in the flow. In the earlier window, one member of {A14, A16, A19} ran between A35 and the A20/A24 step, and one member of {A15, A17, A18} ran between A24 and A25; in the later window, one member of {A15, A17, A18} runs after A35 while one member of {A14, A16, A19} runs after A24, before A25. Each group is still exercised once per trace with a similar split among its members (the three members' frequencies sum to about 100% of traces in both windows), so the difference is one of placement and ordering, not of participation or path frequency. [DIR001, DIR007, DIR008, LAG001, LAG002, REL001, REL007, REL008]

The signs are direct and consistent: the change is shown by relational and positional evidence rather than frequency shifts alone — every cross-group pair reversed order with co-occurrence stable, the successor sets of A35 and A24 swapped exactly, the blocks' exit targets flipped between A20 and A25, and the step distances A35→A20 and A24→A25 moved from two and four positions to four and two, matching a single rearrangement. The 31 qualifying items far exceed the roughly 0.41 expected for an unchanged process, and each item's difference is larger than in about 995 of 1000 random re-splits; although the items describe one underlying difference rather than independent confirmations, none contradicts it. [DIR002, DIR007, DIR008, LAG001, LAG002, REL008, REL020]

An important competing explanation remains: the evidence cannot distinguish a relocation of the two groups from an equivalent rewiring of the surrounding steps (A20, A24 and the entry/exit points) around stationary groups, since the surrounding skeleton is inferred from successor distributions and step gaps rather than observed end-to-end. [DIR001, DIR007, DIR008, LAG001, LAG002]

Other differences: only minor shifts outside the main hypothesis — A16 appears in about 35% of earlier vs 29% of later traces and A19 in about 36% vs 39%, and some within-trio routing shares moved a few percentage points (for example A15 to A17 from about 40% to about 31%); these are small enough to stem from finite-sample variation. [DIR002, REL006, REL007, REL020]
