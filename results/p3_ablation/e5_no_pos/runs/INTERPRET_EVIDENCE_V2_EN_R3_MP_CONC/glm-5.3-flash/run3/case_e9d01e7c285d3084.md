### Observed differences

- The clearest difference is in how often a group of activities runs. In the earlier window, A30, A31 and A36 each occurred exactly once per trace — present in all 200 traces, about 1.0 occurrence per trace, with repeats in at most about 2 in 100 traces. In the later window, each occurs about 3.5 times per trace, still in every trace, with repeats in about 68 in 100 traces. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003]
- A36's successors changed: earlier, about 97 in 100 of A36's observed outgoing transitions led to A25; in the later window, about 70 in 100 lead to A30 and only about 28 in 100 to A25 (203 outgoing transitions observed earlier versus 697 later). [DIR001]
- The fixed ordering inside the A14–A30–A36 group dissolved. In both windows these activities co-occur in 100% of traces, but earlier the strict orders A14-before-A30, A14-before-A36 and A30-before-A36 each held in about 98–99 in 100 traces; later each holds in only about 32 in 100, while interleaved or repeated orderings rose from about 1–2 in 100 to about 68 in 100. - The tight spacing between A30 and A36 disappeared: earlier the pair sat two event positions apart in about 98 in 100 cases; later that dropped to about 12 in 100, with the remaining pairs spread over larger positional gaps. [LAG002]
- A05's ordering with A30 and A36 loosened as well: A05 co-occurs with each of them in about 52–55 in 100 traces in both windows, but A05-first dropped from about 53–54 in 100 to about 16 in 100, with interleaved or repeated orderings rising to about 35 in 100. [REL001, REL002]
- The spacing between A05 and A14 shifted: immediate succession (one position apart) fell from about 36 in 100 to about 17 in 100, while three positions apart rose from about 28 to about 45 in 100 (109 versus 103 observed pairs). [LAG001]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that in the later window A30, A31 and A36 — previously executed exactly once per trace in a fixed sequence, with A14 ahead of them and A25 typically following A36 — are now executed about 3.5 times per trace: A36's transitions most often lead to A30 instead of A25, the strict ordering between A14, A30 and A36 gave way to interleaved or repeated orderings (fixed order in about 32 of 100 later traces versus about 98–99 earlier), and the former tight A30–A36 adjacency is gone. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, DIR001, REL003, REL004, REL005, LAG002]

The repetition change is shown directly: participation stays at 100% of traces in both windows while mean occurrences rise from about 1.0 to about 3.5 and repeat involvement from near zero to about 68 in 100, so the activities did not become optional — they run more often. The ordering change rests on direct relational and successor evidence, not on occurrence counts alone. Eleven items qualified against roughly 0.5 expected for an unchanged process (more than twenty times as many), and all items point in the same direction. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, DIR001, REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, LAG001, LAG002]

Limitations: all items come from the control-flow family and largely describe one underlying difference, so their agreement is not independent confirmation [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, DIR001, REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, LAG001, LAG002]. A31 is covered only by the occurrence evidence, so its position within the repeated block is unobserved [OCC002]. A competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that a new repeating fragment elsewhere re-invokes these activities, rather than the change lying inside the A30–A36 group itself; the observed items would look the same under either account [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, DIR001, REL003, REL004, REL005].

Other differences: the loosened ordering between A05 and A30/A36 [REL001, REL002] and the larger A05–A14 spacing [LAG001] are consistent with the same repeated executions being interleaved with A05, but A05 co-occurs with the block in only about half the traces, so the evidence does not tie it into the main hypothesis.
