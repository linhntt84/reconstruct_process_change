### Observed differences

- The main difference is a swap in which of two alternative activity groups runs. In the earlier window, A16, A17 and A18 each appeared in about 75 in 100 traces (150 of 200), while A19, A20 and A21 each appeared in about 25 in 100 (50 of 200); in the later window this reversed, with A16–A18 down to about 25 in 100 (49 of 200) and A19–A21 up to about 76 in 100 (151 of 200). None of the six activities repeats within a trace in either window. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006]
- Routing out of A15 moved with this swap. Before, about three quarters of A15's observed outgoing transitions went to A16, A17 or A18 (roughly 27%, 23% and 26%) and about a quarter to A19, A20 or A21; in the later window only about a quarter went to A16–A18 (about 8–9% each) while about three quarters went to A19–A21 (about 25%, 22% and 29%). [DIR001]
- The two groups behave as alternatives in both windows. Before, A16, A17 and A18 typically ran together with A15 (about 75 in 100 traces, always after A15) and never shared a trace with A19–A21; in the later window A19–A21 typically ran together with A15 (about 76 in 100, again after A15) while A16–A18 did so in only about 25 in 100. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, REL009, REL014, REL018]
- Within-group order also flipped. Before, A16, A17 and A18 co-occurred in about 75 in 100 traces with both orders about equally frequent (no fixed order); in the later window they rarely share a trace (neither present in about 76 in 100). A19, A20 and A21 went from rarely together (about 25 in 100) to together in about 76 in 100, still without a fixed order between them (both orders roughly 34–42%). [REL007, REL013, REL022, REL023, REL025]
- A25 ran in essentially every trace in both windows, but its company switched: before it usually co-occurred with A16, A17 or A18 (about 75 in 100), in the later window usually with A19, A20 or A21 (about 76 in 100), in mixed orders. [REL012, REL024, REL027]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that, in the later window, two existing alternative paths after A15 are taken with different frequencies: the path through A16, A17 and A18 (executed together, in mixed order) fell from about 75 in 100 cases to about 25 in 100, while the path through A19, A20 and A21 rose from about 25 to about 76 in 100. Both paths, A15 and A25 were well observed in both windows, no activity was added or removed, none of the activities repeats, and A15 still precedes both groups, so this reads as a shift in path frequencies rather than a replacement or bypass of a group. A25 continued in essentially all cases and now co-occurs with the A19–A21 group instead of the A16–A18 group. The signs are direct and mutually consistent — participation counts, A15's outgoing-transition shares and the pairwise relations all move in the same direction — and 35 control-flow items qualified against roughly 0.49 expected if nothing had changed, with every item's difference exceeding about 995 of 1,000 random re-splits of the pooled data. [OCC001, OCC004, DIR001, REL001, REL004, REL009, REL022, REL024]

Limitations: the many items largely describe one underlying difference from different angles rather than independent confirmations, and the evidence cannot rule out that the mix of cases entering the two windows shifted (two coexisting behaviours with different weights) rather than the routing itself. [DIR001, REL001, REL009]

Other differences: among traces containing both A15 and A20, the position gap narrowed — gaps of 1–2 events rose from about 48 in 100 to about 66 in 100, while gaps of 3 events fell from about 52 to about 34 in 100 (based on 50 and 151 traces); this concerns event positions, not clock time. [LAG001]
