### Observed differences

- The order between the activity group A30/A31/A36 and A21 reversed: in the earlier window A21 came before each of A30, A31 and A36 in 200 of 200 traces; in the later window each of them came before A21 in about 199 of 200 traces. Both activities still co-occurred in every trace in both windows, so participation did not change — only the order did. [REL001, REL002, REL003]
- The same reversal occurred between the group and A22: A22 preceded all three group members in essentially every earlier trace, while in the later window all three preceded A22 in about 199 of 200 traces, again with unchanged co-occurrence. [REL004, REL005, REL006]
- A20's routing changed: earlier, all outgoing transitions from A20 went to A22; in the later window they spread roughly equally over A30, A31 and A36 (about one third each), with a direct transition to A22 almost gone (about 1 in 200). [DIR001]
- A21's routing changed: earlier, A21 led to A30, A31 and A36 in roughly equal shares (about 38, 34 and 28 in 100 outgoing transitions); in the later window it led almost always directly to A25 (about 199 of 200). [DIR002]
- The group members' exits changed accordingly: earlier, A30, A31 and A36 each led to the other group members and to A25 (A25 roughly 28–37 in 100); in the later window A25 is nearly absent as a successor (about 1 in 200) and A22 appears at about 31–34 in 100. [DIR003, DIR004, DIR005]
- Position gaps shifted consistently: A22 moved from directly after A20 (gap 1 in all 200 earlier traces) to four positions after A20 (gap 4 in about 199 of 200 later traces), and A25 moved from four positions after A21 to directly after A21. [LAG001, LAG002]
- Weaker items: earlier, the distances from A21 to each group member and from each group member to A25 varied between one and three positions; the later window contains only one observation for each of these pairs (distances of one to three steps), consistent with the new arrangement but based on very few cases. [LAG003, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006, LAG007, LAG008]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a reordering of the control flow around the group A30, A31 and A36. In the earlier window, a case ran A20 directly into A22, while A21 preceded the group A30/A31/A36, which in turn preceded A25 (A25 followed A21 by four positions with the group in between). In the later window, the group runs directly after A20 and before both A22 and A21, A22 follows the group instead of A20, and A21 leads straight to A25. Nineteen control-flow items qualified in screening, where about 0.38 would be expected by chance alone, so the set of differences cannot be explained as random sampling variation. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006, LAG007, LAG008]

Supporting signs: the six order relations flip almost completely (from 200 of 200 one way to about 199 of 200 the other way) while co-occurrence stays at 100%, so every involved activity still occurs in essentially all traces and only the order changed; the successor profiles of A20, A21 and the group members shift exactly as this move predicts; and the A20→A22 and A21→A25 position gaps swap (1 to 4, and 4 to 1). Each difference is larger than in about 995 of 1000 random re-splits of the pooled data. The changes are near-total replacements of successors rather than gradual shifts in how often existing paths are taken, which fits a change in ordering rather than a change in path frequencies. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, DIR001, DIR002, LAG001, LAG002]

Limitations: several items describe the same underlying move (for example, the three order flips per counterpart are one behavioural change seen three times), so they are not independent confirmations. The relative order of A21 and A22 after the group was not measured, so the exact sequence following the group — whether A22 or A21 comes first, and where A25 sits relative to A22 — remains open. The distance items inside the moved group rest on a single later-window observation each and add little weight.
