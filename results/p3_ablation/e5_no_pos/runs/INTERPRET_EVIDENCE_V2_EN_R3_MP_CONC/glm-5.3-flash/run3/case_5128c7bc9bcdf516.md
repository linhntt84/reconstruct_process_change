### Observed differences

- In the earlier window the activities A26, A17, A15, A18, A19, A21, A20 and A27 — each present in all 200 traces of both windows — had no fixed sequence: every one of them was followed by several different activities. For example, A15 led to A18 in about 62 in 100 cases but to A19, A21 or A20 in about 12–14 in 100 each, and A26 split roughly 50/50 between A19 and A17. [DIR001, DIR003, DIR007]
- In the later window each of these activities has essentially a single successor, forming the fixed chain A26 → A17 → A15 → A18 → A19 → A21 → A20 → A27; every link along it is taken in about 995–1000 of 1000 cases, whereas the corresponding dominant shares before ranged only from about 47 to 66 in 100. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006, DIR007]
- Pairwise order flipped or became fixed: A18 preceded A19 in only about 12 in 100 cases before versus about 995 of 1000 after; A15 preceded A19 in about 1 in 4 before versus about 995 of 1000 after; A18 preceded A21 in about 3 in 10 before versus about 995 of 1000 after; and pairs that were previously even, such as A17/A19 and A15/A21 (both about 50/50), now always occur in the chain order. [REL001, REL003, REL004, REL006, REL008]
- Position gaps converged on the exact chain distances: consecutive pairs became immediate (A17→A15 at gap 1 rose from about 56 in 100 cases to about 995 of 1000; A15→A18 from about 62 to about 995 of 1000; A19→A21 from about 56 to about 995 of 1000; A20→A27 from about 47 to about 995 of 1000), and longer-range pairs now sit at exactly the distance the chain implies — A15 reaches A20 four positions later in about 995 of 1000 cases (before, spread over gaps 1–4) and A26 reaches A21 five positions later in about 995 of 1000 (before, spread over gaps 2–5). Several pairs were also often more than five positions apart before: A15→A19 appeared within five steps in only about 51 of 200 earlier traces, against 199 of 200 later. [LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG006, LAG017, LAG020, LAG022, LAG029]
- Participation did not change: in both windows every one of these activity pairs co-occurred in 100 in 100 traces, so the difference concerns the order of execution, not whether the activities run. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, REL007, REL008]
- A handful of items showing the reverse order in the later window (for example A19 before A15, or A20 before A18) rest on a single observation each and carry little weight. [LAG014, LAG015, LAG019, LAG021, LAG028]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the ordering constraints among A26, A17, A15, A18, A19, A21, A20 and A27 changed: in the earlier window these activities appeared in varying orders with no fixed sequence between most pairs, while in the later window they execute in one fixed sequence, A26 → A17 → A15 → A18 → A19 → A21 → A20 → A27, with consecutive activities immediately adjacent. [DIR001, DIR003, REL001, REL006, LAG001, LAG025]

The support is direct and consistent: the relational items show the order change itself, the successor and gap items agree with the same chain in every pair examined, 44 control-flow items qualified against roughly 0.43 expected if nothing had changed (about 100 times the chance expectation), and every item's difference exceeds about 199 of 200 random re-splits of the pooled data, with supports of roughly 200 traces per window. [REL001, REL006, DIR001, DIR007, LAG001, LAG024]

Limitations: the indicators measure event positions rather than clock time, so the evidence shows sequence only; and it cannot distinguish a genuine tightening of the ordering from a drastic shift in the mix of existing variants toward one dominant sequence, since both would produce the same aggregate picture — the reverse-order items resting on single observations do not resolve this. [LAG014, LAG015, LAG019, LAG021, LAG028]
