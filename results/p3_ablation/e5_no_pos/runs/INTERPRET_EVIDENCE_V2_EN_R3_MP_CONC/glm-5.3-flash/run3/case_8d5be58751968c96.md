### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, the six activities A33, A35, A36, A37, A38 and A39 each ran in every trace (participation 100 in 100, about one occurrence per trace). In the later window, each of them runs in only about 47–57 in 100 traces, still at most once per trace (mean occurrences about 0.47–0.57). [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006]
- The six split into two groups of three that have become mutually exclusive. Before, every pair across the groups (A33/A35/A36 with A37/A38/A39) co-occurred in 100 in 100 traces, always with the first group before the second; in the later window, these cross-group pairs co-occur in only about 2–4 in 100 traces, while in about 44 in 100 traces only the first-group activity runs and in about 53 in 100 only the second-group one. [REL015, REL016, REL017, REL019, REL020, REL021, REL022, REL023, REL024]
- Within each group, behaviour is unchanged: A33, A35 and A36 still run together (about 47 in 100 later traces, essentially every trace containing any of them) in no fixed order among themselves, and A37, A38 and A39 still run together (about 56 in 100) in no fixed order. [REL013, REL014, REL018, REL025, REL026, REL027]
- The surrounding frame is stable: A30 and A31 still occur in every trace in both windows, the six activities never run without A30, A30 still precedes them whenever they run, and they still precede A31. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, REL007, REL008, REL009, REL010, REL011, REL012]
- Routing and spacing follow the new exclusivity. Before, A30 led directly only to A33, A35 and A36 (about 34%, 36% and 29% of its outgoing transitions); in the later window, about 15–20% of its outgoing transitions go directly to each of A37, A38 and A39, and A33, A35 and A36 — which never led directly to A31 before — now do so in about 24–34% of their outgoing transitions. A37, A38 and A39 now follow A30 within 1–3 event positions in about 95 in 100 pair observations (before: always 4–5 positions), and A31 now follows A33, A35 and A36 within 1–3 positions (before: 4–5). [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG007, LAG010]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the block of A33, A35 and A36 and the block of A37, A38 and A39, which in the earlier window both always executed in sequence between A30 and A31, became mutually exclusive alternatives in the later window: a case now runs A30, then either the first block (about 47 in 100 cases) or the second block (about 56 in 100 cases), then A31, with both blocks still running together in only about 2–4 in 100 cases. This rests on direct relational evidence — cross-block pairs collapsed from universal co-occurrence with a fixed order to near-zero co-occurrence, while co-occurrence within each block and the participation and order of A30 and A31 stayed intact — and the halved participation of all six activities matches the same picture. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, REL013, REL014, REL015, REL016, REL017, REL018, REL019, REL020, REL021, REL022, REL023, REL024, REL025, REL026, REL027]

The surrounding routing corroborates this reading: A30 gained direct transitions into the second block, the first-block activities gained direct transitions to A31, and the affected event-position distances shortened, exactly as expected when one of the two blocks is not executed in a case. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG007, LAG010]

Screening tested 151 control-flow units and 49 items qualified, far above the fewer than 1 expected by chance when nothing has changed, so the volume of evidence cannot be explained by sampling noise; each item's difference also exceeds about 995 in 1,000 random re-splits of the pooled data. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, REL007, REL008, REL009, REL010, REL011, REL012, REL013, REL014, REL015, REL016, REL017, REL018, REL019, REL020, REL021, REL022, REL023, REL024, REL025, REL026, REL027, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006, LAG007, LAG008, LAG009, LAG010, LAG011, LAG012]

The evidence cannot show what determines which block a case takes, and the roughly 2–4 in 100 later traces in which both blocks run could be residual cases still following the old always-both behaviour, so a small share of the old pattern may persist; six further lag items on cross-block pairs rest on only about 4–5 later-window observations and were not used. [REL015, REL022, REL024, LAG005, LAG006, LAG008, LAG009, LAG011, LAG012]
