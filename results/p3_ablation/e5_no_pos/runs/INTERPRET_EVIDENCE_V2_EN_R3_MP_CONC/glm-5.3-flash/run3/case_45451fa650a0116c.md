### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the branch executed after A20 changed: earlier it ran through A24, A25 and A26 — usually together, in no fixed order, preceded by A20 — and then through A34 to A06 and on to A35; in the later window it runs through A21, A22 and A23 (together, in no fixed order, preceded by A20 in about 77 in 100 of their traces) with A34 passing almost directly to A35, so that A06, which earlier sat between A34 and A35 in roughly 42–44 in 100 traces, is now skipped in most traces. Both branches existed in both windows (the newer one in about 19 in 100 earlier traces, the older one in about 21 in 100 later traces); what changed is which dominates, together with the near-disappearance of A06 from the A34→A35 tail. [DIR005, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, OCC007, OCC008, OCC009, REL010, REL011, REL012, REL013, REL014, REL015, REL016, DIR006, LAG006]

This goes beyond what an unchanged process typically produces: 70 control-flow items qualified where about 0.84 would be expected by chance, and every item's difference exceeds about 199 of 200 random re-splits of the pooled data. Participation, routing, relational and event-distance indicators all point to the same flip, and the two trios remain mutually exclusive in both windows, which fits an exchange between existing branches rather than the appearance of new activities. The evidence cannot rule out that the two windows simply sample different mixes of long-standing process variants rather than a change at this point in time, and parts of the later-window picture rest on few observations (A06 in 24 later traces; some event-distance items on 1–7 observations).
