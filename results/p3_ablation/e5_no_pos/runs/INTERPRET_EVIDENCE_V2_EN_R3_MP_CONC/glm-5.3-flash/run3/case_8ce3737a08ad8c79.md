### Observed differences

- The dominant difference is a reversal between two alternative three-activity blocks that both follow A20. In the earlier window, about 82% of A20's outgoing transitions went to A24 (29%), A26 (29%) or A25 (24%), and only about 19% went to A21–A23; in the later window, about 79% go to A21 (29%), A22 (21%) or A23 (29%) and about 21% to A24–A26. [DIR003]
- Trace participation swapped accordingly: in the later window, A21, A22 and A23 each appear in about 79 in 100 traces (about 19 in 100 before), while A24, A25 and A26 each appear in about 21 in 100 (about 82 in 100 before). All six remain essentially single occurrences per trace in both windows; none disappeared and none started repeating. [OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, OCC007]
- The blocks' internal character is unchanged in kind: A21–A23 now occur together in the same trace in about 79 in 100 cases (about 19 before), in both orders in similar shares, i.e. without a fixed order among them; A24–A26 co-occurred in about 81 in 100 traces before (also in mixed order) and now in about 21 in 100. [REL015, REL016, REL023, REL036, REL037, REL041]
- A20's precedence follows the active block: A20 precedes A21, A22 or A23 in about 77–79 in 100 later-window traces (about 18–19 before), and precedes A24–A26 in about 21 in 100 (about 81 before). [REL009, REL010, REL011, REL012, REL013, REL014]
- The two blocks stay mutually exclusive with roles reversed: before, A24–A26 ran without A21–A23 in about 81 in 100 traces (and vice versa in about 18–19); in the later window this is about 21 vs about 79. [REL017, REL018, REL019, REL024, REL025, REL026, REL030, REL031, REL032]
- Downstream co-occurrence moved with the swap: A30 now co-occurs with A21–A23 in about 79 in 100 traces (about 18–19 before) and with A24–A26 in about 21 (about 81–82 before); A32 and A33 now co-occur with A21–A23 in about 55 in 100 (about 9 before) and typically occur without A24–A26. These items largely re-express the same underlying swap rather than confirming it independently. [REL020, REL027, REL033, REL038, REL042, REL045, REL021, REL022, REL028, REL029, REL034, REL035, REL039, REL040, REL043, REL044, REL046, REL047]
- A separate, weaker difference concerns A10: it appears in fewer traces (roughly 17 in 100 before vs about 7 after; outgoing-transition observations fall from about 33 to 14), and its outflow changed from almost exclusively A33 (about 97% of outgoing transitions) to a spread over A15 (about 21%), A16, A27, A33 and A34 (about 14% each) and others; A32/A33 now usually occur without A10 (about 65 in 100 vs about 44). [DIR002, REL007, REL008]
- A02 became somewhat more frequent (about 2 vs about 9 in 100 traces) and its outgoing transitions redistributed from mainly A11, A20 and A01 to about ten different targets; the position gap from A32 to A10 also spread from mostly one position apart to one-to-three. Both observations rest on very few cases (4–33 before, 4–18 after). [OCC001, DIR001, LAG001]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the two alternative paths after A20 swapped frequencies: in the later window, traces predominantly execute A21, A22 and A23 together, in varying order after A20 (about 79 in 100 cases), where they previously predominantly executed A24, A25 and A26 (about 82 in 100, now about 21 in 100). [DIR003, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, OCC007]

This is shown directly by the routing shares at A20 with full support in both windows, and consistently mirrored by the participation swap, A20's precedence relations, the within-block co-occurrence patterns and the reversed mutual exclusivity between the blocks; all six activities occur in substantial shares in both windows with unchanged repetition and order-mixing, so this reads as a change in how often existing paths are taken rather than activities being added, removed or reordered. The screening context reinforces the judgement: 59 items qualified against roughly 0.84 expected without change, and every item's difference exceeds about 995 of 1000 random re-splits, so the ensemble is not what an unchanged process typically produces. Limitations: the many relation items largely re-express one underlying trace behaviour and are not independent confirmations, and the evidence cannot rule out that the two windows simply captured a different mix of case types (for example a shifted intake of cases taking the A21–A23 path) rather than a modified routing rule. [REL009, REL010, REL011, REL012, REL013, REL014, REL015, REL016, REL023, REL036, REL037, REL041, REL017, REL018, REL019, REL024, REL025, REL026, REL030, REL031, REL032]

Other differences: A10 occurs in fewer traces and its successors spread from almost only A33 to many different activities, with A32/A33 now typically occurring without A10, and A02 became somewhat more frequent with a redistributed outflow — both rest on few later-window observations and are not part of the main hypothesis.
