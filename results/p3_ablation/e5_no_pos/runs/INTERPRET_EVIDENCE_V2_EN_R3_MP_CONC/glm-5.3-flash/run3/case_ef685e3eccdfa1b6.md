### Observed differences

- Before, six activities ran in every single case: A28, A30 and A31 as a first block, then A32, A33 and A34 as a second block, framed by A25 before and A26 after. In the later window, A28, A30 and A31 each appear in about 47 in 100 traces, and A32, A33 and A34 each in about 56 in 100; neither block repeats within a trace in either window. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006]
- The two blocks, which always ran together before, became almost mutually exclusive: cross-block pairs such as A28 and A32 co-occurred in 100 in 100 traces before (first block always earlier) but in only about 2 in 100 in the later window. In the later window about 45 in 100 traces contain only the first block, about 53 in 100 only the second, and about 2 in 100 both. [REL015, REL016, REL017, REL019, REL020, REL021, REL022, REL023, REL024]
- Within each block nothing changed: the three activities still always occur together when the block runs, and still in no fixed order (for example, A28 before A30 in about 52 in 100 of the earlier traces and about 46 in 100 of the later ones where both run). [REL013, REL014, REL018, REL025, REL026, REL027]
- In the later window A25 branches to all six activities in roughly equal shares (about 14–20 in 100 of its outgoing transitions each), whereas before it led only to A28, A30 and A31 (about 30–37 in 100 each). A28, A30 and A31 now lead directly to A26 in about 26–35 in 100 of their outgoing transitions — an edge not observed before — while their transitions into the second block nearly vanished (for example A28 to A32 fell from about 10 in 100 to about 1 in 100). - A25 still precedes and A26 still follows whichever block runs, and both still occur in every trace. Distances shrank accordingly: A25 to A32, A33 or A34 fell from 4–5 event positions to 1–3, and A28, A30 or A31 to A26 likewise from 4–5 positions to 1–3. ### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the two consecutive three-activity blocks between A25 and A26 became mutually exclusive alternative branches. Before, every case executed A25, then the block A28–A30–A31 in any internal order, then the block A32–A33–A34 in any internal order, then A26. In the later window, about 45 in 100 cases run only the first block, about 53 in 100 only the second, and about 2 in 100 both; A25 branches directly into whichever block runs, and A28, A30 and A31 lead directly to A26 when the second block is skipped. [OCC001, OCC004, REL015, DIR001, DIR002, DIR004, LAG001, LAG004]

The support is direct and consistent: participation of all six block activities dropped by roughly half while A25 and A26 kept running in every case; cross-block co-occurrence collapsed from 100 to about 2 in 100 while within-block co-occurrence and mixed internal order persisted; new direct transitions appeared exactly where the removed block used to sit; and position distances shrank from 4–5 to 1–3 steps, as expected when one block is skipped. Screening produced 50 qualifying items against about 0.59 expected for an unchanged process, and every item exceeds about 99 in 100 random re-splits, so chance sampling cannot account for the pattern as a whole. [REL015, REL013, DIR002, LAG004, screening]

The evidence cannot show where the choice between the two blocks is made or what drives it: an unobserved decision upstream of A25, or a different mix of cases entering the later window, would look identical at this level. The rare traces containing both blocks rest on only about four later-window observations in the lag data. [REL015, LAG005]

Other differences: none beyond this restructuring. The remaining lag items concern the rare both-block traces and are too weakly supported (about four later-window observations each) to characterise their internal behaviour. [LAG005, LAG006, LAG007, LAG009, LAG010, LAG012, LAG013]
