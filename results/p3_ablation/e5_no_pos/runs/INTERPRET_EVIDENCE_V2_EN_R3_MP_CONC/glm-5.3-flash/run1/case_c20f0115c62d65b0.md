### Observed differences

- Three activities — A19, A20 and A25 — were regular but optional steps in the earlier window: A19 appeared in about 32 in 100 traces, A20 in about 28, and A25 in about 40 (never repeated within a trace). In the later window each appears in only about 1 in 200 traces. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003]

- In the earlier window these three were mutually exclusive alternatives: for example, A19 alone in about 32 in 100 traces, A20 alone in about 28, neither in about 40, and never both together; the same held for the other pairs. In the later window, essentially no trace contains any of them (about 99 in 100 contain neither member of each pair). [REL001, REL002, REL006]

- In the earlier window, whenever one of the three occurred, it always co-occurred with A27, A28 and A29 (co-occurrence equal to each activity's participation, e.g. 32 in 100 for A19 with each partner), and A29 followed it in essentially every such trace. In the later window A27, A28 and A29 still occur in essentially every trace, but without any of the three (about 995 in 1000 traces show the partner alone). [REL003, REL004, REL005, REL007, REL008, REL009, REL010, REL011, REL012]

- Routing into and out of the block disappeared: A27's outgoing transitions previously went to A28 in about 53 in 100 cases and to A25, A19 and A20 in about 20, 13 and 10; in the later window about 97 in 100 go directly to A28. A28 previously led to A29 in about 45 in 100 cases and to A25, A19 and A20 in about 19, 18 and 17; in the later window about 98 in 100 lead directly to A29. [DIR004, DIR005]

- Position distances tightened accordingly: the span from A27 to A29 was mostly three event positions in the earlier window (about 94 in 100) and is mostly two positions in the later window (about 97 in 100); A27→A28 and A28→A29, previously split between one and two positions apart, are now almost always immediately consecutive (about 98 in 100 each). [LAG001, LAG002, LAG003]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the block of three alternative activities — A19, A20 and A25, which in the earlier window executed in roughly three to four in ten cases, always as mutually exclusive choices sitting between A27/A28 and A29 — is no longer executed in the later window, with the flow now running directly from A27 to A28 to A29. This reads as a removal or bypass of activities rather than a re-weighting of surviving alternatives, because the activities involved are nearly absent in the later window and A27 and A28 now lead directly past where the block used to sit. The signs are direct and consistent: the participation collapse, the loss of all co-occurrences with A27/A28/A29, the redirection of A27's and A28's outgoing transitions, and the shortening of the A27→A29 span from mostly three positions to two, each measured on about 200 traces per window. The screening context also fits: 23 control-flow items qualified against well under one expected without change, so the ensemble is far beyond what random re-splits of a stable process produce, even though many items describe the same underlying difference. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, DIR004, DIR005, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, REL003, REL005, REL010]

An important competing explanation remains: the evidence shows that A19, A20 and A25 were not exercised in the later sample, but not whether they were formally removed or their triggering condition simply stopped occurring; zero participation only means the activities were not observed. The three residual later-window occurrences cannot settle this, since each shows a single outgoing transition to a successor not seen in the earlier window, based on one observation apiece. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003]

Other differences: the later-window routing of A27 includes several marginal new targets (about 0.5 in 100 each), and the rare residual executions of A19, A20 and A25 each continue to a previously unseen successor (A44, A08, A42); with one observation each, neither signal can be characterised further. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004]
