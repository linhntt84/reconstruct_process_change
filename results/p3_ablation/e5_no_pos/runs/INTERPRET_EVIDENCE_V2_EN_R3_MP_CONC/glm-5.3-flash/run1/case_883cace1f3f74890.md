### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, A27, A28 and A29 formed an unbroken backbone: each appeared in essentially every trace, A27 passed to A28 in about 99 in 100 of its outgoing transitions and A28 to A29 in about 99 in 100, and both pairs were adjacent (one position apart) in about 99 in 100 traces. [REL003, REL005, DIR004, DIR005, LAG001, LAG003]

- A19, A20 and A25 did not occur at all in the earlier window (0 of 200 traces). In the later window, A20 runs in about 41 in 100 traces and A19 and A25 in about 30 in 100 each, essentially once per trace. [OCC002, OCC003, OCC004]

- The three are mutually exclusive alternatives: any two of them share a trace in at most about 1 in 200 later traces, and their shares complement one another so that virtually every later trace contains exactly one of the three. [REL001, REL002, REL006]

- The chosen activity sits inside the old chain: A27 precedes it in essentially every shared trace (e.g. A27 before A20 in about 40 in 100 traces, never the reverse), it precedes A29 in essentially every shared trace, while its order against A28 is mixed (for A19: before A28 in about 16 in 100 traces, after A28 in about 13 in 100). [REL003, REL004, REL005, REL007, REL008, REL009, REL010, REL011, REL012]

- Successor profiles shifted accordingly: in the later window A27 sends about 44% of its outgoing flow to A28 (previously about 99%) and roughly 15–22% to each of A19, A20 and A25; A28 sends about 55% to A29 (previously about 99%) and roughly 12–17% to each of the three; the new activities themselves pass on to A28 in about 53–56% of their outgoing transitions and directly to A29 in about 42–46%. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005]

- Position gaps moved as one added step implies: A27→A28 is adjacent in only about 44 in 100 later traces (about 55 in 100 at distance two), A28→A29 is adjacent in about 56 in 100 (about 43 in 100 at distance two), and A27→A29 moved from distance two (about 99 in 100 earlier traces) to distance three (97 in 100). [LAG001, LAG002, LAG003]

- Other: A10 became more frequent, from about 5 in 100 traces earlier to about 13–14 in 100 later. [OCC001]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that, in the later window, a choice among three mutually exclusive activities — A19, A20 and A25 — is executed inside the previously rigid sequence A27 → A28 → A29: virtually every case now runs exactly one of the three (A20 in about 41 in 100 cases, A19 and A25 in about 30 in 100 each), placed between A27 and A28 in roughly 55 in 100 later cases and between A28 and A29 in roughly 43 in 100, breaking the formerly immediate successions A27→A28 and A28→A29 in most cases. [OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, REL001, REL006, DIR004, DIR005, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003]

This reading is backed by the screening context and by direct, consistent relational evidence: 24 control-flow items qualified against roughly 0.51 expected for an unchanged process, and occurrence, successor, relation and gap indicators all show the same picture — participation rises from zero to 30–41%, A27 consistently precedes the new activities and they consistently precede A29, and the gap distributions shift by exactly the one step an added activity implies. [OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, REL003, REL005, REL009, REL012, DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, LAG002]

Limitations: zero participation in the earlier window shows only that A19, A20 and A25 were not observed there, so an occurrence below the detection threshold cannot be excluded, and the evidence cannot show what determines which of the three alternatives a case takes. The main competing explanation — rare-variant sampling noise in finite windows — is implausible given the number of qualifying items and their consistency, but it cannot be fully ruled out. [OCC002, OCC003, OCC004]

Other differences: A10's participation rose from about 5 in 100 traces to about 13–14 in 100; this increase is not part of the hypothesis above. [OCC001]
