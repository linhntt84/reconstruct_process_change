### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, A25, A26 and A31 each occurred exactly once in every case (participation in 100 in 100 cases, no repetitions). In the later window they still occur in every case, but about 3.5 times per case on average, and they recur within a case in roughly 68 in 100 cases. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003]

- The flow out of A31 changed: earlier, A31 was always followed by A20; in the later window, about 71 in 100 of A31's observed outgoing transitions lead to A25 and about 29 in 100 to A20. [DIR001]

- The relationship between A25 and A31 changed from a fixed sequence to a mixed one: earlier the two always co-occurred with A25 before A31; in the later window they still always co-occur, but that fixed order holds in only about 32 in 100 cases, while in about 68 in 100 cases the pair is interleaved or repeated. [REL001]

- The tight spacing of the pair disappeared: earlier, A31 followed A25 two positions later in essentially all cases; in the later window that two-step gap appears in only about 12 in 100 cases, with about 5 in 100 at a five-step gap and the rest not at a fixed small gap. [LAG001]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that, in the later window, the activities A25, A26 and A31 are executed repeatedly within cases, whereas earlier each ran exactly once per case in a fixed sequence (A25 before A31, with A31 always flowing to A20). Recurrence within a case rose from never to about 68 in 100 cases while participation stayed at 100 in 100, so the activities did not become optional — they began repeating. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003] The repetition is coupled between A25 and A31: A31 now typically flows back to A25 rather than to A20, which is consistent with a repeated cycle through these activities. [DIR001] Consistently, the A25–A31 pair shifted from a fixed A25-before-A31 order to interleaved-or-repeated in the same share of cases (about 68 in 100), and the previous two-step spacing of the pair largely vanished. [REL001, LAG001] Six control-flow items qualified against roughly 0.29 expected if nothing had changed (58 units tested), and every item's difference exceeds about 99.5 in 100 random re-splits of the pooled data, so the set of differences is unlikely under a no-change assumption. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, DIR001, REL001, LAG001]

Limitations: the six items likely reflect one underlying behaviour, so they are consistent rather than independent confirmations. A26's role in the repeated block rests only on its occurrence profile, as no relational or directional item covers it. [OCC002] The evidence also does not show how many repetitions a case undergoes or whether the cycle always terminates the same way. An important competing explanation that cannot be ruled out is that the later window mixes two case populations — some following the earlier single-pass flow and others a repeated flow — which these items cannot distinguish from a uniform change in behaviour.
