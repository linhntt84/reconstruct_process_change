### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that, in the later window, the block A14 → A20 → A15 between A36 and A21 is bypassed in roughly 47 in 100 cases: A36 is followed immediately by A21 and the three activities are not executed, whereas before they ran in every case in the fixed order A36 → A14 → A20 → A15 → A21. In other words, A14, A15 and A20 appear to have become optional as a group rather than mandatory. The signs are consistent and direct: the participation of all three dropped in step from 100% to about 53% [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003]; A36 gained a direct transition to A21 that was not observed among its 200 earlier outgoing transitions [DIR001]; the relational profiles show the three activities jointly absent while A36 and A21 still run, rather than being dropped individually [REL001, REL002, REL005, REL003, REL006, REL008]; and the A36-to-A21 gap split between 4 and 1 positions matches the full block versus the direct move [LAG001]. Fourteen items qualified against fewer than 1 expected by chance (0.33 of 66 units tested), so the pattern as a whole is not what an unchanged process typically produces.

Limitations: the evidence cannot rule out that the direct A36 → A21 path existed before as a very rare variant (below roughly 1–2 in 100 cases) that the earlier 200-trace window did not show, and the bypass share of about 47 in 100 is an estimate from finite samples of 200 traces per window.

Other differences: none observed — every qualifying item describes the same bypass behaviour around A36, A14, A15, A20 and A21. [OCC001, DIR001, REL001, LAG001]
