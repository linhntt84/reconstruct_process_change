### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, A30, A31 and A36 each occurred exactly once in essentially every trace (participation 100 in 100 traces, about 1.0 occurrences per trace, virtually never repeated). In the later window, all three still appear in every trace but average about 3.5 occurrences per trace, and about 68 in 100 traces contain at least one repeat of each. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003]
- The flow out of A36 changed: earlier, about 97 in 100 of its observed outgoing transitions went to A25; later, about 70 in 100 go to A30 and only about 28 in 100 to A25. The number of observed outgoing transitions of A36 also grew from about 200 to about 700, matching its increased repetition. [DIR001]
- A14, A30 and A36 occur together in every trace in both windows. Earlier they followed the fixed order A14 → A30 → A36 in about 98–99 in 100 traces; later that fixed order holds in only about 32 in 100 traces, while about 68 in 100 show the activities interleaved or repeated. [REL003, REL004, REL005]
- A05 appears in about 55 in 100 traces before and about 52 in 100 after, always alongside A30. Earlier it came before A30 (and A36) in about 53–54 in 100 of these traces; later it does so in only about 16 in 100, with about 35 in 100 interleaved or repeated instead. [REL001, REL002]
- The spacing between A30 and A36 collapsed: earlier about 98 in 100 observed pairs sat exactly two positions apart; later only about 12 in 100 do, with small shares scattered at three, four and five positions. [LAG002]
- Minor: the gap from A05 to A14 shifted from mostly one to three positions to mostly two to three positions. [LAG001]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that, in the later window, A30, A31 and A36 are re-executed several times within the same case, interleaved with A14, instead of each running once in the fixed sequence A14 → A30 → A36. Earlier, the three activities each ran once per case in that fixed order, with A36 then almost always followed by A25; later, each of A30, A31 and A36 averages about 3.5 occurrences per case and repeats in about 68 in 100 cases, the strict A14 → A30 → A36 order survives in only about 32 in 100 cases, and A36 now leads back to A30 in about 70 in 100 of its outgoing transitions — direct relational evidence of a cycle through A30. A05, present in about half the cases throughout and always alongside A30, changed from consistently preceding A30/A36 to being interleaved with their repetitions in about a third of cases. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, DIR001, REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, LAG002]

The screening balance supports a real rather than sampled difference: eleven control-flow items qualified where roughly 0.47 would be expected for an unchanged process, and all eleven describe this single repetition-and-ordering behaviour rather than unrelated differences; each item also ranks at the top of the reference comparisons (smaller differences in about 995 of 1000 random re-splits). [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, DIR001, REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, LAG001, LAG002]

Limitations: the evidence cannot distinguish one loop run a varying number of times from a mixture of cases with different numbers of passes, since about a third of later-window traces still show no repetition of A30, A31 or A36; and A31 rests only on its occurrence profile, without relational evidence, so its place in the cycle is inferred from its similarity to A30 and A36 rather than observed directly. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003]

Other differences: the modest lengthening of the A05 → A14 gap is consistent with the re-execution behaviour above but is not needed for it. [LAG001]
