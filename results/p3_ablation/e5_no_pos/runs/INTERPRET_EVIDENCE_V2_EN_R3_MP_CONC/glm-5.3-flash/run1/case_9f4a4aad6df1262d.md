### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the ordering constraints among the block of activities A31, A22, A20, A23, A24, A25, A26 and A32 were loosened: in the earlier window they executed as one fixed sequence (A31 → A22 → A20 → A23 → A24 → A26 → A25 → A32), whereas in the later window the same activities still occur together in virtually every trace but in varying orders, with new direct transitions that skip ahead (A23 straight to A32 in about 49 in 100 of its outgoing transitions, a target earlier reached only after A24, A26 and A25; A31 to A24 in about 47 in 100) and step backwards (A24 to A22 in about 23 in 100; A25 to A20 in about 20 in 100). Because several pairs now appear in both orders at similar rates (A20–A26 about 49 vs 51 in 100; A22–A24 about 53 vs 47; A23–A25 about 48 vs 52), this is a loss of fixed ordering between the activities rather than an exchange of two positions. [DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, DIR007, REL003, REL004, REL006, REL007]

The screening produced 43 qualifying control-flow items where roughly 0.62 would be expected if nothing had changed, so the volume of signals cannot be explained by random re-splits of a stable process; the relational evidence is direct and mutually consistent (all eight activity pairs moved from near-fixed order to mixed or reversed order, all seven sources redistributed their outgoing transitions, every gap distribution spread out), each difference exceeds about 199 of 200 random reference comparisons, most items rest on about 200 traces per window, and the unchanged co-occurrence of 100 in 100 rules out that activities were added, removed or became optional. [REL001, REL006, DIR003, LAG013]

Limitations: the indicators are window-level, so the evidence cannot fully separate one process with loosened ordering from a mixture of several fixed-order variants in the later window; and a few before-window gap profiles (A24 to A20, A24 to A22, A25 to A20, A26 to A20) rest on a single observation, although the matching order relations have full support.
