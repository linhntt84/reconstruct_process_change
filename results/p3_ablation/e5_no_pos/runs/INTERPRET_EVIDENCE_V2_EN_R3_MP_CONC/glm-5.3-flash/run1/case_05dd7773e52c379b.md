### Observed differences

- Six activities became near-universal: in the earlier window A28, A30 and A31 each ran in about 49 in 100 traces and A32, A33 and A34 in about 52 in 100; in the later window all six run in about 99–100 in 100 traces, with no repetitions within a trace in either window. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006]
- Two blocks that were alternatives now run together: earlier, each trace contained exactly one of the two triples — cross-triple pairs such as A28–A32 or A30–A34 never co-occurred (0 in 200 traces), with the A28/A30/A31 triple in about 49 in 100 traces and the A32/A33/A34 triple in about 52 in 100. In the later window these cross-triple pairs co-occur in about 98 in 100 traces, always with the A28/A30/A31 activity before the A32/A33/A34 activity. - The block stays framed by A25 and A26: in the later window A25 precedes each of the six activities in about 99 in 100 traces (earlier about 49–52 in 100), and A26 follows each of them in about 99 in 100 (earlier about 49–52 in 100). - Order within each triple remains unfixed: in both windows either order appears in near-equal shares (for example, in the later window A28 precedes A30 in about 49 in 100 of their joint traces and follows it in about 50 in 100). [REL013, REL014, REL018, REL025, REL026, REL027]
- Positions shifted downstream: the gap from A25 to A32, A33 or A34 moved from 1–3 steps (roughly one third each) to 4–5 steps (about 98 in 100 combined), and the gap from A28, A30 or A31 to A26 likewise moved from 1–3 steps to 4–5 steps. [LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006]

A28, A30 and A31 earlier reached A26 directly in about 30–36% of their outgoing transitions; in the later window A26 is nearly absent as a successor (about 0–0.5%), replaced by the other triple-1 members and by A32, A33 and A34. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that two three-activity blocks changed from alternative branches into consecutive stages: in the earlier window each case ran either A28/A30/A31 or A32/A33/A34 (each in a varying internal order) between A25 and A26, in roughly equal shares; in the later window virtually every case runs both blocks in sequence — A25, then A28/A30/A31 in varying order, then A32/A33/A34 in varying order, then A26. This is shown directly by the relational items, where cross-block pairs move from never co-occurring to co-occurring in about 98 in 100 traces with a fixed between-block order. [REL015, REL016, REL017, REL019, REL020, REL021, REL022, REL023, REL024]

The hypothesis is consistent across the other indicators: participation of all six activities rises to about 99–100 in 100 traces, the successor profiles of A25 and of A28/A30/A31 redirect away from A26 towards the second block, and the position gaps of the second block and of A26 shift to 4–5 steps, all matching one structural picture. With 43 qualified items against roughly 0.55 expected for an unchanged process, chance sampling cannot explain the pattern as a whole. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006, screening]

Limitations: the position evidence covers only five-step windows and pairwise relations, so the exact end-to-end sequence is inferred rather than fully observed; and the evidence cannot rule out that a small trace population running both blocks already existed earlier below the detection level (0 in 200 cross-block co-occurrences before), with about 1–2 in 100 later traces still showing the earlier single-block pattern. [REL015, REL024, OCC001, LAG001]
