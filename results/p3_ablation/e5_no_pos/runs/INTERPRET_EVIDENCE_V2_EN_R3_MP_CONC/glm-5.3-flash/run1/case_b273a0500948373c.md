### Observed differences

- The two windows flip between two mutually exclusive groups of activities. In the earlier window, A16, A17 and A18 each appeared in about 19 in 100 traces, while A19, A20 and A21 each appeared in about 82 in 100; in the later window, A16, A17 and A18 each appeared in about 79 in 100 traces and A19, A20 and A21 in only about 21 in 100. None of these activities repeats within a trace in either window. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006]

- At A15, the outgoing transitions moved from one group to the other: earlier, roughly 29, 29 and 24 in 100 of A15's outgoing transitions led to A19, A21 and A20, and only about 7–9 in 100 to A16, A17 and A18; in the later window, roughly 29, 29 and 22 in 100 led to A18, A16 and A17, and only about 6–8 in 100 to A19, A20 and A21. - The two groups behave as strict alternatives in both windows: an A16/A17/A18 activity never shares a trace with an A19/A20/A21 activity (co-occurrence zero throughout). Earlier, traces typically contained A19-group members without the A16 group; in the later window, traces contained A16-group members without the A19 group. - Co-occurrence with A15 flipped accordingly: earlier, A15 co-occurred with A19, A20 or A21 in about 82 in 100 traces (always A15 first) and with A16, A17 or A18 in about 19 in 100; in the later window, A15 co-occurred with A16, A17 or A18 in about 79 in 100 traces (always A15 first) and with A19, A20 or A21 in about 21 in 100. In the later window A16, A17 and A18 co-occur in about 79 in 100 traces, with both orders in similar shares (for example A16 before A17 in about 42 in 100 and A17 before A16 in about 38 in 100); the A19/A20/A21 group showed the same mixed pattern earlier (co-occurrence about 82 in 100) and in the later window rarely co-occur (about 21 in 100, mostly neither is present). - A25 occurs in every trace in both windows and changed companions: earlier it co-occurred with A19, A20 or A21 in about 82 in 100 traces (A19 first in about 46 in 100, interleaved in about 36 in 100); in the later window it co-occurs with A16, A17 or A18 in about 79 in 100 traces (A16 first in about 45 in 100, interleaved in about 34 in 100) and with A19, A20 or A21 in only about 21 in 100. ### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the routing choice after A15 shifted between two existing alternative branches: the share of traces going from A15 into the A16–A17–A18 branch rose from about 19 in 100 to about 79 in 100, while the A19–A20–A21 branch fell from about 82 in 100 to about 21 in 100, and A25, which runs in every trace, now typically accompanies the A16–A17–A18 group instead of the A19–A20–A21 group. The routing change is shown directly by the redistribution of A15's outgoing transitions, and the participation, co-occurrence and ordering items all describe the same flip consistently; each item's difference exceeds about 995 of 1,000 random re-splits of the pooled data. This fits a shift in how often existing paths are taken rather than activities being added, removed or repeated: both branches are observed in substantial shares in both windows (at least about 19 in 100 traces), the groups never co-occur in either window, and no repetitions appear in either window. The screening qualified 34 control-flow items where fewer than 1 would be expected without a change, so the set of differences as a whole is not what an unchanged process typically produces. [DIR001, OCC001, OCC004, REL001, REL004, REL009, REL012, REL024]

Limitations: the many qualifying items largely restate one underlying trace-level shift and are not independent confirmations, and the evidence cannot rule out that the two windows simply draw different case populations with different branch preferences rather than a changed routing rule at A15; a near-maximal empirical strength on a single item reflects unusualness under random re-splits, not the probability of a change. [DIR001, OCC001, REL007]
