### Observed differences

- The clearest difference is a reversal in how often two groups of activities run. In the earlier window, A16, A17 and A18 each appeared in about 75 in 100 traces, while A19, A20 and A21 each appeared in about 25 in 100. In the later window this reversed: A16/A17/A18 each appear in about 25 in 100 traces (about 49 of 200) and A19/A20/A21 in about 76 in 100 (about 151 of 200). No activity repeats within a trace in either window. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006]
- The two groups remain strict alternatives in both windows — a member of A16/A17/A18 and a member of A19/A20/A21 never occur in the same trace. Before, the A16-side activity appeared without the A19-side in about 75 in 100 traces and the A19-side alone in about 25 in 100; in the later window this is about 25 versus about 76 in 100. [REL009, REL014, REL018]
- Within each group the three activities still run together and without a fixed order: A16/A17/A18 co-occurred in 75 in 100 traces before and 25 in 100 after, with both orders in similar proportions in each window; the same holds for A19/A20/A21 (25 in 100 before, 76 in 100 after). [REL007, REL013, REL022, REL025]
- A15 precedes whichever group executes, in both windows: A15 came first in every trace containing A16 (75 in 100 traces before, 25 in 100 after) and likewise before A19 (25 in 100 before, 76 in 100 after). Of A15's observed outgoing transitions, about three quarters went to A16/A17/A18 before (about 27%, 23%, 26%) versus about a quarter after (about 8–9% each), while A19/A20/A21 rose from about 14%, 4% and 8% to about 25%, 22% and 29%. [REL001, REL004, DIR001]
- A25 is essentially always present in both windows, and its position relative to the executing group stays mixed (the group activity precedes A25 in roughly half to six in ten of their joint appearances, the rest interleaved), so its role looks unchanged. [REL012, REL024]
- For context, 35 control-flow items qualified in screening, against roughly 0.5 expected by chance if nothing had changed. [screening]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the choice between two pre-existing alternative branches after A15 reversed its frequencies: before, about three in four cases continued from A15 into A16, A17 and A18 (run together, in no fixed order) and about one in four into A19, A20 and A21; in the later window about three in four run A19/A20/A21 and about one in four run A16/A17/A18, with A15 still preceding the executing group and A25 still present throughout. This is a shift in how often existing paths are taken rather than a replacement of one path by another: all six activities are observed in substantial shares in both windows, both branches remain reachable from A15, and repetition behaviour is unchanged. The signs are direct and consistent — participation flips with large samples on both sides, the relation items show the same reversal for every pair of groups, and A15's outgoing shares redistribute accordingly — and the qualified-item count (35 versus about 0.5 expected) is far beyond what an unchanged process produces. A competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that the two windows simply contain different mixes of case types, which would produce the same share reversal without any change in the routing itself. [OCC001, OCC004, REL001, REL004, REL007, REL009, REL012, DIR001, screening]

Other differences: Within the A19/A20/A21 group, the earlier window leant to a rough internal order (A19 before A20 in about 72 in 100 of their joint appearances, A21 before A20 in about 64 in 100), whereas in the later window both orders occur about equally often; and A20 followed A15 within one or two event positions in about two thirds of its later-window appearances versus about half before — an event-position shift, not a clock-time one. [REL022, REL023, REL025, LAG001]
