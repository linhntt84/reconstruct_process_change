### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the exclusive choice between two activity groups was removed: before, each case executed either the group A33/A35/A36 or the group A37/A38/A39 after A30 (each in about half of the cases, essentially never both), whereas in the later window both groups execute in nearly every case, first A33/A35/A36 and then A37/A38/A39, before reaching A31. This is shown directly by the relation items (cross-group co-occurrence rising from about 1 in 100 to about 98 in 100, with a stable group-one-first order and no increase in repetitions), corroborated by the successor shifts at A30, A33, A35 and A36 and by position gaps growing from 1–3 to 4–5 steps exactly as expected if the second group now follows the first. The screening context supports this reading: 98 control-flow items qualified against roughly 1 expected without a change, and the key relations rest on about 100–200 traces per window, so rare-variant sampling does not explain them. Limitations: the many pair items largely mirror one underlying difference rather than independent confirmations, positions are event positions rather than clock times, and the evidence cannot rule out that a changed mix of cases entering the process, rather than the routing rule itself, produced the same pattern. [REL039, REL045, REL050, REL025, REL031, OCC005, OCC008, DIR005, DIR006, LAG005, LAG008]

Other differences: A04, which before often sat between A14 and A15, is now skipped in about three quarters of the cases (participation about 59 in 100 down to about 26 in 100, with A14 leading directly to A15 in about 79% of its transitions); this is a collapse in participation of an existing detour rather than a re-balanced share between paths. A02, A08 and A10 became more frequent on small counts, and A45/A47 show small new flows towards A02, A04, A06, A08 and A10.
