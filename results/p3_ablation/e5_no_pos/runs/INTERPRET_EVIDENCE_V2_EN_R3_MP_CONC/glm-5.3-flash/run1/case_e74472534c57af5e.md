### Observed differences

- The three consecutive activities A30, A31 and A36 each ran about once per trace in the earlier window (present in every trace, with repeats in only about 1 in 100 traces); in the later window each runs about 3.5 times per trace, repeating in about 69 in 100 traces. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003]
- A36's next step changed: in the earlier window about 98 in 100 of its outgoing transitions went to A25, while in the later window about 71 in 100 go back to A30 and only about 28 in 100 go to A25. Matching this, the ordered pair "A36 then A30" within five steps appeared in just 1 of 200 earlier traces but in 136 of 200 later traces. [DIR005, LAG002]
- The internal order of the block is unchanged: A30 is followed by A31 in about 97–99 in 100 transitions in both windows, and A31 is followed by A36 in the same proportion. [DIR003, DIR004]
- Pairwise relations moved from one fixed pass to a mixed pattern: A14 before A30 fell from about 99 in 100 traces to about 32, with "interleaved or repeated" rising to about 68 in 100 while co-occurrence stays at 100 in 100; the same shift appears for A14–A36 and A30–A36. For A05, which co-occurs with A30/A36 in about 55 in 100 traces (about 52 later), the fixed "A05 first" order fell from about 54 to about 17 in 100. [REL003, REL004, REL005, REL001, REL002]
- The positional gap from A30 to A36, previously two steps in about 96 in 100 cases, is two steps in only about 12 in 100 in the later window, with a new share at a gap of five steps (about 5 in 100) — consistent with the pair now occurring several times per trace. [LAG001]
- Separately, A05's outgoing mix redistributed: A14 as next activity fell from about 36 to about 18 in 100 transitions while A07 rose from about 19 to about 39 (A05 appears in about 55 in 100 traces before, about 52 after). [DIR001]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the consecutive block A30 → A31 → A36, previously executed exactly once per trace, now repeats within traces: A36 returns to A30 in about 71 in 100 of its executions and exits to A25 on the final pass (about 28 in 100), so each of the three activities runs about 3.5 times per trace on average, with repetition in about 69 in 100 traces, up from about 1 in 100. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, DIR005, LAG002]

The reading is direct and consistent: it rests on occurrence counts (mean per trace and recurrence), on the successor profile of A36, and on the appearance of the A36-then-A30 sequence in about two-thirds of later traces, while the block's internal edges stay at about 98–99 in 100 transitions and the pairwise relations shift from a single fixed pass to interleaved/repeated exactly as repetition predicts. Screening makes chance an unlikely overall explanation: 15 control-flow items qualified where fewer than 1 would be expected without a change. Most items reflect one underlying trace behaviour rather than independent confirmations, but the loop-back at A36 is shown by two different indicator types (direction and lag). [DIR003, DIR004, REL003, REL004, REL005, LAG001, OCC001, DIR005, LAG002]

Limitations: the evidence shows the behaviour, not the underlying mechanism — it cannot distinguish a newly added repetition from an existing rare repeat path that is now taken far more often, and the REL indicator alone cannot separate true sequential repetition from interleaving, though DIR005 and LAG002 do so here. It also remains unclear whether the roughly 31 in 100 traces still running a single pass are a stable sub-population. [REL003, DIR005, LAG002]

Other differences: A14's departures to A15 dipped slightly (about 97 to 91 in 100 transitions). The rerouting at A05 (A14 down from about 36 to 18 in 100, A07 up from about 19 to 39) is not explained by the repetition hypothesis; it could be part of the same restructuring or an independent shift, but the evidence shows neither A07's overall participation nor A14's full predecessor set, so it cannot be judged a pure change in how often existing paths are taken. [DIR001, DIR002]
