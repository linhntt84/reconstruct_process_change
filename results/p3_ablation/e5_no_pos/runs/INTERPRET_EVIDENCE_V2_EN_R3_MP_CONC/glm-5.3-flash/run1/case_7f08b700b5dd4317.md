### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, the six activities A33, A35, A36, A37, A38 and A39 each ran in every trace (100 in 100, essentially never repeated within a trace); in the later window, each runs in only about 47–57 in 100 traces [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006].
- A30 and A31 still run in every trace in both windows. Earlier, all six activities always lay between them (A30 before each, and each before A31, in about 99–100 in 100 traces); in the later window, A30 runs without any of the six in about 44–54 in 100 traces [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, REL007, REL008, REL009, REL010, REL011, REL012].
- The six now behave as two alternative sub-groups. Pairs inside {A33, A35, A36} still co-occur in about 47 in 100 traces and pairs inside {A37, A38, A39} in about 55–56 in 100, matching each activity's participation; cross-group pairs such as A33 with A37 dropped from co-occurring in 100 in 100 traces to about 3 in 100, with one of the two running without the other in the remainder [REL013, REL014, REL015, REL018, REL019, REL022, REL025, REL026, REL027].
- Successor profiles changed accordingly: earlier, about 97 in 100 of A30's outgoing transitions went to A33, A35 or A36, and A33, A35 and A36 led almost exclusively to other block activities; in the later window, A30's transitions are spread over all six block activities, and A33, A35 and A36 each lead directly to A31 in about 26–34 in 100 of their outgoing transitions [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004].
- In event positions, A37, A38 and A39 now follow A30 within 1–3 steps in about 94–97 in 100 of their occurrences (earlier almost always 4–5 steps), and A33, A35 and A36 now sit only 1–3 steps before A31 (earlier 4–5 steps) [LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG006, LAG009].

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the block of six activities between A30 and A31, previously executed in full in every trace, became an exclusive choice between two sub-blocks: in the later window, a trace runs either {A33, A35, A36} (about 47 in 100 traces) or {A37, A38, A39} (about 55 in 100 traces) and skips the other group's activities. The signs fit one story: the participation drops match exactly the rates at which each group still co-occurs internally; cross-group co-occurrence collapsed to about 3 in 100, far below the roughly 26 in 100 expected if the two groups were skipped independently, so they behave as alternatives rather than independently optional activities; and the new direct edges from A33, A35 and A36 to A31, together with the shortened position gaps, show the surviving group bridging A30 to A31 directly [OCC001, OCC004, REL001, REL015, REL025, DIR002, LAG001].

With 48 qualifying items against fewer than 1 expected for an unchanged process across 152 tested units, chance sampling cannot account for the pattern as a whole. Limitations: the evidence cannot show whether this exclusive routing existed before with a near-zero take-rate or was newly introduced, and the lag comparisons for cross-group pairs rest on only 4–5 observations [LAG005, LAG007, LAG008, LAG010, LAG011].

Other differences: A30's rare outgoing transitions shifted — the edges to A02, A05 and A13 (about 0.5–1.5 in 100 of its outgoing transitions) are no longer observed, and several new rare edges of about 0.5 in 100 each appeared [DIR001].
