### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, each of the six activities A33, A35, A36, A37, A38 and A39 ran in about 50 in 100 traces (mean occurrences per trace about 0.5, essentially never twice); in the later window each runs in about 99 in 100 traces, once per trace. [OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, OCC007, OCC008, OCC009]
- In the earlier window these six formed two alternative blocks that never shared a trace: A33/A35/A36 always ran together (about 48 in 100 traces) and A37/A38/A39 always ran together (about 52 in 100), with co-occurrence between the two groups at zero; in the later window both blocks run in the same trace in about 98 in 100 cases. [REL037, REL039, REL046, REL049]
- When both blocks now run together, the A33/A35/A36 group precedes the A37/A38/A39 group in about 97 in 100 traces; within each block the order remains mixed, with both directions in near-equal shares. [REL039, REL043, REL046, REL037, REL049]
- Placement shifted accordingly: A30's outgoing transitions earlier spread almost evenly over all six block activities (about 14–18% each) and now go almost only to A35, A33 and A36 (about 30–37% each); A33, A35 and A36 earlier led to A31 in about 30–36% of their outgoing transitions and now virtually never do (under 1%), with A37/A38/A39 taking their place; the distance from A33/A35/A36 to A31 and from A30 to A37/A38/A39 moved from mostly 1–3 to mostly 4–5 event positions. [DIR006, DIR007, DIR008, DIR009, LAG003, LAG005, LAG006, LAG008]
- A04 became rarer: about 30 in 100 traces earlier, about 14 in 100 later; of A04's outgoing transitions, the share to A16 fell from about 81% to about 36%, with new targets such as A15, A48 and A30 appearing (64 vs 28 outgoing transitions observed). [OCC001, DIR002]
- A06 and A10 became more common, from about 3 and 2–3 in 100 traces to about 11 and 12 in 100, though on few observations (6→22 and 5→24 traces). [OCC002, OCC003]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the choice between two alternative activity blocks was removed and both blocks now execute in sequence in almost every case. In the earlier window a case ran either the block A33–A35–A36 or the block A37–A38–A39 (about 48 vs 52 in 100 cases, never both); in the later window all six activities run in about 99 in 100 cases, with A33/A35/A36 preceding A37/A38/A39, sitting between A30 — which now leads almost only into the first block — and A31, which is now reached only several positions after the first block rather than directly from it. [OCC004, OCC007, REL039, REL046, REL049, DIR006, DIR007, LAG003, LAG006]

The pattern is direct and consistent: participation items with roughly 100 vs 200 traces of support per activity, about thirty relation items that turn exclusive-alternative pairs into always-co-occurring pairs, and redirection and distance items that agree with the new sequence. In total 78 control-flow items qualified where about 1 would be expected if nothing had changed, so chance cannot account for the ensemble. [OCC004, OCC009, REL037, REL051, DIR006, DIR009, LAG003, LAG008]

Limitations: the indicators show co-occurrence and event positions, not the routing logic itself; it cannot be ruled out that this region is now traversed twice (once per former block) rather than both blocks running in a single pass, since repetition of A30 and A31 was not measured, and about 1–2 in 100 later traces still resemble the old single-block behaviour. [REL025, REL031, REL039, REL046]

Other differences: A04 became rarer (about 30 → 14 in 100 traces) and its outgoing transitions redistributed, with the A16 share falling from about 81% to about 36% on few observations; A06 and A10 rose to about 11 and 12 in 100 traces from about 3 and 2–3; A02 and A08 show near-complete redistribution of their outgoing transitions but on very few observations (13 vs 23 and 9 vs 28), so these may reflect sampling noise rather than a stable change; the A04→A16 distance also spread from mostly 1 step to often 2–5 steps (15 observations later). These are not part of the main hypothesis. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, LAG001]
