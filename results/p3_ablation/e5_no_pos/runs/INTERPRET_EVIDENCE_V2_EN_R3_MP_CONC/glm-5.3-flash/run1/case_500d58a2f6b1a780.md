### Observed differences

- **Repetition of A25, A26 and A31 largely stopped.** In the earlier window, each of these three activities appeared in every trace, on average about 3.4 times per trace, and about 75 in 100 traces showed them repeated or interleaved. In the later window they still appear in every trace, but only about 1.0 times per trace on average, and repetition is visible in only about 1 in 200 traces. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003]
- **A31 now flows onward instead of back.** In the earlier window, about 71 in 100 of A31's observed outgoing transitions went to A25 and about 29 in 100 to A20; in the later window about 96 in 100 go to A20 and only about 4 in 100 to A25. The number of observed outgoing transitions of A31 also fell from about 689 to about 208, consistent with the drop in occurrences. [DIR001]
- **The order between A25 and A31 became fixed.** In the earlier window the two always co-occurred, but in about 75 in 100 traces they were interleaved or repeated, with A25 strictly before A31 in only 25 in 100. In the later window they still always co-occur, and A25 comes before A31 in about 995 in 1000 traces, with interleaving or repetition almost gone. [REL001]
- **Pair distances tightened in one direction and the reverse pair vanished.** For A25 followed by A31 within five steps, the later window concentrates on a gap of two positions (about 92% of the pair's lag mass, up from about 12%), while gaps of five positions all but disappear (about 5% to under 1%). The reverse pair, A31 followed by A25 within five steps, was observed about 150 times in the earlier window but only once in the later window. [LAG001, LAG002]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that a repeatedly executed block involving A25, A26 and A31 stopped repeating: in the earlier window these three activities each ran about three to four times per trace in every trace, with A31 typically returning to A25 and the two interleaved or repeated in about three quarters of traces; in the later window each runs essentially once per trace, the order is fixed with A25 before A31, and A31 proceeds to A20 rather than back to A25. The signs are direct and mutually consistent: participation stays at 100% in both windows while mean occurrences fall from about 3.4 to about 1.0 and recurrence from 75% to about 0.5% [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003]; A31's outgoing transitions shift from mostly A25 to almost always A20 [DIR001]; the A25–A31 relation changes from mostly interleaved or repeated to a fixed order [REL001]; and the A31-then-A25 pair within five steps drops from about 150 observations to 1 [LAG001, LAG002]. Seven items qualified against roughly 0.29 expected by chance among the 59 tested units, so the set as a whole cannot be explained by random sampling.

Limitations: A26's routing is not directly observed — no directional or relational evidence covers it — so its involvement rests only on an occurrence pattern identical to that of A25 and A31, meaning the three occurrence items may describe one shared behaviour rather than three independent changes. The evidence also covers only A31's outgoing edges, not how A25 or A26 are entered, so it cannot distinguish whether the returns from A31 to A25 were removed outright or the block's repetition was curtailed in some other way; a single later-window trace still shows repetition.
