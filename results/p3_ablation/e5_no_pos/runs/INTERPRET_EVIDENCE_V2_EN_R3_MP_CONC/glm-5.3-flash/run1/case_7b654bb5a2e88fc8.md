### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a structural reordering as the main hypothesis: the alternative step — one of A14, A15 or A20, still executed once per case — moved from before A21 to the segment between A22 and A27. In the earlier window cases ran A40, then the branch activity, then A21, A22, A23, A27; in the later window cases run A40, A21, A22, and then either A23 followed by the branch activity (about 52 in 100) or the branch activity followed by A23 (about 48 in 100), so the previously fixed order between the branch activity and A23 became an either-order relation. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006, REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, REL007, REL008, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004]

Supporting signs: 18 control-flow items qualified where fewer than one (0.36) would be expected by chance in a stable process, so the pattern as a whole is far beyond random re-splitting; every item's difference exceeds about 995 of 1000 random re-splits; successor profiles, pairwise orders and position gaps all move in the same direction; and participation is unchanged, which rules out the activities being added, removed, made optional or repeated. [DIR001, DIR004, REL001, LAG001, LAG002]

Limitations: the two path shapes and the A21–A22 order are reconstructed from aggregate transition and gap distributions rather than observed as explicit variants, and the evidence cannot distinguish a genuine per-case choice of order between A23 and the branch activity from two stable, case-dependent path shapes. [DIR004, DIR005, LAG001, LAG003]

Other differences: the choice among the three branch activities shifted somewhat — A14 from about 38 to about 30 in 100 cases and A20 from about 32 to about 37 — a frequency shift that with 200 traces per window may be sampling noise rather than a routing change.
