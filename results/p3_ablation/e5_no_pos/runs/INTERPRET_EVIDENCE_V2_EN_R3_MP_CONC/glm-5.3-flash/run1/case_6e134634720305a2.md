### Observed differences

- Three activities — A30, A31 and A36 — stopped repeating. In the earlier window each occurred about 3.5 times per trace and appeared more than once in about 75 in 100 traces; in the later window each occurs about once per trace and repeats in only about 1 in 100. Each activity is still present in every trace in both windows (participation 100 in 100), so they did not disappear — they ceased to recur. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003]
- A36's routing changed markedly. Earlier, about 70 in 100 of A36's outgoing transitions led back to A30 and about 28 in 100 led to A25; in the later window about 92 in 100 lead to A25 and only about 4 in 100 still lead back to A30. [DIR002]
- The relation between A30 and A36 changed from repeated/interleaved to a single fixed pass. Earlier the two were interleaved or repeated in about 76 in 100 traces (A30 before A36 in about 25 in 100); in the later window A30 comes before A36 in about 98 in 100 traces, and they still co-occur in every trace in both windows. [REL001]
- The return step and self-repetition of A36 nearly vanished: A36 followed by A30 within five steps fell from about 150 observed pairs to a single one, and A36 followed by itself from about 150 pairs to two. The after-window counts are so small that these proportions alone are weak, but they agree with the routing shift above. [LAG002, LAG003]
- The distance from A30 to A36 concentrated: in the later window about 89 in 100 observed A30→A36 pairs sit two steps apart, against about 12 in 100 earlier, when the distance was spread across two to five steps. [LAG001]
- A30's own successor profile is essentially unchanged: about 99 in 100 of its outgoing transitions went to A31 before and about 97 in 100 after, with only negligible edges shifting. [DIR001]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that a cycle returning from A36 to A30 — which previously ran about three to four times per trace through A30, A31 and A36 — now runs once: in the later window A30, A31 and A36 each execute a single time per trace, and A36 exits to A25 instead of looping back to A30. The screening found 9 qualifying items against roughly 0.46 expected without a change (about twenty times the chance expectation), so the set of differences is unlikely for an unchanged process. The hypothesis is direct and consistent: the occurrence profiles of all three activities show the same drop from about 3.5 to about 1 occurrence with participation unchanged, which rules out the activities being removed or made optional; the collapse of the A36→A30 transition, the shift from interleaved/repeated to strictly A30-before-A36, and the near-disappearance of the A36→A30 and A36→A36 lags all describe the same structural difference; and A30's successors are unchanged, so the entry into the cycle is intact. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, DIR002, REL001, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003]

Limitations: the evidence shows that the back edge from A36 to A30 is now rarely taken, not that the cycle was removed — about 4 in 100 of A36's outgoing transitions still return to A30 and about 1 in 100 later traces still show repetition, so an explanation in which the loop still exists but is almost always exited after one pass cannot be ruled out, nor can the evidence show why the exit behaviour changed. The after-window support for the vanished A36→A30 and A36→A36 lags rests on only one and two observations. [DIR002, LAG002, LAG003, OCC001]

Other differences: a minor redistribution among A30's rare outgoing edges, each involving well under 1 in 100 of its transitions, with no behavioural weight of its own. [DIR001]
