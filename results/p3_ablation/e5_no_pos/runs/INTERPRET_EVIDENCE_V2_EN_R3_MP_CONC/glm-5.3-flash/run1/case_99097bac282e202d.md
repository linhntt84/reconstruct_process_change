### Observed differences

- The dominant difference is a flip in which of two alternative activity groups runs. In the earlier window, about 75% of A20's outgoing transitions went to A21, A22 or A23 (roughly 26%, 23% and 25%) and about 25% to A24, A25 or A26; in the later window this reversed, with about 75% going to A24, A25 or A26 (roughly 25%, 22% and 28%) and about 24% to A21–A23. A20 itself occurs in essentially every trace in both windows. [DIR003, REL007, REL010]
- Trace participation mirrors this swap: A21, A22 and A23 each ran in about 75 in 100 traces earlier (150–151 of 200) and in about 25 in 100 later (49 of 200), while A24, A25 and A26 each rose from about 25 in 100 (50–51 of 200) to about 76 in 100 (151–152 of 200). [OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, OCC007, OCC008]
- The two groups behaved as mutually exclusive alternatives in both windows — a member of A21–A23 and a member of A24–A26 practically never share a trace (co-occurrence at most about 1 in 100) — and each group stays internally coherent: A21–A23 still occur together whenever they occur at all (together in 75% of traces earlier, 24.5% later, matching their participation), and likewise A24–A26 (25% earlier, 75.5% later). A30 stays with whichever group is active: it co-occurred with A21–A23 in about 75 in 100 traces earlier and with A24–A26 in about 76 in 100 later. [REL015, REL018, REL021, REL028, REL031, REL034, REL036]
- The downstream activities A32 and A33 follow the active group: each co-occurred with A21–A23 in about 48 in 100 traces earlier and with A24–A26 in about 52 in 100 later. [REL019, REL020, REL037, REL038]
- A10 became more frequent, from about 6 in 100 traces earlier to about 21 in 100 later (11 vs 42 traces), and now routes almost exclusively to A33 (about 90% of its outgoing transitions later vs about 18% earlier); A32 in turn now leads to A10 in about 20% of its outgoing transitions versus about 1% earlier. [OCC002, DIR002, DIR004]
- A02 became rarer (about 9 in 100 traces earlier versus about 1 in 100 later) with a completely changed outgoing profile, but this rests on very few observations (18 vs 2 traces). [OCC001, DIR001]
- Small positional shifts accompany the flip: A24 now follows A20 more often at three events' distance than immediately (immediate share 54% → 33%), and the gaps from A25 to A30 and from A32 to A33 also shifted slightly later in the trace; these are position-based observations and do not by themselves show that an activity was moved. [LAG001, LAG002, LAG003]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that two existing alternative paths after A20 exchanged their frequencies: earlier, roughly 3 in 4 traces executed the group A21–A23 after A20 (with A30 alongside and A32/A33 following in about half of the traces) and about 1 in 4 executed the group A24–A26; in the later window the shares are reversed, with A24–A26 in about 76 in 100 traces and A21–A23 in about 25 in 100. [DIR003, OCC003, OCC006, REL015, REL018, REL036] Supporting signs: the routing flip at A20 is observed directly, with about 200 outgoing transitions behind each window; all six activities remain well observed in both windows (25–76% participation) while only the shares between the alternatives shift; the groups stay mutually exclusive and internally coherent, and A20 and A30 keep the same relation to whichever group runs — so this is a change in how often existing paths are taken, not a removal, bypass or repositioning of activities. [DIR003, REL007, REL010, REL013, REL021, REL034] With 60 qualifying items against fewer than 1 expected without a change, the pattern as a whole is unlikely to be a sampling artefact, though many items describe this same single flip rather than independent differences. [OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, OCC007, OCC008, DIR003, REL015, REL018, REL036] Limitation: the evidence shows which path is taken more often but not what determines the choice, so a shift in the mix of cases entering the process cannot be distinguished from a change in the routing itself. [DIR003]

Other differences: A10 occurs more often and now leads mainly to A33 and follows A32; A02 nearly disappears from the later window (very low support); the distance from A20 to A24, from A25 to A30, and from A32 to A33 shifted slightly later within the trace. [OCC001, OCC002, DIR001, DIR002, DIR004, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003]
