### Observed differences

- **Exit of A20:** in the earlier window, A20 was followed by A30 in about 40 of 100 cases, by A31 in about 33 and by A36 in about 28; in the later window, it is followed by A22 in about 99 of 100 cases, with A30 and A36 almost no longer observed as successors. [DIR001]
- **Exit of A21:** in the earlier window, A21 was always followed by A25; in the later window, it is followed by A30 (about 36 of 100), A31 (about 33) or A36 (about 31), and by A25 in only about 1 of 100 cases. [DIR002]
- **Exits of the trio A30/A31/A36:** in the earlier window, each of the three led to one of the other two or to A22 (A22 in roughly 29–38 of 100 cases); in the later window, each leads to one of the other two or to A25 (A25 in roughly 31–36 of 100 cases), and A22 is no longer observed as their successor. [DIR003, DIR004, DIR005]
- **Order between the trio and A21/A22:** in the earlier window, A30, A31 and A36 each ran before A21, and before A22, in essentially all traces; in the later window, A21 runs before each of the three in about 99 of 100 traces, and A22 likewise before each of them. All these pairs still co-occur in 100 of 100 traces in both windows, so participation did not change — only the relative order did. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006]
- **Position gaps:** A22 followed A20 four steps later in the earlier window (essentially always) and directly in the later window (about 99 of 100); A25 followed A21 directly in the earlier window and sits four steps after A21 in the later window (about 99 of 100). [LAG001, LAG004]
- Four further gap items (A20–A31, A20–A36, A31–A22, A36–A22) rest on only 2 after-window observations out of 200 and are too weak to support any conclusion. [LAG002, LAG003, LAG005, LAG006]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the relative order of two groups of activities was inverted: the segment A22 → A21, with A25 attached to A21, moved from after the trio A30/A31/A36 to before it. The earlier window is consistent with the sequence A20 → (A30/A31/A36 in varying order) → A22 → A21 → A25, and the later window with A20 → A22 → A21 → (A30/A31/A36 in varying order) → A25. This is shown directly by the six order reversals, by the successor changes at A20, A21 and the trio, and by the gap shifts of A22 (from four steps after A20 to immediately after) and A25 (from immediately after A21 to four steps after). All seven activities occur in essentially every trace in both windows, so this is a reordering of existing activities, not activities becoming optional, repeated or replaced. The volume of qualifying items (17, against roughly 0.38 expected by chance in the control-flow family) is far beyond what an unchanged process typically produces. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, LAG001, LAG004]

Limitations: the gap evidence uses event positions rather than clock time, so only execution order is established; the evidence cannot distinguish one coordinated relocation of the segment from separate moves of A22 and A21, and it cannot tell whether the roughly 1 in 100 later-window traces that keep the earlier order are residual old-order executions or sampling noise. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, LAG001, LAG004]

Other differences: none beyond the four weak gap items noted above, which are consistent with the same reordering but rest on only 2 after-window observations each. [LAG002, LAG003, LAG005, LAG006]
