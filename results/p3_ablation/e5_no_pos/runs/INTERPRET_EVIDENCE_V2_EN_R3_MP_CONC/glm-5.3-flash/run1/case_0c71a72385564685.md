### Observed differences

- Three activities that were absent earlier are now common in the later window: A19 runs in about 38 of 100 traces (0 of 100 earlier), A25 in about 35 of 100 (0 earlier) and A20 in about 30 of 100 (about 0.5 earlier); each appears at most once per trace. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003]
- In the later window the three act as alternatives: any two of them occur together in only about 1–2 of 100 traces, while at least one of them occurs in about 99 of 100 traces. [REL001, REL002, REL005, OCC001, OCC002, OCC003]
- A41, which occurs in every trace in both windows, now routes away from A26: earlier about 82 in 100 of its observed outgoing transitions went to A26, later only about 2 do, while about 33 go to A19, about 30 to A25 and about 24 to A20. [DIR004]
- The new activities lead on to A26: about 95 in 100 of A19's and about 90 in 100 of A25's observed outgoing transitions go to A26 (earlier these two had no observed outgoing transitions), and A20 precedes A26 in about 29 of 100 later traces. [DIR002, DIR003, REL006]
- Ordering in the later window is consistent: A41 comes before each of A19, A20 and A25, and each of them comes before A26, in nearly every trace where both occur; the reverse order appears in at most about 1 of 100 traces. [REL003, REL004, REL006, REL007, REL008, REL009]
- The A41-to-A26 distance grew: earlier A26 followed A41 one step later in about 82 of 100 cases (two steps in about 18); later it follows two steps later in about 83 and three steps later in about 14, with one-step adjacency in only about 1 of 100. [LAG001]
- A02's outgoing transitions were also redistributed: A26, earlier the target of about 26 in 100 of A02's observed outgoing transitions, is no longer observed as a target, and new targets appear (A16 about 18 in 100, A45 about 16 in 100); this profile rests on only about 35 earlier versus 44 later outgoing transitions. [DIR001]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that, in the later window, a pass through exactly one of three alternative activities — A19, A20 or A25 — sits between A41 and A26: earlier, A41 was followed directly by A26 in about 82 of 100 cases and the three activities did not occur; later, A41 precedes one of them (in roughly even shares of its outgoing transitions) and that activity in turn precedes A26, so the direct A41-to-A26 step has all but disappeared. [DIR004, LAG001, OCC001, OCC002, OCC003]

The hypothesis is supported directly and consistently: relational and positional evidence between the same activities shows the change (A41's redirection, the new activities' edges to A26, the before/after orderings in both directions, and the distance shift from one to two–three steps), occurrence, direction, relation and lag items all describe the same behaviour, and 17 qualifying items is many times the fewer than one expected by chance among the 99 tested units, so the ensemble is not what an unchanged process typically produces. [DIR002, DIR003, REL003, REL004, REL006, REL007, REL008, REL009, LAG001]

Limitations: the five-step relation window cannot show the segment's full internal structure — in about 14 of 100 later cases A26 follows A41 three steps later, so further activity may sit inside the segment — and the evidence cannot show what determines the choice among A19, A20 and A25. An important competing explanation that remains is that the whole A41–A26 segment was restructured more broadly, with the three new activities only part of the change. [LAG001, DIR004]

Other differences: A02's outgoing transitions shifted away from A26 (about 26 in 100 earlier to none observed later) towards targets including A16 and A45; this redistribution is not covered by the main hypothesis and rests on only about 35 versus 44 observed outgoing transitions. [DIR001]
