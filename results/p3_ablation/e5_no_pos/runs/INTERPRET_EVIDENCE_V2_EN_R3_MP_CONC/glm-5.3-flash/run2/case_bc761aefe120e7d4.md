### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, A30, A31 and A36 each occurred about 3.4–3.5 times per trace and repeated within a trace in about 75 in 100 traces; in the later window each occurs just over once per trace (about 1.05) and repeats in only about 1–2 in 100 traces, while all three still appear in every trace in both windows [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003].
- The routing out of A36 changed accordingly: in the earlier window about 70 in 100 of A36's outgoing transitions led back to A30 and about 29 in 100 led to A25; in the later window about 93 in 100 lead to A25 and only about 4 in 100 lead back to A30 [DIR003].
- The relation between A30 and A36 changed from mostly interleaved or repeated (75 in 100 traces) to a fixed order with A30 before A36 (98 in 100 traces); the two activities still occur together in every trace in both windows [REL001].
- Repeat pairs within five steps nearly vanished: A36 followed by A30 fell from 150 observed pairs to 1, A36 followed by A36 from 150 to 2, and A30 followed by A30 from 150 to 2 [LAG003, LAG004, LAG001].
- In the later window the positional gap from A30 to A36 concentrates at two steps (weight about 0.89, versus about 0.12 before), whereas before it was spread across gaps of two to five steps [LAG002].
- The chain itself is stable: about 97–99 in 100 of A30's outgoing transitions go to A31 and of A31's go to A36 in both windows; the drop in outgoing-transition counts (about 689 to 210) matches the drop in occurrences [DIR001, DIR002].

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the repeating block A30 → A31 → A36 stopped repeating: in the earlier window the three activities formed a loop that ran about three to three-and-a-half times per trace, with A36 returning to A30 in about 70 in 100 of its outgoing transitions, while in the later window the block runs about once per trace, A36 proceeds to A25 in about 93 in 100 of its outgoing transitions, and repeats survive in only about 1–2 in 100 traces [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, DIR003, REL001].

This reading is consistent across all eleven qualified items (against about 0.45 expected by chance in an unchanged process): the repetition drop is measured directly with full participation in both windows, the loop-back edge and the repeat pairs collapse (150 pairs before versus 1–2 after), the order becomes a single fixed A30-before-A36 pass, and the A30 → A31 → A36 chain itself keeps about 97–99 in 100 of its transitions in both windows, so the chain is intact and only its repetition changed [OCC001, DIR003, LAG003, LAG004, LAG001, REL001, DIR001, DIR002].

Limitations: the evidence cannot show whether the loop was removed entirely or only made very rare, since repeats still occur in about 1–2 in 100 traces; the after-side profiles of the repeat pairs rest on only 1–2 observations; and whether the single pass is now mandatory for every case cannot be confirmed [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, LAG003, LAG004].

Other differences: the remaining outgoing edges of A30 and A31 show only minor redistribution, with a few rare edges (each corresponding to a single transition at the observed support) appearing or disappearing between the windows [DIR001, DIR002].
