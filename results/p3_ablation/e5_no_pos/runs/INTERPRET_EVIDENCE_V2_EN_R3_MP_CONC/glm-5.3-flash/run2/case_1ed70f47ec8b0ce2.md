### Observed differences

- The dominant difference is an exchange of sequence positions between two activity groups, while the activities' participation itself stayed stable: A15, A17 and A18 occur in virtually every case in both windows, and each of A14, A16 and A19 occurs in about 29–39 of 100 cases in both windows. [DIR001, DIR003, DIR006, REL001, REL008, REL010, REL017]

- The groups' entry points swapped. Earlier, A24 led into A15, A17 or A18 (about 37, 30 and 33 in 100 of A24's outgoing transitions) and A35 led into A14, A16 or A19 (about 30, 35 and 36 in 100); later, A24 led into A14, A16 or A19 (about 33, 29 and 39 in 100) and A35 led into A15, A17 or A18 (about 33, 36 and 31 in 100). [DIR007, DIR008]

- The groups' exits swapped. Earlier, A14, A16 and A19 each led to A20 in 100 of 100 cases, and A15, A17 and A18 led to A25 in about 30, 33 and 38 of 100 of their outgoing transitions; later, A14, A16 and A19 each led to A25 in 100 of 100 cases, and A15, A17 and A18 led to A20 in about 37, 28 and 36 of 100. [DIR001, DIR003, DIR006, DIR002, DIR004, DIR005]

- Every order relation between the two groups and the connector activities reversed. Earlier, A14, A16 and A19 preceded A15, A17 and A18, and preceded both A20 and A24, in every case where both occurred, while A20 and A24 preceded A15, A17 and A18 (A20 before A15 in 100 of 100 cases); later, A15, A17 and A18 preceded A14, A16 and A19 and preceded A20, and A24 came after them (A15 before A20 in 100 of 100 cases; A15 before A14 in about 33 of 100). [REL001, REL004, REL005, REL008, REL009, REL016, REL020]

- Event distances match this exchange: the gap from A24 to A25 shrank from 4 to 2 event positions, and the gap from A35 to A20 grew from 2 to 4. [LAG001, LAG002]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that two three-activity segments exchanged their places in the process. In the earlier window, exactly one of A14, A16 and A19 executed between A35 and A20, and A15, A17 and A18 executed together (all three, in varying order) between A24 and A25; in the later window, A15, A17 and A18 executed between A35 and A20 and exactly one of A14, A16 and A19 executed between A24 and A25, with A35, A20, A24 and A25 keeping their connector roles. [DIR007, DIR008, DIR001, DIR002, LAG001, LAG002, REL008, REL009]

Supporting signs:
- Chance is an unlikely explanation: 31 control-flow items qualified where about 0.41 would be expected without a change, and every item's difference was larger than in about 995 of 1,000 random re-splits of the pooled data. [DIR001–DIR008, REL001–REL021, LAG001–LAG002]
- The change is shown directly and consistently: all 21 cross-pair order relations flipped completely (from 100-in-100 to 0-in-100 or the reverse), successor profiles were fully replaced rather than merely shifted, and the distance changes match the exchange quantitatively. [REL004, REL008, REL016, REL020, DIR001, DIR003, DIR007, LAG001, LAG002]
- Participation rates were stable, which rules out activities being added, removed or made optional as the explanation. [REL001, REL010, REL017, DIR001, DIR003, DIR006]

An important competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out: the observationally equivalent reading that the connector activities exchanged roles (A20 with A25, A24 with A35) while the two segments stayed in place — either way the change is an exchange of two segments, but the evidence cannot distinguish which labels moved. No evidence item covers the relation between A20 and A24, so any connectors between the segments are unobserved. [DIR001, DIR007, LAG001, LAG002]

Other differences: Within the A15/A17/A18 group the same internal transitions persisted with somewhat shifted shares (A15→A17 fell from about 40 to about 31 in 100; A17→A15 rose from about 31 to about 36 in 100), which the evidence cannot separate from a side effect of the group's new entry point or from sampling variation. [DIR002, DIR004, DIR005]
