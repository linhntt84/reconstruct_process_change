### Observed differences

- **Successors of A20.** In the earlier window, A20's observed outgoing transitions split roughly evenly between A30 (about 40 in 100), A31 (about 33) and A36 (about 28). In the later window, A20 leads almost exclusively to A22 (about 99 in 100), with transitions to A30/A31/A36 practically unobserved. [DIR001]
- **Successors of A21.** In the earlier window, A21 was followed by A25 in essentially all cases (100 in 100). In the later window, A21 leads to A30, A31 or A36 (about 36, 33 and 31 in 100 respectively) and almost never to A25 (about 1 in 100). [DIR002]
- **Exits of the A30/A31/A36 group.** Earlier, each of A30, A31 and A36 passed on to A22 in roughly 29–38 of 100 of its outgoing transitions. In the later window these A22 transitions have nearly vanished (under 1 in 100) and each group member instead leads to A25 in about 31–36 of 100. [DIR003, DIR004, DIR005]
- **Order flips.** Earlier, A30, A31 and A36 each ran before A21 and before A22 in every trace (100 in 100); in the later window, A21 and A22 each run before all three group members in 99 of 100 traces. All six pairs still co-occur in 100 of 100 traces in both windows, so participation itself is unchanged — only the order changed. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006]
- **Position gaps.** The gap from A20 to A22 changed from 4 event positions in all earlier cases to 1 position in 99 of 100 later cases, while the gap from A21 to A25 changed from 1 position to 4 positions. [LAG001, LAG004]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the group of activities A30, A31 and A36 — executed in varying internal order — changed its placement in the sequence. In the earlier window the flow ran A20 → (A30/A31/A36 in varying order) → A22, with A21 → A25 after that; in the later window it runs A20 → A22 directly, and A21 now feeds the group, which then ends in A25. In short, the group's position relative to A20, A22, A21 and A25 inverted: it now sits between A21 and A25 instead of between A20 and A22. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, LAG001, LAG004]

Supporting signs: the order change is shown directly by the relational items (group-before-A21/A22 at 100 in 100 earlier, versus A21/A22-before-group at 99 in 100 later); the successor changes at A20, A21 and the group members point the same way; and the position gaps swap symmetrically (A20→A22 from 4 to 1, A21→A25 from 1 to 4). Co-occurrence stays at 100 in 100 for every pair in both windows, so this is a re-sequencing of activities that all still execute, not a change in participation. The 17 qualifying items far exceed the roughly 0.38 expected for an unchanged process, and all items describe one consistent picture — though they largely reflect the same traces and are not independent confirmations. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, LAG001, LAG004]

Limitations: the evidence shows relative orders but does not directly observe the order between A21/A25 and A20/A22, so it cannot distinguish "the group moved later" from "A22 and A21 moved earlier relative to a stationary group" — both readings fit. The gap indicator uses event positions, not clock time, so no conclusion about timing is implied. [REL001, REL004, LAG001, LAG004]

Other differences: Four further gap items (A20→A31, A20→A36, A31→A22, A36→A22) show shifted position gaps, but each rests on only about 2 traces in the later window; they involve the same activities as the main hypothesis and are too weakly supported to count as separate findings. [LAG002, LAG003, LAG005, LAG006]
