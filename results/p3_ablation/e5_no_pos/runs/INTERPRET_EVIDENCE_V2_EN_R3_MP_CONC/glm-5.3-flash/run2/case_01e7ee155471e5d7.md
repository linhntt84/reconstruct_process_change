### Observed differences

- The sequence flipped between the segment A22–A21 and the trio A30/A31/A36. In the earlier window, A21 preceded each of A30, A31 and A36 in all 200 traces, and A22 likewise preceded all three in all 200 traces; in the later window each of the three precedes A21 and A22 in about 199 of 200 traces, with roughly 1 in 200 traces keeping the earlier order. All six pairs still occur together in every trace of both windows, so participation did not change — only the order of execution did. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006]

- Successors moved with the same flip. A20, earlier always followed directly by A22, is in the later window followed by A30, A31 or A36 in roughly equal shares (about 33 in 100 each). A21, earlier followed by one of the trio (about 28–38 in 100 each), is later followed directly by A25 in about 199 of 200 traces. Each trio member earlier led to another trio member or to A25 (about a third each); later each leads to another trio member or to A22, and A25 almost never follows directly. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005]

- The earlier close successions have essentially disappeared: A21 within five steps before each trio member, and each trio member within five steps before A25, occurred in all 200 earlier traces but in only about 1 later trace (each of these later profiles rests on a single observation). ### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the trio A30, A31, A36 changed its placement relative to A22 and A21: in the earlier window it executed after A21 (typical shape A20, A22, A21, then the trio in varying order, then A25), while in the later window it executes directly after A20 and before A22, with A21 now leading directly to A25. The order change is shown directly for all six activity pairs and is essentially complete rather than partial; the successor changes of A20, A21 and the trio and the distance changes for A20–A22 and A21–A25 match the same relocation; and 19 items qualified where about 0.4 would be expected without a change, each difference exceeding about 99.5 in 100 random re-splits of the pooled data. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, LAG001, LAG002]

Limitations: the six LAG items on the old close successions rest on a single later-window observation each, and the DIR, REL and LAG items describe one underlying trace pattern, so they are not independent confirmations of each other. The evidence shows event positions, not clock time, and cannot show whether the trio moved earlier or the A22/A21 segment moved later; nor can it show what now runs between A22 and A21 — A25 is no longer within five steps of any trio member in about 199 of 200 later traces, so other, unobserved activities may sit between the segments, and the evidence does not resolve this competing possibility.
