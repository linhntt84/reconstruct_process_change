- Four activities became near-universal: A27, A36, A37 and A42 each appeared in roughly 46–55 in 100 traces in the earlier window, and in essentially every trace (about 199–200 of 200) in the later window, in each case about once per trace with no meaningful repetition in either window. - A36, A37 and A42 keep no fixed order among themselves in either window (each order roughly equally common), but their co-occurrence rose from about 46 in 100 traces to about 995 in 1000. [REL020, REL021, REL022]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the branch structure around A25, A26, A27, A28, A31, A36, A37 and A42 was reorganised from two mutually exclusive alternatives into one combined sequence. In the earlier window, the flow split at A26: either A27 ran and was followed by A31 (about 54 in 100 traces), or the group A36/A37/A42 ran in mixed order and was followed by A31 (about 45 in 100), with A28 directly after A25 preceding both branches and A27 and the group practically never sharing a trace. In the later window, essentially every trace runs A25, then A36/A37/A42 in mixed order, then A28, then A26 immediately followed by A27, and finally A31, each about once. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, REL010, REL011, REL012, REL013, LAG001, LAG003]

The hypothesis is direct and consistent: the successor changes at A25, A26, A36, A37 and A42 show the rewiring directly; the relational items show the change from alternatives to joint execution and the reversal of A28's order relative to the group; and the lag items confirm A28's new position and A31's consolidated distance from A26. The volume of flagged items (40, all control-flow) also far exceeds the roughly 0.7 expected by chance among the 140 tested units, so the pattern as a whole is not what random re-splits of a stable process produce. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, REL006, REL007, REL008, REL014, REL015, REL016, LAG001, LAG003]

An important competing explanation remains: the two windows may draw on different mixes of pre-existing process variants (a shift in which variant is executed) rather than a modification of a single process, which this evidence cannot distinguish. In addition, a few lag items rest on only 1–2 observations and were not used for the hypothesis. [LAG002, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006, LAG007, LAG008, LAG009]

Other differences: small new low-frequency edges (about 0.5–1 in 100 of outgoing transitions, e.g. from A25 to A13/A14/A40 and from A36/A37/A42 to A10/A22/A04) and small interleaved-or-repeated shares (up to about 3 in 100) appear in the later window; these are at levels a stable process can occasionally produce. [DIR001, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, REL001, REL005, REL009]
