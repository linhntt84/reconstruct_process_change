### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that in the later window the activities A31, A22, A20, A23, A24, A26 and A25 are executed in a fixed sequential order — A31 → A22 → A20 → A23 → A24 → A26 → A25, continuing to A32 — whereas in the earlier window the same activities occurred together in every trace but in mixed or partially reversed orders with several alternative successors each. This is a change in ordering constraints, not in participation: co-occurrence for these pairs is 100 in 100 traces in both windows, so only the relative order and successor choice changed. The support is direct (relational, successor and step-distance evidence all show the same chain), consistent across many items, and the screening count (51 qualifying items against about 0.7 expected without change) cannot be explained by random re-splitting of a stable process. Limitations: the lag evidence only covers gaps up to five positions, so deviations beyond that range would be invisible, and a few lag items rest on a single after-window observation, though the chain conclusion does not depend on them; a competing explanation the aggregates cannot rule out is that the earlier mixed orders came from a mixture of coexisting case variants rather than per-case flexibility. Other differences: A08's participation fell from about 32 to about 13 in 100 traces and its outgoing transitions diversified away from A15, with A14 now rarely leading to A08; A10's participation rose from about 3 to about 12.5 in 100 traces (6 vs 25 traces). The evidence cannot show whether these shifts are connected to the re-sequencing above. [OCC001, OCC002, DIR001, DIR002, REL001, LAG001]
