### Observed differences

- Four activities were mandatory earlier and are optional now: A27, A36, A37 and A42 each appeared in every trace in the earlier window, but in the later window they appear in only about 54%, 47%, 48% and 48% of traces respectively. A25, A26, A28 and A31 still appear in all traces in both windows. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, REL009, REL010]
- In the earlier window a trace typically ran A25, then the group A36/A37/A42 in varying internal order, then A28, then A26, then A27, then A31: each group member preceded A28 and A26 in about 99–100 in 100 traces, A28 preceded A27 in about 98, A26 preceded A27 in about 98, and A25 reached A28 mostly 4 positions later (about 91 in 100). [REL005, REL006, REL009, REL014, REL020, LAG001, LAG005]
- In the later window A25 leads almost directly to A28: about 97% of A25's outgoing transitions go to A28 (earlier they split roughly 34% to A42, 32% to A36, 30% to A37), and the A25→A28 distance is 1 step in about 98 in 100 cases, versus 4 steps in about 91 in 100 earlier. [DIR001, LAG001]
- A26's routing split in two: earlier about 99% of its outgoing transitions went to A27; in the later window about 52% go to A27 and roughly 13–16% each go to A36, A37 and A42. [DIR002]
- The group A36/A37/A42 still executes as a unit in varying order, but it moved: earlier each member preceded A26 in about 99–100 in 100 traces, while in the later window A26 precedes each member in about 45–46 in 100; the group occurs in about 47% of later traces and is absent as a whole in about 52%. [REL006, REL007, REL008, REL014, REL015, REL016, REL020, REL021, REL022]
- A27 and the group became mutually exclusive alternatives: earlier A27 co-occurred with each of A36, A37 and A42 in 100% of traces; in the later window co-occurrence is about 1% or less, with A27 alone in about 53% of traces and the group alone in about 46–47%. [REL011, REL012, REL013]
- The group now exits towards A31 instead of A28: earlier A36, A37 and A42 each led to A28 in about 31–35% of their outgoing transitions; in the later window each leads to A31 in about 26–35% and to A28 in about 1%. A31 follows A37 within 1–3 steps in about 93 later traces, versus 1 earlier trace. [DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, LAG008]
- The A26→A31 distance changed from 2 steps in about 98 in 100 traces to a mix of 2 steps (about 53%) and 4 steps (about 43%), matching the short A27 branch and the three-activity branch. [LAG005]
- Several lag items (A25/A36, A25/A37, A25/A42, A36/A28, A37/A28, A42/A28) rest on only 1–2 observations in the later window and carry little weight. [LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG006, LAG007, LAG009]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the segment between A25/A26 and A31 was re-routed. In the earlier window, every trace executed the group A36, A37, A42 (in varying order) between A25 and A28 and then ran A26 → A27 → A31; in the later window, traces run A25 → A28 → A26 and then take one of two branches — A27 → A31 in about half of the traces, or the group A36/A37/A42 → A31 in the other half — so that A27 and the group, previously both mandatory in sequence, became mutually exclusive alternatives and the group was relocated from before A28/A26 to after A26. Supporting signs: participation fell from 100% to roughly half for exactly these four activities; relational items show the order flip directly and the collapse of A27–group co-occurrence from 100% to about 1%; successor profiles show the new direct A25→A28 edge and the group's new exits to A31; and the bimodal A26→A31 distance matches the two branches. The volume of qualifying items (40 control-flow items versus well under 1 expected without a change) cannot be explained by chance sampling. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, REL006, REL011, REL012, REL013, REL014, LAG001, LAG005, LAG008]

The evidence does not show where in the process the choice between the two branches is made, and it cannot rule out that each window mixes old-style and new-style trace variants rather than reflecting one uniform routing rule; a few lag items rest on only 1–2 later observations and were not used. [LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG006, LAG007, LAG009]

Other differences: a handful of rare outgoing transitions with roughly 1–2% shares appear only in the later window (for example from A26, A36, A37 or A42 to activities not previously observed as successors); at these sizes they are consistent with sampling noise. [DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005]
