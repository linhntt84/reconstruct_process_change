### Observed differences

- The main difference is a reversal in which of two alternative routes is taken after A15. In the earlier window, A19, A20 and A21 each appeared in about 82 in 100 traces (163 of 200), while A16, A17 and A18 each appeared in about 19 in 100 (37 of 200); in the later window, A16, A17 and A18 each appeared in about 79 in 100 traces (158 of 200), while A19, A20 and A21 each appeared in about 21 in 100 (42 of 200). None of these activities repeats within a trace in either window, and each difference is larger than in about 995 of 1,000 random re-splits of the pooled data. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006]

- The outgoing transitions of A15 were redistributed accordingly: the share leading to A16 rose from about 7% to about 29%, to A17 from about 9% to about 22%, and to A18 from about 3% to about 29%, while the shares leading to A19, A20 and A21 fell from about 29%, 24% and 29% to about 7%, 8% and 6% (shares of A15's observed outgoing transitions, not of cases). [DIR001]

- The two groups behave as alternatives in both windows: cross-group pairs such as A16 with A19 never occur in the same trace (co-occurrence zero in both windows), whereas pairs within a group co-occur whenever their group runs — for example A16 with A17 went from about 19 to about 79 in 100 traces. [REL007, REL009, REL010, REL011, REL014, REL015, REL016, REL018, REL019, REL020]

- The ordering around and within the groups did not change: A15 precedes the group activities whenever they co-occur in both windows; within-group pairs appear in both orders in similar proportions (for example A16 before A17 in about 42% and A17 before A16 in about 38% of their later co-occurrences); and A25, which occurs in essentially every trace in both windows, is typically preceded by the active group's activity, with about a third of the co-occurrences interleaved — before with A19, A20 and A21 (together in about 82 in 100 traces), later with A16, A17 and A18 (together in about 79 in 100). [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, REL012, REL013, REL021, REL024, REL026, REL027]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the choice between two existing alternative paths after A15 shifted: the path through A16, A17 and A18, previously the minority route at about 19 in 100 cases, became the majority route at about 79 in 100 cases in the later window, while the path through A19, A20 and A21 fell from about 82 to about 21 in 100, with A25 continuing to run after whichever group is chosen. This is shown directly by the redistribution of A15's outgoing transitions together with the mirrored participation shifts, and it meets the conditions for a shift in how often existing paths are taken: every involved activity appears in a substantial share of traces in both windows, both routes remain in use, the groups never co-occur, and the order around and within the groups is unchanged — which argues against an activity being added, removed, moved or swapped. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, DIR001, REL009, REL012]

On screening: 34 control-flow items qualified from 96 tested units, against roughly 0.48 expected if nothing had changed; a set this many times larger than the chance expectation cannot be explained by random re-splits as a whole. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, DIR001, REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, REL007, REL008, REL009, REL010, REL011, REL012, REL013, REL014, REL015, REL016, REL017, REL018, REL019, REL020, REL021, REL022, REL023, REL024, REL025, REL026, REL027]

Limitations: the qualifying items overlap heavily — the occurrence, direction and relation indicators largely re-express the same branch reversal from different angles, so they are not independent confirmations — and with 200 traces per window the evidence cannot distinguish a changed routing rule at A15 from a shift in the mix of case types that the two existing routes serve. [OCC001, DIR001, REL009]
