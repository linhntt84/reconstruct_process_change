### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that, in the later window, two groups of activities exchanged their places around A25 and A29: A19, A21 and A24 — which earlier ran after A25/A29 and each typically continued to A30 — now run before A25/A29 and lead to A25, while A20, A22 and A23 — which earlier preceded A25/A29 — now follow them and lead to A30, with A40 feeding the group in both windows (earlier into A20/A22/A23, now into A19/A21/A24). The support is direct and consistent: every tested precedence pair between the two groups and A25/A29 flipped direction in about 19 of every 20 co-occurring traces, with 200 traces per window behind each relation, and the successor profiles of A19, A20, A21, A22, A23, A24, A29 and A40 moved in the same direction. [REL015, REL020, REL022, REL023, REL029, REL032, REL034, DIR005, DIR006, DIR007, DIR009, DIR010, DIR011, DIR012]

The adjacency evidence lines up exactly where the flip predicts: A21→A25, A24→A25 and A40→A19/A21/A24 became immediate links, A24→A30 and the A22/A23→A25 and A40→A22 links disappeared, and the screening found 78 qualifying control-flow items against roughly 1 expected without change, so the pattern as a whole is not what an unchanged process typically produces. Limitations: all order and distance statements rest on event positions within traces rather than clock time, and the evidence cannot fully distinguish a resequencing of these activities from an equivalent rerouting that visits the same activities in a different order; the many indicators also describe overlapping behaviour of the same traces, so they corroborate but do not independently confirm one another.
