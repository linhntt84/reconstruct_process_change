### Observed differences

- The six activities A33, A35, A36, A37, A38 and A39 each appeared in about half of the cases in the earlier window (97–103 of 200 traces) and in nearly every case in the later window (197–199 of 200) [OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, OCC007, OCC008, OCC009].
- In the earlier window, the group A33/A35/A36 and the group A37/A38/A39 were alternatives: pairs spanning the two groups never occurred in the same trace (co-occurrence 0 in 100; one group alone in about 49 of 100 traces, the other alone in about 52 of 100). In the later window these pairs co-occur in about 98 of 100 traces, with the first group before the second in about 97 of 100 [REL039, REL040, REL041, REL043, REL044, REL045, REL046, REL047, REL048].
- Within each group the activities already co-occurred when present (about 49–52 of 100 traces earlier) and now co-occur in nearly every trace (about 98–99.5 of 100); in both windows both internal orders occur in similar shares, so the order inside each group is unfixed [REL037, REL038, REL042, REL049, REL050, REL051].
- Routing around this region changed: earlier, A30's outgoing transitions were spread roughly evenly over the six activities (about 14–18 in 100 each), while later A30 leads almost only to A35, A33 and A36 (about 37, 30 and 30 in 100) and practically never directly to A37–A39 (under 1 in 100) [DIR006]. Earlier, A33, A35 and A36 each led to A31 in about 30–36 of 100 outgoing transitions; later A31 is essentially no longer a direct successor (0–0.5 in 100), replaced by edges among A33/A35/A36 and on to A37–A39 [DIR007, DIR008, DIR009].
- Event distances stretched accordingly: the gap from A30 to A37/A38/A39 was mostly 1–3 positions earlier and is mostly 4–5 positions later (A30→A37: lags 1–3 covered about 99 in 100 earlier, lags 4–5 about 98 in 100 later); the gap from A33/A35/A36 to A31 stretched the same way (A33→A31: lags 1–3 about 100 in 100 earlier, lags 4–5 about 99 in 100 later) [LAG003, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006, LAG007, LAG008]. A31 still follows the region: A33/A35/A36 run before A31 in about 98 of 100 later traces, against about 49 of 100 earlier, when the block ran in only half the cases [REL031, REL032, REL033, REL034, REL035, REL036].
- A04 became much rarer — present in about 31 of 100 traces earlier (61 of 200) and about 13 in 100 later (27 of 200) — and of its observed outgoing transitions, those to A16 fell from about 81 in 100 to about 36 in 100, with A15, A48, A46, A30 and A34 appearing as new destinations [OCC001, DIR002]. A06 and A10 became more common, from about 3 in 100 traces earlier to about 11–12 in 100 later [OCC002, OCC003].

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that an either-or choice between two activity groups was replaced by both groups running in sequence in almost every case. In the earlier window, A33, A35 and A36 formed one block and A37, A38 and A39 another; the two blocks were mutually exclusive (each running in about half of the cases, never together) and A31 followed whichever block ran. In the later window, all six activities run in nearly every case, A33/A35/A36 before A37/A38/A39 in about 97 of 100 traces, between A30 and A31 — that is, these activities became nearly mandatory and the exclusive routing between the groups gave way to a fixed relative order between them [OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, OCC007, OCC008, OCC009, REL039, REL046, REL031, DIR006, DIR007].

The signs are direct and mutually consistent, though they describe one underlying difference rather than independent confirmations: participation of all six activities roughly doubled; cross-group co-occurrence rose from 0 to about 98 in 100 with a stable order; A30's fan-out collapsed onto the first group; A31 lost its direct predecessors in the first group and now lies 4–5 event positions behind them. Screening qualified 78 items against fewer than 1 expected without change, so the set of differences as a whole is far beyond what random re-splits of an unchanged process produce [OCC004, OCC009, REL039, REL046, DIR006, DIR007, LAG003, LAG006].

Limitations: the earlier mutual exclusivity rests on zero co-occurrence in a 200-trace sample, so a co-occurrence that was possible but very rare earlier cannot be excluded; and because both internal orders occur within each group, the hypothesis covers the order between the two groups, not a fixed sequence of all six activities. The evidence also cannot fully separate "the second group now runs between the first group and A31" from "A31 now runs later"; the lost direct edges and the stretched lags favour the former reading [REL039, REL037, LAG006, DIR007].

Other differences: A04's decline (about 31 to about 13 of 100 traces) together with the drop of its A16 share from about 81 to about 36 of its outgoing transitions; the increased occurrence of A06 and A10 (about 3 to about 11–12 of 100 traces); and A15 and A16 now interleaving with the A33–A39 activities in about 40 of 100 traces, against about 15–18 in 100 earlier [OCC001, DIR002, OCC002, OCC003, REL013, REL019].
