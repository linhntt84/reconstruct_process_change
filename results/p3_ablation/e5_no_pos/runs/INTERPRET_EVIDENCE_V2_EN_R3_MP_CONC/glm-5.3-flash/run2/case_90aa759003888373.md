### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the block A14 → A15 → A20, which ran mandatorily between A36 and A21 in every earlier trace, became optional in the later window: in about 47 in 100 traces A36 now leads directly to A21 and the whole block is skipped, while in the remaining traces the original chain A36 → A14 → A15 → A20 → A21 is executed once, in the same order as before. [OCC001, DIR001, REL001, REL004, LAG001]

The reading is direct and consistent: the participation of all three block activities dropped in lockstep from 100% to about 53 in 100; all within-block pairs are absent together in the same roughly 47 in 100 traces, pointing to joint skipping rather than independent drops; the new A36-to-A21 link appears both as a routing share and as a lag-1 connection; and 14 qualifying items against only 0.33 expected by chance for the 66 tested units cannot be explained as a chance artefact of the split. The items overlap heavily, however, so they are converging views of one underlying behaviour, not independent confirmations. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, DIR001, REL003, LAG001]

An important competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that the later window mixes two kinds of cases (for instance a case type that never required the block) rather than reflecting one process in which the block became skippable per case; the joint skipping of all three activities is also inferred from pairwise relations rather than observed as a single three-activity pattern. [REL001, REL002, REL005]

Other differences: none — all qualifying items describe this single structural shift. [OCC001, DIR001, REL001, LAG001]
