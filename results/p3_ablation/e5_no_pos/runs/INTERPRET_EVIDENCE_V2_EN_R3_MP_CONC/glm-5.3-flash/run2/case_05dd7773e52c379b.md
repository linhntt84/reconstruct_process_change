### Observed differences

- Six activities became near-universal: in the earlier window, A28, A30 and A31 each ran in about 97 of 200 traces and A32, A33 and A34 in about 103 of 200; in the later window, all six run in about 197–199 of 200 traces, still at most once per case. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006]
- Two activity groups that were strict alternatives now run together: earlier, A28 and A32 (and equally A28/A33, A28/A34, A30/A32, A30/A33, A30/A34, A31/A32, A31/A33, A31/A34) never appeared in the same trace — exactly one of the two was present in every case; in the later window they appear together in about 98 in 100 traces, always with the A28/A30/A31-side activity first. [REL015, REL016, REL017, REL019, REL020, REL021, REL022, REL023, REL024]
- A25 now precedes each of the six in about 99 in 100 traces (earlier about half), and A26 now follows each of the six in about 99 in 100 traces (earlier about half). [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, REL007, REL008, REL009, REL010, REL011, REL012]
- Routing shifted accordingly: from A25, the direct share into A32/A33/A34 collapsed (A34 from about 19% to about 1% of A25's outgoing transitions; A33 no longer observed as a direct target) while the share into A28/A30/A31 roughly doubled (A30 from about 17% to about 38%); from A28, A30 and A31 the direct transition to A26 essentially disappeared (A28 to A26 from about 36% to under 1%) and new direct transitions into A32/A33/A34 appeared (roughly 8–13% each). [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004]
- Positional distances grew: the gap from A25 to A32/A33/A34 moved from 1–3 steps (spread roughly evenly) to 4–5 steps, and the gap from A28/A30/A31 to A26 likewise moved from 1–3 to 4–5 steps. [LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006]
- Within each triple the order stayed mixed: pairs inside {A28, A30, A31} and inside {A32, A33, A34} occur in both orders in similar proportions in both windows, while their joint presence rose from about half of traces to nearly all. [REL013, REL014, REL018, REL025, REL026, REL027]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that two three-activity blocks that were previously alternatives became sequentially composed. In the earlier window, a case ran A25, then either the block A28–A30–A31 or the block A32–A33–A34 (all three of the chosen block, in varying order), then A26; in the later window, nearly every case runs both blocks in sequence — A25, then A28/A30/A31 in varying order, then A32/A33/A34 in varying order, then A26. [REL015, REL019, REL022, OCC001, OCC004, DIR001, LAG001, LAG004]

The change is shown directly by relational evidence (cross-block pairs went from never co-occurring to co-occurring in about 98 in 100 traces with a fixed cross-block order) and is consistent across occurrence, routing and positional indicators: the transitions that short-circuited the sequence (A25 directly into the second block, the first block directly into A26) faded as first-block-to-second-block transitions appeared, and the inserted activities explain the distance growing to 4–5 steps. Screening supports the pattern as a whole: 43 qualifying items against roughly 0.55 expected if nothing had changed, so chance cannot account for it. [REL015, REL022, DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, LAG001, LAG004]

Limitations: the evidence is pairwise and aggregated, so the case-level order is inferred from about 98-in-100 pairwise shares; roughly 1–2 traces in 100 in the later window still show the old single-block pattern, and the evidence cannot rule out that a small residual of the earlier behaviour (or a mixture of trace populations) persists. Near-zero routing shares show a transition became rare, not that the edge was removed. [REL001, REL015, DIR002]

Other differences: none — all 43 evidence items describe this one restructuring; ordering within each triple remained without a fixed order in both windows. [REL013, REL025]
