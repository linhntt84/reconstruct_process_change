### Observed differences

- **The fixed order between two groups of activities was inverted.** In the earlier window, A25 and A29 ran before A20, A22 and A23 in almost every case: A25 preceded A20 in about 98 in 100 of their co-occurrences, A29 preceded A20 in about 98, A25 preceded A22 in about 97, A29 preceded A22 in about 96–97, and A25/A29 preceded A23 in about 99 in 100 — with every one of these pairs co-occurring in all traces of both windows. In the later window the order is inverted: A20 precedes A25 in about 97–98 in 100 and A20 precedes A29 in about 99–100 in 100, with the same flip for A22 and A23. [REL023, REL024, REL030, REL031, REL033, REL034]

- **A19, A21 and A24 also moved from before to after this group.** Earlier, A19 preceded A20 in about 95 in 100 of their roughly 31-in-100 co-occurrences, A21 preceded A20 in about 94 in 100, and A24 preceded A20 in all of about 36 in 100; in the later window, A20 precedes A19 in essentially all of about 33 in 100, A21 in about 97 in 100, and A24 in all of about 39 in 100, and the same reversal holds against A22, A23, A25 and A29. - **The successor roles of A25 and A30 were exchanged.** Earlier, A19, A21 and A24 led almost exclusively to A25 (about 93–97 in 100 of their outgoing transitions); in the later window they lead to A30 (about 92–97 in 100). Conversely, A20, A22 and A23 earlier had A30 among their main targets (about 29–37 in 100); later their main new target is A25 (about 28–37 in 100) and A30 is no longer prominent. In the later window A19 and A21 are typically followed immediately by A30 (about 95 in 100 of 58–65 pairs); the earlier profiles for these pairs rest on a single observation each. - **A29 and A40 now lead to different activities.** A29 earlier led mainly to A20, A23 and A22 (about 29–37 in 100 each) and later leads to A24 (about 38 in 100), A19 (about 32) and A21 (about 28); A40 earlier led to A21, A24 and A19 and later to A22, A20 and A23. Distances moved consistently: A29 reaches A30 at lag 4 in about 94 in 100 cases earlier versus lag 2 in about 94–95 in 100 later (about 200 cases each window), and A40 reaches A25 at lag 2 in about 90 in 100 earlier versus lags 4–5 in about 99 in 100 later (lags are event positions, not clock time). [DIR012, DIR013, LAG026, LAG029]

- **A08 is executed much less often.** It appeared in about 64 in 100 traces earlier (127 of 200; about 0.68 occurrences per trace) and in about 29 in 100 later (57 of 200; about 0.32 occurrences per trace). A15 now goes directly to A16 in about 88 in 100 of its outgoing transitions and to A08 in only about 7 in 100 (earlier about 61 and 39 in 100); A08's own outgoing transitions, earlier about 93 in 100 towards A16, are now spread over many activities, and the immediate A08→A16 adjacency (lag 1 in about 99 in 100 of 124 earlier pairs) loosened into a spread across lags 1–5 (30 later pairs). - **A04, A06 and A10 appear roughly three times as often.** Participation rose from about 7 to 21 in 100 traces for A04 (14→41 traces), about 7 to 24 for A06 (13→47) and about 9 to 18 for A10 (17→37); they also occur as new minor successors of A14, A43 and A45, whose main transitions became slightly less exclusive (A14→A15 about 97→80 in 100, A43→A44 about 96→85, A45→A46 about 97→83, with immediate A45→A46 falling from about 97 to 84 in 100). ### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that, in the later window, the ordering and routing among A19, A20, A21, A22, A23, A24, A25, A29, A30 and A40 were reorganised: the fixed order in which A25 and A29 (fed by A19, A21 and A24) ran before A20, A22 and A23 was reversed, so that A20, A22 and A23 now run first and A19, A21 and A24 run after them; and the successor targets of A25 and A30 were exchanged, with A19/A21/A24 now leading to A30 instead of A25, A20/A22/A23 now leading to A25 instead of A30, and A29 and A40 connecting to different successors than before. [REL016, REL021, REL022, REL023, REL024, REL030, REL031, REL033, REL034, REL035, DIR006, DIR007, DIR008, DIR009, DIR010, DIR011, DIR012, DIR013]

The reorganisation is shown directly by relational evidence rather than inferred from participation alone: complete order reversals in pairs that co-occur in 100 in 100 traces of both windows cannot arise from sampling differences or rare variants; the successor changes rest on roughly 60–205 observed outgoing transitions per window; the distance shifts agree with the new order; and screening qualified 85 control-flow items where roughly 1 would be expected without a change, with every item's difference larger than in 199 of 200 random re-splits. [REL023, REL030, DIR006, DIR011, LAG026, LAG029]

Limitations: the evidence determines pairwise orders and dominant successors but not the complete revised structure, so it cannot distinguish a reversal of one contiguous stretch of the process from a re-routing through different alternative sequences that produces the same pairwise orders; a few later-window regularities (immediate A19→A30 and A21→A30 in about 95 in 100 of 58–65 pairs, A29→A21 in about 98 in 100 of 58) have earlier profiles based on a single observation and were not relied upon. [LAG018, LAG020, LAG025]

Other differences: A08 was executed in about 64 in 100 cases in the earlier window and about 29 in 100 in the later one, and A15 now typically proceeds directly to A16 (about 88 in 100 of its outgoing transitions) instead of via A08 (about 7 in 100, earlier about 61 and 39) — consistent with A08 having become skippable on that path, though the evidence cannot establish why fewer cases include it. [OCC003, DIR002, DIR005, LAG001] The increased appearance of A04, A06 and A10, and the slightly reduced exclusivity of A14→A15, A43→A44 and A45→A46, are reported above but are not part of this hypothesis, and their small earlier counts (13–17 traces) leave their exact sizes uncertain. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC004, DIR014, DIR015]
