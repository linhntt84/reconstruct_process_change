### Observed differences

- The activities form two three-activity blocks that behave as alternatives. In the earlier window, A16, A17 and A18 each ran in about 75 in 100 traces, while A19, A20 and A21 each ran in about 25 in 100; in the later window these shares reversed, with A16–A18 down to about 25 in 100 and A19–A21 up to about 76 in 100. None of the six activities repeats within a trace in either window. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006]
- A15 occurs in every trace in both windows, and its outgoing flow redistributed: earlier, about 75% of its observed outgoing transitions led to A16–A18 (about 26%, 23% and 26%) and about 25% to A19–A21 (about 14%, 4% and 8%); later, only about 25% led to A16–A18 (about 8–9% each) while about 75% led to A19–A21 (about 25%, 22% and 29%). [DIR001]
- The relation to A15 flipped accordingly: earlier, A15 was followed by A16, A17 or A18 in about 75 in 100 traces and ran without them in about 25 in 100; later, A15 ran without them in about 76 in 100 and was followed by A19, A20 or A21 in about 76 in 100. Whenever A15 and a block member both occur, A15 comes first, in both windows. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006]
- The two blocks never occur in the same trace, in either window, and within each block the members appear in both orders in similar shares (no fixed internal order) before as well as after. [REL007, REL009, REL013, REL018, REL022, REL023, REL025]
- A25 also occurs in every trace in both windows and stays with whichever block runs: earlier it co-occurred with A16–A18 in about 75 in 100 traces (interleaved or repeated in about 32 in 100) and ran without A19–A21 in about 75 in 100; later it co-occurred with A19–A21 in about 76 in 100 traces (interleaved or repeated in about 36 in 100) and ran without A16–A18 in about 76 in 100. [REL012, REL017, REL021, REL024, REL026, REL027]
- A minor positional detail: among observed A15→A20 pairs, the most common distance was 3 event positions earlier (about 52 in 100), while later the distances spread over 1–3 positions with about 66 in 100 now at 1–2 positions. [LAG001]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a change in how often two existing alternative paths after A15 are taken. In the earlier window, roughly three in four traces continued from A15 into the block A16–A17–A18 — executed together in no fixed internal order, with A25 alongside and sometimes interleaved or repeated — and roughly one in four continued into A19–A20–A21; in the later window these shares reversed to roughly one in four and three in four. The blocks remain mutually exclusive alternatives, A15 precedes whichever block runs, and A25 accompanies it, in both windows. [DIR001, OCC001, OCC004, REL001, REL004, REL009, REL022]

Supporting signs: the redistribution is observed directly in A15's outgoing transition shares [DIR001]; all six block activities show a matching swing of about 50 percentage points in opposite directions [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006]; and the relational items show the exclusivity between the blocks and the ordering relative to A15 and A25 preserved while only the shares flip [REL001, REL004, REL009, REL012, REL022]. The 35 qualifying items are roughly 70 times the 0.49 expected without change across 98 tested units, so chance sampling cannot account for the ensemble, and each item's difference exceeds about 199 of 200 random re-splits of the pooled traces. [screening, DIR001]

Limitations: the items largely re-describe one underlying trace behaviour, so they are not independent confirmations; and the evidence cannot distinguish a changed routing probability within one process from a shift in the mix of two case variants (for example, one variant becoming dominant), since each window is a finite sample of 200 traces. [OCC001, OCC004]

Other differences: the A15→A20 distance shortened slightly, with about two-thirds of observed pairs now 1–2 event positions apart versus about half earlier; this is consistent with the same swap but is a minor redistribution on its own, and it is based on event positions rather than clock time. [LAG001]
