### Observed differences

- Before, A40 was followed by one of A14, A15 and A20 (about 38, 31 and 31 in 100 of its outgoing transitions respectively), and each of the three then led to A21 in every observed case, two positions after A40. In the later window, A40 leads directly to A21 in all cases, and A14, A15 and A20 no longer lead to A21; each now leads to A23 or A27 (A14 about 52/48, A15 about 54/46, A20 about 39/61 per 100). - Before, the chain A22 → A23 → A27 was unbroken: A22 was always followed directly by A23 and A23 directly by A27, so A27 always sat two positions after A22. In the later window, A22 leads directly to A23 in about 52 in 100 cases and to one of A14/A15/A20 in the remaining 48, while A23 leads directly to A27 in about 48 in 100 cases and to one of A14/A15/A20 in the remaining 52; A27 now always sits three positions after A22. [DIR004, DIR005, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003]

- The order between the branch activities and A21/A22 flipped: before, A14, A15 and A20 each ran before A21 and before A22 in essentially all traces where they co-occurred (about 30–38 in 100 traces each); in the later window, A21 and A22 each run before A14, A15 and A20 in about 30–37 in 100 traces. [REL001, REL002, REL004, REL005, REL006, REL007]

- Before, A14 and A20 always ran before A23 when both occurred; in the later window the order is mixed (A14 before A23 in about 16 in 100 traces and A23 before A14 in about 15; A23 before A20 in about 23 and A20 before A23 in about 15). [REL003, REL008]

- The choice among the three branch activities stayed roughly stable: A14 ran in about 38 in 100 traces before versus about 30 in 100 after, A15 about 31 versus 33, A20 about 32 versus 37. [REL001, REL004, REL006]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the three-way choice among A14, A15 and A20 moved from a position between A40 and A21 to a position after A22, inside the A22–A23–A27 segment. Before, every case ran A40, then one of A14/A15/A20, then A21, with the unbroken chain A22 → A23 → A27 later in the case; in the later window, A21 immediately follows A40, and the chosen branch activity runs after A22, sitting between A22 and A23 in roughly half of the cases (about 48 in 100) and between A23 and A27 in the other half (about 52 in 100). [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, REL001, REL002, REL004, REL005, REL006, REL007, REL008]

The supporting signs are direct and consistent: successor changes are observed at A40, A22, A23 and the three branch activities; all measured order relations between the branch activities and A21/A22 flip in the same direction; and the position-gap changes (A40–A21 from two to one, A22–A27 from two to three, A22–A23 and A23–A27 now split between one and two) match the same relocation, as does the newly mixed order between the branch activities and A23. Screening produced 18 qualifying items where about 0.36 would be expected without a change, so the ensemble is not what random re-splittings of stable behaviour produce. [DIR004, DIR005, DIR006, LAG001, LAG002, LAG004, REL001, REL003, REL006, REL008]

Limitations: the mutual order of A21 and A22 is not directly measured in either window, so the exact linear sequence around those two is inferred rather than observed; and because the evidence fixes only relative order, it cannot distinguish "the branch block moved later" from "A21 and A22 moved earlier" — both describe the same observed relations. [LAG002, LAG004, REL001]

Other differences: the selection shares among the three branch activities shifted slightly (A14 down from about 38 to 30 in 100 traces, A20 up from about 32 to 37), and in the later window A20 leads to A27 more often (about 61 in 100) than A14 or A15 do (about 48 and 46 in 100); these shifts are small next to the structural relocation. [REL001, REL004, REL006, DIR001, DIR002, DIR003]
