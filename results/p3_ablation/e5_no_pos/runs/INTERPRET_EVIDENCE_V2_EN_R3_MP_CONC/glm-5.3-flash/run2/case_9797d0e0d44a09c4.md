### Observed differences

- A19, A20 and A25 each appeared in about 47 in 100 traces in the earlier window (once per trace, no repetitions) but in about 995 in 1000 traces in the later window, still once per trace and without repetitions. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003]
- Outgoing routing from A41 changed from a near-even split — about 45 in 100 observed transitions to A19 and about 53 in 100 directly to A26 — to about 92 in 100 to A19 in the later window, with the direct move to A26 down to about 1 in 200. [DIR002]
- The distance between A41 and A26 shifted accordingly: one step apart in about 53 in 100 traces before versus about 1 in 200 after, and four steps apart in about 42 in 100 traces before versus about 89 in 100 after. [LAG001]
- The internal order is the same in both windows — A41 before A19, then A19 before A25, A25 before A20, and A20 before A26 — but this full ordering, which earlier could only occur in the roughly 47 in 100 traces containing the segment, holds in about 98 in 100 traces in the later window. [REL001, REL002, REL004, REL007, REL008, REL012, REL013]
- A20's outgoing behaviour is essentially stable, going to A26 in about 96 in 100 of its observed transitions before and about 99 in 100 after. [DIR001]
- A45 kept roughly the same overall frequency (present in about 60 in 100 traces before, 62 in 100 after) but was previously seen without A19 in about 33 in 100 traces, whereas in the later window it always co-occurs with A19; A42 occurs in essentially all traces in both windows, with its order relative to A19/A20/A25 mixed in both but interleaved-or-repeated orderings rising from about 17 in 100 to about 46 in 100 traces. ### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the segment A19 → A25 → A20, entered from A41 and followed by A26, changed from optional to essentially mandatory: in the earlier window it was executed in about 47 in 100 traces while A41 passed directly to A26 in the other half of the cases, whereas in the later window it is executed in about 995 in 1000 traces and the direct A41 → A26 bypass survives in only about 1 in 200 of A41's outgoing transitions. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, DIR002, LAG001]

The signs are direct and consistent: the participation of all three segment activities rose in lockstep, the routing split at A41 flipped from roughly even to almost entirely toward A19, and the A41–A26 gap moved from often adjacent to almost always four steps apart, which is what executing the segment on every trace produces; the unchanged internal order and the stable A20 → A26 edge indicate the segment itself was not reordered. The volume of qualifying items (21 control-flow items against roughly 0.61 expected for an unchanged process) makes chance sampling a poor explanation for the pattern as a whole. [OCC001, DIR002, LAG001, REL001, REL007, DIR001]

Limitations: the evidence cannot distinguish whether the bypass A41 → A26 was removed altogether or only became rare, and it cannot show whether this reflects a modified process or a shift in the mix of pre-existing case types between the windows; moreover, the participation, routing and lag items largely track the same underlying trace behaviour and are not independent confirmations.

Other differences: A45, previously observed both with and without A19 (without in about 33 in 100 traces), now appears only together with A19, which is consistent with A19's near-ubiquity rather than an independent change in A45; A42's ordering relative to the segment shows more interleaving after (about 46 in 100 traces versus about 17 in 100), a secondary difference not covered by the main hypothesis. [REL006, REL011, REL015, REL005, REL010, REL014]
