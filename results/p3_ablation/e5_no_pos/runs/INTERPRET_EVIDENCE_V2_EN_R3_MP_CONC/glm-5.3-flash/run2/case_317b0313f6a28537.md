### Observed differences

- The three activities A19, A20 and A25 now lead somewhere different. In the earlier window, the outgoing transitions of each split mainly between A28 and A32 (A19 about 47 and 48 in 100; A20 about 56 and 43; A25 about 50 and 45). In the later window, about 88–94 in 100 of their transitions go to A26, with only scattered alternatives. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003]
- A45 changed its routing in the matching way: earlier, about 85 in 100 of its outgoing transitions went to A26; later, about 85 in 100 go to A19, A20 or A25 (roughly 29, 29 and 27 in 100), and A26 was no longer observed among its transitions. [DIR006]
- The relative order of the block A19/A20/A25 and the pair A26/A27 flipped. Earlier, when A19 co-occurred with A26 (about 39 in 100 traces), A26 came first in about 38 in 100 and A19 never came first; later, A19 came first in about 36 in 100 and A26 never came first. The same flip holds for A20 and A25 against both A26 and A27, while co-occurrence stayed around 31–39 in 100 traces, so the flip is not driven by an activity appearing or disappearing. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006]
- The chain A27→A28→A32 became tight and lost its side branches: A27 to A28 within one step rose from about 48 to 98 in 100, A28 to A32 within one step from about 51 to 96 in 100, and A27 to A32 now typically takes two steps instead of three. Earlier, A19, A20 and A25 appeared as alternative successors of A27 (about 16–19 in 100 each) and of A28 (about 14–19 in 100); later they do not. [DIR004, DIR005, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006]
- The distance from A45 to A26 grew from one step (about 86 in 100 traces) to two or three steps (about 98 in 100), consistent with the block now sitting between them. [LAG007]
- A15 still occurs in every trace but repeats less: mean occurrences per trace fell from about 1.9 to about 1.6, and the share of traces in which it recurs fell from about 47 to 38 in 100. [OCC001]
- Three further qualified lag items (A19–A32, A25–A28, A25–A32) rest on only 1–3 after-window observations and are not interpreted here. [LAG001, LAG002, LAG003]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the group of activities A19, A20 and A25 was repositioned: earlier they ran after A26 and A27, branching mainly from A27 or A28 toward A32, while in the later window they run before A26, entered from A45, and A27, A28 and A32 form a tight, near-deterministic chain without them. The order flip is shown directly in six activity pairs with stable co-occurrence and full-trace support; the successor rewiring of the three activities and of A45 matches it; and the position shifts corroborate it (A45 to A26 pushed from one to two–three steps; A27/A28 to A32 tightened). Screening also shows the control-flow family as a whole far exceeds what chance produces (20 items qualified where about 0.56 were expected without change). [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006, REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006, LAG007]

Limitations: the relational evidence shows the relative order flipped and edges were rewired, but it cannot establish which side changed position in absolute terms, nor the block's exact placement relative to activities other than A26, A27, A28, A32 and A45; there is no outgoing profile for A26, so other entries into A26 cannot be checked. A mere shift in how often existing paths are taken is largely ruled out, since all involved activities occur in a substantial and stable share of traces in both windows while successor sets changed wholesale. [DIR001, DIR006, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003]

Other differences: A15 repeats less often per trace (mean about 1.9 down to about 1.6 occurrences, still present in every trace), which is not part of the hypothesis above. [OCC001]
