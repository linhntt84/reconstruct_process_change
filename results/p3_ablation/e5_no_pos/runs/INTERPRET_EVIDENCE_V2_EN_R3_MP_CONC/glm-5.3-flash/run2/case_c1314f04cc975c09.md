### Observed differences

- The activities A25, A26 and A31 were each performed exactly once in every before-window trace (mean 1.0 occurrence per trace, present in 100% of traces, never repeated). ### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that a cyclic, repeated execution involving A25, A26 and A31 was introduced: three activities that previously ran exactly once per trace in a fixed order (A25 before A31, with A31 proceeding to A20) are now repeated about 3.5 times per trace in roughly two-thirds of traces, A31 returns to A25 in about 70 in 100 of its outgoing transitions instead of proceeding to A20, and the previously fixed order between A25 and A31 became interleaved or repeated in about 68 in 100 traces. The signs are direct and mutually consistent: repetition is shown directly by the occurrence indicators, the new dominant A31-to-A25 transition by the direction indicator, and the breakdown of the fixed order by the relation indicator, with the positional spread corroborating; six items qualified against fewer than 0.3 expected by chance among 58 tested units, and each difference is unusual for random re-splits of the data (empirical strength about 0.995). [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, DIR001, REL001, LAG001]

Limitations: the outgoing transitions of A25 and A26 were not part of the evidence, so whether A26 lies inside the cycle and where the cycle eventually exits is inferred from the shared repetition pattern rather than from directly observed edges; the exit to A20 still occurs in about 30 in 100 of A31's outgoing transitions, so the repeated execution does not replace the original path in every trace. A competing explanation the evidence cannot fully rule out is independent repetition of the three activities rather than one coupled cycle, although the dominant A31-to-A25 redirection makes a coupled cycle the more specific reading. [DIR001, REL001]
