### Observed differences

- The sequence of two activity groups reversed. In the earlier window, A35, A36 and A41 ran before A26, and before A27, in almost every trace where they co-occurred (about 98–100 in 100); in the later window, A26 and A27 ran before A35, A36 and A41 in about 97–99 in 100 traces. Both groups still co-occurred in essentially all 200 traces in each window, so the activities did not become rarer — their order relative to each other flipped. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006]
- Routing moved with the reversal. Earlier, A25's outgoing transitions went into the group (about 39% to A35, 32% to A36, 27% to A41) and A26 led almost always straight to A30 (about 99 in 100); later, A25 led almost exclusively to A27 (about 98 in 100) and A26 fed the group (about 34% A35, 32% A36, 30% A41), whose members now exited to A30 (about 30–35% each) instead of to A27 (about 28–37% earlier, under 1% later). A25 was also almost never followed by A35, A36 or A41 within five steps in the later window (2 traces, against 200 earlier). [DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006, DIR007, LAG006, LAG007, LAG008]
- Event distances match this: A25 reached A27 about 4 positions later in about 94 in 100 earlier traces but immediately (1 position) in about 98–99 in 100 later traces; A26 reached A30 immediately in about 99 in 100 earlier traces but about 4 positions later in about 91 in 100 later traces. [LAG005, LAG009]
- Separately, A06 became less prominent: it appeared in about 27 in 100 traces earlier versus about 12–13 in 100 later, and its outgoing transitions shifted from about 86% towards A16 earlier to about 39% later, spread over many activities; A15's transition share to A06 fell from about 15% to about 3% while its share to A16 rose to about 95%. A02 moved the other way, appearing in about 4–5 in 100 traces earlier versus about 11–12 in 100 later. [OCC001, OCC002, DIR001, DIR002, LAG001, LAG002]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the placement of the activity group A35, A36, A41 relative to A26 and A27 was reversed: in the earlier window the group ran before A26/A27 (fed by A25 and exiting to A27, with A26 leading directly to A30), while in the later window A26/A27 ran first, with A25 flowing directly to A27 and A26 flowing through the group, which then exits to A30. The sign is direct — the relation indicators show the near-complete order reversal with both sides co-occurring in about 200 traces per window — and consistent, since the routing shares and the position distances (A25→A27 shortening from about 4 steps to 1; A26→A30 lengthening from 1 to about 4) point to the same rearrangement. The 27 qualifying control-flow items far exceed the fewer than 1 expected by chance among 143 tested units, so the ensemble is not what an unchanged process typically produces. Limitations: the six relation items and most routing and distance items describe one underlying reversal rather than independent confirmations, the internal order within the A35/A36/A41 group stays flexible in both windows, and an important competing explanation remains: the two windows may simply draw mostly from two different process variants rather than reflecting a modification of one process, which finite samples cannot fully rule out. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006, DIR007, LAG005, LAG009, screening]

Other differences: A06 participation dropped from about 27 to about 12–13 in 100 traces, with its outgoing profile dispersing (share to A16 from about 86% to about 39%) and A15's transitions to A06 falling from about 15% to about 3%; A02 participation rose from about 4–5 to about 11–12 in 100 traces. These participation changes are separate from the ordering hypothesis. [OCC001, OCC002, DIR001, DIR002, LAG001, LAG002]
