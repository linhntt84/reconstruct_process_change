### Observed differences

- Activities A30, A31 and A36 were each executed essentially exactly once per case before (mean about 1.0 occurrence per trace, present in all traces, repeats in at most about 1–2 in 100 traces). In the later window, each occurs about 3.5 times per trace on average and repeats within the same trace in about 68 in 100 traces, while still appearing in every trace. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003]
- The successor profile of A36 changed sharply: before, about 97 in 100 of A36's observed outgoing transitions led to A25; in the later window, about 70 in 100 lead to A30 and only about 28 in 100 still lead to A25, which is consistent with a back edge returning from A36 to A30. [DIR001]
- The fixed ordering among A14, A30 and A36 broke down: before, the three always co-occurred and the order A14 before A30, and A30 before A36, held in about 98–99 in 100 traces; in the later window co-occurrence is still universal, but in about 68 in 100 traces each pair is interleaved or repeated rather than in a fixed order. [REL003, REL004, REL005]
- A05's relation to A30 and A36 weakened: before, A05 co-occurred with them in about 55 in 100 traces and preceded them in about 53–54 in 100; in the later window it precedes them in only about 16 in 100 traces, with interleaving in about 35 in 100. [REL001, REL002]
- The position gap between A30 and A36, almost always 2 event positions before, became spread out in the later window: only about 12 in 100 pairs are still 2 apart, and some are 4–5 or more apart. [LAG002]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the block containing A30, A31 and A36 changed from being executed once per case to being repeated about three to four times per case, most plausibly through a loop that returns from A36 to A30; as a result, the previously fixed sequence A14 → A30 → A36 became interleaved or repeated in roughly two thirds of traces. This rests on direct occurrence evidence (once per trace rising to about 3.5 per trace, repeats rising from near zero to about 68 in 100 traces, with participation unchanged at 100%), the redirected successor profile of A36 towards A30, and the matching breakdown of the fixed pairwise order. The 11 qualifying control-flow items compare with fewer than 1 expected by chance, and every item's difference exceeds about 995 in 1000 random re-splits of the pooled data; the items all describe one coherent behaviour rather than isolated anomalies. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, DIR001, REL003, REL004, REL005, LAG002]

Limitations: the relation and gap items largely mirror the same repetition behaviour rather than constituting independent confirmations. The evidence does not show whether A31 belongs to the same loop as A30 and A36, since A31 has no relational or successor data, nor whether A14 and A05 lie inside the repeated block or before it. An important competing explanation that cannot be fully ruled out is a structure other than a simple A36 → A30 cycle — for example, re-entry into the block from elsewhere — because only A36's outgoing transitions are observed. [OCC002, DIR001, REL003]

Other differences: the position gap between A05 and A14 shifted from mostly 1–3 event positions to mostly 2–3 (based on roughly 100 paired observations per window); this is plausibly a side effect of the repetition but the evidence cannot confirm it. [LAG001]
