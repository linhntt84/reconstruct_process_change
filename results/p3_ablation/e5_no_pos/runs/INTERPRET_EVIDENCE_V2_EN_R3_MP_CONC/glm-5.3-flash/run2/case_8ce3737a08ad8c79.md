### Observed differences

- The clearest difference is a reversal at the branch point A20, which appears in all traces in both windows. In the earlier window its outgoing transitions went mainly to A24 (about 29%), A25 (about 24%) and A26 (about 29%), with only about 18% combined going to A21, A22 and A23; in the later window about 79% go to A21 (about 29%), A22 (about 21%) and A23 (about 29%), and only about 21% to A24–A26. [DIR003]
- Trace participation mirrors this reversal: A24, A25 and A26 each ran in about 82 in 100 traces earlier but only about 21 in 100 later, while A21, A22 and A23 rose from about 19 in 100 to about 79–80 in 100; none of the six activities repeats within a trace in either window. [OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, OCC007]
- The two groups behave as internally unordered, mutually exclusive blocks in both windows. Earlier, A24, A25 and A26 co-occurred in about 82% of traces with no fixed order between them (A24 before A25 in about 47% of traces, A25 before A24 in about 34%) and almost never co-occurred with A21–A23; in the later window A21, A22 and A23 co-occur in about 79% of traces, again in both orders (about 37–42% each way), and almost never co-occur with A24–A26. [REL015, REL023, REL036, REL017, REL024]
- Partner relations switched with the blocks: A30 co-occurred with A24–A26 in about 82% of traces earlier and with A21–A23 in about 79% later (A30–A24 co-occurrence about 82% → 21%; A30–A21 about 19% → 79%), and A32/A33 similarly moved from co-occurring with A24–A26 (about 52%) to co-occurring with A21–A23 (about 55–56%). [REL020, REL038, REL021, REL039]
- A10 became rarer: outgoing transitions from A10 appear in about 14 traces in the later window versus about 33 earlier, its earlier near-exclusive link to A33 (about 97% of those transitions) dissolved into a spread over several successors, A32's transitions to A10 fell from about 20% to about 1%, and A10–A32/A33 co-occurrence dropped from about 17% to about 5%; a positional-gap item also shows A32→A10 moving from mostly immediate to distances of 2–3 event positions, but only four later observations back it. [DIR002, DIR004, REL007, REL008, LAG001]
- A02 appeared in about 2 in 100 traces earlier and about 9 in 100 later, with a completely different successor profile (earlier only A11, A20 and A01; later spread over ten successors); both profiles rest on very few observations (4 and 18 outgoing transitions). [OCC001, DIR001]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the choice between two existing alternative blocks after A20 was re-weighted: the A21–A23 block, whose activities run together in a mixed order, was executed in about 19 in 100 traces in the earlier window and about 79 in 100 in the later window, while the A24–A26 block fell from about 82 to about 21 in 100, with A30 and A32/A33 now accompanying the A21–A23 block instead of A24–A26. This rests on direct evidence at the branch point itself (A20's successor shares reversed, observed in all 200 traces per window), consistent participation and relation items for all six activities, and a screening result far above chance (59 qualifying items against roughly 0.84 expected if nothing had changed). All six activities and both blocks are well observed in both windows, their mutual exclusivity and internal mixed ordering are unchanged, and repetitions do not change — the conditions under which a shift in how often existing paths are taken applies. Limitations: the later shares are estimates from 200 traces per window, and the evidence cannot distinguish a re-weighting of the two existing branches at A20 from the two groups of activities exchanging their roles in the branch, nor show whether the re-weighting originates in A20's routing or in an upstream case characteristic the log does not record. [DIR003, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, OCC007, REL015, REL023, REL036, REL020, REL038, REL021, REL039]

Other differences: A10 became less frequent and lost its near-exclusive transition to A33 and its co-occurrence with A32/A33, and A02 became slightly more frequent with a different successor profile; both observations rest on few later cases (about 14 and 18 outgoing transitions respectively) and are not part of the main hypothesis.
