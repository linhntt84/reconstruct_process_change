### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, A30, A31 and A36 each occurred about 3.5 times per trace and appeared in every trace; in the later window each occurs almost exactly once per trace (means about 1.0), still in every trace — the repetition stopped, while participation stayed complete. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003]
- Within-trace recurrence collapsed accordingly: before, A30 occurred more than once per trace in about 75 in 100 traces (A31 and A36 about 76 in 100); in the later window this holds for only about 1 in 100 traces for each of the three. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003]
- Routing out of A36 changed: before, about 70 in 100 of A36's outgoing transitions led back to A30 and about 28 in 100 led to A25; in the later window about 92 in 100 lead to A25 and only about 4 in 100 return to A30. [DIR002]
- A30's own outgoing behaviour barely moved: A30 → A31 carries about 99 in 100 of its outgoing transitions in the earlier window and about 97 in 100 in the later one. [DIR001]
- The relationship between A30 and A36 became fixed: before, the two were interleaved or repeated in about 76 in 100 traces (A30 strictly first in about 25 in 100); in the later window A30 comes before A36 in about 98 in 100 traces. [REL001]
- In the later window, A36 follows A30 two positions later in about 89 in 100 of the observed instances of this pair; before, no single distance dominated (roughly 12 in 100 at two positions, 5 in 100 at five). [LAG001]
- The old cycle's back-edges nearly vanished: A36 followed by A30 within five steps appears in about 150 of 200 traces before but in only 1 trace after, and A36 repeating itself within five steps in about 150 of 200 traces before versus 2 traces after (the after-window counts are too small for reliable shares). [LAG002, LAG003]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the repetitive cycle through A30, A31 and A36 stopped repeating: in the earlier window the flow passed through A30 → A31 and A36 about three to four times per trace, with A36 returning to A30 in about 70 in 100 of its outgoing transitions and otherwise leaving to A25, whereas in the later window each of the three activities runs once per trace in a fixed order (A30 first, A36 two positions later) and the flow leaves the group via A25 in about 92 in 100 of A36's outgoing transitions. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, DIR002, REL001, LAG001]

Supporting signs: participation is unchanged (all three activities appear in every trace in both windows, so none became optional); the repetition collapse is measured directly on all three activities; the return edge A36 → A30 and the self-repeat of A36 both fell from about 150 of 200 traces to near zero; every item shows a difference larger than in all but about 1 in 200 random re-splits; and nine items qualified against roughly 0.5 expected by chance, far more than an unchanged process typically produces. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, DIR002, LAG002, LAG003]

Limitations: the evidence cannot distinguish whether the cycle was removed or is now only rarely taken, since a small return share from A36 to A30 (about 4 in 100 of its outgoing transitions) remains in the later window; the connection from A31 onwards is not directly observed (no outgoing profile for A31 exists in the evidence), so the exact loop body is inferred rather than shown; and two lag items rest on only 1–2 after-window observations. [DIR002, LAG002, LAG003]

Other differences: A30's rare outgoing transitions show minor redistribution, with a few targets at well under 1 in 100 appearing or disappearing between the windows, while its dominant edge to A31 stays stable; this looks like background variation around the main difference rather than a separate behavioural change. [DIR001]
