### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, the eight activities covered by the evidence (A26, A17, A15, A18, A19, A21, A20, A27) ran as one fixed sequence: every measured pair co-occurred in 100 in 100 traces and always in the same order, each source activity had exactly one observed successor (A26→A17, A17→A15, A15→A18, A18→A19, A19→A21, A21→A20, A20→A27), and each pair was always the same number of event positions apart. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006, DIR007, REL001, LAG001, LAG017]
- In the later window, the same activities still occur together in every trace, but the fixed order is gone: pairs now appear in both orders across cases — A15 precedes A19 in only about 26 in 100 cases (previously always), A18 precedes A19 in about 15 in 100 (previously always), and A15 precedes A21 in about half of the cases (previously always); no interleaving or repetition within a case was observed. [REL001, REL003, REL006, REL008]
- Successor choices spread out as well: A15 now leads to A18 in about 68 in 100 of its outgoing transitions and to A19, A20 or A21 in about 10 in 100 each; A18 now leads most often to A27 (about 52 in 100) instead of always to A19; A26 splits about evenly between A17 (53 in 100) and A19 (47 in 100). [DIR001, DIR003, DIR007]
- Spacing became variable: pairs that were always a fixed number of positions apart now occur at distances of one to five — A15 to A20 was always four positions apart and is now spread over one to four; A18 to A27 was always four and is now directly adjacent in about 52 in 100 cases; several ordered pairs are also seen within five steps in fewer traces (A15 then A19 in 52 of 200 later traces versus 200 of 200 earlier), which matches the reversed-order shares. [LAG003, LAG013, LAG002, REL001]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the ordering constraints among A15, A17, A18, A19, A20, A21, A26 and A27 were loosened in the later window: the earlier fixed chain, in which every pair kept one order and a fixed spacing, gave way to a flexible order in which all eight activities still run in every case, but pairs appear in either order and at varying distances. The relational evidence shows this directly — co-occurrence stays at 100 in 100 for every measured pair while order shares move from 100/0 to mixed (about 15/85 for A18–A19, about 50/50 for A15–A21) — and the positional and successor evidence agree: fixed gaps became distributions over one to five positions, and single-successor profiles became multi-successor. The counts of ordered pairs observed within five steps match the new order shares almost exactly (106 of 200 later traces for A17 before A19 against 53 in 100), so the items describe one coherent behaviour rather than scattered noise; participation itself did not change, so this is a reordering, not activities being added, removed or skipped. [REL001, REL003, REL006, REL008, LAG005, LAG013, LAG008, DIR001, DIR003, DIR007]

The volume of qualifying evidence far exceeds what an unchanged process produces: 39 control-flow items qualified where about 0.43 would be expected by chance, and each item's difference is larger than in about 995 of 1000 random re-splits of the pooled traces. A competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that the later window contains a small set of fixed sequence variants rather than free per-case ordering — both would produce the same mixed order shares here. The evidence is also limited to the measured activity pairs and to event positions, so it says nothing about clock-time timing or about parts of the process outside these activities. [screening, DIR001, REL001, LAG002]
