### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, A27 ran in every case (200 of 200) and A26 led to A27 in about 95 in 100 of its observed outgoing transitions; in the later window A27 runs in only about 52 in 100 cases (103 of 200), and A26 leads to A27 in about 51 in 100 of its outgoing transitions, with the remainder going to A32 (about 15 in 100), A33 (about 18) and A38 (about 17). [OCC003, DIR006]
- A32, A33 and A38 do not appear at all in the earlier window (0 of 200 traces each) but each occurs in about half of the later cases (about 98–100 of 200). [OCC004, OCC005, OCC006]
- The three new activities run together: each pair of them co-occurs in about 49 in 100 later cases, in either order (roughly 21–27 in 100 each way), whereas they never co-occurred earlier. [REL028, REL029, REL030]
- A27 and each of the three new activities now behave as alternatives: a later case contains A27 or one of them (about 50 versus 48–49 in 100) and both together in only about 1 in 100, while earlier A27 was always present and they never were. [REL022, REL023, REL024]
- The new group takes the place A27 previously held before A28: A32, A33 and A38 each precede A28 in about 48 in 100 later cases (never earlier), and the distance from A26 to A28, previously 2 steps in about 90 in 100 cases, is now about 50 in 100 at 2 steps and about 46 in 100 at 4 steps, consistent with three activities now lying between them on the longer route; when A27 does run, it still precedes A28 as before (about 52 in 100 later cases, previously about 98 in 100). ### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that, in the later window, about half of the cases take a new route in which the group A32–A33–A38 — three activities executed together, without a fixed internal order — runs between A26 and A28, while A27, previously executed in every case and always preceding A28, became optional and now runs in only about half of the cases; A27 and the new group act as alternatives that rarely appear in the same case. [OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, DIR006, REL021, REL022, REL023, REL024, REL025, REL026, REL027, REL028, REL029, REL030, LAG003]

The hypothesis is direct and consistent: the routing split at A26 is observed outright, the three activities appear with substantial support (about 98–100 of 200 cases each) after being absent from all 200 earlier cases, each precedes A28, and the A26-to-A28 distance splits from 2 steps to a mix of 2 and 4 steps, matching the longer route. Fifty control-flow items qualified against fewer than one expected without a change, so chance sampling cannot explain the pattern as a whole; note, however, that the many relation items largely re-describe one underlying trace difference rather than confirming it independently. The evidence cannot show what determines which route a case takes, and it cannot fully rule out that the earlier window simply did not exercise an already existing path, since zero participation there means only "not observed" — though 200 earlier cases without a single occurrence make an unchanged process unlikely. [DIR006, DIR007, DIR008, DIR009, LAG003, REL025, OCC004]

Other differences: A04 became about three times as frequent and gained a fixed position, now running directly after A14 and before A15, where earlier it was rare and its outgoing transitions were spread across many activities; A06 declined to about 4 in 100 cases and A10's outgoing transitions were redistributed, both on small support. These are not part of the main hypothesis. [OCC001, DIR001, DIR004, LAG001, LAG002, OCC002, DIR002, DIR003]
