### Observed differences

- The fixed order between the activities A26/A27 and the three-activity block A35/A36/A41 reversed. In the earlier window A26 came before A35, A36 and A41 in 98–100 in 100 traces, and A27 likewise came before them in 98–100 in 100; in the later window the block comes first, with A35, A36 and A41 before A26 in 99–99.5 in 100 traces and before A27 in 98–98.5 in 100. All six pairs still occur together in 100 in 100 traces in both windows, so participation did not change. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006]

- Successor behaviour around this segment changed accordingly. Before, A25 led to A27 in about 99 in 100 outgoing transitions; in the later window A25's outgoing transitions split roughly equally among A36, A41 and A35 (about 33 each), with A27 nearly unobserved (about 0.5 in 100). Before, A26 led to A41, A35 or A36 (about 37, 34 and 27.5 in 100); in the later window A26 leads to A30 in about 98.5 in 100. Before, A35, A36 and A41 each led to A30 in about 28–37 in 100 of their outgoing transitions; in the later window these transitions to A30 have practically disappeared (at most about 0.5 in 100) and each block activity instead leads to A27 in about 31–34 in 100, while transitions within the block continue in both windows. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005]

- Event distances moved with the reversal. A25 to A27 stretched from a 1-step gap in about 99 in 100 cases to gaps of 4 steps (about 92 in 100) or 5 steps (about 6 in 100); A26 to A30 contracted from gaps of 4–5 steps (about 99 in 100) to immediately consecutive (about 98.5 in 100). [LAG001, LAG003]

- The observed ordered pairs flip in the same direction: pairs measured from A26 into the block and from the block to A30 had about 200 observations in the earlier window and only 1–2 in the later one, while A25 to A35 and A35 to A27 had a single observation before and about 199 after, now spread over gaps of 1–3 steps. [LAG002, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006, LAG007, LAG008, LAG009, LAG010]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the ordering of this segment reversed: in the earlier window the flow ran A25, then immediately A27, with A26 leading into the block A35/A36/A41 (executed in varying internal order) which ended in A30; in the later window A25 leads into the block A35/A36/A41, the block leads to A27, and A26 immediately precedes A30. In other words, the three activities A35, A36, A41 and the two activities A26/A27 exchanged places, while A25 remained the entry to and A30 the exit from the segment, and the participation of all involved activities stayed unchanged. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, LAG001, LAG003]

This is unlikely to be a sampling artefact: 21 control-flow items qualified against roughly 0.6 expected by chance across the 119 tested units — about 35 times the chance expectation — and every item's difference exceeds about 995 of 1000 random re-splits of the pooled data. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006, LAG007, LAG008, LAG009, LAG010]

Limitations: the direction, relation and gap items are computed from the same traces and are not independent confirmations, and several gap items rest on only 1–2 observations in one window. The evidence also cannot determine which group moved — an earlier execution of A35/A36/A41 and a later execution of A26/A27 describe the data equally well — and, since gaps are measured in event positions rather than clock time, no conclusion about timing is implied. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, LAG001, LAG003]
