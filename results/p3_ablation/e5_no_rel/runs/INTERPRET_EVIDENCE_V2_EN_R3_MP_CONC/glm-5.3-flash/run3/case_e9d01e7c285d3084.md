### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, A30, A31 and A36 each occurred almost exactly once per trace (means 1.00–1.02, present in every trace, repeated in at most about 1–2 in 100 traces). In the later window, each occurs about 3.5 times per trace and is repeated in about 68 in 100 traces, while still appearing in every trace — the activities did not become optional; they became repeated [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003].
- The outgoing flow of A36 changed sharply: before, about 97 in 100 of A36's observed outgoing transitions led to A25; in the later window about 70 in 100 lead to A30 and only about 28 in 100 to A25. The number of observed outgoing transitions of A36 rose from about 200 to about 700, in line with its higher frequency [DIR001].
- The tight A30→A36 adjacency largely dissolved: before, A36 followed A30 exactly two steps later in about 97 in 100 of the observed pairs; in the later window only about 12 in 100 pairs sit at that two-step gap, and lags of one to five steps together cover only about 17 in 100 pairs, so the two activities are now usually separated by more steps [LAG002].
- A30 and A36 now occur later and far more variably within traces (by event position, not clock time): A30's mean relative position moved from about 0.41 to 0.44 with its spread roughly doubling (about 0.07 to 0.12); A36's spread also roughly doubled (about 0.07 to 0.12), with its 75th percentile moving from about 0.52 to 0.57 [POS002, POS003].
- Separately, the gap between A05 and A14 grew: before, A14 followed A05 within one or two steps in about 71 in 100 cases; in the later window the typical gap is two or three steps (about 82 in 100), and A14 also sits slightly earlier in the trace (mean relative position about 0.14 to 0.12) with somewhat more spread [LAG001, POS001].

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the sequence involving A30, A31 and A36, previously executed exactly once in each trace, is now executed several times per trace: in the later window these three activities occur about 3.5 times per trace (before: once), repeat in about 68 in 100 traces (before: essentially never), and A36 now typically leads back to A30 (about 70 in 100 of its outgoing transitions, where before about 97 in 100 led on to A25), so these activities recur within a trace instead of the flow passing on to A25. Supporting signs: the repetition is measured directly for all three activities with participation unchanged at 100% in both windows; the redirection at A36 is directly observed in its transition shares; the once near-universal two-step A30→A36 gap dissolved and the positions of A30 and A36 spread later, both consistent with repetition. Nine control-flow items qualified against fewer than 1 expected without a change, and each difference is larger than in all but about 1 in 200 random re-splits, so the ensemble is not what a stable process typically produces. Limitations: the evidence contains no co-occurrence items, so it cannot show whether A30, A31 and A36 repeat together as one block or repeat independently of each other; and in roughly 32 in 100 traces each activity still occurs only once, so the repetition does not extend to all cases. A competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that the increased repetitions and the redirection from A25 to A30 are two separate changes rather than one connected restructuring, since no observed relation ties A31's repetition to the A36→A30 redirection [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, DIR001, LAG002, POS002, POS003].

Other differences: The A05→A14 gap moved from one–two steps to two–three steps, and A14 sits slightly earlier and more variably within traces; these involve activities with no evidenced link to A30, A31 or A36 and are not part of the main hypothesis [LAG001, POS001].
