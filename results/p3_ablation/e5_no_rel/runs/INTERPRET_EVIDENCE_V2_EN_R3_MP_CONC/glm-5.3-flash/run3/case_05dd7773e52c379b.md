### Observed differences

- Six activities became near-universal. In the earlier window, A28, A30 and A31 each appeared in about 49 in 100 traces, and A32, A33 and A34 in about 52 in 100; in the later window, all six appear in essentially every trace (197–199 of 200). None of the six occurs more than once per trace in either window. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006]
- The entry point A25 stopped routing into the second group. Before, its observed outgoing transitions were spread almost evenly over all six activities (about 0.15–0.19 each); in the later window, about 99 in 100 of them lead to A28, A30 or A31 (about 0.30–0.38 each), with only traces of transitions to A32 and A34 and none observed to A33. [DIR001]
- The first group's exits were rerouted. Before, A28, A30 and A31 each led mainly to A26 and to the other two members of their own group (shares about 0.30–0.36); in the later window their direct transitions to A26 disappeared or fell below 0.01, and new transitions to A32, A33 and A34 appeared (about 0.08–0.13 each). [DIR002, DIR003, DIR004]
- Positions shifted accordingly: A28, A30 and A31 now occur earlier in the trace (mean relative position about 0.74 before, 0.68 after), A32, A33 and A34 later (about 0.73 to 0.77), and A26 later still (about 0.80 to 0.83); A25 itself moved slightly earlier (about 0.67 to 0.62). [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006, POS007, POS008]
- Step distances confirm the reshuffle: A32, A33 and A34 now follow A25 by 4–5 steps (before, 1–3 steps), and A26 now follows A28, A30 and A31 by 4–5 steps (before, 1–3 steps). [LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that two activity groups that were previously alternative branches are now executed one after the other in the same cases. In the earlier window, a case ran either the group A28–A30–A31 (about 49 in 100 cases, entered from A25 and followed by A26) or the group A32–A33–A34 (about 52 in 100 cases); in the later window, both groups run in almost every case — first A28/A30/A31, then A32/A33/A34, with A26 after the second group. The previously optional groups thus became near-mandatory, and the second group was placed after the first rather than instead of it. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006]

The hypothesis is direct and consistent across indicator types: participation, routing, position and step-distance items all point the same way, and 24 control-flow items qualified against roughly 0.45 expected for an unchanged process, so chance sampling cannot account for the pattern as a whole; each item also exceeds the difference seen in 199 of 200 random re-splits of the pooled data. [DIR001, POS003, POS006, LAG001, LAG004]

Limitations: the outgoing transitions of A32, A33 and A34 were not profiled, so how the second group connects onward to A26 is inferred from positions and step distances rather than observed edges, and routing shares are computed over outgoing transitions rather than cases, so exact per-case branch probabilities cannot be reconstructed. A competing explanation the evidence cannot fully exclude is that the two windows capture different process variants rather than a modification of one process, although the uniform direction of all 24 items makes a pure sampling artifact unlikely. [POS006, POS008, LAG006]
