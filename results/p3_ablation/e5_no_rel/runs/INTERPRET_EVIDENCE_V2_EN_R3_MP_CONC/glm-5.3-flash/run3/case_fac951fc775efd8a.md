### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a reordering of the control flow in which two blocks of activities exchanged their place in the case. The block A19–A21–A24, which previously ran late and handed over to A30, now runs early — reached from A40 and, newly, from A02 — and hands over to A25; the block A20–A22–A23, which previously ran early and handed over to A25, now runs late — reached from A29 — and hands over to A30. As part of this, A02 turned from a rare mid-case step into a frequent early step (8% vs 34% of traces) feeding the relocated A19/A21/A24 block. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006, DIR007, DIR008, DIR009, POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006, POS007, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006, LAG007, OCC001]

The successor changes are observed directly on the outgoing transitions of the activities involved and are nearly total, the two blocks move in opposite directions in event position, and the step-distance changes match the new adjacencies (A29→A30 stretched from about 2 to 4 steps, A40→A25 shortened from about 4 to 2–3 steps, A02→A25 at 2 steps). Participation of the moved activities stayed broadly stable, which fits a reordering rather than activities being added or removed. Screening also qualified 28 items against roughly 0.6 expected if nothing had changed, so the collection as a whole is not what a stable process typically produces. [DIR002, DIR004, DIR007, DIR008, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006, POS007, LAG001, LAG004, LAG007]

Limitations: positions and step distances are measured in event order, not clock time, so nothing follows about timing; and the evidence cannot distinguish a reordering applied within each case from a shift in the mix of process variants (for example a new variant replacing an old one), which would look the same at this level. [POS002, POS003, LAG004, DIR002]
