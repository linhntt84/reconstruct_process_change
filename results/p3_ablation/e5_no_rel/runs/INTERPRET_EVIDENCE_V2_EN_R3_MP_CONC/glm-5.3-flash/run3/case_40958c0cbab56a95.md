### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, A26, A27 and A28 each ran in every trace (200 of 200, about once per trace); in the later window they appear in only about 2 in 100 traces (3 of 200). [OCC005, OCC006, OCC007]
- A19, A20 and A21 were rare before (1–2 in 100 traces each) and are common in the later window: about 30, 33 and 38 in 100 traces respectively, so together they cover essentially every later trace. [OCC002, OCC003, OCC004]
- A44, present in all traces in both windows, redirected its follow-up activity: before, about 82 in 100 of its outgoing transitions went to A26, A27 or A28 (roughly a quarter each); in the later window, about 79 in 100 go to A19, A20 or A21 (roughly 25–27 in 100 each). [DIR007]
- In the later window, A19, A20 and A21 each pass control almost always to A29 (about 95 in 100 of their outgoing transitions); their few earlier outgoing observations (2–4 per activity) pointed to other activities instead. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003]
- Before, A26, A27 and A28 formed an interconnected cluster, each leading mostly to one of the other two or to A29 (shares of roughly 28–35 in 100); afterwards only about 3 outgoing observations exist for each. [DIR004, DIR005, DIR006]
- The step distance from A44 to A29 shrank: before it was mostly 4–5 steps (about 78 and 22 in 100 of 195 observations), in the later window mostly 2 steps (about 79 in 100) or 3 (about 19 in 100). [LAG009]
- The new activities occupy a similar trace position to the old cluster (medians around 0.36–0.37 of the trace in both windows), and A29, still present in every trace, now sits at a slightly earlier event position (mean about 0.44 before, 0.40 after). [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006, POS007]
- Separately, A10's participation rose from about 14 in 100 traces (27 of 200) to about 23 in 100 (45 of 200). [OCC001]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the segment between A44 and A29 was restructured by replacement: the three-activity cluster A26, A27, A28, which executed once in every earlier trace in varying internal order and handed over to A29, is nearly absent in the later window, and in its place each later trace typically executes one of A19, A20, A21 — about 30, 33 and 38 in 100 traces respectively — each leading almost directly to A29. [OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, OCC007, DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006, DIR007, LAG009]

Supporting signs: the successor profile of A44 flipped from the old cluster to the new activities with strong observation in both windows (about 200 outgoing transitions on each side); the participation swings (100 down to 2 in 100 traces) are far beyond what sampling variation in 200-trace windows produces; 31 items qualified against roughly 0.64 expected by chance for the 128 tested units; and the A44→A29 distance dropping from 4–5 to 2–3 steps, together with A29's slightly earlier position, fits a shorter replacement segment. [DIR007, LAG009, POS007]

Limitations: the earlier routing of A19, A20 and A21 and the later routing of A26, A27 and A28 rest on only 2–4 observations each, so their behaviour in the respective other window is essentially unobserved; the evidence also cannot distinguish whether the old cluster was removed outright or survives at a low rate of about 2 in 100 traces. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006, OCC005, OCC006, OCC007]

An important competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out: because A19, A20 and A21 were almost unobserved before, they may already have run on other, rare paths and merely become frequent, rather than being inserted as a replacement for the old cluster. [OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, DIR001, DIR002, DIR003]

Other differences: A10's trace participation increased from about 14 to about 23 in 100 traces; this increase is not part of the replacement hypothesis above. [OCC001]
