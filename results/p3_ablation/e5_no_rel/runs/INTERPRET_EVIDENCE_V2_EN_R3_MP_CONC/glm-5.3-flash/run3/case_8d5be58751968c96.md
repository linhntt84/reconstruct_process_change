### Observed differences

- A group of six activities ran in every case before but in only about half of the cases afterwards: A33, A35, A36, A37, A38 and A39 each appeared in 100 in 100 traces in the earlier window (exactly once per case), but in the later window in only about 47 in 100 (A33, A35, A36) to about 56 in 100 (A37, A38, A39), still at most once per case and essentially never repeated. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006]
- Entry into the group widened: before, A30's observed outgoing transitions went almost only to A33 (about 34%), A35 (about 36%) and A36 (about 29%); in the later window they spread roughly evenly over all six activities (about 13–20% each), including direct flows to A37, A38 and A39 that were not observed before. - The hand-over inside the group largely stopped: before, A33, A35 and A36 each sent roughly 30% of their observed outgoing transitions on to A37, A38 or A39 (about 10–14% to each); in the later window about 95% of their outgoing transitions stay within A33/A35/A36 or go to A31, with the flows to A37/A38/A39 down to about 1% or unobserved. - Co-occurrence across the two trios collapsed: an A33/A35/A36 activity followed within five steps by an A37/A38/A39 activity appeared in about 196–200 of 200 earlier cases but in only 4–5 later cases (very few later observations, so weak on its own). - The two trios converged in position: A33/A35/A36 moved later in the case (mean relative position about 0.68 → 0.73) while A37/A38/A39 moved slightly earlier (about 0.76–0.77 → 0.74); these are event positions, not clock times. [POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006, POS007, POS008]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the six-activity passage between A30 and A31 — A33, A35, A36, A37, A38 and A39 — changed from a mandatory block, executed once in every case, into a shorter, partially executed passage: in the later window each of the six runs in only about half of the cases, A30 feeds directly into any of the six (previously only into A33, A35 and A36), the hand-over from A33/A35/A36 to A37/A38/A39 has largely stopped, and cases traverse the segment in 1–3 steps instead of 4–5, while A30 and A31 themselves still occur in every case. Thirty control-flow items qualified against roughly 0.66 expected for an unchanged process, so chance re-splits cannot explain the volume of differences, and the participation drop, the rerouting and the distance compression each show the change directly and consistently [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG007, LAG010].

Limitations: the occurrence, direction, position and lag items largely describe one and the same underlying trace difference, so they are not independent confirmations; the cross-trio collapse rests on only 4–5 later-window observations; and the evidence cannot show the exact new routing — for example, whether a case now skips the second trio as a whole or drops activities individually, or whether a small share of cases still runs the full six-activity block — which would be needed to fully rule out a mere shift between pre-existing path frequencies [LAG005, LAG006, LAG008, LAG009, LAG011, LAG012].

Other differences: A30 itself now sits later in the case (mean relative position about 0.62 → 0.67) and A31 slightly earlier (about 0.82 → 0.80) with somewhat more spread; both shifts are consistent with the shortened passage rather than separate changes. [POS001, POS002]
