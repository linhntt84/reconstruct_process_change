### Observed differences

- The choice between two alternative paths after A20 has effectively flipped. In the earlier window, about 4 in 5 traces continued from A20 into the branch containing A24, A25 and A26 — A20's observed outgoing transitions went about 29% to A24, 29% to A26 and 24% to A25, against about 19% combined to A21/A22/A23 — and each of A24, A25 and A26 appeared in about 82% of traces. In the later window, that branch appears in only about 21–22% of traces, while A21, A22 and A23 rose from about 19–20% to about 79–80% of traces (A20's outgoing: about 29% to A23, 28% to A21, 21% to A22). In both windows each of the six activities occurs about once per trace that contains it, so this is a swap in which existing branch is taken, not a change in repetition. [OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, OCC007, OCC008, OCC009, DIR005]

- The placement of the swapped branch did not move: A26 still sits at about two-thirds of the trace length when it runs (mean relative position about 0.66 in both windows); only how often it runs changed (about 82% → 22% of traces). [POS002]

- A06 is now largely bypassed near the end of the process. Earlier, A34 led to A06 in about 44% of its outgoing transitions (about 56% to A35), A06 led on to A35 in about 92% of its outgoing transitions — immediately, in about 98% of observed A06→A35 pairs — and A06 ran in 46% of traces at a mean position of about 0.91. In the later window, A34 leads directly to A35 in about 90% of its outgoing transitions (A06 about 2%), A34→A35 is immediate in about 92% of cases (vs about 56% before), A06 runs in only 12% of traces at a lower and more variable position (mean about 0.52), and its outgoing transitions are spread over many activities with A35 at only about 15%; the after-side of the A06→A35 pair rests on only 7 observed pairs. - Two previously rare activities occur more often: A02 in about 8% → 17% of traces and A04 in about 5% → 19%. [OCC001, OCC002]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the two alternative paths out of A20 are now taken with very different frequencies: the branch through A24, A25 and A26, which ran in about 4 in 5 traces earlier, is now largely skipped in favour of the branch through A21, A22 and A23, which now runs in about 4 in 5 traces. [OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, OCC007, OCC008, OCC009, DIR005]

The shift is shown directly in A20's outgoing transitions, with A20 itself observed in essentially every trace in both windows, and it is consistent across the occurrence, direction and position indicators; all six activities appear in substantial shares (about 1 in 5 or more) in both windows and none disappears, which is what a change in how often existing paths are taken requires. The 24 qualifying items against roughly 0.65 expected by chance across 131 tested control-flow units make a chance explanation of the ensemble implausible, though the occurrence, direction and position items largely mirror one underlying difference rather than confirming it independently. What the evidence cannot rule out is that the changed shares stem from a different mix of cases reaching this part of the process rather than from the routing at A20 itself, nor whether the branch switch and the A06 bypass are one coordinated restructuring or two separate differences. [DIR005, POS002, OCC004, OCC007]
