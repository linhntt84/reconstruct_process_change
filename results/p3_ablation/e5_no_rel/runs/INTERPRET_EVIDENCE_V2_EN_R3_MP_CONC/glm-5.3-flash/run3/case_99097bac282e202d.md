### Observed differences

- The dominant difference is a flip between two groups of activities. In the earlier window, A21, A22 and A23 each appeared in about 75 in 100 traces while A24, A25 and A26 each appeared in about 25 in 100; in the later window these shares reversed, with A24–A26 each at about 76 in 100 and A21–A23 each at about 25 in 100. [OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, OCC007, OCC008]
- The successor profile of A20 flipped accordingly: in the earlier window about 75% of A20's outgoing transitions led to A21, A22 or A23 (roughly a quarter each) and about 25% led to A24–A26; in the later window about 75% led to A24, A25 or A26 while A21–A23 together fell to about 24%. A20 itself occurs about once per trace in both windows. [DIR003]
- When A24 follows A20, the gap lengthened: one-step pairs fell from 54% to 33%, with more pairs at two to three steps, and the number of observed A20→A24 pairs rose from about 50 to about 150 traces. [LAG001]
- A10 became more frequent and later: participation rose from about 6 to about 21 in 100 traces, its median relative position moved from about 0.60 to about 0.86 of the trace, its outgoing transitions concentrated on A33 (about 18% before versus about 90% after), and A32 now leads to A10 in about 20% of its outgoing transitions versus about 1% earlier. [OCC002, POS001, DIR002, DIR004]
- A02 nearly disappeared, from about 9 in 100 traces to about 1 in 100; its later-window outgoing profile rests on only two observed transitions. [OCC001, DIR001]
- Smaller gap shifts: A32→A33 one-step pairs fell from 56% to 39% with a rise at two steps, and A25→A30 pairs spread toward longer lags, the latter mirroring A25's higher participation. [LAG003, LAG002]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

Main hypothesis: the routing choice after A20 flipped between two alternative activity groups. In the earlier window, traces reaching A20 usually continued with A21, A22 and A23 (each in about 75 in 100 traces) and only rarely with A24, A25 and A26 (about 25 in 100); in the later window this reversed, with A24–A26 each in about 76 in 100 traces, A21–A23 at about 25 in 100, and A20's outgoing transitions now leading predominantly to A24–A26. [OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, OCC007, OCC008, DIR003]

Supporting signs: 17 qualifying items against roughly 0.69 expected by chance in the control-flow family, so the ensemble is not what an unchanged process typically produces; the six participation items move consistently in two mirrored groups, each backed by 49–152 traces per window; DIR003 shows the successor change directly at A20 while A20's own frequency is stable; and all six activities remain observed at 25–76% in both windows with no repetition (recurrence support near zero), which fits a shift in how often existing alternative paths are taken rather than activities being added, removed or repeated differently. [OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, OCC007, OCC008, DIR003]

Limitations: the evidence shows that the branch shares at A20 changed but cannot establish whether the flip originates in A20's own choice or in an upstream change in the mix of cases reaching it; whether the three activities of each group always execute together is inferred from their near-identical shares, not observed directly. [DIR003, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, OCC007, OCC008]

Other differences: A10's increased participation, later position and redirected outgoing transitions (including a new frequent link from A32), A02's near-disappearance, and the mild lengthening of the A32→A33 gap are not part of the main hypothesis; the A02 profile rests on very few later-window observations. [OCC002, POS001, DIR002, DIR004, OCC001, DIR001, LAG003]
