### Observed differences

- **A20's successor changed.** In the earlier window, A20 passed to A30, A31 and A36 (about 40, 33 and 28 in 100 of its observed outgoing transitions); in the later window, A20 leads almost always directly to A22 (about 99 in 100), and transitions from A20 into that group have practically disappeared. [DIR001, LAG001]

- **A21's successor changed the opposite way.** In the earlier window, A21 was followed immediately by A25 in essentially every trace (a one-step gap throughout); in the later window, A21 feeds A30, A31 and A36 (about 36, 33 and 31 in 100), and A25 now follows A21 about four steps later. [DIR002, LAG004]

- **The group's exit moved from A22 to A25.** Before, each of A30, A31 and A36 passed to A22 in roughly 29–38 in 100 of its outgoing transitions; in the later window these exits are nearly absent (at most about 0.5 in 100) and each group member instead passes to A25 (about 31–36 in 100). Handovers among A30, A31 and A36 themselves look similar in both windows. [DIR003, DIR004, DIR005]

- **Positions within the trace shifted consistently.** A30, A31 and A36 now occur later (mean relative position about 0.41 before vs about 0.59 after), while A22 (about 0.47 vs 0.38) and A21 (about 0.62 vs 0.53) occur earlier. All five activities still appear in essentially every trace in both windows (200 of 200), so participation did not change — only the ordering around them did. [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005]

- **Weakly supported gap items point the same way.** Before, A31 and A36 each reached A22 within one to three steps in essentially all traces, and A20 reached A31/A36 similarly; in the later window these ordered pairs occur in only about 2 of 200 traces, so these items rest on very few observations. [LAG002, LAG003, LAG005, LAG006]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the group of activities A30, A31 and A36 changed its place in the flow: in the earlier window it ran between A20 and A22 (A20 entered the group, the group led on to A22, and A21 led directly to A25), whereas in the later window it runs between A21 and A25 (A20 leads directly to A22, A21 enters the group, and the group leads on to A25). The mixed internal ordering among A30, A31 and A36 appears unchanged. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, LAG001, LAG004]

The support is direct and consistent: the successor profiles of A20 and A21 swapped roles, the group's exits moved from A22 to A25, the boundary gaps flipped (A20→A22 from four steps to one; A21→A25 from one step to four), and every position shift points in the same direction. Screening produced 16 qualifying items against roughly 0.39 expected for an unchanged process, and each item's difference exceeds about 199 of 200 random re-splits, so the pattern as a whole is unlikely to be a sampling artefact. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, LAG001, LAG004, POS003, POS004, POS005]

Limitations: the evidence cannot distinguish a genuine relocation of the group from the group being dropped after A20 while an equivalent group is inserted after A21 — the two look identical in these windows — and it covers only the activities listed here; four of the gap items are based on about 2 later-window traces. [LAG002, LAG003, LAG005, LAG006]
