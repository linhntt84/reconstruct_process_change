### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the fixed sequential block A31 → A22 → A20 → A23 → A24 → A26 → A25 → A32 was rewired into more flexible routing: in the later window A23 passes directly to A32 in about 47 in 100 of its outgoing transitions instead of almost always passing to A24, the later steps A24, A25 and A26 send a substantial share of their transitions back to the earlier block activities A20, A22 and A23, and the previously rigid one-step gaps between all block pairs spread out, with some pairs now lying more than five steps apart. This is shown directly by the successor redistributions at all seven block activities and by the lag distributions, and the position shifts (A23 later; A24–A26 earlier) are consistent with it; with 55 qualifying items against roughly 1 expected for an unchanged process, sampling noise cannot explain the pattern as a whole. Limitations: successor shares are measured over outgoing transitions rather than cases, some later-window profiles rest on limited observations (A20→A24 in 52 traces, A25→A22 in 26, A26→A22 in 49), and the evidence cannot rule out that the later window mixes traces still following the old chain with traces using the new shortcuts, rather than rewiring every case uniformly [DIR004, DIR005, DIR006, DIR007, DIR008, DIR009, DIR010, LAG008, LAG011, LAG016, LAG019, LAG020, LAG022, LAG028, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006, POS007, POS008].
