### Observed differences

- The two windows wire the same activities to different neighbours. In the earlier window, A24 led to A15, A18 or A17 (about 37, 33 and 30 in 100 of its outgoing transitions) and A35 led to A16, A14 or A19 (about 35, 30 and 36 in 100). In the later window these successor sets are exchanged: A24 leads to A19, A16 or A14 (about 39, 29 and 33 in 100) and A35 to A17, A15 or A18 (about 36, 33 and 31 in 100). [DIR007, DIR008]
- The exits of the six group members flipped between A20 and A25. A14, A16 and A19, which in the earlier window always led to A20 (in 60–77 traces each), always lead to A25 in the later window. A15, A17 and A18, which earlier exited to A25 in roughly 30–38 of 100 cases from each member, now exit to A20 in roughly 28–36 of 100 cases. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006]
- Positions moved in opposite directions by about a third of the trace. A15, A17 and A18, present in every trace in both windows, shifted from a mean position of about two-thirds to about one-third of the trace; A14, A16 and A19, each present in about 30–39 of 100 traces (their shares together covering essentially every trace), shifted from about one-third to about two-thirds. [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006]
- Distances between the anchor activities changed accordingly: the gap from A35 to A20 grew from 2 steps to 4, and the gap from A24 to A25 shrank from 4 steps to 2, in essentially all traces of both windows. A20 and A24 themselves now occur slightly later (mean positions about 0.38 to 0.44 and 0.57 to 0.64). [LAG001, LAG002, POS007, POS008]
- What stayed the same: participation (the trio A15/A17/A18 in 200 of 200 traces in both windows; each of A14/A16/A19 in about 30–39 of 100 traces, together covering essentially every case) and the trio's mixed internal order, with no fixed sequence among its members in either window. [DIR002, DIR004, DIR005, POS002, POS004, POS005]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the two activity groups exchanged their places between the two segments of the process. In the later window, the mandatory trio A15, A17 and A18 is executed between A35 and A20 in varying order, while a single activity chosen among A14, A16 and A19 is executed between A24 and A25; in the earlier window the trio sat between A24 and A25 and the single choice sat between A35 and A20. The change is shown directly by the exchanged successor sets of A24 and A35, the retargeted exits of all six group members, the opposite position shifts of about one third of the trace, and the gap changes from 2 to 4 and from 4 to 2 steps. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006, DIR007, DIR008, POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006, LAG001, LAG002]

Sampling variation is an unlikely explanation for the pattern as a whole: 18 items qualified against roughly 0.36 expected by chance for the 72 tested control-flow units, and every item's difference exceeded about 995 of 1,000 random re-splits of the pooled traces. Limitations: the successor, position and gap items largely describe the same underlying movement, so they are not independent confirmations; positions are event positions rather than clock times; and the evidence covers only these activities' immediate relations, so the possibility that additional, unobserved parts of the process (for example what precedes A35 or A24) also changed cannot be ruled out, and the cause of the exchange is not identifiable from this evidence. [DIR001, DIR008, POS002, LAG001, LAG002]
