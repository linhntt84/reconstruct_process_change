### Observed differences

- The largest difference is a flip in the branch following A20, which occurs in essentially every trace in both windows. In the earlier window, each of A21, A22 and A23 appeared in about 75 in 100 traces, while each of A24, A25 and A26 appeared in only about 26 in 100; in the later window these shares reversed, with A21–A23 each in about 25 in 100 traces and A24–A26 each in about 76 in 100. [OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, OCC007, OCC008, OCC009]
- The successor profile of A20 flipped accordingly: before, about three quarters of A20's outgoing transitions led to A21, A22 or A23 (roughly 23–27% each) and about one quarter to A24–A26; after, about three quarters led to A24, A25 or A26 and only about one quarter back to A21–A23. [DIR002]
- The run-up to this point is essentially unchanged: A29 is still followed directly by A19 and then by A20 at similar or slightly higher rates, and A19 remains A29's dominant successor (about 84% before, 94% after). [LAG007, LAG008, DIR003]
- Lag distributions touching the two groups moved with the flip: the ordered pair A20→A24 within five steps appears in about 25 in 100 traces before versus about 75 in 100 after, gaps from A29 to A24 became longer while gaps to A25 became shorter. These items largely restate the same rerouting rather than adding independent effects. [LAG002, LAG003, LAG009, LAG010]
- A second difference concerns A06 near the end of the trace. A06 rose from about 17 to about 53 in 100 traces, and its median position moved from about 48% to about 96% of the trace. From A34, the direct transition to A35 fell from about 89% to about 48% of outgoing transitions while about 50% now go to A06; from A06, about 90% of outgoing transitions now lead to A35 (previously about 12%, spread over many targets); A06→A35 occurs within one step in about 97% of later-window cases; and the A34→A35 gap grew from one step (90% of cases) to two steps (about half of cases). In the later window, roughly half of traces run A34 → A06 → A35 where they previously ran A34 → A35 directly. [OCC002, POS001, DIR001, DIR005, LAG001, LAG013]
- Two low-frequency activities participate less often: A02 fell from about 17 to about 8 in 100 traces, and A08 from about 13 to about 5 in 100. [OCC001, OCC003]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that, in the later window, the choice of path after A20 flipped between two existing alternative groups: flows that previously continued into A21, A22 and A23 (about three quarters of A20's outgoing transitions, each activity in about 75 in 100 traces) now continue into A24, A25 and A26 (about three quarters of outgoing transitions, each in about 76 in 100 traces), while the earlier group remains available at about 25 in 100 traces. Every involved activity is observed in a substantial share of traces in both windows (roughly 50 and 150 traces) and only the shares between the alternatives shifted, so this is a change in how often existing paths are taken rather than a replacement or removal of a path. Supporting signs are the six mirrored participation shifts of about 50 percentage points, the flipped successor distribution of A20 itself, and the lag shifts for pairs touching both groups; the unchanged run-up A29 → A19 → A20 localises the difference to the branch point. Limitations: the participation, direction and lag items overlap and are not independent confirmations, and the evidence cannot show what determines the choice at A20 — for example a changed branch condition versus a different mix of cases reaching that point. With 29 qualifying control-flow items against fewer than one expected if nothing had changed, sampling alone cannot account for the pattern as a whole, even though any single item could still reflect sampling noise; every item's difference also exceeds about 995 of 1,000 random re-splits of the pooled data. [OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, OCC007, OCC008, OCC009, DIR002, DIR003, LAG002, LAG003, LAG007, LAG008, LAG009, LAG010]
