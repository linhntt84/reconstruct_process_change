### Observed differences

- A30, A31 and A36 were heavily repeated in the earlier window: each occurred about 3.5 times per trace, was present in every trace, and repeated within the same trace in about 75 in 100 traces. In the later window each occurs about once per trace, still in every trace, with repeats in only about 1 in 100 traces [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003].
- The routing out of A36 flipped: earlier, about 70 in 100 of A36's outgoing transitions went to A30 and about 28 in 100 to A25; in the later window, about 92 in 100 go to A25 and only about 4 in 100 go back to A30 [DIR002].
- The loop relations largely disappeared: A36 followed by A30 within five steps was observed in 150 of 200 traces earlier but in only 1 trace later, and A36 repeating itself within five steps in 150 of 200 traces earlier but in only 2 traces later [LAG002, LAG003].
- A tight succession A30 followed by A36 two positions later became the norm: about 12 in 100 observations earlier versus about 89 in 100 in the later window [LAG001].
- A30 → A31 remained the dominant edge in both windows (about 99 in 100 of A30's outgoing transitions earlier, about 97 in 100 later), but the number of outgoing transitions from A30 fell from 690 to about 210, matching the drop in repetitions [DIR001].
- The relative positions of A30 and A36 in the trace are roughly unchanged (means around 0.42–0.44 and 0.49), but their spread narrowed (standard deviation from about 0.12 to about 0.07) [POS001, POS002].

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the repeated execution of the A30–A31–A36 block stopped. In the earlier window the three activities each ran about three to four times per trace and cycled A30 → A31 → … → A36 → back to A30, with the final pass leaving the block to A25; in the later window each activity runs about once per trace, A36 leads to A25 (about 92 in 100 transitions) instead of back to A30 (about 4 in 100), and A30 is followed two positions later by A36 in about 89 in 100 observations, consistent with a single pass A30 → A31 → A36 → A25 [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, DIR001, DIR002, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003].

This is a change in repetition rather than a mere shift of shares between existing paths: participation stays at 100 in 100 traces for all three activities in both windows while recurrence collapses, and the routing change at A36 is observed directly. The ten qualified control-flow items are roughly twenty times the 0.46 expected by chance for that family, and every item's difference exceeded those of about 995 of 1,000 random re-splits; note, however, that the items largely describe one underlying difference and are not independent confirmations [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, DIR002, LAG002, LAG003].

Limitations: the "after" side of the vanished loop relations rests on only 1–2 observed traces, and the evidence cannot rule out that the two windows sample different case mixtures (for example, a looping variant present mainly in the earlier window) rather than a change applying to all cases [LAG002, LAG003, OCC001].

Other differences: the spread of A30's and A36's positions narrowed and A30's rare outgoing edges were slightly redistributed while A30 → A31 stayed dominant; both are consistent with the same loss of repetition rather than separate changes [POS001, POS002, DIR001].
