### Observed differences

- In the earlier window the stretch from A22 to A24 was fully rigid: A22's only observed successor was A23, A23's only observed successor was A24, A23 followed A22 one step later in every case, and A24 followed A22 exactly two steps later in every case. [DIR004, DIR005, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003]
- In the later window, three activities that were not observed at all earlier (0 of 200 traces each) now take part in a large share of cases: A15 in about 41 in 100 cases, A14 in about 29 and A20 in about 30, each at most once per case and together roughly one occurrence per case. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003]
- In the later window these three activities sit near the middle of the trace (mean relative position about 0.51), and each is followed by A23 in roughly 53–58% of its outgoing transitions and by A24 in the remaining 42–47%. [POS001, POS002, POS003, DIR001, DIR002, DIR003]
- The earlier rigid routing is redistributed around these activities: A22 now leads directly to A23 in only about 44 in 100 of its outgoing transitions (previously all of them) and to one of A14/A15/A20 in about 56 in 100; A23 now leads to A24 in about 57 in 100 (previously all) and to one of the three in about 43 in 100. - Step distances show that exactly one event now lies between A22 and A24 in almost every case: A24 follows A22 three steps later in 199 of 200 later cases (previously always two steps), A23 follows A22 two steps later in about 56 in 100 cases (previously one), and A24 follows A23 two steps later in about 44 in 100 cases (previously one). [LAG001, LAG002, LAG003]
- A minor accompanying shift: A22 itself now occurs slightly earlier in the trace (mean relative position about 0.46, down from about 0.48). [POS004]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that a single extra step — appearing as A14, A15 or A20 — now executes in nearly every case inside the formerly rigid A22 → A23 → A24 stretch: in about 56 in 100 later cases it runs directly after A22 and before A23, and in about 44 in 100 directly after A23 and before A24, with the choice among the three varying from case to case (A15 about 41 in 100, A14 about 29, A20 about 30). In the earlier window this stretch contained no activity beyond A22, A23 and A24 themselves. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003]

Signs supporting this: 15 control-flow items qualified against roughly 0.34 expected for an unchanged process, a count far too large to be a sampling artefact. The items also fit one single underlying difference rather than many independent changes: A22's successor split (about 44% to A23, 56% to the new activities) matches the A22→A23 distance split (44% at one step, 56% at two), the new activities' successor split complements the A23→A24 distance split, the per-activity occurrence counts reconcile with the predecessor shares almost exactly, and the distance items rest on all 200 traces in both windows. Activities absent from 200 earlier traces but present in 58–82 of 200 later traces, combined with near-deterministic distance shifts, is not what rare-variant sampling produces. [screening, OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, DIR004, DIR005, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003]

Limitations: zero participation in the earlier window strictly shows only that A14, A15 and A20 were not observed then, not that they are new to the process; and these samples cannot show whether the 56/44 placement split or the mix among the three activities is stable beyond the windows, nor whether the choice of activity is tied to its placement. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, DIR004, DIR005]

Other differences: none — the only remaining qualifying item, A22's slight move to an earlier trace position, is a corollary of the extra step rather than a separate difference. [POS004]
