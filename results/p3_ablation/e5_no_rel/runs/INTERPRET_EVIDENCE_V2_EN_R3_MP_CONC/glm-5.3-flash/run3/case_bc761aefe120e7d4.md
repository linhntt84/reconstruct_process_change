### Observed differences

- The activities A30, A31 and A36 stopped repeating. In the earlier window each occurred about 3.4–3.5 times per trace and appeared in every trace, with repeats in about 75 in 100 traces; in the later window each occurs about once per trace (1.0–1.1), still in every trace, with repeats in only about 1–2 in 100 traces. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003]
- The step after A36 changed accordingly: earlier, about 70 in 100 of A36's outgoing transitions led back to A30 and about 29 in 100 led to A25; in the later window about 93 in 100 lead to A25 and only about 4 in 100 return to A30. Observed outgoing transitions from A36 fell from about 690 to about 210, matching the drop in occurrences. [DIR003]
- The direct successions A30→A31 and A31→A36 dominated in both windows (about 98–99 in 100 of outgoing transitions earlier, about 97 in 100 later), so the chain itself was preserved; their transition counts also fell from about 690 to about 210. [DIR001, DIR002]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the repeating cycle A30 → A31 → A36 → A30 stopped repeating: in the earlier window traces typically ran this cycle about three to four times (each of A30, A31 and A36 about 3.4 occurrences per trace, repeated in about 75 in 100 traces, with A36 returning to A30 in about 70 in 100 of its outgoing transitions), whereas in the later window each activity runs about once per trace, still in every trace and in the same order, and A36 now leads on to A25 in about 93 in 100 cases instead of looping back (about 4 in 100). This is a change in repetition, not in participation: none of the three activities became optional or disappeared. Supporting signs: three occurrence items show the same drop with full participation in both windows; the rerouting at A36 is observed directly on about 209 outgoing transitions; the pair-level evidence matches a single pass; every item's difference exceeded about 99.5% of random re-splits; and the 12 qualifying items — all control-flow — are roughly 26 times the 0.46 expected by chance, so chance alone cannot account for them. Limitations: the self-repeat and return-pair lag items rest on only 1–2 later-window observations, and the aggregate evidence cannot distinguish a reduction of the loop's repetition count from a shift in the mix between a repeating and a single-pass variant; a small residual loop-back (about 4 in 100 of A36's outgoing transitions) remains. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, LAG002, LAG003]

Other differences: A30 and A31 each show a handful of outgoing transitions to assorted other activities that appear or disappear between the windows at shares below 1 in 100 (single observations each); these are within what sampling noise produces and are not part of the main hypothesis.
