### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, A19, A20 and A25 were each optional: each appeared in about 47–48 of 100 traces and, when present, occurred about once. In the later window, each of the three appears in about 199 of 200 traces, almost exactly once per trace (mean occurrences per trace rose from about 0.5 to about 1.0 for each of them). [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003]
- The routing out of A41 changed fundamentally. Before, A41 led directly to A26 in about 52 of 100 of its outgoing transitions and to A19 in about 45; in the later window it leads to A19 in about 92 of 100 and directly to A26 in only about 0.5 of 100, with the rest spread over rare successors. [DIR002]
- The spacing between A41 and A26 changed accordingly: before, A26 immediately followed A41 in about 52 of 100 observed pairs and sat four to five steps away in about 47; in the later window, immediate succession is nearly unobserved (about 0.5 in 100) and A26 sits four steps after A41 in about 89 of 100 pairs, five steps in about 11. [LAG001]
- A20's own outgoing behaviour is essentially stable: it leads to A26 in about 96 of 100 transitions before and about 99 in 100 after, so the connection into A26 via A20 exists in both windows. [DIR001]
- Minor positional shifts accompany this: A26's average relative position moved slightly later (about 0.41 to 0.43 of the trace) and A41's slightly earlier (about 0.33 to 0.31). [POS001, POS002]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the group of activities A19, A20 and A25, which lies between A41 and A26, changed from optional to mandatory. In the earlier window, about half of the cases bypassed this group by running directly from A41 to A26, while the other half ran A41 → A19 … A20 → A26 with A26 four to five steps after A41; in the later window the direct A41→A26 connection has all but disappeared, A41 proceeds to A19 in about 92 of 100 cases, and A19, A20 and A25 are each executed once in virtually every trace, leaving A26 consistently four (about 89 in 100) or five (about 11 in 100) steps after A41. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, DIR002, LAG001]

Supporting signs: the participation rise is large and consistent across all three activities (about 47–48 in 100 traces to about 199 in 200 each), the rerouting is shown directly by A41's successor shares and by the collapse of the A41–A26 lag distribution to a single four-to-five-step pattern, and eight control-flow items qualified in screening where fewer than one (about 0.57) would be expected if nothing had changed, so chance cannot account for the pattern as a whole. The items are mutually consistent but largely describe one underlying rerouting rather than independent confirmations. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, DIR002, LAG001]

Limitations: the internal order of A19, A20 and A25 is not directly observed, as there is no outgoing-transition evidence for A19 or A25; and the evidence cannot distinguish whether the direct A41→A26 edge was removed outright or survives only as a rare alternative (about 0.5 in 100 transitions). An alternative reading of the same observations — that A26's placement shifted to after the group rather than the group becoming mandatory — cannot be separated from the hypothesis with this evidence. [DIR002, LAG001, OCC001]

Other differences: A41 and A20 show a handful of rare successors (A06, A10, A02, A27, A36 after A41; A03, A06 after A20), each at well under 5 in 100 transitions, and the small position shifts of A26 and A41 noted above; all are minor and consistent with the main rerouting. [POS001, POS002, DIR001, DIR002]
