### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, every observed outgoing transition of A20 led to A22; in the later window only about 1 in 200 does, and roughly one third each lead to A30, A31 and A36. [DIR001]
- In the earlier window, outgoing transitions of A21 split about 38, 34 and 28 in 100 across A36, A30 and A31; in the later window about 199 of 200 lead to A25. [DIR002]
- In the earlier window, each of A30, A31 and A36 passed to A25 in roughly 28–37 of 100 of its outgoing transitions; in the later window A25 is almost never chosen (about 1 in 200 or less) while A22 receives about 31–34 of 100. Transitions among A30, A31 and A36 themselves stay mixed at roughly one third each in both windows. [DIR003, DIR004, DIR005]

[LAG001, LAG002]
- A30, A31 and A36 each occur clearly earlier in the trace (mean relative position about 0.59 before, 0.41 after), while A21 and A22 occur later (about 0.53 → 0.62 and 0.38 → 0.47, roughly three event positions). [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the group of activities A30, A31 and A36 was relocated: in the earlier window it executed between A21 and A25, while in the later window it executes between A20 and A22 — in effect, A22 and this three-activity group exchanged places, and A21 now leads directly to A25. This is shown directly by the successor changes at A20 and A21 and the group's exit rewired from A25 to A22 [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005], by the pair-gap changes A20→A22 (immediate to four steps) and A21→A25 (four steps to immediate) observed in essentially all traces of both windows [LAG001, LAG002], and by position shifts that match a three-activity block being inserted after A20 and removed from before A25 [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005]. With 18 qualifying items against roughly 0.38 expected for an unchanged process, chance sampling cannot explain the pattern as a whole, and because every involved activity remains present in about 200 of 200 traces in both windows, this is a reordering of existing activities rather than a change in participation or in how often existing paths are taken.

Limitations: the internal order within the A30/A31/A36 group remains mixed in both windows and cannot be pinned down further, and the six gap items about the old neighbourhoods rest on a single after-window observation each [LAG003, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006, LAG007, LAG008]. An important competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that the old ordering persists in a small share of cases: about 1 in 200 later-window traces still shows A20 leading directly to A22 and A21 leading to A31, so a complete relocation and one leaving a small residue of old-order traces look identical at this sample size [DIR001, DIR002].
