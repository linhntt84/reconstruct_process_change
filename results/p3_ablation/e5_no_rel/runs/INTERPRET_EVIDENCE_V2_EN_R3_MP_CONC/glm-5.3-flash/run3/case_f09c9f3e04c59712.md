### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that two blocks of activities exchanged their places in the flow: the trio A15, A17, A18, which earlier ran together in the early segment between A35 and A20, now runs together in the late segment between A24 and A25, while the one-of-three choice among A14, A16 and A19 moved from the late segment into the early one; the exits were rewired so that A14, A16 and A19 now lead to A20 instead of A25, and A15, A17 and A18 lead to A25 instead of A20. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006, DIR007, DIR008, LAG001, LAG002, POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006]

The change is shown directly, not only through frequencies: the successor profiles of A24 and A35 swapped wholesale, the A24→A25 and A35→A20 distances changed by exactly the block sizes (2↔4 steps in all 200 traces per window), and the two blocks shifted in opposite directions by roughly a quarter to a third of the trace. The screening context also fits: 19 items qualified against roughly 0.36 expected by chance for the 72 tested control-flow units, far beyond what an unchanged process typically produces, and all items describe this one consistent rearrangement. [DIR007, DIR008, LAG001, LAG002, POS002, POS003]

Limitations: positions are event positions, not clock times; the evidence does not reveal the internal ordering within the A15/A17/A18 trio, and rare transitions outside the observed profiles cannot be excluded. A competing reading — that the endpoints rather than the blocks exchanged places — is largely ruled out because A20 stayed early (mean position about 0.38), but A25's position is not measured, so the arrangement at the late exit cannot be pinned down completely. [POS007, LAG001]

Other differences: within the choice among A14, A16 and A19, the routing share shifted toward A14 (about 31 to 44 in 100 cases), with A16 and A19 each dropping to about 28 in 100; this is a change in how often an existing alternative is taken, on top of the block exchange. [OCC001, POS003, POS006]
