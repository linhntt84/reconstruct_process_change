### Observed differences

- The routing choice after A20 flipped between the windows. In the earlier window, A20's outgoing transitions led mostly to A24 (about 29 in 100), A26 (about 29) and A25 (about 24), with A21, A22 and A23 together receiving under 20; in the later window they lead mostly to A23 (about 29), A21 (about 28) and A22 (about 21), while A24, A25 and A26 receive only about 6–8 each. Mirroring this, A21, A22 and A23 each ran in about 20 of 100 traces before and about 80 of 100 after, whereas A24, A25 and A26 each fell from about 80 of 100 to about 20 of 100; essentially no case repeats any of these six activities in either window, and both groups remain in use in both windows [DIR005, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, OCC007, OCC008, OCC009].
- A06 was largely bypassed in the later window: it appeared in about 46 of 100 traces before versus about 12 after; A34, which previously split its outgoing transitions between A06 (about 45%) and A35 (about 55%), now goes almost always directly to A35 (about 90%, A06 about 2%); when A06 still runs, its formerly dominant edge to A35 (about 92% of its outgoing transitions) fell to about 15%, replaced by a spread including A15 (about 23%) and A33 (about 15%), and the A06→A35 pair within five steps is now rare (about 89 versus 7 supporting traces) and immediate in under half of them; A06's typical event position also moved from near the end of the trace to mid-trace [OCC003, DIR006, DIR001, LAG001, POS001].
- Smaller shifts: A02 and A04 participation roughly doubled (about 8 to 17 in 100 and about 5 to 19 in 100 traces); A34→A35 became almost always immediate (adjacent in about 56 of 100 cases before, about 92 after); A26 kept its typical event position (mean about 0.66 in both windows) while its support fell from about 163 to 44 traces; A08's outgoing mix changed, but on only 9 versus 23 observed transitions. A14→A15 (about 96% to 87%) and A19→A20 (about 97% to 93%) stayed dominant with negligible change [OCC001, OCC002, LAG006, POS002, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004].

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the two alternative continuations after A20 exchanged frequencies: the branch activities A24, A25 and A26, which previously followed A20 in about 80 of 100 cases, now do so in about 20 of 100, while A21, A22 and A23 rose from about 20 to about 80 of 100. This rests on the direct redistribution of A20's successor shares (about 200 outgoing transitions observed in each window, with A20 itself present in nearly all traces throughout) and on the consistent, mirrored participation changes of all six activities, each backed by roughly 40–160 traces per window. With 24 qualifying items against fewer than one (about 0.65) expected by chance across the 131 tested control-flow units, sampling variation between the two 200-trace windows cannot account for the ensemble [DIR005, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, OCC007, OCC008, OCC009, screening].

Limitation: the evidence contains no direct co-occurrence (REL) data, so it cannot confirm that A21–A23 form one mutually exclusive branch and A24–A26 the other; if cases can contain both groups, the flip would concern how often the groups are executed together rather than an either/or rerouting, and a changed mix of cases entering the two windows cannot be ruled out either [DIR005, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, OCC007, OCC008, OCC009].

Other differences: A06 became largely skippable — A34 now leads almost directly to A35 instead of through A06, A06's participation fell to about 12 of 100 traces, its outgoing connections diversified, and its typical event position moved earlier; because a predecessor now connects past A06 to its own successor, this reads as A06 becoming optional or bypassed rather than an existing path being taken with different frequency. A02 and A04 also occur somewhat more often, and A34→A35 became almost always immediate [OCC003, DIR006, DIR001, LAG001, POS001, OCC001, OCC002, LAG006].
