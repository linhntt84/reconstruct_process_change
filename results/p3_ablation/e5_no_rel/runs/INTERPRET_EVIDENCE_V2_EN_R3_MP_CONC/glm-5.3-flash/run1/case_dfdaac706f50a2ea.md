### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the A36/A37/A42 group was reconnected within the process: in the earlier window A25 fed into the group, the group circulated among A36, A37 and A42 and exited to A28, while A26 led almost always through A27 towards A31; in the later window A25 bypasses the group and connects directly to A28, and the group instead appears after A26 as an alternative branch to A27, now exiting to A31. Through this rerouting A27, A36, A37 and A42 became optional, each running in only about half of the traces, while A25, A26, A28 and A31 continued to occur in every trace. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, LAG001, LAG005, OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, POS004, POS005]

The routing changes are observed directly at the edge level, each profile resting on about 200 outgoing transitions in both windows (94–96 for the group activities in the later window), and the participation, gap and position indicators all move in the directions this rerouting predicts. Screening flagged 28 control-flow items where fewer than one (0.61) would be expected by chance among 123 tested units, so sampling variation cannot account for the pattern as a whole. [DIR001, DIR002, LAG001, LAG005, POS006, POS007, POS008]

The evidence cannot show whether the A25-to-A28 and A26-to-group connections are genuinely new or were rare existing paths that became frequent, because an edge unobserved in a window is not proven absent. Also, no direct successor profiles exist for A27, A28 and A31, so the wiring downstream of them is inferred from step gaps rather than observed edges. [DIR001, DIR002, LAG005]
