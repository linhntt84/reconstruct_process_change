### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that, in the later window, the routing through A20, A22, A23, A24, A25 and A26 was consolidated into one fixed sequence, A31 → A22 → A20 → A23 → A24 → A26 → A25 → A32, whereas earlier these activities were executed in a variable order in which A20 and A22 branched among A23, A24, A25 and A26, and A23–A26 frequently led back to A20, A22 or A23. This is shown directly by the successor distributions, which moved from multi-way splits to single successors of about 92–100 in 100 transitions on roughly 200 observations each, by the pair distances, which now sit at exactly the chain distance in virtually every case, and by the five return relations falling from over 100 observations each to a single one. Since all six activities occur in every trace in both windows and only the routing shifted, this is a change in ordering constraints rather than activities appearing, disappearing or being bypassed. The many items describe one underlying reordering rather than independent confirmations, and a few before-window distance profiles rest on only 50–100 observations. [DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006, DIR007, DIR008, DIR009, LAG016, LAG017, LAG021, LAG023, LAG030, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006, POS007]

The volume of flagged evidence also exceeds what an unchanged process produces: 49 items qualified where about 0.72 were expected by chance in the control-flow family, and every item shows an empirical strength of about 0.995. An important competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is a shift in the mix of process variants between the two windows (the later window being dominated by the chain-shaped behaviour) rather than a rewiring of a single behaviour; the evidence also cannot indicate why the order tightened.
