### Observed differences

- The three activities A30, A31 and A36 were executed essentially once per trace in the earlier window (about 1.0 occurrence per trace each, with repeats in at most about 1 in 100 traces) and appeared in every trace. In the later window, each occurs about 3.5 times per trace on average (roughly 4–5 times in the traces that repeat them), and about 68–69 in 100 traces contain the activity more than once, while participation remains 100% in both windows. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003]
- The handover at A36 changed: earlier, about 98 in 100 of A36's observed outgoing transitions went to A25 and almost none to A30; in the later window, about 71 in 100 go to A30 and about 28 in 100 to A25. [DIR005]
- Relationally, a succession of A36 followed by A30 within five steps appeared in only 1 of 200 traces before, but in 136 of 200 traces (about 68 in 100) in the later window. [LAG002]
- The internal chain itself is preserved: A30 was followed by A31 in about 98 in 100 of its outgoing transitions in both windows, and A31 by A36 likewise. [DIR003, DIR004]
- The relative positions of A30 and A36 spread out (standard deviations roughly doubled, about 0.06 to 0.12 for both), with their upper quartiles moving later; the A30→A36 gap profile changed from almost always two events apart (about 97 in 100 of observed instances) to a spread in which the two-event share fell to about 12 in 100 and larger gaps, such as five events, appeared. [POS003, POS004, LAG001]
- Away from this segment, the successor shares at A05 shifted (A07 from about 19 to 39 in 100; A14 from about 35 to 18 in 100; A09 stable at about 39–42 in 100, based on roughly 110 vs 103 outgoing transitions), A05 and A14 appear slightly earlier in the trace (mean relative positions about 0.07→0.05 and 0.14→0.12), and A14 still leads almost always to A15 (about 96 vs 91 in 100). [DIR001, DIR002, POS001, POS002]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

In the later window, the segment A30 → A31 → A36 is executed repeatedly within traces. Earlier, every trace ran this chain exactly once and left it via A36 → A25; in the later window, A36 passes back to A30 in about 71 in 100 of its outgoing transitions (before: about 0.5 in 100), so the same chain runs about 3.5 times per trace on average — about 69 in 100 traces repeat it at least once, while the rest still pass through once before exiting to A25 (now about 28 in 100 of A36's outgoing transitions, down from about 98 in 100). Supporting signs: the repetition increase rests on all 200 traces in both windows; the new A36→A30 succession is observed directly (136 of 200 traces vs 1 of 200); the chain order A30→A31→A36 is unchanged; and the widened position spread of A30 and A36 fits several iterations per trace. The 14 qualifying items also far exceed the 0.47 expected for an unchanged process. Limitations: the occurrence, direction, position and lag items largely re-measure one underlying behaviour, so they are not independent confirmations; and the evidence cannot distinguish a repeat of exactly these three activities from a larger repeated fragment containing them, nor show what determines the number of iterations or the exit to A25. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, LAG001, LAG002, POS003, POS004]

Other differences: At A05, the shares between existing successors shifted (A07 up from about 19 to 39 in 100, A14 down from about 35 to 18 in 100), and A05 and A14 now sit slightly earlier in relative position; that position shift may simply reflect traces being lengthened by the repeated segment, which position-based evidence cannot separate from a real move. A14's outgoing behaviour is otherwise nearly unchanged (about 96 vs 91 in 100 to A15). [DIR001, DIR002, POS001, POS002]
