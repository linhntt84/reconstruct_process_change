### Observed differences

- Participation in a group of three activities changed sharply: in the earlier window, A14, A15 and A20 each appeared in about 47 in 100 cases (94 of 200 traces), at most once per case; in the later window, each appears in essentially every case (199 of 200), still without repeats [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003].
- Routing out of A36 changed accordingly: earlier, about 47 in 100 of A36's observed outgoing transitions went to A14 and about 53 in 100 went to A21; in the later window, about 995 in 1000 go to A14 and only about 1 in 200 still go to A21 [DIR001].
- The distance between A36 and A21 changed: earlier, A21 immediately followed A36 in about 53 in 100 cases and sat four steps away in about 47 in 100; in the later window, the four-step gap holds in about 995 in 1000 cases, consistent with the three activities A14, A15 and A20 now sitting between the two [LAG001].
- Positions shifted only slightly: A36's mean relative position moved a little earlier (about 0.33 to 0.30 of the trace) and A21's a little later (about 0.41 to 0.43); these are event-position shifts, not clock-time movements [POS001, POS002].

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the block of activities A14, A15 and A20, previously optional, was executed in essentially every case in the later window. Earlier, the block ran in about 47 in 100 cases and was skipped in the remainder, with A36 passing directly on to A21; in the later window it ran in about 199 of 200 cases, so A36 now almost always flows into A14, and A21 follows four steps after A36 — with the three block activities between them — instead of immediately after it. The participation change is measured directly for all three activities with large support, the successor redistribution at A36 and the changed A36–A21 distance show the rerouting relation directly, and the two views mirror each other almost exactly (about 47 in 100 with the block, 53 without, earlier; the reverse in the later window); the small position shifts fit the same picture, though position evidence alone would not establish it [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, DIR001, LAG001, POS001, POS002].

Chance is an unlikely explanation for the evidence set as a whole: seven items qualified where roughly 0.34 were expected without change across the 68 tested control-flow units, and every difference exceeded that seen in about 199 of 200 random re-splits of the pooled windows [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, DIR001, POS001, POS002, LAG001]. Limitations: the three occurrence items have identical profiles and most likely describe one behaviour rather than three independent confirmations; the internal order of A14, A15 and A20 between A36 and A21 is inferred from the four-step gap rather than directly observed; and about 1 in 200 later cases still bypasses the block, so it is near-mandatory rather than strictly so. A competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that the later window captured almost exclusively the block-executing variant of an otherwise unchanged process; the behavioural shift itself is well established, but the evidence cannot show that the routing logic rather than the case mix changed [DIR001, LAG001].

Other differences: none — the positional shifts are consequences of the same behaviour, and all seven items describe this one difference [POS001, POS002, OCC001, DIR001, LAG001].
