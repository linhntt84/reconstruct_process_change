### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that, in the later window, the two three-activity blocks exchanged their relative order: A19, A21 and A24, which previously ran late — after A29, each leading almost always to A30 — now run early, directly after A40 and leading almost always to A25, while A20, A22 and A23, which previously ran early after A40 and led into A25, now run late after A29 and often lead to A30. The exchange is shown directly in the predecessor and successor profiles of both blocks with substantial support in both windows (for example, A29's outgoing transitions went about 97 in 100 to A19/A21/A24 before and about 97 in 100 to A20/A22/A23 after), and the positional shifts and distance changes move consistently with it, while participation of all six activities stayed broadly similar — so the blocks were reordered rather than added, removed or made optional. [DIR005, DIR006, DIR007, DIR008, DIR009, DIR010, DIR011, DIR012, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006, POS007, LAG013, LAG016, LAG018, LAG019, LAG021, LAG022, LAG024, LAG025]

Chance is an unlikely explanation for the set as a whole: 52 items qualified across 165 tested units where fewer than one would be expected without change, and the key relations are near-deterministic within each window (A19 led to A30 in about 97 in 100 of its outgoing transitions before and to A25 in about 98 in 100 after), which a mere shift in the mix of two coexisting variants would not produce. The evidence cannot rule out that the new ordering existed as a rare variant that the earlier window's 200 traces would mostly miss, nor show whether the reordering happened at once or gradually across the boundary; gaps are observed only up to five steps and positions are relative event positions, so clock-time placement and longer-range order remain unobserved. [DIR005, LAG019, POS003]
