### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, A25 was followed by A27 in about 99 of 100 observed outgoing transitions; in the later window, A25's outgoing transitions split almost equally among A36, A41 and A35 (about 33% each), with A27 nearly gone (under 1 in 100). [DIR001]
- In the earlier window, A26's outgoing transitions went mainly to A41 (about 37 in 100), A35 (about 34) and A36 (about 28); in the later window, A26 leads almost exclusively to A30 (about 98–99 in 100). [DIR002]
- All three block activities changed successors in the same way: A35, A36 and A41 each lost A30 as a successor (from roughly 28–37 in 100 outgoing transitions to at most about 0.5) and each gained A27 (about 31–34 in 100), while their transitions to one another stayed at roughly three tenths each. [DIR003, DIR004, DIR005]
- Relative positions moved in step: A35, A36 and A41 occur earlier in the trace in the later window (mean relative position about 0.58–0.59 before versus about 0.41 after), while A26 (about 0.53 to 0.62) and A27 (about 0.38 to 0.47) occur later. [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005]
- Pairwise gaps changed in step: A26 reached A30 typically four positions later in the earlier window (about 93 in 100) but immediately in the later window (about 98–99 in 100), and A25 reached A27 immediately before (about 99 in 100) but typically four positions later afterwards (about 93 in 100). [LAG001, LAG003]
- Proximities switched sides: the pairs A26–A35, A26–A36, A26–A41, A35–A30, A36–A30 and A41–A30 occurred within five steps in about 200 of 200 earlier traces but in only 1–2 later traces, while A25–A35 and A35–A27 rose from 1 earlier trace to about 199 later traces. - None of the activities involved became rare: A25, A26, A27, A35, A36 and A41 still appear in essentially every trace in both windows (about 200 of 200). [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a structural relocation in which the alternative block A35/A36/A41 and the activity A27 effectively exchanged places. In the earlier window, A25 was followed directly by A27, and A26 fed the block, whose members then converged on A30; in the later window, A25 feeds the block (about one third each), the block's members lead to A27 (about one third each), and A26 connects straight to A30, so the block is bypassed on the A26 route and now sits on the A25 route. The block activities still occur in essentially every trace in both windows, so they were relocated rather than removed or made optional. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, LAG001, LAG003]

The hypothesis rests on direct relational evidence: successor profiles changed at A25, A26 and all three block members, the A26–A30 gap collapsed from four steps to one while the A25–A27 gap grew from one to four, and the within-five-step co-occurrences switched exactly as a relocation predicts (A26–block and block–A30 disappearing, A25–block and block–A27 appearing). The positional shifts agree with the same story. With 20 qualifying items against roughly 0.6 expected by chance for 120 tested units, chance sampling cannot explain the pattern as a whole, though the items largely reflect one underlying restructuring rather than independent confirmations. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG007, POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005]

Limitations: the DIR shares are computed over outgoing transitions rather than cases, and several after-window gap distributions rest on only 1–2 observations; the evidence also cannot fully separate one coordinated relocation from two independent rewirings (A25 taking over the block while A26 skips to A30), although the mirrored gap changes make a single relocation the more parsimonious reading. [LAG004, LAG005, LAG006, LAG008, LAG009, LAG010]

Other differences: a handful of low-frequency transitions (about 0.5 in 100 of a source's outgoing transitions) appear or disappear at A25, A26, A35, A36 and A41; these are at the level expected from sampling variation in 200-trace windows. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005]
