### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, the activities from A26 to A27 formed one rigid sequence, A26 → A17 → A15 → A18 → A19 → A21 → A20 → A27: each of these activities had exactly one observed successor (100 in 100 outgoing transitions), and each measured activity pair was always separated by the same fixed distance of 1–5 steps. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006, DIR007, LAG001, LAG006, LAG015, LAG017, LAG021]
- In the later window, every one of these activities has several successors. A15 still leads to A18 in about 68 in 100 transitions but also to A19, A20 and A21 in about 11, 10 and 11 in 100; A18 leads directly to A27 in about 52 in 100 (A27 used to sit four steps after A18); A26 leads to A17 in about 53 in 100 and directly to A19 in about 47 in 100, although A19 previously followed A26 only four steps later via A17, A15 and A18. [DIR001, DIR003, DIR007, LAG013, LAG023]
- The fixed pair distances dissolved into ranges: A15→A18 was always 1 step apart and is now 1 step in about 68 in 100 cases, 2 steps in about 22, and up to 4 steps; A17→A18 was always 2 steps and is now spread over 2–5 steps; A26→A15 was always 2 steps and is now spread over 2–5, with 5 steps the most common (about 32 in 100). [LAG001, LAG007, LAG020]
- Several pairs now occur in both orders: A19 leads to A17 in about 24 in 100 and to A15 in about 11 in 100 of its transitions (before, it always led to A21), and A20 leads to A15, A18 and A17 in about 20, 20 and 12 in 100 (before, always to A27), so the earlier one-way order between these activities is gone. [DIR004, DIR005]
- Typical positions moved toward each other: A15, A17 and A18 now occur later in the trace (mean relative position about 0.59 to 0.64, 0.56 to 0.59 and 0.62 to 0.68), while A19, A20 and A21 occur earlier (about 0.65 to 0.59, 0.71 to 0.68 and 0.68 to 0.64), so the formerly strictly ordered activities now overlap in position. [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006]
- The direct A21→A20 succession largely persisted (about 67 in 100 still immediate, versus all cases before) but now shows gaps of 2–4 steps in roughly a third of cases. [DIR006, LAG018]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the ordering constraints among A15, A17, A18, A19, A20 and A21 — the block between A26 and A27 — were relaxed: activities that previously always followed one another at fixed distances in the single chain A26 → A17 → A15 → A18 → A19 → A21 → A20 → A27 now execute in varying orders with variable gaps, A26 now often enters the block directly at A19 (about 47 in 100 of its transitions), and A18 now often leads straight to A27 (about 52 in 100). [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006, DIR007, LAG001, LAG007, LAG013, LAG020, LAG023]

Supporting signs: all seven successor profiles changed from a single successor to several, all 24 measured pair distances spread from one fixed value to a range, and several pairs now appear in both orders — relational evidence of lost ordering constraints rather than a repositioning of individual activities; the position shifts (earlier activities later, later activities earlier) are consistent with the same reshuffling. The 37 qualifying items are roughly 85 times the 0.43 expected for an unchanged process, and each item's difference exceeded about 199 of 200 random re-splits, so chance sampling cannot account for the pattern as a whole. [DIR001–DIR007, POS001–POS006, LAG001–LAG024]

Limitations: the evidence cannot distinguish a uniform loosening of the order from a mixture of variants in which the old strict sequence coexists with reordered executions, and some pair-level distance items rest on modest support (for example A15→A19 with about 52 of 200 later-window traces, and A18→A21 with about 64). POS and LAG use event positions, so no conclusion about clock-time timing follows. [LAG002, LAG004, LAG012]

Other differences: none that stand apart from this reshuffling — the loosened links into A27 (from A18, A19 and A21) and the partially preserved A21→A20 succession fit the same pattern. [LAG013, LAG016, LAG018, LAG019]
