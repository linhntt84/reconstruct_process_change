### Observed differences

- The successors of A19, A20 and A25 changed almost completely. In the earlier window, their outgoing transitions split between A28 (about 47–56 in 100) and A32 (about 43–48 in 100); in the later window they go to A26 in about 88–94 in 100 of transitions, with only scattered transitions to other activities. These shares rest on about 62–79 outgoing transitions per window [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003].
- A27 and A28 became tightly chained. Earlier, A27 led to A28 in about 47 in 100 transitions and to one of A19/A20/A25 in about 50 in 100; later, about 98 in 100 of A27's transitions go to A28. A28 earlier led to A32 in about half of its transitions and to A19/A20/A25 in about 47 in 100; later, about 96 in 100 go to A32. Distances agree: A27→A28 within one step rose from about 48 to 98 in 100, A28→A32 within one step from about 51 to 96 in 100, and A27→A32 moved from typically three steps apart (96 in 100) to two steps (94 in 100) [DIR004, DIR005, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006].
- A45 rerouted from A26 to the A19/A20/A25 group. Earlier, about 85 in 100 of A45's transitions went to A26, typically one step later; later, A45 leads to A19, A20 and A25 (about 29, 29 and 27 in 100), and A26 now follows A45 two to three steps later (82 in 100 at distance two), with no direct A45→A26 transitions observed [DIR006, LAG007].
- A19, A20 and A25 occur earlier in the trace: their mean relative position moved from about 0.60–0.61 to about 0.32–0.34, close to A26's position (about 0.33–0.36), while their participation stayed roughly stable (about 31–39 in 100 traces in both windows), suggesting they still act as alternatives with roughly one of them per trace [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004].
- The old pairings A19→A32, A25→A28 and A25→A32 nearly vanished (observed in 76, 33 and 61 before-window traces but only 1, 1 and 3 after-window traces), so the apparent new distances for these pairs rest on very few observations [LAG001, LAG002, LAG003].
- A15 still occurs in every trace but repeats less often: mean occurrences per trace fell from about 1.9 to 1.6, and recurrence from about 47 to 38 in 100 traces [OCC001].

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the alternative group A19, A20 and A25, which in the earlier window sat in the later part of the trace between A27/A28 and A28/A32, is placed in the earlier part of the trace in the later window, between A45 and A26: A27 now flows directly to A28 and A28 directly to A32, while A45 feeds one of A19/A20/A25, which then leads to A26. This is shown directly by the successor changes (A19/A20/A25 now lead to A26, A45 now leads to the group, and A27 and A28 lost their edges into the group), corroborated by the group's position shift from about 0.6 to about 0.33 at stable participation and by the lag distributions with full support [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006, POS001, POS002, POS003, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006, LAG007].

The screening context supports this reading: 20 control-flow items qualified, many times the roughly 0.57 expected for an unchanged process, and every item's difference exceeds about 995 of 1,000 random re-splits, so the ensemble is not what sampling alone produces [OCC001, DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006, POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006, LAG007]. Limitations: the new distances of the nearly vanished pairs rest on only 1–3 after-window observations, and the evidence cannot distinguish a relocation of the same steps from the old block being removed and an equivalent block inserted before A26 — a competing explanation the trace data cannot rule out [LAG001, LAG002, LAG003].

Other differences: A15 repeats less often (mean about 1.9 → 1.6 occurrences per trace; recurrence in about 47 → 38 in 100 traces) while still appearing in every trace, and A26, A27 and A28 each sit slightly later in the trace (about 0.02–0.03 in relative position) [OCC001, POS004, POS005, POS006].
