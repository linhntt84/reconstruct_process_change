### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that, in the later window, the six-activity block A33, A35, A36, A37, A38 and A39 was executed in nearly every case — earlier in only about half of the cases — and that the flow through it was rerouted: A30 now leads almost exclusively into A35, A33 and A36, which in turn lead on to A37, A38 and A39, while A31, previously a direct successor of A33, A35 and A36, now follows that group at a distance of four to five steps. The signs are direct and consistent: participation roughly doubled for all six activities (about 98–105 versus 197–199 of 200 traces), the direct A30→A37/A38/A39 and A33/A35/A36→A31 transitions collapsed to under 1% of outgoing transitions while transitions into A37–A39 appeared, and the pairwise distances moved from 1–3 to 4–5 steps. The overall yield also far exceeds what a stable process produces: 46 control-flow items qualified where fewer than one would be expected without change. [screening, OCC005, OCC010, DIR005, DIR006, DIR007, DIR008, LAG005, LAG008, LAG011, LAG013]

Limitations: the evidence contains no relation (REL) items, so the exact new sequence inside the block cannot be pinned down, and a few qualifying lag items were too sparse in one window to carry weight on their own (for example A30→A31 with only 5 later observations, and pairs with 1–26 observations). [LAG004, LAG009, LAG010, LAG012, LAG014]

An important competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out: the same successor shares and pairwise distances could also arise from other internal arrangements of the block — for example, other activities inserted between A30 and A37–A39, or A31 placed elsewhere in the later part of the trace — so the rerouting is pinned down at the level of edges and distances, not as a unique full ordering. [DIR005, DIR006, DIR007, DIR008, LAG005, LAG008, LAG011, LAG013]
