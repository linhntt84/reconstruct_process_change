### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, A24 was always followed by exactly one of A14, A16 or A19 (roughly 31, 38 and 32 in 100 of A24's outgoing transitions), and each of these three activities led on to A25 in every observed case. In the later window, A35 is followed by exactly one of them (A14 about 44 in 100, A16 and A19 about 28 each), and each of the three now always leads to A20. [DIR001, DIR003, DIR006, DIR007, DIR008]

- A15, A17 and A18 run in every trace in both windows, leading to one another in varying order. Earlier, A35 led into this group and its members exited to A20 (about 29–39 in 100 of each member's outgoing transitions); in the later window, A24 leads into the same group and its members exit to A25 instead (about 28–39 in 100). [DIR002, DIR004, DIR005, DIR007, DIR008, POS002, POS004, POS005]

- The single-activity block moved from the late to the early part of the trace (mean relative position about 0.68–0.69 before, about 0.34–0.35 in the later window), while A15, A17 and A18 moved the opposite way (about 0.39 to about 0.64). A24 and A20 each sit slightly earlier than before (about 0.65 to 0.57, and 0.46 to 0.38). [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006, POS007, POS008]

- In every trace, the gap from A24 to A25 was 2 steps before and is 4 steps in the later window, while the gap from A35 to A20 was 4 steps before and is now 2 steps. [LAG001, LAG002]

- Within the single slot, the choice shifted toward A14: it appeared in about 31 in 100 traces before and about 44 in 100 in the later window (62 vs 87 of 200 traces), while A16 fell from about 38 to about 29 in 100 and A19 from about 32 to about 28; exactly one of the three still runs per trace in both windows. [OCC001, POS003, POS006]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that two blocks of the process exchanged places. In the earlier window, the early slot ran from A35 through the three-activity group A15/A17/A18 to A20, and the late slot ran from A24 through one activity chosen from A14/A16/A19 to A25; in the later window the slots' contents are reversed, with A35 leading through one of A14/A16/A19 to A20 early in the trace, and A24 leading through the A15/A17/A18 group to A25 late in the trace. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006, DIR007, DIR008, LAG001, LAG002]

The reordering is shown directly and consistently: every affected successor profile flipped completely, the A24–A25 and A35–A20 step distances flipped from 2 to 4 and from 4 to 2, exactly as expected if a one-activity block and a three-activity block changed places, and the two blocks' mean positions moved by about a third of the trace in opposite directions while A24 and A20 stayed in the later and earlier halves. Nineteen control-flow items qualified against fewer than one (0.36) expected if nothing had changed, so the pattern as a whole is unlikely to be a sampling artefact; the items nevertheless largely mirror one underlying change rather than serving as independent confirmations. [DIR001, DIR007, DIR008, LAG001, LAG002, POS001, POS002, POS004, POS005, POS007, POS008]

An important competing explanation remains: the evidence cannot distinguish a genuine exchange of the two blocks from an equivalent rewiring in which A24 and A35 simply redirect to the other block, since both produce the same observed transitions and positions. Positions are event positions rather than clock times, and A25's own position is not observed, so the exact placement of the late slot's exit rests on the transition and distance evidence alone. [DIR007, DIR008, POS001, LAG001]

Other differences: the routing within the single-activity slot changed, with A14 now chosen more often (about 31 to about 44 in 100 traces) and A16 and A19 less often (about 38 to 29 and about 32 to 28 in 100), while exactly one of the three still runs per trace; this is a shift in how often existing alternatives are taken and is not part of the reordering hypothesis. [OCC001, POS003, POS006]
