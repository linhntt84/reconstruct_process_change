- The three activities A25, A26 and A31 repeated far less often in the later window: earlier each occurred about 3.4 times per trace and appeared more than once in about 75 in 100 traces; in the later window each occurs about once per trace (1.04) and repeats in essentially no trace (about 1 in 200). ### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the cycle involving A25, A26 and A31 stopped repeating: in the earlier window traces cycled through these activities about three to four times, with A31 returning to A25 between cycles, while in the later window each of the three activities executes about once per trace and A31 proceeds to A20 instead of back to A25. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, DIR001, LAG002]

The screening context supports going beyond chance: eight control-flow items qualified where only about 0.3 would be expected without a change, and every item has an empirical strength of about 0.995, meaning such differences rarely arise from random re-splits of stable behaviour. The items also describe one coherent difference rather than scattered effects: the repetition collapse (OCC001–OCC003), the vanishing return edge and reverse pair (DIR001, LAG002), the tightened forward spacing (LAG001) and the halved position spread (POS001, POS002) are all facets of the same trace behaviour, so they should be read as one body of evidence, not independent confirmations. The routing and ordered-pair items show the change directly in the relations between the activities, not only in frequencies. [DIR001, LAG001, LAG002, POS001, POS002]

Limitations: the return path is strongly reduced but not shown to be gone — A31 still led to A25 in about 4 in 100 transitions and the reverse pair appeared once, so rare repetitions cannot be ruled out. A26's involvement is inferred only from its occurrence pattern matching A25 and A31 exactly; there is no direct transition or ordering evidence for A26, so its place in the cycle is less certain.
