### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the block of activities A31, A20, A22, A23, A24, A25 and A26, which in the earlier window was executed in a flexible order with several alternative transitions among them (including direct jumps from A23 or A25 to A32), is in the later window executed as one fixed sequence, A31 → A22 → A20 → A23 → A24 → A26 → A25 → A32, each activity followed by its successor within one step. The change is shown directly by relational evidence: every successor profile in the block collapsed to a single dominant edge (about 90–99 in 100), and more than a dozen activity pairs now occur at deterministic lags that exactly match their chain distances, where previously they occurred at mixed, often shorter lags; the position shifts of A23, A24 and A26 agree with the new order. The screening qualified 61 control-flow items against roughly 0.9 expected without change, so sampling variation cannot explain the pattern as a whole. Limitations: the evidence shows the observed ordering became rigid, but it cannot distinguish a change in the ordering constraints themselves from a changed mix of pre-existing execution variants (the earlier flexibility could reflect several fixed variants whose relative frequencies later shifted toward one rigid variant); the many LAG items also describe one underlying trace behaviour and are not independent confirmations.
