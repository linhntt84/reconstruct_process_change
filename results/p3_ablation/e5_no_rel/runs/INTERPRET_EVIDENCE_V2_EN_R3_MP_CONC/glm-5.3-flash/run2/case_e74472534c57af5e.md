### Observed differences

- The dominant difference is repetition of the sequence A30 → A31 → A36. In the earlier window each of the three activities occurred almost exactly once per trace (mean about 1.0, with repeats in at most about 1 in 100 traces); in the later window each occurs about 3.5 times per trace on average, and about 69 in 100 traces contain at least one repeat. Participation is unchanged: every trace contains each of the three activities in both windows. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003]
- The chain itself is stable: A30 is followed directly by A31 in about 98 of 100 observed transitions in the earlier window and about 99 in 100 in the later one, and A31 is followed directly by A36 in the same proportions; the number of observed outgoing transitions from each of the three activities roughly triples (about 200 to about 700), matching the higher occurrence counts. [DIR003, DIR004]
- The exit from A36 changed: earlier, A36 was followed by A25 in about 98 of 100 transitions; later, it is followed by A30 in about 71 of 100 and by A25 in only about 28 of 100. Accordingly, the ordered pair A36 then A30 within five steps appears in about 1 of 200 traces earlier but about 136 of 200 traces later. [DIR005, LAG002]
- Event positions reflect the repetitions: A30 and A36 occur slightly later within traces and with about twice the spread (standard deviation about 0.06 to about 0.12 for both), and the previously fixed gap of two steps from A30 to A36 (about 97 in 100 instances) is no longer dominant — about 12 in 100 later, with a gap of five steps appearing in about 4–5 in 100. [POS003, POS004, LAG001]
- Separately, A05 (present in about half of the traces in both windows) redistributed its outgoing transitions: the share continuing to A14 fell from about 35 to about 18 in 100, while the share continuing to A07 rose from about 19 to about 39 in 100; A09 stayed roughly stable at about 40 in 100. A05 and A14 also appear at earlier relative event positions (A05 median about 0.07 to 0.05; A14 about 0.14 to 0.12, with more spread), and A14's outgoing traffic remains dominated by A15 (about 96 to about 91 in 100). [DIR001, POS001, POS002, DIR002]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the sequence A30 → A31 → A36, previously executed once per trace and exited via A25, is now repeated within traces: in the later window A36 typically returns to A30 (about 71 of 100 of its transitions, versus about 1 in 200 earlier) instead of proceeding to A25, so the same chain runs about 3.5 times per trace, with repeats in about 69 of 100 traces versus about 1 in 100 or fewer earlier. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, DIR005, LAG002]

Supporting signs: the repetition shows directly in the occurrence and recurrence counts; the successor swap at A36 and the A36 → A30 pair appearing in about 136 of 200 traces (versus 1) show the repeat relation directly; and the chain edges A30 → A31 and A31 → A36 are stable, so the repetition reuses the existing sequence. Each item's difference exceeds about 995 of 1,000 random re-splits, and 14 qualifying control-flow items against roughly 0.47 expected by chance makes a sampling artefact unlikely. Limitations: POS and LAG use event positions, so the exact repeat structure and placement cannot be pinned down; the A36 → A30 path was already observable, rarely, in the earlier window, so the evidence shows a rare existing path becoming dominant rather than proving a newly added construct; and the occurrence, direction and lag items all describe one underlying behaviour, so they are not independent confirmations. An important competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that the A05 routing shift is an independent change rather than a consequence of the repetition; the distribution of repeat counts per trace is also unknown beyond the average. [DIR005, LAG002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR001]

Other differences: the redistribution at A05 (away from A14, toward A07) and the earlier relative positions of A05 and A14 are not part of this hypothesis, although earlier relative positions are consistent with traces simply becoming longer; A14's slight move away from A15 as successor is minor. [DIR001, DIR002, POS001, POS002]
