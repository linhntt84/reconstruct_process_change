### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the branch choice after A15 flipped between two alternative groups of successors: in the earlier window A15 led predominantly to A19, A20 and A21, whose activities then ran in about 82 in 100 traces, while in the later window it led predominantly to A16, A17 and A18, which then ran in about 79 in 100 traces, with the former group falling to about 21 in 100. [DIR001, OCC001, OCC004] The change is shown directly in the successor distribution of A15 and is matched by consistent participation shifts in all six branch activities, with no activity disappearing, no new transition appearing and no change in repetition. [DIR001, OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006] Seven items qualified against roughly 0.4 expected if nothing had changed, so chance alone cannot account for the set as a whole; however, the occurrence and direction items likely describe one underlying behaviour rather than serving as independent confirmations. [OCC001, DIR001] The evidence cannot rule out that the two 200-trace windows capture a shift in the mix of cases arriving at A15 rather than a changed routing decision, and it says nothing about what drove the flip. [DIR001]
