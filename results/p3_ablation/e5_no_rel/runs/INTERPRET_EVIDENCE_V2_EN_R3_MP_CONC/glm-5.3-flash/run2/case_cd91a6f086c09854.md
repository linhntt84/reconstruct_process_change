### Observed differences

- Successors of A20 changed completely: in the earlier window, A20's outgoing transitions went to A30 (about 40 in 100), A31 (about 33) and A36 (about 28); in the later window, about 99 in 100 go directly to A22, with A30 and A36 almost never observed as successors. [DIR001]
- Successors of A21 changed in the opposite direction: in the earlier window, A21 was always followed by A25; in the later window, its transitions split across A30 (about 36 in 100), A31 (about 33) and A36 (about 31), while A25 almost disappears (about 1 in 100). [DIR002]
- The exit of the A30/A31/A36 group changed: earlier, each of the three was followed by A22 in roughly 29–38 of 100 of its outgoing transitions and by A25 never; in the later window, each is followed by A25 in about 31–36 of 100 and by A22 almost never. [DIR003, DIR004, DIR005]
- Pair distances flipped: A22 followed A20 after four steps in all 200 earlier traces but immediately in about 99 of 100 later traces; A25 followed A21 immediately in all earlier traces but after four steps in about 99 of 100 later traces. [LAG001, LAG004]
- The earlier adjacencies became rare: A20→A31, A20→A36, A31→A22 and A36→A22 within five steps occurred in all 200 earlier traces but in only about 2 of 200 later traces; each of these later profiles rests on about 2 observations. [LAG002, LAG003, LAG005, LAG006]
- Event positions shifted accordingly: A30, A31 and A36 moved later (mean relative position from about 0.41 to about 0.58–0.59), while A22 (about 0.47 to 0.38) and A21 (about 0.62 to 0.53) moved earlier. [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005]
- Participation and the group's internal behaviour are unchanged: every listed activity appears in essentially all traces in both windows, and within the group each member still follows the other two with roughly equal shares. [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the group of activities A30, A31 and A36 was relocated between the two windows: in the earlier window it sat between A20 and A22 (A20 led into the group, A22 closed it), whereas in the later window it sits between A21 and A25 (A21 leads into the group, A25 closes it), so that A22 now immediately follows A20 and A25 is reached only after the group. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, LAG001, LAG004]

- The rewiring is shown directly by the successor changes at A20, A21 and the group members, each based on all 200 traces per window, and by the pair-gap changes (A20→A22 from four steps to one; A21→A25 from one to four). This is a change in ordering constraints, not a shift in how often existing paths are taken, since successor pairs appear that were previously unobserved and A20 now bypasses the group entirely. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, LAG001, LAG004]
- The position shifts match the relocation: the group moved later, A22 and A21 moved earlier. [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005]
- Sixteen control-flow items qualified, many times the roughly 0.39 expected by chance for an unchanged process, and every item's difference exceeds about 995 of 1,000 random re-splits, so the set as a whole is not what sampling variability typically produces. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006]

Limitations: occurrence counts per trace were not measured, so an alternative reading in which A22 or A25 occur several times per case and only one occurrence changed its surroundings cannot be fully ruled out, although the unimodal position distributions with similar spread argue against it; positions are event positions, not clock times; and the four items on the disappearing relations corroborate only their near-disappearance, not the exact later gaps, as each rests on about 2 observations. [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, LAG002, LAG003, LAG005, LAG006]
