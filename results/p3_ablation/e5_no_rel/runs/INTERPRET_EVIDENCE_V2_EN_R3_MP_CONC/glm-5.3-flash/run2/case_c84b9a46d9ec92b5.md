### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, A19, A20 and A25 each took part in roughly a third of traces (about 37, 31 and 35 in 100 respectively); in the later window they are almost or entirely unobserved — A19 appears in 1 of 200 traces, A20 and A25 in none. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003]
- In the earlier window, the flow out of A41 split almost evenly among A25 (about 32%), A19 (about 29%) and A20 (about 26%), with no direct flow to A26; in the later window about 86 in 100 of A41's outgoing transitions lead directly to A26. [DIR005]
- In the earlier window, each of A19, A20 and A25 led almost exclusively to A26 (about 94–97 in 100 of their outgoing transitions); in the later window A20 and A25 show no outgoing transitions at all (they did not occur), and A19's after-profile rests on a single trace. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR004]
- The distance from A41 to A26 shrank: before, A26 typically followed A41 two positions later (about 85 in 100 cases); in the later window it typically follows immediately (about 86 in 100 cases). [LAG001]
- A26 now appears slightly earlier within traces (mean position about 0.39 vs 0.37) and A41 slightly later (about 0.32 vs 0.33), both measured on all traces; these small shifts are consistent with the shortened gap between the two. [POS004, POS005]
- At A21, the outgoing mix shifted: A24 rose from about 14% to about 35% of A21's outgoing transitions, while A31 fell from about 54% to about 38% and A23 from about 29% to about 25%; all these successors remain in use in both windows. [DIR003]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the intermediate activities A19, A20 and A25, which previously sat between A41 and A26, are bypassed in the later window: a trace reaching A41 formerly continued through one of the three (in roughly equal shares) and then reached A26, whereas A41 now proceeds directly to A26 in about 86 in 100 cases, and the A41→A26 distance dropped from typically two positions to one. [DIR005, LAG001, OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, DIR001, DIR002, DIR004]

Supporting signs: the participation of all three activities collapsed from about a third of traces to at most 1 in 200; A41's redirection is observed directly on essentially all outgoing transitions in both windows (200 vs 202 observations); the lag change is measured on all 200 traces in both windows; and the small position shifts of A41 and A26 point the same way. The volume of qualified items also far exceeds what an unchanged process typically produces: 14 items against about 0.49 expected by chance. [DIR005, LAG001, OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, POS004, POS005]

Limitations: zero participation only means the activities were not observed, so the evidence cannot distinguish a complete removal of A19/A20/A25 from a drastic reduction to rates below about 1 in 200 traces; A19's after-window outgoing profile rests on a single observation and should not be interpreted. The items are mutually consistent but largely describe one underlying difference, so they are not independent confirmations. [OCC001, DIR001]

Other differences: At A21, existing paths are taken with different frequencies — A24 is chosen in about 35 in 100 of A21's outgoing transitions (previously about 14), mainly at the expense of A31 (about 54% → 38%); all involved activities remain well observed in both windows, so this is a routing-share change rather than a replacement of paths, and it is separate from the bypass above.
