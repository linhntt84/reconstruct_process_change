### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the cycle A30 → A31 → A36 stopped repeating: in the earlier window traces typically passed through A30, A31 and A36 three to four times, with A36 returning to A30 in about 70 in 100 of its outgoing transitions, whereas in the later window the cycle typically runs once and A36 exits to A25 in about 93 in 100 of its outgoing transitions. The repetition drop is direct and large (mean occurrences 3.45 → 1.05 with participation unchanged), the routing flip at A36 is observed directly, the A30→A31 and A31→A36 edges persist in both windows, and the loop-back pairs fall from about 150 to about 1–2 of 200 traces. The 12 qualifying items far exceed the roughly 0.5 expected by chance among the 92 tested control-flow units, and each item's difference exceeds about 995 in 1,000 random re-splits of the pooled data, so the ensemble is not what an unchanged process typically produces. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, LAG001, LAG003, LAG004]

Limitations: the later-window profiles for the self-pairs rest on only 1–2 observations and are weak on their own, and the POS and LAG002 items, while agreeing with the hypothesis, reflect the same trace behaviour and are not independent confirmations. A competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is a shift in the mix of case variants between the windows — for example, the later window containing mostly cases that never repeat the cycle — which would produce the same pattern without any change in the routing itself.
