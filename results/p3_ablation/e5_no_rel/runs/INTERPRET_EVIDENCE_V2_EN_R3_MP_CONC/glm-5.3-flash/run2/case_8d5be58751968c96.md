### Observed differences

- Six activities — A33, A35, A36, A37, A38 and A39 — ran in every case in the earlier window (100 in 100 traces, once per case, essentially never repeated). In the later window each of them appears in only about 47–57 in 100 cases (A33/A35/A36 about 47, A37/A38/A39 about 56), still once per case when present. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006]
- The stretch from A30 to the later activities compressed sharply. Before, A30 reached A37, A38 and A39 only across 4–5 steps (for A30→A37, about 48 in 100 pairs at 4 steps and 52 at 5); in the later window about 94–98 in 100 of these pairs are only 1–3 steps apart. The same collapse from 4–5 steps to 1–3 steps (about 95–97 in 100) holds for A33→A31, A35→A31 and A36→A31. [LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG007, LAG010]
- A30's outgoing transitions redistributed: before, they went almost only to A33 (about 34 in 100), A35 (36) and A36 (29); in the later window they spread over all six activities, each receiving roughly 14–20 in 100. [DIR001]
- Direct successions into A31 appeared: in the later window about 33 in 100 of A33's observed outgoing transitions go straight to A31 (about 34 for A35, 24 for A36), whereas before none did. At the same time, these activities' transitions to A37/A38/A39 nearly disappeared (for example A36→A38 from about 14 in 100 to 0, A35→A37 from 13 to 0). [DIR002, DIR003, DIR004]
- Positions shifted consistently with a shorter middle: A33, A35 and A36 moved later in the trace (mean relative position about 0.68 to about 0.73) and A37, A38 and A39 slightly earlier (about 0.76–0.77 to about 0.74); A30 moved later (0.62 to 0.67) and A31 slightly earlier (0.82 to 0.80), with A30 and A31 still occurring in every case in both windows. [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006, POS007, POS008]
- The former close sequencing between the two groups of activities is largely gone: pairs such as A33→A37, A33→A38, A35→A38, A35→A39, A36→A37 and A36→A39 occurred within five steps in about 196–200 of 200 cases before, but in only 4–5 cases in the later window; the later side rests on just 4–5 observations, so the new relative order of the groups cannot be characterised. [LAG005, LAG006, LAG008, LAG009, LAG011, LAG012]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the six-activity stretch A33, A35, A36, A37, A38, A39 between A30 and A31 became optional: before, every case executed all six activities exactly once, while in the later window each of them is skipped in roughly half of the cases (participation from 100 to about 47–57 in 100), and the stretch runs more compactly when it runs — A30 now reaches A37/A38/A39 within 1–3 steps, and A33/A35/A36 lead directly on to A31, successions that were not observed before. Supporting signs: the participation drop is measured directly for all six activities; the distance shortening is observed on six activity pairs with 91–143 supporting cases in the later window; the successor profiles of A30, A33, A35 and A36 show the new direct successions; and 30 evidence items stand against fewer than 1 expected by chance if nothing had changed, each item scoring above 199 of 200 random re-splits. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG007, LAG010, DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004]

Limitations: the 30 items largely describe one underlying difference in one region of the process rather than independent confirmations; the evidence cannot tell whether the block is skipped as a whole or activity by activity, and the new internal ordering of the six activities is only weakly constrained because the cross-group pair comparisons rest on 4–5 later cases. An important competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that the later window mixes two behaviour variants — cases with the full six-activity block and cases without it — which would look the same in these aggregates as a single flow in which each activity is independently skipped. [LAG005, LAG006, LAG008, LAG009, LAG011, LAG012]

Other differences: a few rare new outgoing transitions appear in the later window (about 1 in 200 of the source's transitions), for example from A30 to A02/A03/A06, from A33 to A10/A26, from A35 to A07/A10/A28 and from A36 to A11/A21/A27; they are too infrequent to characterise. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004]
