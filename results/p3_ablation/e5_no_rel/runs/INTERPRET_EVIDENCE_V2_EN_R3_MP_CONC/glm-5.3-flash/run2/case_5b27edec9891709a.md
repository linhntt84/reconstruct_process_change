### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that, in the later window, A23 is executed directly after A20, while the previously mandatory three-activity block A31, A32, A37 — always run between A20 and A23 in varying order before — now runs in only about 47 in 100 traces and is inserted between A21 and A26, where it alternates with A22: in roughly 46 in 100 traces A21 leads into the block, which ends in A26, and in roughly 54 in 100 traces A21 leads to A22 and then A26 with the block skipped. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, LAG001, LAG005, OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004]

Supporting signs: 25 control-flow items qualified where only about 0.4 would be expected without a change, so chance cannot account for the pattern as a whole; the rerouting is shown directly by the successor changes at A20, A21 and the block members and by the A20→A23 distance changing from always four steps to always one; the participation drops are large and well supported (94–107 of 200 traces); and the earlier window shows both A22 and the block were mandatory, so the later alternation is not a shift in the mix of pre-existing variants. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, LAG001, LAG005, OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004]

Limitations: the evidence cannot show whether the block is skipped at random or in a specific subset of cases, and the after-window distances for the (A20, block member) and (block member, A23) pairs rest on one observation each, so only the disappearance of these close relations is established, not any new distance. The six single-observation lag items also describe the same trace behaviour as the successor changes and are not independent confirmation. [LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG006, LAG007, LAG008]

Other differences: A20's mean relative position moved slightly later (0.34 to 0.36) and A26's slightly earlier (0.66 to 0.63); both are small and consistent with the rerouting above rather than independently interpretable changes. [POS001, POS005]
