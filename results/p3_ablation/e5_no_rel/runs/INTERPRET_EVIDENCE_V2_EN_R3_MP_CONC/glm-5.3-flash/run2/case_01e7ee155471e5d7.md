### Observed differences

- **A20's successor changed completely.** In the earlier window, A22 followed A20 in essentially all of A20's observed outgoing transitions (100 in 100). In the later window, A22 almost never does (about 0.5 in 100); instead A30, A31 and A36 each follow A20 in roughly a third of transitions (about 32–34 in 100 each). [DIR001]
- **A21's successor changed in the opposite direction.** Earlier, A21's outgoing transitions went to A36 (about 38 in 100), A30 (about 34) and A31 (about 28). In the later window, A25 follows A21 in about 99.5 in 100 transitions, and the three former targets have nearly vanished (about 0.5 in 100). [DIR002]
- **The exit target of the group A30/A31/A36 changed while its internal routing did not.** Earlier, each group member led to another group member in about two-thirds of its outgoing transitions and to A25 in roughly a third (28–37 in 100). In the later window, the two-thirds internal share is unchanged, but the exit now goes to A22 (about 31–34 in 100) while A25 is nearly gone (about 0.5 in 100). [DIR003, DIR004, DIR005]
- **Pair distances flipped.** A22 used to come immediately after A20 (a lag of 1 step in all 200 traces) and now comes four steps after A20 (about 99.5 in 100); A25 used to come four steps after A21 (all 200 traces) and now comes immediately after A21 (about 99.5 in 100). [LAG001, LAG002]
- **Positions shifted coherently.** A30, A31 and A36 each occur earlier in the trace (mean relative position about 0.59 before vs about 0.41 after), while A22 (about 0.38 to 0.47) and A21 (about 0.53 to 0.62) occur later. All of these activities still appear in essentially every trace in both windows (200 of 200), so nothing disappeared. [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005]
- Six further lag items qualify, but each rests on a single after-window observation: pairs that occurred within one to three steps in most earlier traces (A21 with A30/A31/A36, and A30/A31/A36 with A25) co-occur within five steps in only about 1 in 200 later traces. They corroborate the residual shares above but carry little weight alone. [LAG003, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006, LAG007, LAG008]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the three activities A30, A31 and A36 changed their place in the flow: in the earlier window they ran after A21 and exited to A25, whereas in the later window they run directly after A20 and exit to A22. Along with this, A22 moved from immediately after A20 to after the group, and A25 moved from after the group to immediately after A21. The group's internal behaviour — its members succeed one another in about two-thirds of transitions, in no fixed order — is unchanged; what changed is where the group sits in the sequence and where it leads. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, LAG001, LAG002, POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005]

Supporting signs: 18 evidence items qualified where roughly 0.38 would be expected if nothing had changed, so the pattern as a whole is far beyond what random re-splits produce; the decisive items rest on all 200 traces in each window; and successor changes, pair distances and positions all show the same reordering directly and consistently. Since every involved activity occurs in about 100 in 100 traces in both windows, this is a change in ordering, not in participation.

Limitations: the evidence cannot rule out that the new successor pattern at A20 and the new successor pattern at A21 are independent routing changes rather than one relocation of the group, because no single indicator spans the whole segment. The six lag items based on single after-window observations were not used as load-bearing support, and lag/position statements refer to event positions (steps), not clock time.
