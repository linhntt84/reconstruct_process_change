### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the fixed sequence A31 → A22 → A20 → A23 → A24 → A26 → A25 → A32 was loosened into a variable order in the later window: single fixed successions gave way to several successors, spacing between the activities spread from one deterministic distance to one-to-five steps, A23 now often leads directly to A32 so that A24, A26 and A25 are bypassed, and some pairs (for example A24 and A22) now occur in both successions where previously only one direction and one distance existed. [DIR004, DIR005, DIR006, DIR007, DIR008, DIR009, DIR010, LAG004, LAG011, LAG013, LAG016, LAG028, LAG029]

This rests on direct relational evidence — successor profiles and ordered-pair distances — with about 200 outgoing transitions or pair observations per activity in each window, and it is consistent across routing, spacing and position indicators. The screening tested 180 control-flow units and flagged 55 items where fewer than 1 would be expected if nothing had changed, so the ensemble cannot be explained as chance sampling. [DIR004, DIR010, LAG004, LAG029, POS003, POS008]

Limitations: positions and distances are measured in event positions, not clock time, so a reordering of the chain cannot be distinguished from parallel or interleaved execution; and because the old successions still occur (A20→A23 about 67 in 100, A24→A26 about 61 in 100), the evidence cannot tell whether the fixed chain itself was modified or whether the later window mixes the old variant with a new one — the main competing explanation. [DIR004, DIR007]

Other differences: A10 now typically runs between A14 and A15 (A14→A10 in about 66 of 100 of A14's outgoing transitions, A10→A15 usually adjacent, A14–A15 direct adjacency down from about 49 to 20 in 100); A04 and A08 appear in fewer traces (about 21 to 11 and 20 to 4 in 100, with A08's routing profile based on only 8 observations afterwards); and A45→A46 and A47→A48 became near-exclusive.
