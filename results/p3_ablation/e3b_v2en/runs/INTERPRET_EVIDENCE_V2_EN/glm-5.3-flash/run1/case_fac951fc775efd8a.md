### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the placement of two groups of activities was exchanged around A25 and A29: the group A19, A21, A24 — now commonly entered from the much more frequent A02, which in turn follows A40 — moved from the late part of traces to before A25/A29, while the group A20, A22, A23 moved from before A25 to after it. Accordingly, A19/A21/A24 now flow into A25 (previously A30), A20/A22/A23 now flow into A30 (previously A25), and A29's and A40's routing changed exactly as this rearrangement would predict. [REL017, REL024, REL027, POS002, POS003, POS005, POS006, DIR002, DIR003, DIR007, DIR008, DIR009]

The support is direct and consistent: the order reversals are near-total in both directions across 200-trace windows, the positional shifts of all six activities match the reversal, and the successor changes mirror each other (A30→A25 for one group, A25→A30 for the other). The volume of evidence (58 items against roughly 0.75 expected for an unchanged process) cannot be explained by chance as a whole. [REL017, REL025, DIR002, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006]

Limitations: many items describe the same underlying trace behaviour — the numerous relation items are highly correlated rather than independent confirmations — and a few lag details rest on only 1–2 observations in one window [LAG001, LAG002, LAG005, LAG006]. A02's outgoing profile in the earlier window is based on only 16 traces, so its exact former successors are uncertain [DIR001]. Importantly, the evidence cannot distinguish a within-trace relocation of these groups from a complete shift in the mix of process variants (a variant with the old order disappearing and one with the new order appearing), since both would produce identical indicator patterns; sampling differences in variant frequencies remain a competing explanation in principle, though near-deterministic reversals make pure noise implausible. A41's increased repetition appears to be a separate change not explained by the rearrangement [OCC002].
