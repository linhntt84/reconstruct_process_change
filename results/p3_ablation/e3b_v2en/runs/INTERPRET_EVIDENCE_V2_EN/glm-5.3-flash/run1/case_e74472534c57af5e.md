### Observed differences

- The activities A30, A31 and A36 still occur in every trace, but each now occurs about 3.5 times per trace on average, up from about 1.0; within-trace repetition rises from roughly 1 in 100 traces to about 68–69 in 100. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003]
- The successor profile of A36 flips: before, about 98 in 100 of its outgoing transitions went to A25; in the later window about 71 in 100 go to A30 and only about 28 in 100 to A25. The forward steps A30→A31 and A31→A36 remain dominant (about 99 in 100) in both windows. [DIR003, DIR004, DIR005]
- Strict precedence around this block largely disappears: A14 precedes A30 in about 99 in 100 traces before versus about 32 in 100 after, with interleaved-or-repeated orderings rising to about 68 in 100; the same pattern holds for A14–A36 and A30–A36. A14's own outgoing flow still goes mainly to A15 (about 90 in 100, down from about 96). [REL003, REL004, REL005, DIR002]
- Close succession changes accordingly: A30 followed by A36 two positions later drops from about 97 in 100 traces to about 12 in 100, while the reverse pair A36 then A30 — seen in only 1 of 200 traces before — now appears in about 136 of 200 traces, with one-position gaps (about 11 in 100 cases) and four-position gaps (about 5 in 100) the most common spacings. [LAG001, LAG002]
- A05 shifts secondarily: it appears in about 52 in 100 traces (down from 55) at a slightly earlier position, its outgoing share to A14 halves (about 35 to 18 in 100) while the share to A07 roughly doubles (about 19 to 39 in 100), and A05 now precedes A30/A36 in only about 17 in 100 traces (previously about 54). [DIR001, POS001, REL001, REL002]
- The positions of A30 and A36 become roughly twice as variable, with later upper quartiles, consistent with their repeated occurrence at varying points of the trace. [POS003, POS004]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the sequential block A30 → A31 → A36, previously executed exactly once per case after A14, is now re-executed several times within the same case — a cyclic repetition in which A36 feeds back into A30 — with A14 interleaved between iterations rather than strictly preceding the block. The repetition is directly visible in the roughly tripled per-trace counts and the rise of within-trace recurrence to about two-thirds of traces at unchanged full participation [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003], and the cycle's back edge is observed directly in routing, where A36's dominant successor switches from A25 to A30 while the forward edges persist [DIR003, DIR004, DIR005].

The relational evidence agrees: strict precedence among A14, A30 and A36 gives way to mostly interleaved orderings, the tight two-position A30→A36 succession collapses, and the reverse close succession A36→A30 emerges; with 19 qualifying items against roughly 0.5 expected for an unchanged process, the collection as a whole is unlikely to arise from sampling noise alone. [REL003, REL004, REL005, LAG001, LAG002]

Limitations: the items overlap in describing one underlying behaviour, so they are not independent confirmations, and the evidence cannot rule out that the windows reflect a shift in the mix of case variants — for example a previously rare variant that repeats the block becoming dominant — rather than a modification of one shared flow; before the change none of the 200 traces shows repetition of A30, A31 or A36, so such a variant would have been very rare, but finite samples can miss rare variants entirely. The reverse-succession profile rests on a single before-window observation [LAG002], and the positional changes alone do not establish that any activity was relocated.
