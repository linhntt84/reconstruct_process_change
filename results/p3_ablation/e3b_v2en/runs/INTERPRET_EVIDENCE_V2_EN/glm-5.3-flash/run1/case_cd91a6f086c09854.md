### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the segment formed by A30, A31 and A36, which previously executed between A20 and the pair A22/A21, now executes after A21 and before A25 — equivalently, A22 and A21 now run ahead of that segment. Before, A20 fed the segment, the segment handed partly to A22, and A21 passed straight to A25; in the later window A20 feeds A22 directly, A21 feeds into the segment, and the segment hands on to A25. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, LAG001, LAG004]

The order change is shown directly by the pairwise relations, which flip from 100 in 100 to about 99 in 100 for all six activity pairs, and it is consistent with the successor changes at A20, A21, A30, A31 and A36, the step-distance changes and the position shifts. With 22 qualifying items against roughly 0.42 expected if nothing had changed, chance alone cannot account for the pattern as a whole. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, LAG001, LAG004, POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005]

Limitations: the evidence cannot tell whether the segment moved later or whether A21 and A22 moved earlier, since both readings describe the same relational change; the link from A22 to A21 and the segment's internal order (which varies in both windows) are not directly observed; several items restate the same trace behaviour and are not independent confirmations; and four lag profiles rest on only 2 after-window traces. The main competing explanation is a different mix of trace variants between the two 200-trace windows — the near-deterministic flips at this scale make a pure sampling effect unlikely, but it cannot be ruled out.
