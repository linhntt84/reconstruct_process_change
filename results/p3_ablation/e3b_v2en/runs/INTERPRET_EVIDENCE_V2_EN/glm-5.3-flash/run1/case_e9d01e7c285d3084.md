### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, A30, A31 and A36 each occurred about once per case (mean roughly 1.0 occurrence per trace, present in every trace, with repeats in at most about 1–2 in 100 traces). In the later window, each occurs about 3.5 times per case on average, and about 68 in 100 traces contain repeats of each of them, while participation stays at 100 in 100 traces in both windows. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003]
- The dominant successor of A36 changed: before, about 97 in 100 observed outgoing transitions of A36 led to A25; in the later window, about 70 in 100 lead to A30 and about 28 in 100 to A25. The number of observed outgoing transitions of A36 rose from about 200 to about 700, in line with its extra repetitions. [DIR001]
- The fixed one-pass sequencing gave way to interleaved-or-repeated orderings: the pairs A14–A30, A14–A36 and A30–A36 still co-occur in every trace, but the strict "first A14, then A30, then A36" order fell from about 98–99 in 100 traces to about 32 in 100, while interleaved-or-repeated orderings rose from about 0–2 in 100 to about 68 in 100. [REL003, REL004, REL005]
- A05 keeps roughly the same participation — it co-occurs with A30/A36 in about 52–55 in 100 traces in both windows — but "A05 before A30/A36" dropped from about 53–54 in 100 to about 16 in 100, with interleaving in about 35 in 100. [REL001, REL002]
- Positions and gaps shifted consistently with longer, more repetitive traces: A14 now sits slightly earlier within the trace (mean relative position about 0.14 vs 0.12) and A30/A36 slightly later with roughly double the spread; the tight two-step succession A30 then A36, which accounted for about 97 in 100 observed cases before, accounts for about 12 in 100 in the later window, with four- and five-step gaps now appearing; A05-to-A14 gaps moved from mostly one to two steps towards two to three steps. [POS001, POS002, POS003, LAG002, LAG001]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the segment formed by A30, A31 and A36, previously executed exactly once per case in a fixed sequence, is now repeated several times per case in an interleaved cycle, with A36 followed by A30 as the dominant repeated transition; A14 remains near the start of the trace, A05 keeps its roughly 50-in-100 participation but loses its fixed position before the segment, and A25 lost its role as the near-universal successor of A36. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, DIR001, REL001, REL003, REL004, REL005]

Supporting signs: the screening produced 14 items where only about 0.49 would be expected by chance in an unchanged process, so the volume of findings cannot be explained by random re-splits; the repetition is observed directly (recurrence in about 68 in 100 traces in the later window versus about 0–2 in 100 before, with participation unchanged), the routing change is observed directly on A36's outgoing transitions, and the order change is observed directly on three activity pairs; every item's difference exceeds 199 of 200 random re-splits of the pooled traces. [OCC001, OCC003, DIR001, REL005]

Limitations: the occurrence, relation and direction items partly describe the same traces, so they are not independent confirmations; the exact shape of the repetition is not pinned down, since A31's repetition is seen only in occurrence counts and no relational or directional evidence places it inside the cycle with A30 and A36; the positional and gap shifts are also consistent with traces simply becoming longer through repetition rather than with A14 being moved; and a large shift in the mix of pre-existing variants between the two finite windows cannot be fully ruled out, although a behaviour present in about 1 in 200 traces before and about 136 in 200 after is very difficult to attribute to sampling alone. [OCC002, POS001, LAG002, REL003]
