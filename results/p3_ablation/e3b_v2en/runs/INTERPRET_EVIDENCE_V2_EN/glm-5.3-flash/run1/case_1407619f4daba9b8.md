### Observed differences

- Four activities became near-universal in the later window: A22 appears in all 200 later traces versus about 108 of 200 (54 in 100) earlier, and A31, A32 and A37 each appear in about 199 of 200 later traces versus about 92 of 200 (46 in 100) earlier. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004]
- Successors were redistributed. A20, which earlier always led to A23, now leads to A31, A32 or A37 (about 32–36 in 100 of its outgoing transitions each) and almost never to A23. A21, which earlier split its outgoing transitions between A22 (about 54 in 100) and A31/A32/A37 (together about 46 in 100), now always leads to A22. A31, A32 and A37, which earlier exited mainly to A26 (about 29–36 in 100), now exit mainly to A23 (about 31–37 in 100). [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005]
- A31, A32 and A37 now occur much earlier within traces by event order (mean relative position about 0.59 before versus about 0.40 after), while A23, A21 and A22 shift slightly later (for example A23 from about 0.39 to about 0.46). [POS006, POS007, POS008, POS004, POS002, POS003]
- Before, A22 and each of A31/A32/A37 were mutually exclusive (co-occurrence 0); in the later window they co-occur in about 199 of 200 traces, with A31/A32/A37 preceding A22. The block A31/A32/A37 also flipped from following A21 and A23 (about 46 in 100 earlier) to preceding them (about 199 of 200 later), and now precedes A26 as well; its internal order stays roughly evenly mixed in both windows. [REL011, REL012, REL013, REL006, REL007, REL008, REL014, REL015, REL016, REL017, REL018, REL019, REL020, REL021, REL022]
- Event distances changed accordingly: A20 and A23 went from immediate succession (gap of 1 in all 200 earlier traces) to a gap of 4 events in about 199 of 200 later traces, and A21 to A26 went from a gap of 2 (about 54 in 100) or 4 (about 46 in 100) to always a gap of 2. [LAG001, LAG002]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the three-activity segment A31–A32–A37, which earlier ran in varying internal order as an alternative to A22 after A21 (each trace showing either A21 → A22 → A26 or A21 → block → A26), now always executes as a consecutive block of three events between A20 and A23, while A22 has become an unconditional step after A21 leading to A26. In the later window, traces show A20 followed by the block and then A23, and A21 followed by A22; the former either/or choice after A21 no longer appears. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, LAG001, LAG002, REL006, REL011, REL014, REL017]

The support is direct and consistent: the successor changes at A20, A21 and the block members, the order flips in the relation indicator, and the gap changes (A20→A23 growing from 1 to 4 events; the gap-4 A21→A26 pattern disappearing) show the changed placement itself rather than only a frequency shift, and all indicator families agree. The number of flagged items (41, against roughly 0.49 expected by chance if nothing had changed) also means the differences as a whole are not what an unchanged process typically produces. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, LAG001, LAG002, REL006, REL011, REL014, REL017, OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004]

Limitations: both windows are samples of 200 traces, so a shift in the mix of process variants or case types between the windows is a competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out, even though near-universal participation (199–200 of 200 traces) and the flip from mutual exclusivity to universal co-occurrence make pure sampling variation an unlikely full account. The evidence also shows only the block's membership and placement; its internal sequence remains variable in the later window. [REL020, REL021, REL022, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004]
