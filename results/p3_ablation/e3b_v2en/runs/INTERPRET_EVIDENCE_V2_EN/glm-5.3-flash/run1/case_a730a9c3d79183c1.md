### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the process was restructured so that two sets of activities that previously ran as alternatives — A33 with A35 and A36 in one, A37 with A38 and A39 in the other, each in roughly half of the cases — are now executed together in one sequence (A33/A35/A36 first, then A37/A38/A39, then A31) in nearly every case, while A04, previously on the path in about 59 in 100 cases typically between A14 and A15/A30, is now usually skipped (about 26 in 100). [OCC002, OCC005, OCC006, OCC007, OCC008, OCC009, OCC010, REL013, REL025, REL031, REL039, REL045, REL050, REL042]

The ordering change is shown directly by relational and lag evidence rather than inferred from frequencies alone: cross-group co-occurrence rose from about 1–3 in 200 traces to about 195–196 in 200 with the same direction across nine activity pairs, the A30→A31 pair within five steps all but disappeared, and A33→A37 appeared in 195 traces. Participation, routing, lag and position indicators agree, the profiles rest on typically 100–200 traces per window, 109 items qualified against about 1.17 expected for an unchanged process, and every difference is larger than about 199 of 200 random re-split comparisons. [REL039, REL045, LAG004, LAG009, DIR004, OCC010]

This remains a possibility supported by the evidence rather than a confirmed model modification: the windows are finite samples, and a shift in the mix of pre-existing process variants (a different case mix entering the two windows) could produce similar frequency and ordering differences. The order within each group also stays mixed (A33 precedes A35 in about 46 in 100 traces and follows it in about 50), and the increases for A02, A08 and A10 rest on about 11–50 supporting observations. Many items describe the same underlying shift, so they are not independent confirmations. [REL037, REL055, OCC001, OCC003, OCC004]
