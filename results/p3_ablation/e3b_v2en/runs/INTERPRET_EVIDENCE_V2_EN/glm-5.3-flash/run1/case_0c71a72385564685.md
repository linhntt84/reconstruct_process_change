### Observed differences

- In the later window, three activities that were essentially absent earlier now occur regularly: A19 appears in about 38 in 100 traces (0 of 200 earlier), A25 in about 35 in 100 (0 earlier) and A20 in about 30 in 100 (1 earlier); each occurs essentially at most once per trace. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003]
- These three activities almost never co-occur — each pair appears together in only about 1–2 in 100 traces — so nearly every later-window trace contains exactly one of them. [REL001, REL002, REL005]
- A41's routing changed sharply: about 82 in 100 of its outgoing transitions previously went to A26, whereas in the later window about 33 go to A19, 30 to A25 and 24 to A20, with only about 1–2 in 100 still going to A26. [DIR004]
- The new activities in turn lead mostly to A26: about 95 in 100 of A19's observed outgoing transitions go to A26, and about 90 in 100 of A25's. [DIR002, DIR003]
- In order terms, each new activity sits between A41 and A26 whenever it occurs: A41 precedes it and it precedes A26 in shares matching its participation — about 37–38 in 100 traces for A19, 28 for A20 and 33 for A25. [REL003, REL004, REL006, REL007, REL008, REL009]
- Accordingly, the A41-to-A26 distance shifted one step later: direct succession fell from about 82 in 100 cases to about 1 in 100, while a two-step gap rose to about 83 in 100 (three steps in about 14 in 100). [LAG001]
- A26 still occurs in every trace in both windows but sits slightly later within the trace (mean relative position about 0.37 to 0.40), while the new activities occupy relative positions around 0.35–0.37. [POS004, POS001, POS002, POS003]
- A secondary difference: the share of A02's outgoing transitions going to A26 fell from about 26 in 100 to none observed, with small new shares to A19, A20 and A25; this rests on few transitions (about 35 earlier, 44 later) and is weakly supported. [DIR001]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that, in the later window, an additional step — executed as one of three mutually exclusive activities, A19, A20 or A25 — now runs between A41 and A26, so that the former direct succession A41 → A26 typically becomes A41 → (A19, A20 or A25) → A26. [REL003, REL004, REL006, REL007, REL008, REL009, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, LAG001]

- The placement change is shown directly, not only through frequencies: A41 precedes the new activity and the new activity precedes A26 in shares that match each activity's participation, A41's successor distribution flips from mostly A26 to mostly the three new activities, and the A41-to-A26 gap moves from one to two steps. [REL003, REL004, DIR004, LAG001]
- The picture is internally consistent: participation rates (about 38, 30 and 35 in 100), near-mutual exclusivity of the three activities, and the relational shares line up almost exactly, and the key relations rest on all 200 traces in each window. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, REL001, REL002, REL005, LAG001]
- The differences are larger than those arising in about 995 of 1000 random re-splits of the pooled data, so their size is unusual for an unchanged process. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, LAG001, DIR004]

Limitations: the evidence cannot show whether A19, A20 and A25 are genuinely new steps or renamed existing activities, since no activity is observed to disappear — A26 and A41 remain present in essentially every trace in both windows. [POS004, DIR004] Sampling differences between finite windows can make a rare variant appear in one window only, which fits A20's single earlier occurrence, but fluctuation alone cannot plausibly explain activities jumping from 0–1 to 59–76 occurrences in 200 traces together with the coordinated order changes. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, REL003, REL004] Finally, the redistribution at A02 is not covered by this hypothesis and rests on few observations, so it may be a separate change or sampling noise. [DIR001]
