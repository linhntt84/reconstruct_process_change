### Observed differences

- The relative order of A26 versus A35, A36 and A41 reversed. Before, A26 came before each of the three in about 99 in 100 of the cases where they co-occur; in the later window each of the three comes before A26 in about 99 in 100 cases, while the activities still co-occur in every case [REL001, REL002, REL003].
- The same reversal appears against A27: before, A27 preceded A35, A36 and A41 in about 99–100 in 100 cases; in the later window each of the three precedes A27 in about 98–99 in 100 cases [REL004, REL005, REL006].
- Successor shares moved accordingly. A25's outgoing transitions shifted from about 99% to A27 to an even three-way split of roughly one third each to A35, A36 and A41 [DIR001]. A26's outgoing shifted from a split across A41 (about 37%), A35 (about 34%) and A36 (about 28%) to about 99 in 100 cases to A30 [DIR002]. For A35, A36 and A41, A30 as a successor fell from about 28–37% to under 1%, while A27 rose to about 32–34% [DIR003, DIR004, DIR005].
- A35, A36 and A41 each occur earlier in the case in the later window (mean relative position about 0.58–0.59 before versus about 0.41 after), while A26 (about 0.53 to 0.62) and A27 (about 0.38 to 0.47) occur later [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005].
- Distances changed consistently: A25 and A27 went from being 1 step apart in about 99 in 100 cases to 4–5 steps apart in about 98 in 100, and A26 and A30 went from 4–5 steps apart in about 99 in 100 cases to 1 step apart in about 99 in 100 [LAG001, LAG003]. The remaining lag items rest on only 1–2 observations in one window and carry little weight [LAG002, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006, LAG007, LAG008, LAG009, LAG010].

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the block of three activities A35, A36 and A41, which still execute consecutively in varying order, changed its placement: in the later window it runs directly after A25 and before A27, whereas before it ran after A26 and before A30. Correspondingly, A27, which previously immediately followed A25, now follows the block, and A30, which previously followed the block, now immediately follows A26; participation itself did not change, as every pair still co-occurs in all cases, so the difference is one of ordering rather than participation [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, DIR001, DIR002, LAG001, LAG003].

The support is direct and consistent: six near-complete order reversals with about 200 observations per window, successor redistributions for five source activities that match the same picture, position shifts in the matching directions, and distance changes that fit exactly (three activities inserted between A25 and A27, stretching that gap from 1 to 4–5 steps, and A30 now adjacent to A26) [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, LAG001, LAG003]. With 26 qualifying items against roughly 0.63 expected for an unchanged process, and reversals that are near-total rather than marginal, chance variation on individual indicators is an unlikely explanation for the pattern as a whole [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006].

The main competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that both windows sample the same two coexisting variants — one with the old ordering, one with the new — and only their mix shifted between windows, rather than a single variant having been modified; the screening also covered control-flow indicators only, and the lag items with 1–2 observations were not relied upon [LAG002, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006, LAG007, LAG008, LAG009, LAG010].
