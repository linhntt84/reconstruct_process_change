### Observed differences

- Four activities became near-universal in the later window: A27 appears in essentially every trace (about 200 in 200, up from about 109 in 200) and now occurs about once per trace on average (mean 0.55 → 1.03); A36, A37 and A42 each appear in about 199 in 200 traces (up from about 93–95 in 200), also about once per trace [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004].
- Routing around this group changed sharply. Out of A25, transitions that previously went to A28 about 97 in 100 times now split almost evenly across A36, A37 and A42 (about 31–34 in 100 each), with A28 rarely the direct successor [DIR001]. Out of A26, transitions to A27 rose from about 54 in 100 to about 98 in 100, while the former minority successors A36/A37/A42 (about 13–17 in 100 each) are no longer observed [DIR002]. Each of A36, A37 and A42 now leads to A28 in about 31–34 in 100 of its outgoing transitions, whereas before they led mainly to A31 and to one another [DIR003, DIR004, DIR005].
- Order relations reversed: A36, A37 and A42, which previously ran after A26 (about 46–47 in 100 traces, never before it), now precede A26 in about 98–99 in 100 traces [REL006, REL007, REL008]; they now precede A28 (about 97–98 in 100) where previously A28 preceded them [REL014, REL015, REL016]; and they now co-occur with A27 in essentially all traces and precede it (about 96–97 in 100), whereas before A27 and this trio almost never co-occurred (about 1–2 in 100) [REL011, REL012, REL013].
- Distances changed consistently: A28 now follows A25 about four event positions later in about 91 in 100 traces (before: one position later in about 96 in 100) [LAG001]; A36, A37 and A42 each reach A28 within 1–3 positions in about 199 of 200 later traces, a relation seen in only 1–2 earlier traces [LAG007, LAG008, LAG009].
- Relative positions moved consistently: A36/A37/A42 occur earlier (mean relative position about 0.59 → 0.41), while A28 and A26 occur later (about 0.40 → 0.46 and 0.54 → 0.60) [POS006, POS007, POS008, POS004, POS002]. The internal order of A36/A37/A42 remains variable, with each ordering roughly equally likely in the later window [REL020, REL021, REL022].

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the three-activity block A36/A37/A42 was relocated: before, it ran after A26 as an alternative to A27 (the two were essentially mutually exclusive, co-occurring in about 1 in 100 traces), while in the later window it runs between A25 and A28, and A27 follows A26 in essentially every trace. This is shown directly by relational and routing evidence — the order reversals between the trio and A26, A28 and A27, the successor changes at A25, A26 and the trio, and the A25-to-A28 distance growing from one to four positions — and it is consistent across participation, routing, order, distance and position indicators. With 48 qualifying items against roughly 0.74 expected if nothing had changed, the volume of evidence is about 65 times what an unchanged process typically produces, so chance alone cannot explain the pattern as a whole [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, REL006, REL007, REL008, REL011, REL012, REL013, REL014, REL015, REL016, LAG001, LAG007, LAG008, LAG009].

Limitations: the items are not independent confirmations, as several indicators describe the same trace behaviour; the later-window LAG items linking A26 to the trio rest on only 1–2 observations and are weak; and positions are event positions, not clock time. An important competing explanation is a shift in the mix of pre-existing variants rather than a rerouting: a "trio before A28" variant could have existed below detection in the earlier window (0 of 200 traces) and become dominant while the old variant fell below detection, and finite samples cannot fully exclude this; the completeness of the flips (0 of 200 versus about 199 of 200) makes pure sampling noise unlikely, but the evidence cannot distinguish a modified structure from such a drastic frequency flip between pre-existing variants [LAG004, LAG005, LAG006, REL011, REL014].
