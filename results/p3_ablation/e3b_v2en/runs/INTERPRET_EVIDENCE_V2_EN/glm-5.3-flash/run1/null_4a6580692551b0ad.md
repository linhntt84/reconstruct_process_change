### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that **A35 and A36 exchanged their order in the segment between A31 and A32**: in the earlier window the behaviour reads A31 → A36 → A35 → A32, while in the later window it reads A31 → A35 → A36 → A32. [DIR001, DIR002, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003]

The support is direct and consistent. The relational evidence shows the swap itself: A31-to-A35 moved from mostly two steps to mostly one, A31-to-A36 moved from mostly one to mostly two, and A32 moved from directly after A35 to two steps after it, matching A36 taking the intermediate position [LAG001, LAG002, LAG003]. The routing evidence agrees: the A35→A36 and A36→A32 shares rose to about 60% while the reverse successors fell to roughly 36–37% [DIR001, DIR002]. Six items qualified where only about 0.5 would be expected by chance, and all point to the same reordering; note, however, that they likely reflect one underlying trace behaviour rather than independent confirmations. [DIR001, DIR002, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004]

Limitations remain. The windows are finite samples of 200 traces each, so a shift in the mix of pre-existing variants — for example more traces taking one of the alternative routings that already existed — could produce similar share changes without a structural modification. The roughly 1-in-100 new successor targets may be rare variants appearing in only one window, and the weakening of the immediate A36→A35 succession rests on only about 34 later-window observations. [DIR001, DIR002, LAG004]
