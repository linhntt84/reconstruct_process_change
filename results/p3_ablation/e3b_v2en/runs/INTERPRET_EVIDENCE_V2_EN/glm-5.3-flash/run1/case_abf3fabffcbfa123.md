### Observed differences

- Three activities are almost never observed in the later window: A32, A33 and A38 each fall from about 48–49% of traces (roughly 97–99 of 200) to about 1 of 200, and pairs among them (e.g., A32 with A33, A32 with A38) are present together in essentially no later traces, versus about half of traces before. [OCC005, OCC006, OCC007, POS004, POS005, POS006, REL028, REL029, REL030]
- A27 moves in the opposite direction: it appears in essentially every later trace — participation rises from about 52% (103 of 200) to about 100% (199 of 200), with about one occurrence per trace. [OCC004]
- Routing after A26 changes sharply: outgoing transitions from A26 to A27 rise from about 50% to about 98%, while the roughly 48% that previously went to A32, A33 or A38 disappear; A27 now precedes A28 in about 96% of all traces (before, about 51%), and A28 follows A26 two positions later in about 95% of traces (before, the gap was split between two and four positions). [DIR006, REL017, REL021, LAG003]
- A04 is executed far less often and in a different place: participation drops from about 68% (136 of 200) to about 22% (43 of 200), and when it occurs it sits later in the trace (mean relative position about 0.21 → 0.40, upper quartile 0.19 → 0.70). The A14 → A04 → A15 route largely disappears: A14's outgoing transitions to A04 fall from about 63% to 10% while those to A15 rise to about 82%, A15 follows A14 immediately in about 47% of cases (19% before), A04 precedes A15 in only about 5% of traces (38% before), and A04's own outgoing spread widens (to A15: about 90% → 48% of its transitions). [OCC002, POS001, DIR004, DIR002, LAG001, LAG002, REL002]
- A02 and A08 appear in a larger share of traces (A02 about 5% → 15%, A08 about 5% → 21%) with substantially redistributed outgoing transitions; these profiles rest on fewer observations (roughly 10–41 per window). [OCC001, OCC003, DIR001, DIR003]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis of a rerouting in which the alternative continuation after A26 through A32, A33 and A38 is no longer executed, while A27 — previously chosen in about half of the cases — now runs in nearly every trace between A26/A14/A15 and A28; in addition, the A04 step between A14 and A15 is now usually skipped, with A14 flowing directly to A15 and the remaining A04 executions occurring later in traces, always alongside A27. [OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, OCC007, DIR006, REL021, LAG003, DIR004, REL002, REL004, POS001]

Supporting signs: the 62 qualifying items are many times the roughly 0.8 expected for an unchanged process, so the pattern as a whole cannot be explained by chance; the key changes are large (A32/A33/A38 from about 97–99 to 1 of 200 traces; A26 → A27 from about 50% to 98% of outgoing transitions) and agree across occurrence, routing, position, relation and lag indicators with substantial per-window support (roughly 100–200 observations for the main items). Note that the many relation items largely restate the same rerouting from different angles, so they are consistent rather than independent confirmations. [OCC005, DIR006, REL021, LAG003, POS001]

Limitations: zero participation in the later window shows only that A32, A33 and A38 were not observed, not that they are unreachable — they could still be executable in fewer than about 1 in 200 cases; and with finite windows, variant frequencies can differ by sampling alone, so the residual single executions and the modest rises in A02 and A08 could partly reflect which variants happened to be sampled, although differences of this magnitude are hard to attribute to sampling alone. [OCC005, OCC006, OCC007, OCC001, OCC003]
