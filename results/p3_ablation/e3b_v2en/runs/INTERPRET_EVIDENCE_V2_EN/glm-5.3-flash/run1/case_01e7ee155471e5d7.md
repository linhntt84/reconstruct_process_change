### Observed differences

- The activities concerned (A20, A21, A22, A25, A30, A31, A36) all still run in every trace: for each tested pair, both activities co-occur in 100% of traces in both windows, so the differences concern order and routing, not participation. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006]
- The order around A21 and A22 reversed. In the earlier window A21 ran before A30, A31 and A36, and A22 ran before A30, A31 and A36, each in 100% of traces; in the later window both orders are reversed in about 99.5% of traces (roughly 199 of 200). [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006]
- Successors changed accordingly. A20, which earlier passed control to A22 in 100% of its outgoing transitions, now passes to A30, A31 or A36 in roughly equal thirds (about 33% each) and to A22 in under 1%. A21, which earlier continued to A36, A30 or A31 (about 38%, 34% and 28%), now continues to A25 in about 99.5% of cases. A30, A31 and A36, which earlier each led to A25 in roughly 28–35% of their outgoing transitions, now lead to A22 in about 31–34%, with A25 almost never their successor. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005]
- Positions within the trace shifted in the same direction (by event order, not clock time): A30, A31 and A36 moved earlier (mean relative position about 0.59 to about 0.41), while A21 (about 0.53 to 0.62) and A22 (about 0.38 to 0.47) moved later by roughly the same amount. [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005]
- Distances changed consistently: A20 to A22 went from immediate (lag 1 in 100% of traces) to four steps apart (about 99.5%), and A21 to A25 went from four steps (100%) to immediate (about 99.5%). The ordered pairs A21→A30/A31/A36 and A30/A31/A36→A25, previously seen within five steps in all traces, now occur in only about 1 in 200 traces, so the later-window lag profiles reported for them rest on a single trace each. [LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006, LAG007, LAG008]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the block formed by A30, A31 and A36 changed its placement in the sequence: in the earlier window it ran after A21 and led to A25 (A20 → A22 → … → A21 → {A30/A31/A36 in varying order} → A25), while in the later window it runs directly after A20 and before A22, with A25 now immediately following A21 (A20 → {A30/A31/A36} → A22 → … → A21 → A25). This is a possibility supported by the evidence, not a confirmed modification. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006]

The supporting signs are direct and consistent: the order reversals are relational evidence and near-deterministic (100% before versus about 99.5% reversed after, with 200 traces per window); the successor changes match the new sequence exactly; and the distance changes fit (A20→A22 stretched from 1 to 4 steps, A21→A25 shortened from 4 to 1). With 24 evidence items against roughly 0.41 expected if nothing had changed, the pattern as a whole cannot be explained by chance re-splits, and every item's difference exceeded 199 of 200 random re-splits. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, LAG001, LAG002, POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005]

Limitations: the six lag items on the rerouted pairs (A21 with A30/A31/A36, and A30/A31/A36 with A25) each rest on a single after-window trace, so the exact new distances there are weak; several items restate one underlying reorder (for example the three A21-versus-A30/A31/A36 relations) and are not independent confirmations; and the evidence cannot distinguish whether the A30/A31/A36 block moved earlier or the A21→A25 part moved later, since exchanging the two segments fits the data equally, nor whether the internal ordering among A30, A31 and A36 (variable in both windows) also changed. Sampling differences in variant frequencies cannot plausibly produce reversals of this size in 200-trace windows, but the finite samples cannot exclude further changes outside the tested units. [LAG003, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006, LAG007, LAG008]
