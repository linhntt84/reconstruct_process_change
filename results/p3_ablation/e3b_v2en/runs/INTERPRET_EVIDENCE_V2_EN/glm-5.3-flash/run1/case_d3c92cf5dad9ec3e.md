### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the block A20–A22–A23 and the block A19–A21–A24 exchanged their places in the later window: A20, A22 and A23, which previously ran after A29 and handed over to A30, now run early between A40 and A25, while A19, A21 and A24, which previously ran between A40 and A25, now run after A29 and hand over to A30. The support is direct and consistent: the order reversals are observed relationally for many pairs, all six position distributions shift in the predicted direction, the successor changes at A25, A30, A29 and A40 line up with the same exchange, and the two well-supported distance items behave exactly as that exchange predicts. With 46 qualifying items against roughly 0.7 expected without any change, the pattern as a whole is far larger than random re-splits of stable behaviour produce. [REL008, REL015, REL018, POS001, POS002, DIR001, DIR002, DIR007, DIR008, LAG005, LAG009]

Limitations: the items overlap heavily — the five relations of A19 and the position shifts within each group largely restate one underlying move, so the judgement rests on the coherence of the pattern rather than on item count. Several lag items rest on only 1–2 observations in one window and could be rare variants. The evidence also cannot fully pin down the anchoring, since A30 and A40 have no position profiles, so an alternative reading in which the exit points A25/A30 changed which block precedes them cannot be entirely excluded; and small shifts in how often A19, A21 and A24 appear — for example A21 from about 35 to about 30 in 100 traces — may reflect sampling differences in variant frequencies between the two 200-trace windows rather than a participation change.
