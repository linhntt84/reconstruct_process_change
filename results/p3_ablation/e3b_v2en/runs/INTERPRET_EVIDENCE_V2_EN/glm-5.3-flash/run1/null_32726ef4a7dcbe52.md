### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that, in the later window, control flow around A36 and A37 is re-routed so that the route A32 → A36 → A37 → A33 is executed more often: A36 is followed by A37 in about 62 in 100 of its outgoing transitions (previously about 34) and A37 proceeds to A33 in about 58 in 100 (previously about 34) rather than returning to A36 (about 37 in 100, previously about 62). The relational items fit this picture: A32 reaches A36 immediately more often, reaches A37 one step later more often, and the adjacent A36–A37 pair occurs in 56 of 200 later traces against 33 of 200 earlier ones. [DIR001, DIR002, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004]

Supporting signs: the routing changes are measured directly on outgoing-transition shares and on event distances, they are consistent across both directions of the A36/A37 exchange and across three pair-distance profiles, each difference exceeds about 199 of 200 random re-splits of the pooled traces, and 7 qualifying items against roughly 0.5 expected by chance over 96 tested units is many times the no-change expectation. [DIR001, DIR002, POS001, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004]

Limitations: the indicators use event positions and transition shares rather than clock time or case-level counts, so the evidence shows existing routes being used in different proportions and cannot establish that a new edge or path was introduced; an important competing explanation is that the later window simply samples an existing, previously rare variant (a path through A32, A36 and A37 to A33) more heavily. The A07 and A14 changes are not linked by the evidence to the A36/A37 cluster and may be a separate shift, and the minor successors of A36 and A37 that vanish from the later profiles rest on only about one or two observations each, so their absence from the sample does not show those edges are gone. [DIR001, DIR002, POS001, LAG001]
