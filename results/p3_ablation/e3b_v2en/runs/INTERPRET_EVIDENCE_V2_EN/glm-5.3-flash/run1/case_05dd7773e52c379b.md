### Observed differences

- In the later window, six activities that each took part in only about half of the cases now take part in nearly all: A28, A30 and A31 rise from about 49 to about 99 in 100 traces, and A32, A33 and A34 from about 52 to about 100 in 100. None of them repeats within a trace in either window, so the difference is wider participation, not extra repetitions. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006]
- The two groups of three were mutually exclusive before: for example, A28 and A32 never shared a trace (A28 without A32 in about 49 in 100 cases, A32 without A28 in about 52 in 100). In the later window, every cross-group pair co-occurs in about 98 in 100 traces, always with the A28/A30/A31 activity first. [REL015, REL016, REL017, REL019, REL020, REL021, REL022, REL023, REL024]
- A25 and A26 occur in every trace in both windows and bracket the six activities. Before, A25 preceded only whichever single group was present; in the later window A25 precedes each of the six activities, and each of the six precedes A26, in about 98–100 in 100 traces. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, REL007, REL008, REL009, REL010, REL011, REL012]
- Routing moved the same way. From A25, transitions into A32/A33/A34 fall from about 52 to about 2 in 100 of its outgoing transitions, while those into A28/A30/A31 rise to about 98 in 100. From A28, A30 and A31, the share of outgoing transitions into A26 drops from about 30–36 in 100 to about 0–1 in 100, replaced by transitions into A32/A33/A34 (about 31–37 in 100). [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004]
- Distances changed accordingly: A32, A33 and A34 now follow A25 typically 4–5 event positions later (before, 1–3), and A28, A30 and A31 now precede A26 typically 4–5 positions earlier (before, 1–3), consistent with the other group now sitting in between. [LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006]
- Relative positions shift in the same direction (A25 mean position 0.67 → 0.62, A26 0.80 → 0.83, A28/A30/A31 about 0.74 → 0.68, A32/A33/A34 about 0.73 → 0.77), while the ordering inside each group of three stays mixed, with roughly even shares in both directions and no interleaving or repetition, in both windows. [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006, POS007, POS008, REL013, REL014, REL018, REL025, REL026, REL027]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

A possibility supported by the evidence is that cases now execute both three-activity groups in sequence — A25, then A28/A30/A31 (in varying order), then A32/A33/A34 (in varying order), then A26 — whereas before, each case executed only one of the two groups between A25 and A26: the A28/A30/A31 group in about 49 in 100 cases and the A32/A33/A34 group in about 52 in 100. Each group thus changed from being absent in roughly half the cases to being executed in almost all. [OCC001, OCC004, REL015, REL022, REL001, REL007, REL010]

The support is direct and consistent rather than merely voluminous: 51 items qualified where about 0.59 would be expected by chance if nothing had changed; participation rose for all six activities; cross-group co-occurrence went from 0 in 200 before-traces to about 98 in 100 with a fixed one-way order, which no re-weighting of the two before-window variants can produce; and the routing and distance changes match the same picture. Every item's difference is also larger than in about 199 of 200 random re-splits of the pooled data. [OCC001, OCC004, OCC006, REL015, REL024, DIR001, LAG001]

Limitations remain: the windows are finite samples, so a both-groups behaviour that existed before but went unobserved cannot be excluded — zero co-occurrences in 200 before-traces bound it to at most about 1–2 in 100 cases — making an extreme shift toward a pre-existing rare variant the main competing explanation. The items also describe largely the same traces, so they are not independent confirmations, the internal ordering of each group stays unresolved, and POS/LAG positions reflect event order, not clock time. [REL015, REL013, LAG001]
