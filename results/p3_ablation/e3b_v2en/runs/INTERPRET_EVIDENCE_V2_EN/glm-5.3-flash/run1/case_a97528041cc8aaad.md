### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility of a structural reordering with two main components. First, A02 now runs as an early step between A14 and A15: it occurs in about 65 in 100 later traces (against about 15 in 100 before), immediately after A14 in nearly all of those cases, and is followed by A15 in about 87 in 100 of its outgoing transitions. Second, the order of two recurring groups was exchanged: A20/A22/A23, which previously ran before the connectors A25/A29 and before A19/A21/A24, now run after them, with the handovers redirected (A19/A21/A24 now exit to A25, A20/A22/A23 to A30). In addition, A06 and A10 are now observed in only about 5–6 in 100 traces. [OCC001, DIR004, DIR001, REL022, REL015, DIR005, DIR006, OCC002, OCC003]

The supporting signs are that the number of qualifying items (87) is far above the roughly 1 expected if nothing had changed, that the order reversals are direct relational observations made across all 200 traces in both windows rather than marginal frequency shifts, and that occurrence, position, direction and relation indicators all point to the same two underlying differences. Many items repeat one behaviour across partner activities (for example the A02 pairs), so they are not independent confirmations, but each component is directly observed with substantial support. [REL022, DIR004, POS003, OCC001]

An important competing explanation remains: the two windows are finite samples and could draw different case mixes, so part of the difference could reflect a re-weighting of existing variant frequencies rather than a modified process; the evidence cannot separate these. Some lag items rest on only 1–3 observations and were not relied on, and the A06/A10 decline rests on 10–12 after-window traces, so those activities may still occur rarely.
