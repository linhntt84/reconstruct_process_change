### Observed differences

- Participation itself is unchanged: A26, A27, A35, A36 and A41 occur in all 200 traces of both windows, and the ordered pairs A25→A27 and A26→A30 are observed in all 200 traces in each window, so the differences below concern order and routing, not activities appearing or disappearing. ### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the segment A35–A36–A41 was repositioned within the flow: before, it ran between A26 and A30 while A25 passed directly on to A27; in the later window it runs between A25 and A27, now leading to A27 instead of A30, and A26 passes directly to A30. This is shown directly by relational evidence — six activity pairs reverse from about 98–99 in 100 one way to about 97–98 in 100 the other, with co-occurrence unchanged at 100% — by successor changes on all five routing profiles, and by the two lag items with full support, with the positional shifts agreeing in direction. The item count (26 against roughly 0.63 expected without change) cannot be explained by chance as a whole, though the items largely reflect one underlying difference rather than independent confirmations. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, LAG001, LAG003, POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005]

A competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that both orderings existed as variants in both windows and only their mix flipped; sampling differences in variant frequencies can move such shares by a few percentage points, but a reversal of this size at 200 traces per window makes pure sampling an unlikely complete explanation. Also unresolved is the segment's internal sequencing: A35, A36 and A41 route to one another in roughly equal shares in both windows, consistent with a varying or repeated internal order, and the ten remaining lag items (1–2 observations in one window) were not relied upon.
