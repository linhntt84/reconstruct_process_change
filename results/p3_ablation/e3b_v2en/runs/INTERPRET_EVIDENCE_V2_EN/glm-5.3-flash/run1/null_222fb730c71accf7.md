### Observed differences

- The dominant successor of A36 flipped: in the earlier window A36 was followed by A33 in about 64 of 100 observed outgoing transitions and by A37 in about 34 of 100; in the later window A36 is followed by A37 in about 60 of 100 and by A33 in about 37 of 100. [DIR001]
- The successor shares of A37 flipped in the complementary direction: earlier, A37 was followed by A36 in about 64 of 100 transitions and by A33 in about 35 of 100; later, A37 is followed by A33 in about 58 of 100 and by A36 in about 38 of 100. [DIR002]
- The ordered pair A36 then A37 became more frequent and more tightly coupled: it occurs in 56 of 200 later traces versus 32 of 200 earlier traces, and where it occurs it is at a step distance of 1 in all 56 later cases versus about 97 of 100 earlier cases (one earlier case at distance 2). [LAG001]
- All three differences are larger than those in about 995 of 1,000 random re-splits of the pooled traces, and the three qualifying items are about six times the roughly 0.48 items expected by chance when nothing changed (96 units screened). [DIR001, DIR002, LAG001]
- A few minor successors of A36 and A37 (each about 1 in 100 transitions, e.g. A07 earlier; A01, A27, A30, A10, A22, A32, A43 later) appear in only one window each and look like rare variants rather than systematic rerouting. [DIR001, DIR002]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the relative order of A36 and A37 reversed with respect to each other and to A33: in the earlier window the dominant chain ran A37 → A36 → A33, while in the later window it runs A36 → A37 → A33. This rests on direct relational evidence — the dominant successor of A36 changed from A33 to A37 and that of A37 changed from A36 to A33 — corroborated by the lag evidence that A36 is now always immediately followed by A37 wherever the pair occurs, and more often overall. [DIR001, DIR002, LAG001]

The pattern is consistent across all three items, and an ensemble of three items against roughly 0.48 expected without change is unlikely for an unchanged process as a whole; the two routing flips point in complementary directions rather than being isolated anomalies. [DIR001, DIR002, LAG001]

The main competing explanation is a sampling shift in the mix of pre-existing variants: both directions between A36 and A37 occur in both windows, so the later window could simply contain more traces of an A36 → A37 variant without any structural change. The routing shares are computed over outgoing transitions (about 90–93 per window), not over cases, and the lag comparison rests on only 32 versus 56 observations, so its proportions are moderately supported; the minor ~1% successors are likely window-specific rare variants. The three items also likely describe one underlying trace behaviour, so they are consistent but not independent confirmations. [DIR001, DIR002, LAG001]
