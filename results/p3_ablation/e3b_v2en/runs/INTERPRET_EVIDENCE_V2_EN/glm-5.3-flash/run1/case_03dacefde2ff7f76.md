### Observed differences

- Before, nearly all observed outgoing transitions of A19, A20 and A25 went to A26 (about 97, 89 and 98 in 100 respectively, on 61–77 transitions per activity). In the later window, none go to A26; instead roughly half go to A28 and half to A32 (A19: ~51% to A28, ~46% to A32; A20: ~51%/~43%; A25: ~38% to A28, ~58% to A32). [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003]
- A45's routing flipped: before, its outgoing transitions spread across A19 (~31%), A25 (~28%) and A20 (~26%); in the later window about 89 in 100 go to A26. [DIR006]
- New links back into the A19/A20/A25 group appear: in the later window A27 sends about 17%, 16% and 15% of its transitions to A20, A19 and A25 (before ~98% went to A28), and A28 sends about 22%, 15% and 14% to A25, A20 and A19 (before ~94% went to A32). [DIR004, DIR005]
- A19, A20 and A25 occur much later within cases: their mean relative position rises from about 0.31–0.34 to about 0.58–0.59, while A26 (~0.35 to ~0.32) and A27 (~0.58 to ~0.55) shift slightly earlier. Participation itself is stable: A26 and A27 appear in every trace in both windows, A19/A20/A25 in about 30–38 in 100. [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005]
- The order of the two groups reverses: before, A19, A20 or A25 preceded A26/A27 in roughly 30–38 in 100 traces and virtually never followed them; in the later window A26/A27 come first in about 30–36 in 100 traces and the original order has nearly vanished (about 1 in 100 or less). Pairs with A28 become mixed, both orders at roughly 15–22 in 100. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, REL007, REL008]
- Adjacencies change accordingly: A25 followed by A26 one step later drops from 63 traces to 1; A45 reaches A26 in one step in about 89 in 100 cases (before mostly two steps); A27→A28 and A28→A32 split about evenly between one and two steps; A27→A32 shifts from two to three steps. [LAG003, LAG008, LAG004, LAG007, LAG005]
- New close pairs appear only in the later window: A20→A28 within one step in 36 traces, A28→A20 in 30 traces, and A20→A32 in 66 traces (~45% one step, ~53% two); the before-side of these three items rests on a single trace each. [LAG001, LAG006, LAG002]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the block formed by A19, A20 and A25 was repositioned relative to A26, A27 and A28: in the earlier window the flow ran A45 → one of A19/A20/A25 → A26 → A27 → A28 → A32, whereas in the later window A45 feeds A26 directly and the block runs after A27 and A28, partly looping back into A28 before reaching A32. This is shown directly by the order reversal in the relation items, the rerouting in the direction items, the loss of the A25→A26 immediate succession and the gain of A45→A26 and A28→A19/A20/A25 successions in the lag items, with the position shifts agreeing; with 27 qualifying items against roughly 0.63 expected if nothing had changed, chance alone cannot account for the pattern as a whole, and every item's difference exceeds about 995 of 1,000 random re-splits. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006, POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, REL007, REL008, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006, LAG007, LAG008]

This remains a possibility supported by the evidence rather than a confirmed modification. The before-side of three lag items rests on a single trace each, so the claim that those pairs were previously far apart is weak [LAG001, LAG002, LAG006]. The main competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is a sampling shift in variant frequencies — for instance a previously rare routing becoming frequent in the later window — rather than a structural change; with 200 traces per window and order reversals of 30–38 percentage points across eight activity pairs, such sampling variation is unlikely but not impossible.
