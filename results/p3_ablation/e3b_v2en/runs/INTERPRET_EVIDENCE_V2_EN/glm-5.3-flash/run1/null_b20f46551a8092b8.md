### Observed differences

- The dominant hand-off from A35 flipped. Before, about 59 in 100 of A35's observed outgoing transitions went to A32 and about 38 in 100 to A36 (three further successors at about 1 in 100 each); in the later window, about 62 in 100 went to A36 and about 38 in 100 to A32, and the previously seen rare successors are not observed. [DIR001]
- A36 changed in the mirror image. Before, about 58 in 100 of its outgoing transitions went to A35 and about 39 in 100 to A32; in the later window, about 59 in 100 went to A32 and about 35 in 100 to A35, with a few new successors at about 1–2 in 100. [DIR002]
- Read together, the dominant hand-offs ran A36 → A35 → A32 before, and A35 → A36 → A32 in the later window. Both directions between A35 and A36 carry substantial traffic in both windows, so the dominant direction reversed rather than one route disappearing. [DIR001, DIR002]
- Both shifts are large relative to random re-splits (each exceeds about 995 of 1,000 reference comparisons) and rest on roughly 103 outgoing transitions per source before versus 87–88 after. [DIR001, DIR002]
- A39's routing barely moved: A40 took 100 in 100 of its outgoing transitions before and about 91 in 100 in the later window, with three successors appearing once each (about 3 in 100 of 34 transitions) — a negligible difference consistent with a rare variant surfacing in one window only. [DIR003]

### Inference

Support level: Weak hypothesis

The evidence suggests that the dominant routing between A35 and A36 reversed: before, the dominant hand-offs were A36 → A35 and A35 → A32, while in the later window they are A35 → A36 and A36 → A32 (A35 to A36 rose from about 38 to about 62 in 100; A36 to A32 rose from about 39 to about 59 in 100). Read structurally, this is consistent with the two activities exchanging their order in the main sequence. [DIR001, DIR002]

Supporting signs: the successor changes at the two sources are reciprocal, each difference exceeds about 995 of 1,000 random re-splits, and three flagged items is about seven times the 0.44 items expected for an unchanged process across the 89 tested units, so chance as a whole poorly explains the evidence. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003]

What is missing: both directions between A35 and A36 remain heavily used in both windows (roughly 35–62 in 100), so the evidence cannot separate a structural re-ordering of the two activities from a shift in the relative frequency of two coexisting routing variants; the two routing items describe one correlated behaviour rather than independent confirmations, no positional or relational evidence (REL, LAG, POS) is available to confirm a placement change directly, and sampling differences in variant frequencies remain a live competing explanation. The A39 item is negligible in size and typical of the noise an unchanged process produces. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003]
