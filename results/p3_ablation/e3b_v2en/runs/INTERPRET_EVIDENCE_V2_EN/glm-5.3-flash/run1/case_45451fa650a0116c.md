### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that, in the later window, the path after A20 runs through A21, A22 and A23 (about 79 in 100 traces, up from about 19) instead of through A24, A25 and A26 (about 21 in 100, down from about 82), and that the late A06 step between A34 and A35 is largely dropped, with A34 now typically followed immediately by A35. This is shown directly by relational and directional evidence — A20 precedes A21/A22/A23 in about 77–78 in 100 of their joint appearances (before about 18–19), A34→A06 drops from about 45 to about 2 in 100 of A34's outgoing transitions, and the A34→A35 gap collapses to one step in about 92 in 100 cases — and the occurrence, relation, direction, position and lag indicators all agree; the new A21/A22/A23 group still precedes A34/A35 in about 79 in 100 traces, as the old A24/A25/A26 group did. With 73 qualifying items against roughly 1 expected in an unchanged process, the pattern as a whole is unlikely to be a sampling artefact. [DIR005, REL011, REL012, REL013, REL014, REL015, REL016, REL023, REL030, REL036, DIR006, LAG006, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, OCC007, OCC008, OCC009]

Limitations: the many relation items largely restate one underlying shift rather than confirming it independently; A06's outgoing profile in the later window rests on 26 transitions and the A06→A35 gap on 7, so the details of A06's remaining role are uncertain; and the evidence cannot rule out that the two windows simply sampled different proportions of already-existing process variants (for example a stable mix of an A21–A22–A23 path and an A24–A25–A26 path), although a swing from about 19 to about 79 in 100 traces with 200 traces per window far exceeds what the random re-split comparisons produce. The modest rises of A02 and A04 are not accounted for by this hypothesis. [DIR001, LAG001, OCC001, OCC002, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, OCC007, OCC008, OCC009]
