### Observed differences

- Six middle activities became near-universal: before, A28, A30 and A31 each appeared in about 49 in 100 traces and A32, A33 and A34 in about 52 in 100; in the later window, each of the six appears in about 99 in 100 traces, still at most once per case. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006]
- Before, the two trios were mutually exclusive: A28, A30 or A31 never co-occurred with A32, A33 or A34 (co-occurrence in 0 of 200 before-traces), so each case ran one trio or the other. In the later window, members of both trios co-occur in about 98 in 100 traces, and in every observed case the A28/A30/A31 trio came first. [REL015, REL016, REL017, REL019, REL020, REL021, REL022, REL023, REL024]
- A25 and A26 still bracket the middle section, but coverage changed: in the later window A25 precedes each trio member in about 99 in 100 traces (previously about 49–52 in 100, since half the cases lacked one trio), and A26 follows each trio member in about 99 in 100 traces (previously about 49–52 in 100). [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, REL007, REL008, REL009, REL010, REL011, REL012]
- Routing shifted accordingly: in the later window about 98 in 100 of A25's outgoing transitions go to A28, A30 or A31 (roughly 30–38% each) instead of being spread evenly over all six trio members (15–19% each); transitions from A28, A30 and A31 into A26 fell from about 30–36% of their outgoing transitions to at most 0.5%, while about 31–37% now go into A32, A33 or A34. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004]
- Ordering within each trio remains mixed in both windows: co-occurring members precede one another in roughly even splits before and after. [REL013, REL014, REL018, REL025, REL026, REL027]
- Event distances are consistent with a longer middle section: the gap from A25 to A32/A33/A34 and from A28/A30/A31 to A26 moved from 1–3 event positions (roughly equal thirds) to 4–5 positions (about 96–98% of observed pairs), with A25 sitting slightly earlier (mean relative position about 0.62 vs 0.67) and A26 slightly later (about 0.83 vs 0.80). [LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006, POS001, POS002]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the middle of the process changed from running one of two alternative three-activity groups to running both groups in sequence: before, each case contained either the A28/A30/A31 group or the A32/A33/A34 group between A25 and A26, whereas in the later window nearly every case contains both groups, with A28/A30/A31 before A32/A33/A34, A25 before all six activities and A26 after all six. [REL015, REL019, REL001, REL007, OCC001, OCC004]

The change is shown directly by relational evidence — co-occurrence of the two groups went from exactly 0 of 200 before-traces to about 98 in 100 after-traces, always in the same order — and it is consistent across participation, routing (new transitions from A28/A30/A31 into A32/A33/A34, with A25→A32/A33/A34 and group-to-A26 transitions largely disappearing) and event distances. The volume of flagged behaviour (51 items against roughly 0.6 expected if nothing had changed, each difference larger than in about 995 of 1,000 random re-splits) exceeds what an unchanged process typically produces. [REL015, REL016, REL017, DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, LAG001, LAG004]

Limitations remain: the windows are finite samples of 200 traces each, so a rare pre-existing variant in which both groups ran could in principle have existed before at very low frequency and simply become dominant; the before-window's zero co-occurrence makes that unlikely but cannot fully distinguish a newly added sequence from a large shift in variant frequencies. In addition, ordering within each group stays variable in both windows, so the evidence concerns which groups run, not a fixed internal sequence. [REL013, REL018, REL025]
