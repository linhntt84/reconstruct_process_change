### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the segment in which A44 was followed by the block A26 → A27/A28 and then A29 is no longer executed, and that in roughly a third of later cases a single activity chosen among A19, A20 and A21 now runs between A44 and A29. This rests on direct relational and directional evidence, not only on frequencies: A44's successors switched from the old block to the new activities, the A44-before-block and block-before-A29 relations collapsed while the corresponding relations with A19/A20/A21 appeared, and the A44→A29 distance shortened from about 4–5 steps to 2–3, consistent with the span now containing one activity instead of three. [DIR007, LAG009, REL018, REL019, REL025, REL026, REL032, REL033, REL038, REL039, REL043, REL044, REL047, REL048]

The sizes involved make sampling noise implausible for the core change: the old block fell from 200 of 200 traces to 3 of 200, and the 81 flagged items far exceed the roughly 0.89 expected if nothing had changed. The newcomers' position (median about 0.36 of the trace) also matches the old block's position (about 0.37), supporting like-for-like placement. [OCC005, OCC006, OCC007, POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004]

Limitations: the evidence does not show what, if anything, occupies the A44→A29 span in the remaining ~60% of later traces, and the old block's residual behaviour in the later window rests on only 3 traces, as do several flagged routing and distance details (for example DIR004–DIR006 and LAG001–LAG008), which should not be over-interpreted. A shift in the mix of pre-existing variants cannot be fully ruled out — A19/A20/A21 had only 2–4 supporting traces before — although that would itself mean variant shares moved from near zero to about a third, a behavioural shift rather than random fluctuation.
