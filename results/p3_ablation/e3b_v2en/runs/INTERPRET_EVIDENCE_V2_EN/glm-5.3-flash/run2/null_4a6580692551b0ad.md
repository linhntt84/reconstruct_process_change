### Observed differences

- The dominant successor of A35 flipped: before, about 58 in 100 of A35's observed outgoing transitions went to A32 and about 38 in 100 to A36; in the later window, about 61 in 100 go to A36 and about 37 in 100 to A32, with a few rare successors (about 1 in 100 each) appearing that were not observed earlier. - The successor profile of A36 flipped in the opposite direction: before, about 60 in 100 of A36's outgoing transitions went to A35 and about 39 in 100 to A32; in the later window, about 60 in 100 go to A32 and about 36 in 100 to A35, again with rare successors at about 1 in 100 each. [DIR002]
- A35 now typically follows A31 one step later instead of two: before, the gap was 2 steps in about 60 in 100 of observations and 1 step in about 39 in 100; in the later window it is 1 step in about 61 in 100. [LAG001]
- A36 moved the other way relative to A31: before it followed A31 after 1 step in about 60 in 100 of observations; in the later window it follows after 2 steps in about 61 in 100. [LAG002]
- A32 receded from A35: before, A32 followed A35 after 1 step in about 58 in 100 of observations; in the later window it follows after 2 steps in about 62 in 100. [LAG003]
- The direct A36→A35 adjacency persists for a share of cases: it occurred at a 1-step gap in all 62 before-window observations and in about 94 in 100 of the 34 after-window observations, though the later figure rests on fewer observations. [LAG004]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that A35 and A36 exchanged their typical positions in the segment between A31 and A32: in the later window the dominant flow runs A31 → A35 → A36 → A32, whereas before it ran A31 → A36 → A35 → A32. [DIR001, DIR002, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003]

The signs are direct and mutually consistent: the successor shares of A35 and A36 flipped in opposite directions, the position gaps from A31 moved in mirror image (A35 closer, A36 further away), and A32 now typically sits two steps after A35, exactly as expected if A36 now lies between them. Six items qualified where about 0.5 would be expected if nothing had changed, and every difference exceeds those seen in about 99.5 in 100 of random re-splits, so chance is unlikely to explain the pattern as a whole. [DIR001, DIR002, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004]

Limitations: the six items largely describe one underlying trace behaviour and are not independent confirmations; the reversal is dominant rather than complete, since A36 is still followed by A35 in about 36 in 100 of its outgoing transitions and the direct pair persists on only 34 later-window traces; the rare new successors may be sampling noise. [DIR001, DIR002, LAG004]

An important competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is a shift in the mix of two pre-existing execution orders (one with A36 before A35, one with A35 before A36) between finite samples of 200 traces per window, which could produce the same flips without any structural change to the process. [DIR001, DIR002, LAG004]
