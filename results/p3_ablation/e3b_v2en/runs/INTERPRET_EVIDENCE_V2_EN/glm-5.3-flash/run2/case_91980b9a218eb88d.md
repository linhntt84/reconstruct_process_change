### Observed differences

- In the later window, A19, A20 and A25 appear much later in traces: their mean relative position moved from roughly one third of the trace to roughly three fifths, while A26 and A27 shifted slightly earlier (about 0.35 to 0.33 and 0.58 to 0.56) [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005].
- In the later window, the order between these activities reversed: before, A19, A20 and A25 typically ran before A26 and A27 (for example, A19 preceded A26 in about 36 in 100 traces and the reverse in almost none); in the later window A26 and A27 typically run first (A26 before A19 in about 31 in 100 traces, A19 before A26 in almost none), with the same flip for the pairs of A20 and A25 with A26 and A27, while orderings involving A28 became mixed rather than uniformly reversed [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, REL007, REL008].
- In the later window, routing out of A19, A20 and A25 largely left A26: before, about 92–99% of their observed outgoing transitions went to A26; after, almost none do, and instead about 39–56% go to A32 and about 39–48% to A28 [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003].
- In the later window, A27 and A28 also spread their outgoing transitions: A27, which sent about 98% to A28 before, now sends about 54% there and 11–16% each to A19, A20 and A25; A28, which sent about 99% to A32 before, now sends about 45% there and 15–21% each to A19, A20 and A25 [DIR004, DIR005].
- In the later window, A45's outgoing routing inverted: before it split across A25, A19 and A20 (about 35%, 35% and 24% of observed outgoing transitions); after, about 94% goes to A26 [DIR006].
- In the later window, A24 takes part in more traces: about 58 in 100 versus about 43 in 100 before [OCC001].
- Typical step distances moved with the reorderings: A27 to A32 is now typically three steps apart rather than two (about 93 in 100 observed pairs), A45 to A26 typically one step rather than two (about 94 in 100), and the A28-to-A19 and A28-to-A20 successions, each seen only once before, now typically follow one step apart (about 92 and 98 in 100 of 32 and 42 observed pairs); several other lag profiles rest on only 1–2 observations in one window [LAG006, LAG011, LAG007, LAG008, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG010].

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the segment containing A19, A20 and A25 was repositioned to a later stage of the process: in the later window these three activities follow A26 and A27 instead of preceding A26, their outgoing routing is redirected from A26 towards A28 and A32, A45 now feeds A26 directly, and A27 and A28 partly hand over to A19, A20 and A25 [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006, REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL006, REL007, POS001, POS002, POS003].

The supporting signs are direct and consistent: 31 evidence items qualified against roughly 0.62 expected if nothing had changed, so the volume alone cannot be explained by chance; the order reversal is shown directly by relational evidence on six activity pairs observed in all 200 traces of each window, the position shift by positional evidence, and the successor changes by routing evidence based on roughly 50–200 observations per window, and all three views agree on the same relocation [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL006, REL007, POS001, POS002, POS003, DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006].

Limitations remain: a change in the mix of existing process variants between the windows — for example one variant family becoming far more frequent — could produce the same rerouting and order reversal without any activity being moved, and the evidence cannot separate a repositioning of the block from such a variant-frequency shift. Several lag profiles rest on only 1–2 observations in one window, so the precise step distances there are weak, and many items describe overlapping behaviour of the same traces rather than independent confirmations [LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG007, LAG008, LAG010].
