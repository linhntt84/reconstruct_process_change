### Observed differences

- In the later window, activity A27 runs in nearly every trace (about 199 in 200, roughly one occurrence per trace) versus about 103 in 200 (about 52%) before. [OCC001]
- Activities A32, A33 and A38, each present in about 97–98 of 200 before traces (roughly 49%), are each seen in only about 1 in 200 after traces. [OCC002, OCC003, OCC004]
- Outgoing routing from A26 changed from a split across exits (A27 about 51%, A38 about 18%, A33 about 16%, A32 about 14% of observed exits) to almost exclusively A27 (about 97 in 100) in the later window. [DIR001]
- The A26–A27 and A27–A28 sequences now occur in about 96–97 in 100 traces each, versus about 52 in 100 before; conversely, co-occurrence of A26 with A32/A33/A38 and of A32/A33/A38 preceding A28 collapsed from about 48–49 in 100 to about 0–1 in 100. [REL001, REL005, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL009, REL010, REL011]
- The distance from A26 to A28, previously split between 2 steps (about 50 in 100) and 4 steps (about 47 in 100), is concentrated at 2 steps in about 97 in 100 after traces. [LAG001]
- The A27-to-A28 link itself is essentially stable (about 97% of A27's exits go to A28 before, about 98.5% after, at a 1-step distance in both windows), and A28's relative position shifted only slightly (mean about 0.55 to about 0.53). [DIR002, LAG004, POS001]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the detour from A26 through A32, A33 and A38 towards A28 is executed far less often or skipped in the later window, so that nearly all cases now run A26 → A27 → A28. This rests on direct, mutually consistent signs: the participation shift (A27 up from about 52% to about 99.5% of traces; A32/A33/A38 down from about 49% each to under 1%) [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004]; the routing concentration of A26's exits onto A27 [DIR001]; the relational evidence that A26-before-A27 and A27-before-A28 became near-universal while all pairings of A26 or A28 with A32/A33/A38 vanished [REL001, REL005, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL009, REL010, REL011]; and the A26-to-A28 distance fixing at 2 steps, matching a single intermediate activity [LAG001]. The 35 evidence items far exceed the roughly 0.6 expected by chance, and participation, routing, relational and lag indicators all point to the same rerouting, although they largely describe one underlying trace behaviour rather than independent confirmations.

Limitations: the after-window profiles of A32, A33 and A38 (their residual exits, positions and lags) rest on a single trace each and are weak, so the evidence cannot distinguish complete removal of the branch from a drastic reduction of its execution probability — near-zero participation only means the activities were barely observed [OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, DIR003, DIR004, POS002, POS003, POS004]. Sampling differences in variant frequencies between finite windows are a competing explanation in principle, but a fall from about 49% to about 0.5% across 200-trace windows lies far outside the variation produced by 200 random re-splits of unchanged data [OCC002, OCC003, OCC004].
