### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, every one of the 200 cases contained A28, A30, A31, A32, A33 and A34 exactly once (participation 100 in 100, no repetition). In the later window, A28, A30 and A31 each appear in only about 46–47 in 100 cases, and A32, A33 and A34 in about 55–56 in 100 cases; mean occurrences per trace fall from 1.0 to about 0.47 and 0.55 respectively. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006]
- The six activities now behave as two mutually exclusive groups. Pairs spanning the two groups (e.g. A28–A32, A30–A32, A31–A34), which co-occurred in every earlier case, co-occur in only about 2 in 100 later cases; instead, about 44–45 in 100 cases contain only the A28/A30/A31 member and about 53–54 in 100 only the A32/A33/A34 member. Within each group, the activities still always appear together (co-occurrence about 0.47 and 0.55). A small share of later cases (about 2 in 100) shows activities from both groups, but this rests on very few traces. [REL013, REL015, REL019, REL024, REL025, LAG005]
- Routing around the segment changed accordingly. A25's outgoing transitions, previously directed only at A28, A30 and A31 (about 34, 37 and 29 in 100), now split between both groups — roughly 46 in 100 to A28/A30/A31 combined and 54 in 100 to A32/A33/A34 combined. Transitions from A28, A30 and A31 directly to A26, absent from the earlier profiles, now account for roughly 26–35 in 100 of their observed outgoing transitions. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004]
- Order is preserved but distances compress: A25 still precedes both groups and the group activities still precede A26 whenever both are present, while the gap from A25 to A32/A33/A34 shrinks from 4–5 steps to mostly 1–3 steps, and from A28/A30/A31 to A26 likewise — consistent with the other group no longer sitting in between. [REL001, REL004, REL007, REL010, LAG001, LAG004, LAG011]
- Relative positions shift only slightly (A25 mean position 0.62 to 0.67 of the trace; A26 0.83 to 0.81), consistent with traces now containing one group of three activities instead of two; a position shift alone does not show that an activity was moved. [POS001, POS002]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the six-activity segment between A25 and A26, which in the earlier window ran in every case — A25, then A28/A30/A31 in varying order, then A32/A33/A34 in varying order, then A26 — now runs as an either-or routing in the later window: each case executes either the group A28–A30–A31 (about 47 in 100 cases) or the group A32–A33–A34 (about 55 in 100), with A25 passing directly into whichever group runs and that group flowing straight on to A26. [OCC001, REL015, REL013, DIR001, DIR002]

This judgement rests on direct relational and routing evidence rather than frequencies alone: pairs spanning the two groups that co-occurred in 100% of earlier cases now almost never do, while within-group co-occurrence and the A25 → group → A26 order are preserved; A25's branching and the new direct transitions into A26 match the same picture, and the step-distance compression corroborates it. With 58 qualifying items against roughly 0.63 expected if nothing had changed, the volume of evidence as a whole is also far beyond what chance screening would produce. [REL015, REL007, DIR001, DIR004, LAG001, LAG011]

Limitations remain: the group shares (about 46.5% vs 55.5%) are estimates from 200 traces per window and could shift in other samples; the few cases containing both groups rest on about four traces; and in principle the either-or behaviour could have existed earlier as a variant that the 200 earlier traces did not sample, although its complete absence alongside 100% co-occurrence in every earlier case makes a pure sampling explanation unlikely. Several indicators also describe the same trace behaviour, so the agreeing items are not independent confirmations. [REL015, LAG005, OCC001]
