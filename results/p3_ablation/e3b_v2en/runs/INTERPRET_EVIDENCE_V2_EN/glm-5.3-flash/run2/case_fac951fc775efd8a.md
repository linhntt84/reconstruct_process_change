### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that two groups of activities exchanged their relative order: A19, A21 and A24 — together with the now much more frequent A02, placed after A40 — now run before A25 and A29, while A20, A22 and A23 now run after them; consequently the hand-over to A30 moved from the A19/A21/A24 group to the A20/A22/A23 group, and A25 changed from following A20/A22/A23 to following A19/A21/A24. In addition, A02's participation rose from about 8 to about 34 in 100 traces and A41 recurs somewhat more often [REL017, REL024, REL027, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006, DIR007, POS002, POS003, POS005, POS006, OCC001, OCC002].

The support is direct and consistent: the order reversals are observed relationally in pairs that always co-occur (for example, A20 preceded A25 in about 98 in 100 traces before, and A25 precedes A20 in essentially all traces after), the successor changes at six activities point the same way, and the position shifts match; the number of qualifying items (58) is many times the roughly 0.75 expected for an unchanged process, and each difference exceeds about 995 of 1,000 random re-splits of the pooled traces, though neither fact alone identifies what changed [REL017, DIR004, POS003, OCC001].

Limitations: the evidence cannot rule out that both orderings already existed as process variants and only their frequencies shifted between the windows, rather than the process structure itself being modified; a few items also rest on very few observations (LAG002 and LAG006 have a single supporting observation in the later window, LAG003 a single one in the earlier window, and DIR001's before-profile is based on 16 outgoing transitions), and one item (DIR010) shows a negligible difference [LAG002, LAG003, LAG006, DIR001, DIR010].
