### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that routing after A20 changed: the route through A21, A22 and A23, previously taken in about 19 in 100 traces, is now the dominant route (about 79 in 100, with A20 preceding the trio), while the route through A24, A25 and A26 fell from about 82 to about 21 in 100 traces. A related possibility is that the A06 step between A34 and A35 is now usually skipped, with A34 proceeding straight to A35. [OCC004, OCC007, DIR005, REL011, REL014, OCC003, DIR006, LAG006]

Screening flagged 73 items where roughly 1 would be expected by chance in an unchanged process, and the flagged items agree across occurrence, pairwise-order and direction indicators rather than pointing in different directions; the participation swings (about 60 in 100 traces, e.g. 39 versus 158 traces for A21) are far larger than random re-splits of 200-trace windows produce, and the rerouting is observed directly in the successor shares at A20 and A34. [OCC004, OCC007, REL011, REL014, DIR005, DIR006]

Limitations: the items overlap heavily — occurrence, relation and direction items here describe the same trace behaviour — so they are not independent confirmations. The evidence cannot rule out that both routes already existed and only their frequencies swapped (REL017 and REL038 show each trio appearing as a block in both windows), i.e. a shift in the mix of process variants rather than a modified model, and sampling differences in variant frequencies between finite windows remain possible. The apparent repositioning of A06 rests on 24 and 7 after-window traces, and the increases in A02 and A04 on 33 and 37 traces, so these aspects are weakly supported.
