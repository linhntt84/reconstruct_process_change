### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, every trace contained A30, A31, A33, A35, A36, A37, A38 and A39, each occurring once per trace. In the later window, A33, A35 and A36 each appear in only about 46–47 in 100 traces, and A37, A38 and A39 in about 56–57 in 100 traces, while A30 and A31 still appear in every trace; no activity became repeated, so the drop reflects whole cases without these activities. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, POS001, POS002]

- The six activities now split into two groups that are almost never seen together: A33/A35/A36 co-occur with A37/A38/A39 in only about 3 in 100 traces (roughly 5–7 of 200), whereas before they co-occurred in every trace. Within each group the activities still accompany each other whenever they appear (A33–A35 co-occurrence about 46 in 100, matching their participation; A37–A38 about 55–56 in 100). [REL013, REL015, REL017, REL021, REL024, REL025]

- Routing changed accordingly: A30 now leads to A37, A38 or A39 in about 15–19% each of its outgoing transitions (essentially never before), while its transitions into A33/A35/A36 fell from about 98% combined to about 45%. A33, A35 and A36 now lead to A31 in about 26–34% of their outgoing transitions (absent before), and their transitions onward to A37/A38/A39 have nearly disappeared. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004]

- Distances shrank: the gap from A30 to A37/A38/A39, and from A33/A35/A36 to A31, was 4–5 event positions in essentially all cases before and is 1–3 positions in about 93–99% of cases after, consistent with the other group no longer sitting in between. [LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG006, LAG009]

- Relative event positions shifted modestly: A33/A35/A36 now sit later in the trace (mean about 0.68 to 0.74) and A39 earlier (about 0.77 to 0.73), consistent with traces containing fewer of these middle activities; A30 moved slightly later (0.62 to 0.67) and A31 slightly earlier (0.82 to 0.80). [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that, in the later window, each case runs only one of two activity groups between A30 and A31 — either {A33, A35, A36} or {A37, A38, A39} — whereas in the earlier window every case ran both groups in sequence (A30, then A33/A35/A36, then A37/A38/A39, then A31). This rests on direct relational and routing evidence: cross-group co-occurrence collapsed from 100% to about 3%, within-group co-occurrence was preserved, A30 gained transitions into the second group while the first group gained transitions to A31 and lost its transitions into the second group, and the positional gaps between the groups and A31 shrank from 4–5 steps to 1–3 steps. [OCC001, OCC004, DIR001, DIR002, REL015, REL025, LAG001, LAG004]

Chance is an unlikely explanation for the pattern as a whole: 54 evidence items qualified against fewer than one expected for an unchanged process, and every item's difference exceeds 199 of 200 random re-splits of the pooled traces. The occurrence, relation, direction, distance and position items all describe one coherent behavioural pattern rather than isolated anomalies. [OCC001, DIR001, REL015, LAG001]

Limitations remain: the windows are finite samples of 200 traces each, and the evidence cannot rule out that this either/or behaviour existed before as a variant too rare to appear in the earlier window (where all 200 traces contained all six activities), so it may reflect a shift in the mix of pre-existing variants rather than a modification introduced between the windows. Many items also describe the same underlying trace behaviour and are not independent confirmations, a few distance items rest on only 4–5 after-window observations and were not relied upon, and the residual co-occurrence of about 3 in 100 traces between the groups is not further explained. [REL015, REL017, LAG005, LAG011]
