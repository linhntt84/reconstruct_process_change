### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that, in the later window, cases split after A26 between two mutually exclusive routes — one running A27 (about 53 in 100 cases) and one running A36, A37 and A42 together (about 47 in 100) — whereas earlier every case ran A36/A37/A42 between A25 and A28 and then A27 after A26, with A31 following throughout. The signs are direct and consistent: participation halves for exactly A27, A36, A37 and A42 while A25, A26, A28 and A31 stay universal; A27 becomes nearly mutually exclusive with each group member while the group still co-occurs internally; A25 is rerouted to A28 and A26 splits toward A27 or the group; and order relations and step distances reverse together (A25-to-A28 lag 4 to 1, A26-to-A31 lag 2 to 2 or 4, the group now after A28/A26 and before A31). With 50 qualifying items against roughly 0.7 expected if nothing had changed, and each item's difference exceeding all 200 random re-splits of the pooled data, the set as a whole is unlikely under chance. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, REL006, REL011, REL012, REL013, REL014, REL017, REL020, DIR001, DIR002, LAG001, LAG005]

Limitations: the two windows are 200-trace samples, so the split (about 53 vs 47 in 100) is a point estimate, and a rare pre-existing variant could contribute disproportionately in one window; the evidence cannot fully rule out that the later window reflects a shift in the mix of pre-existing variants rather than a modified flow, although the earlier window's 100% participation for all four activities across 200 traces makes a purely sampling-based explanation unlikely. Several items also describe the same underlying trace behaviour (occurrence, relation and position items for the same activities), so they are not independent confirmations, and some lag comparisons rest on a single observation in one window. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, LAG002, LAG006]
