### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, activities A14, A15 and A20 each ran in about 47 in 100 traces — always together as a group and exactly once per trace — while in the later window each runs in about 199 of 200 traces, still exactly once and still together [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, REL001, REL007]
- The transition out of A36 was redistributed: before, it led to A21 in about 53 of 100 observed outgoing transitions and to A14 in about 47 of 100; in the later window it leads to A14 in about 995 of 1000 and to A21 in only about 5 of 1000 [DIR001]
- The event distance from A36 to A21 changed accordingly: before, A21 followed A36 immediately in about 53 of 100 cases and four positions later in about 47 of 100; in the later window the four-position distance holds in about 995 of 1000 cases and the immediate one has nearly disappeared [LAG001]
- Ordering relations place the three activities as a block between A36 and A21: A36 before A14, A14 before A20, A20 before A15, and A15 and A20 before A21 each hold in about 995 of 1000 later-window traces, versus about 47 of 100 earlier, when A21 otherwise followed A36 directly without the three activities [REL004, REL002, REL007, REL008, REL003, REL012]
- Secondary shifts consistent with the block becoming near-universal: A37, present throughout in both windows, now co-occurs with A14 in about 995 of 1000 traces (before about 47 of 100), and A40 keeps roughly the same overall participation (about 60 of 100 traces) but now appears only alongside the block, whereas before it appeared without A14 in about 33 of 100 traces; the relative positions of A36 and A21 shifted only slightly (about 0.02–0.03 on the position scale) [REL005, REL006, POS001, POS002]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the block A14 → A20 → A15, executed between A36 and A21, was previously not executed in roughly half of the cases — A21 then followed A36 directly — and that in the later window this bypass is taken in only about 1 of 200 cases, so the full sequence A36 → A14 → A20 → A15 → A21 runs in nearly every trace. The signs are direct and consistent: participation of the three activities rose from about 47 to about 199 of 200 traces [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003], the routing out of A36 flipped from mostly A21 to almost always A14 [DIR001], the A36-to-A21 distance moved from immediate to four steps [LAG001], and the pairwise orderings all match one and the same sequence [REL004, REL002, REL007, REL008, REL003, REL012]. The 22 evidence items far exceed the roughly 0.41 items expected without a change, so chance alone cannot explain the set as a whole, although most items describe the same underlying trace behaviour rather than independent changes [OCC001, DIR001, LAG001, REL001].

Limitations: the windows are finite samples, so a shift in the mix of case variants between windows — the no-block variant naturally becoming rare in the later sample — cannot be fully ruled out, even though a swing from about 53 of 100 to about 5 of 1000 is hard to attribute to sampling variation alone with 200 traces per window [DIR001, LAG001]. The evidence also does not explain why A40's overall participation stayed near 60 of 100 traces while it became tied to the block [REL006], and it cannot show whether the direct A36 → A21 route was removed from the process or is still chosen in the roughly 1 of 200 later traces without the block [REL003].
