### Observed differences

- The relative order of two groups of activities reversed. In the earlier window, A14, A16 and A19 (each present in about 31, 38 and 32 of 100 traces) consistently occurred after A15, A17, A18, A20 and A24; in the later window they consistently occur before them. For example, A15 preceded A20 in all 200 earlier traces, while A20 precedes A15 in all 200 later traces. [REL001, REL006, REL008, REL010, REL015, REL020]
- All four connection points around these groups flipped their routing. In the later window, A35 leads only to A14, A16 or A19 (about 44, 29 and 28 in 100 traces; earlier it led to A17, A18 or A15), A24 now leads only to A15, A18 or A17 (earlier: A16, A14, A19), A14, A16 and A19 now always continue to A20 (earlier: always to A25), and A15, A17 and A18 now each exit to A25 in roughly a third of their uses (earlier: to A20). [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006, DIR007, DIR008]
- Event positions moved accordingly: A14, A16 and A19 shifted from about 0.68–0.69 of the trace to about 0.34–0.35, A15, A17 and A18 from about 0.39 to about 0.64, and A20 and A24 both moved modestly earlier (about 0.46 to 0.38, and 0.65 to 0.57). [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006, POS007, POS008]
- Distances between the stable anchors changed: A25 now follows A24 four events later instead of two, and A20 now follows A35 two events later instead of four. [LAG001, LAG002]
- Within the one-of-three choice, the mix shifted towards A14: it appears in about 44 of 100 later traces versus 31 of 100 earlier ones, while A16 (about 29 vs 38) and A19 (about 28 vs 32) lost share; the three shares still add up so that exactly one of the three appears per trace. [OCC001, POS003, POS006, DIR007, DIR008]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that a one-of-three choice block (exactly one of A14, A16, A19 per trace) and the three-step sequence through A15, A17 and A18 (each executed once per trace) exchanged their places around the A20-to-A24 middle of the flow: the earlier window ran A35 → A15/A17/A18 in some order → A20 → … → A24 → one of A14/A16/A19 → A25, and the later window runs A35 → one of A14/A16/A19 → A20 → … → A24 → A15/A17/A18 in some order → A25. Every cross-pair between the two blocks reversed its order while co-occurrence levels stayed tied to unchanged participations, all four boundary routings flipped coherently, and the lag changes (A24→A25 stretching from 2 to 4 events, A35→A20 shrinking from 4 to 2) match a three-event block and a one-event block swapping sides. [OCC001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006, DIR001, DIR003, DIR006, DIR007, DIR008, REL001, REL008, REL015, LAG001, LAG002]

The pattern is unlikely to be a sampling artefact: 40 items qualify where fewer than 0.5 would be expected in an unchanged process, and the key order reversals are complete rather than partial — A15/A20, A15/A24, A17/A20, A17/A24, A18/A20 and A18/A24 each flip from a 100-in-100 ordering to the opposite 100-in-100 ordering — which a rare variant appearing in one window or random re-splitting of the same traces cannot produce. [REL008, REL009, REL015, REL016, REL018, REL019]

Limitations: with two finite samples of 200 traces each, the evidence cannot distinguish a modification at the change point from an unchanged process containing two variants whose sampling composition shifted completely between the windows, and the shift inside the choice block (A14 rising from about 62 to 87 of 200 traces) could partly reflect sampling even though it is unusual under random re-splits. No qualifying item characterises the segment between A20 and A24 itself, so the routing inside that middle part is inferred only from the positions of A20 and A24. [OCC001, POS007, POS008]
