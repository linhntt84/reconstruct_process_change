### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the later window reflects a restructured control flow: A02 now operates as an early step of the case — typically entered from A14, leading to A15, and placed before the mid-process block — and the block itself was reordered so that A19, A21 and A24, which previously ran after A25/A29 and led on to A30, now run before A25 and lead to A25, while A20, A22 and A23 moved after A29 and now often lead to A30; A06 and A10 are executed in fewer cases. [OCC001, POS001, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006, DIR007, DIR010, REL015, REL022, OCC002, OCC003]

The support is direct and consistent: the order reversals are shown relationally, not only positionally — for pairs co-occurring in every trace of both windows (A20–A25, A22–A25, A23–A25, A20–A29, A22–A29, A23–A29) the dominant order flipped almost completely with 200-trace support on both sides, the successor reassignment from A30 to A25 is visible directly in the routing profiles of A19/A21/A24, and the positional shifts agree in direction. The volume of qualifying items (87, against roughly 1 expected for an unchanged process) also cannot be explained by chance as a whole. [REL022, REL029, REL032, DIR005, DIR007, DIR010, POS003, POS005]

Limitations remain: each window is a 200-trace sample, so frequency shifts such as A02's rise (about 15 to about 65 in 100) could partly reflect sampling differences in variant frequencies — a rare variant dominating the later sample — rather than a modified structure, and several lag-based items rest on only 1–2 observations and were not relied on; many items also describe the same underlying traces, so they are not independent confirmations. [OCC001, LAG012, LAG015]
