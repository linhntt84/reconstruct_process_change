### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, A25's outgoing transitions went almost exclusively to A27 (about 99 in 100); in the later window they split almost evenly across A35, A36 and A41 (roughly one third each), with A27 chosen in only about 1 in 200 transitions. [DIR001]
- A26's outgoing transitions flipped the other way: earlier they spread over A41 (about 37%), A35 (about 34%) and A36 (about 28%); in the later window about 99 in 100 go to A30. [DIR002]
- Earlier, A35, A36 and A41 each sent roughly 28–37% of their outgoing transitions to A30; in the later window A30 receives essentially none from them, and each sends about 29–38% to A27 instead. [DIR003, DIR004, DIR005]
- The order of A26 and A27 relative to the block {A35, A36, A41} reversed: earlier, A26 preceded each block member in about 98–100 in 100 cases (and A27 likewise); in the later window each block member precedes A26 and A27 in about 98–99 in 100 cases. Every pair still co-occurs in all cases in both windows, so participation itself did not change. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006]
- Adjacency changed in line with this: A25 and A27 were one step apart in about 99 in 100 cases earlier and are about 4–5 steps apart in the later window (about 93% at distance 4); A26 and A30 were about 4 steps apart earlier (about 93%) and are adjacent in about 99 in 100 later cases. [LAG001, LAG003]
- Relative positions agree: A35, A36 and A41 each moved earlier (mean relative position from about 0.58–0.59 to about 0.41), while A26 (about 0.53 to 0.62) and A27 (about 0.38 to 0.47) moved later. [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005]
- Several lag results (A25–A35, A26 with A35/A36/A41, and the block members towards A30) rest on only 1–2 observations in one window and carry little weight individually. [LAG002, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006, LAG007, LAG008, LAG009, LAG010]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the alternative block A35/A36/A41 — apparently executed one member per case in about equal shares — swapped its placement with the segment containing A27 and A26: the earlier chain A25 → A27 → A26 → {A35|A36|A41} → A30 appears in the later window as A25 → {A35|A36|A41} → A27 → A26 → A30, with A30 now directly following A26 instead of following the block. This rests on direct relational and positional evidence rather than frequency shifts alone: six near-deterministic order reversals (about 98–100 in 100 in each window, in opposite directions), the loss of A25–A27 adjacency and the gain of A26–A30 adjacency, successor profiles that flip almost completely (A25 from A27 to the block; A26 from the block to A30; the block from A30 to A27), and positional shifts of all seven activities in the same direction. The 26 items far exceed the roughly 0.63 expected by chance under no change, and every difference was larger than all but about 1 in 200 of the random re-splits, so chance is not a plausible explanation for the evidence as a whole. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, LAG001, LAG003, POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005]

Limitations: the evidence cannot distinguish a genuine rewiring from a drastic shift in the mix of two pre-existing variants whose orderings differ exactly in this way; variant shares can differ between finite windows purely through sampling, although a reversal this complete (for example A25→A27 falling from about 99 in 100 to about 0 in 100) goes beyond what the reference comparisons produced. The items also largely describe one underlying reordering rather than independent confirmations, and a few lag results rest on 1–2 observations. [DIR001, LAG002, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006, LAG008, LAG009, LAG010]
