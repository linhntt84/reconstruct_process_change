### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the block A30 → A31 → A36, which previously executed exactly once per trace before exiting to A25, now executes as a repeated cycle: after each pass A36 returns to A30 for roughly two or three further passes, and the flow leaves to A25 about once per trace after the final pass. The routing evidence shows this directly — A36's transitions now return to A30 in about 71 in 100 cases while about 28 in 100 exit to A25, which matches the occurrence counts of about 3.5 passes and one exit per trace — and the internal order A30→A31→A36 is preserved. The relation and distance items agree: A30 and A36 alternate in about 68 in 100 traces, A36 followed by A30 within five steps appears in about two thirds of traces after being essentially unobserved, and the formerly rigid precedence of A14 and A05 over the block dissolves into interleaving because the block now repeats after them [DIR005, DIR003, DIR004, OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, REL005, REL003, REL004, LAG002, REL001]

Nineteen items were flagged where roughly half an item would be expected if nothing had changed, but they largely describe this one underlying behaviour rather than independent changes. The main competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is a partial variant shift: about 31 in 100 later traces still show no repetition, so the later window may mix a new looping variant with the previous single-pass behaviour rather than every trace having gained the loop, and sampling differences in variant frequencies between the two 200-trace windows could also account for the smaller shifts at A05 and in the positions — though not for repetition rising from about 1 in 100 to about 68 in 100 traces. The A05 routing change (share moving from A14 to A07) is an additional difference that this hypothesis does not cover [OCC001, DIR001, POS001, POS003, REL005]
