- The largest change is a flip in how often two groups of activities run. In the later window, A21, A22 and A23 each appear in about 25 in 100 traces, down from about 75 in 100 before; A24, A25 and A26 each rise from about 25 in 100 to about 75 in 100 (supports of roughly 150 vs 50 traces per activity). ### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility of a specific restructuring: the choice between two alternative activity blocks after A19/A20 flipped, so that in the later window about three in four cases run A24/A25/A26 between A19/A20 and A34/A35, where they previously ran A21/A22/A23; and A06, previously a mid-trace activity with varied successors, now typically occurs late in the trace, after A34/A30 and immediately before A35. The support is direct — precedence, successor-share and gap evidence rather than frequency shifts alone — consistent across all five indicator families, and the number of flagged items (92, against roughly 1 expected for an unchanged process) far exceeds what chance would produce. [OCC004, OCC007, DIR002, DIR005, POS001, REL013, REL016, REL051, REL053, LAG001, LAG013]

The items are not independent: most relation items restate the same block flip and the same A06 relocation, so the count overstates the amount of separate confirmation. The two windows are finite samples, so a difference in variant frequencies alone can never be fully excluded, although swings of about 50 percentage points backed by roughly 150 versus 50 traces per activity go well beyond typical sampling noise. The evidence also shows observed behaviour only: A21–A23 still run in about a quarter of later traces, so the change lies in how often and where the blocks execute, not in their removal. Finally, every item's empirical strength sits at the same ceiling (about 0.995), and a few flagged routing changes are near zero in magnitude, so strength values were not treated as confirmation on their own. [OCC004, OCC007, REL013, REL025, DIR003, DIR004]
