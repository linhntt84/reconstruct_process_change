### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the segment formed by A36, A37 and A42 was repositioned to run between A25 and A28 and made mandatory, while A26 now leads almost exclusively to A27. In the later window A25 feeds the segment directly instead of leading to A28; the three activities run together in varying order, hand over to A28 rather than to A31, and occur in essentially every trace rather than in about 46 in 100; A26 routes almost solely to A27, which in turn precedes A31. The relational and routing evidence shows this directly — order reversals of the segment against A26, A27, A28 and A31 (96–99 in 100 of co-occurrences), the replaced transitions at A25, A26 and the segment, and the A25–A28 distance growing from 1 to 4–5 steps — and the participation, position and lag indicators agree with the same rearrangement. The number of qualifying items (48) is far above the roughly 0.74 expected if nothing had changed. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, REL006, REL011, REL014, REL017, REL020, LAG001, POS006]

These items are not independent confirmations: the occurrence, position, relation, direction and lag entries for A36/A37/A42 largely describe one and the same trace behaviour, so the case rests on a single coherent difference seen from several angles, and some lag profiles rest on 1–2 observations in one window. A competing explanation that cannot be ruled out is a sampling shift in variant frequencies — a trace variant containing the A36/A37/A42 segment being rare earlier and dominant later without any structural change — although the joint reversal of order, the rewired transitions and the changed A25–A28 distance make pure sampling noise an unlikely account of the whole pattern. The evidence also does not directly show how A28 connects onward: A28 is shown to precede A27, but its relation to A26 is inferred only from event positions.
