### Observed differences

- In the later window, A25, A26 and A31 still occur in every case, but their repetition has largely stopped: mean occurrences fell from about 3.4 to about 1.0 per case, and the share of cases in which these activities repeat fell from about 75 in 100 to about 1 in 200. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003]
- Outgoing flow from A31 was redistributed: earlier, about 71 in 100 of A31's outgoing transitions went to A25 and about 29 in 100 to A20; in the later window, about 96 in 100 go to A20 and only about 4 in 100 to A25. [DIR001]
- The relationship between A25 and A31 changed from largely interleaved or repeated to strictly sequential: A25 preceding A31 rose from about 25 in 100 cases to about 995 in 1000, while interleaved-or-repeated ordering fell from about 75 in 100 to about 5 in 1000; the two activities still co-occur in every case in both windows. [REL001]
- In the later window, A31 follows A25 two positions later in about 92 in 100 observed pairs, against about 12 in 100 earlier; the reverse order (A31 before A25 within five steps) appears in only 1 of 200 later cases versus 150 of 200 earlier ones, though the later figure rests on a single observation. [LAG001, LAG002]
- The relative positions of A25 and A31 barely moved (mean positions about 0.44 to 0.41 and 0.48 to 0.48), but their position distributions tightened markedly (spread about 0.12 to 0.06 in both cases), consistent with each activity now occurring once at roughly the same place in the case. [POS001, POS002]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the repeated execution of the A25–A26–A31 block stopped and its exit was rerouted: previously the block recurred in most cases and A31 typically flowed back to A25, whereas in the later window each of the three activities runs about once per case and A31 proceeds to A20 instead. The routing change at A31 is observed directly [DIR001], as is the ordering change between A25 and A31 [REL001, LAG001], and both agree with the collapse in repetition [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003]; the nine qualifying items are many times the roughly 0.3 expected by chance alone, so the ensemble of differences is unlikely to arise from random splitting as a whole. A26 shares the occurrence collapse but has no direct routing or ordering evidence of its own [OCC002].

The main competing explanation the evidence cannot fully rule out is sampling: with 200-case windows, a shift in the mix of process variants — cases with many repetitions dominating the earlier sample and being rare in the later one — could produce similar frequency swings without any structural change, although a swing from about 75 in 100 repeating cases to about 1 in 200 makes pure sampling fluctuation unlikely. In addition, the reverse-order result rests on a single later observation, and the empirical strengths (about 0.995 for every item) show only that the differences are unusual for random re-splits, not that a change is certain. [OCC001, OCC003, LAG002]
