### Observed differences

- The routing after A25 and A26 effectively swapped: in the earlier window A25 was followed by A27 in about 98 in 100 of its outgoing transitions, while A26 split across A41 (about 38 in 100), A35 (about 33) and A36 (about 28); in the later window A26 is followed by A30 in about 98 in 100, while A25 splits almost evenly over A41, A36 and A35 (about 31–33 in 100 each) and A27 is down to about 0.5 in 100. [DIR001, DIR002]
- The three activities A35, A36 and A41 changed their main shared successor from A30 to A27: earlier, each led to A30 in about 28–34 in 100 outgoing transitions and to A27 in at most about 1 in 100; in the later window each leads to A27 in about 30–33 in 100, while A30 appears in at most about 0.5 in 100. [DIR003, DIR004, DIR005]
- The order between this trio and A26/A27 reversed: earlier, A26 preceded A35, A36 and A41 in about 98–99 in 100 traces and A27 preceded them in about 99–100 in 100; in the later window the trio comes first in about 97–98 in 100 traces for all six pairs. All six pairs still co-occur in every trace, so participation did not change — only the ordering did. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006]
- Relative event positions shifted accordingly: A35, A36 and A41 each occur earlier (mean relative position about 0.58 before versus about 0.42 after), while A27 (about 0.38 to about 0.47) and A26 (about 0.53 to about 0.62) occur later. [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005]
- Next-event distances flipped: A25 was followed immediately by A27 in about 99 in 100 earlier traces but is now 4–5 events away in about 99 in 100; A26 reached A30 only after 4–5 events earlier but now does so at distance 1 in about 99 in 100. [LAG001, LAG003]
- Pairs within five steps appeared or disappeared with the reversal: A25 followed by A35 occurs in about 199 of 200 later traces (mostly 1–3 events apart) versus 1 earlier, A35 followed by A27 likewise about 199 versus 1, while A26 followed by a trio member, and trio members followed by A30, drop from about 200 traces to 1–2; some of these figures rest on only 1–2 observations in one window. [LAG002, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006, LAG007, LAG008, LAG009, LAG010]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the block of three alternative activities A35, A36 and A41 changed its placement in the case sequence: in the earlier window it executed after A26 and before A30 (A26 → one of the trio → A30, with A25 leading directly to A27), whereas in the later window it executes after A25 and before A27 (A25 → one of the trio → A27, with A26 leading directly to A30). This is a placement hypothesis and it is shown directly rather than inferred from marginal frequencies: all six trio-versus-A26/A27 pairs reverse order in about 97–99 in 100 traces, the successor profiles of A25, A26, A35, A36 and A41 rewire consistently with that reversal, and the position and distance shifts point the same way; every item's difference also exceeds 199 of 200 random re-splits of the pooled data. With 26 qualifying items against roughly 0.63 expected for an unchanged process, the pattern as a whole is not what random window-to-window variation typically produces. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, LAG001, LAG003]

Limitations: the items are not independent confirmations, since the routing, order, position and distance indicators largely describe the same reordering of the same traces, and a few lag results rest on 1–2 observations in one window. A competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is a shift in the mix of pre-existing variants: the later ordering may have existed as a rare variant earlier (it is seen in only about 0–1 of 200 earlier traces, for example A26 reaching A30 at distance 1) and simply dominated the later sample; its near-absence earlier makes pure sampling noise unlikely, but these two windows cannot distinguish a rewiring of one behaviour from a variant-mix shift. [LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006, LAG007, LAG008, LAG009, LAG010]
