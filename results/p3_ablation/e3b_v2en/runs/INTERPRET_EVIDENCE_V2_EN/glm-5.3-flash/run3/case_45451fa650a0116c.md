### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the control flow was restructured on two consistent points: after A20, executions now run the block A21–A22–A23 (typically all three, in varying order) where they previously ran A24–A25–A26, and the late segment A34 → A06 → A35 is now usually shortened to a direct A34 → A35 handover, with A06 largely bypassed. The change is visible directly in routing shares (A20's and A34's outgoing transitions) and in pairwise order and co-occurrence relations, not only in activity frequencies, and all items touching these activities point the same way; the number of qualifying items (73, against roughly 0.9 expected for an unchanged process) means the pattern as a whole is unlikely under random re-splits of a stable process. [OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, OCC007, OCC008, OCC009, DIR005, DIR006, REL009, REL010, REL011, REL014, REL017, REL038, LAG006]

Limitations: many of the 73 items describe the same two block-level movements rather than independent changes, and a few lag items (for example A25→A06 and A26→A06) rest on only 1–2 later-window observations and were not relied on. The evidence cannot distinguish a genuine rewiring from a shift in the mix of two variants that already existed in both windows — the A21–A23 block already ran in about 19 of 100 earlier traces and the A24–A26 block still runs in about 21 of 100 later traces — and the smaller rises of A02 and A04, each based on tens of traces, could reflect sampling differences in variant frequencies. Finally, the evidence shows A24–A26 and A06 are now rarely executed, not that they were removed from the process definition. [LAG002, LAG003, REL017, REL038, OCC001, OCC002, OCC007]
