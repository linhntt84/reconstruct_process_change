### Observed differences

- In the later window, A14 takes part in about 44 in 100 cases (87 of 200 traces), up from about 31 in 100 (62 of 200); A16 and A19 take part somewhat less often (from about 38 to about 29, and from about 32 to about 28 in 100). The three shares together still cover essentially every case, and A14 never repeats within a trace in either window [OCC001, POS003, POS006].

- Successors changed accordingly: A14, A16 and A19 led exclusively to A25 before and now lead exclusively to A20; A15, A17 and A18 previously flowed into A20 (and among themselves) and now flow into A25 (and among themselves); A24's outgoing transitions moved from A16/A14/A19 to A15/A18/A17, and A35's from A17/A18/A15 to A19/A16/A14 [DIR001–DIR008].
- Distances moved consistently with an exchange of segments: the typical gap from A24 to A25 grew from 2 to 4 event positions, and from A35 to A20 it shrank from 4 to 2 [LAG001, LAG002].

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that two segments exchanged their placement around the A20–A24 sequence, which itself keeps its order and full participation in both windows: the single alternative step (one of A14, A16 or A19 — their shares together cover essentially every case) and the three-activity block A15/A17/A18 swapped sides. In the later window the alternative step runs between A35 and A20 and the block runs between A24 and A25; before, the block ran between A35 and A20 and the alternative step between A24 and A25 [REL008, REL009, REL015, REL016, REL018, REL019, DIR001, DIR003, DIR006, DIR007, DIR008, LAG001, LAG002, POS007, POS008].

The support is direct and consistent: for pairs that co-occur in every trace the precedence relation reversed totally (100 in 100 before, 0 in 100 after), which random re-splits of a stable process do not produce — each item's difference exceeds about 199 of 200 reference re-splits; positions, successor sets and inter-activity distances all shift exactly as the exchange predicts; and the 40 qualifying items far exceed the fewer than one expected by chance when nothing has changed. The many items describe this one reordering from different angles rather than independent changes, so the judgement rests on the directness and consistency of the relational evidence [OCC001, DIR001–DIR008, POS001–POS008, REL001–REL021, LAG001–LAG002].

Two limitations remain. The shift in choice shares — A14 rising from about 31 to about 44 in 100 cases while A16 and A19 decline — could be a sampling difference in variant frequencies between the two 200-trace windows rather than a routing change, and the evidence cannot separate these; the internal ordering within each segment (members show mixed transitions among themselves) is also not resolved beyond segment level [OCC001, POS003, POS006, DIR002, DIR004, DIR005].
