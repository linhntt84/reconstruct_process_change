### Observed differences

- The largest change is in how often two groups of activities run. In the later window, A21, A22 and A23 each appear in about 25 in 100 traces, down from about 75 in 100 earlier; A24, A25 and A26 each appear in about 75 in 100, up from about 25 in 100. ### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the two alternative continuations after A20 exchanged their frequencies: in the earlier window about 3 in 4 traces continued from A20 into the path containing A21, A22 and A23 (which typically ran together in one trace) and about 1 in 4 into the path with A24, A25 and A26; in the later window these shares are reversed, with A20 preceding A24, A25 or A26 in about 74 in 100 of the traces containing both. [OCC003, OCC006, DIR003, REL007, REL010, REL011, REL012]

The signs are direct and consistent: participation, routing and relational indicators all show the same mirror-image shift, each backed by roughly 100 or more traces per window, and 62 qualifying items against fewer than 1 expected if nothing had changed makes a chance explanation of the set as a whole implausible. A related shift concerns A10, which now typically accompanies the A24–A26 side, occurs later in traces and leads mostly to A33; A02's near-disappearance rests on only 2 later traces and is weak. [REL013, REL034, REL015, OCC002, DIR002, POS001, OCC001]

Limitations: many items restate one underlying behaviour — the A21–A23 and A24–A26 relation items largely describe the same traces — so they are not independent confirmations, and all evidence is control-flow based. The windows are finite samples of 200 traces each, so differences in variant frequencies, including a rare variant appearing in only one window, remain a competing explanation for individual items, and the evidence cannot show what drove the change in branch shares. [REL013, REL021, REL034, REL039, OCC003, OCC006]
