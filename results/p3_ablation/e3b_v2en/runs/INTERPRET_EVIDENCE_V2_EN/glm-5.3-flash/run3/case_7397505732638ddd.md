### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, every profiled activity had a single observed successor, forming one fixed chain: A26 → A17 → A15 → A18 → A19 → A21 → A20 → A27 (for example, all observed outgoing transitions of A15 went to A18, and all of A18 went to A19). [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006, DIR007]
- In the later window, routing spread over several targets: A15 still goes to A18 in about 68 in 100 of its outgoing transitions (the rest split roughly equally among A19, A20 and A21); A18 now goes most often to A27 (about 52 in 100), a target not observed for it before; A26 splits about 53/47 between A17 and A19; and the other sources keep their original main target at reduced shares (A19→A21 about 61, A21→A20 about 67, A20→A27 about 49, A17→A15 about 56 in 100). [DIR001, DIR003, DIR007, DIR004, DIR006, DIR005, DIR002]
- The strict relative order between the two groups of activities became mixed: before, the A15/A17/A18-side activity came first in 100 in 100 traces for all eight tested pairs; in the later window the reverse order occurs in about 24–85 in 100 traces (A19 before A18 in about 85, A19 before A15 in about 74, A21 before A18 in about 68, while A17 still precedes A21 in about 77). [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, REL007, REL008]
- Relative positions shifted the same way: A15, A17 and A18 now occur later within the trace (e.g., A18's mean relative position from about 0.62 to 0.68, A15 from 0.59 to 0.64), while A19, A20 and A21 occur earlier (A19 from about 0.65 to 0.59, A20 from 0.71 to 0.68). [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006]
- Fixed step distances dissolved into ranges: pairs that always appeared at one distance now appear at several (A15–A18: 1 step apart in about 68 in 100 of occurrences, otherwise 2–4; A18–A27: 1 step in about 52 in 100 versus always 4 before; A15–A27: spread over 2–5 steps versus always 5). [LAG001, LAG013, LAG005]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the ordering of the process around activities A15, A17, A18, A19, A20 and A21 was rearranged: the previously fixed chain (A26 → A17 → A15 → A18 → A19 → A21 → A20 → A27) gave way to mixed execution in which A19, A21 and A20 frequently run before A15, A17 and A18, single fixed successors gave way to two to four alternatives, and fixed step distances became variable. [REL001, REL006, DIR003, DIR007, LAG013]

- The change is shown directly by relational and positional evidence rather than inferred from frequency shifts alone: all eight tested cross-group pairs reversed or balanced their order in the same direction, and the position shifts (A15/A17/A18 later, A19/A20/A21 earlier) agree with that picture. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, REL007, REL008, POS003, POS004]
- The set of signals is far beyond what sampling produces: 45 items qualified against roughly 0.47 expected for an unchanged process, and each item's difference was larger than in about 199 of 200 random re-splits of the pooled traces. [DIR001, POS003, REL001, LAG013]
- The items are not independent: direction, relation, lag and position profiles for the same activities largely summarise the same traces, so they corroborate one underlying shift rather than confirming it several times over. [DIR001, REL001, LAG002, POS001]
- A competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is a shift in the mix of pre-existing process variants — for instance a reordered variant that was essentially absent in the earlier window becoming dominant — rather than a change in the process structure itself; because the earlier window shows no deviation at all (single successors, strict order and fixed distances in all 200 traces), such a mix shift would have to be large, but the two windows cannot distinguish it from a structural change. [DIR001, REL001, LAG001]
- Some pair-level lag profiles rest on fewer observations (about 52–176 of 200 after-window traces), so those proportions carry more sampling uncertainty than the rest of the picture. [LAG002, LAG012, LAG011]
