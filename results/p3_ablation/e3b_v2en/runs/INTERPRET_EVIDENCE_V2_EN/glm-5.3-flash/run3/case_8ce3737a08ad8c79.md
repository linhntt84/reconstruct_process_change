### Observed differences

- The dominant difference is a swap between two groups of activities. In the later window, A21, A22 and A23 each appear in about 79 in 100 traces, up from about 19 in 100 before, while A24, A25 and A26 each drop from about 82 in 100 traces to about 21 in 100. Within each group the activities typically occur together: joint appearance of A21–A23 rose from about 19 to about 79 in 100 traces, and joint appearance of A24–A26 fell from about 82 to about 21. Before, about 82% of A20's outgoing transitions led to A24 (29%), A26 (29%) or A25 (24%); in the later window about 79% lead to A23 (29%), A21 (29%) or A22 (22%). - A10 became rarer and shifted: it appears in about 7 in 100 later traces versus about 17 before, its outgoing transitions spread from almost only A33 (about 97%) across several targets (largest share A15, about 21%), and its mean position moved from about 0.86 to about 0.52 on the trace-position scale. These A10 figures rest on few observations (33 versus 14 traces; the lag comparison on only 4 after), so they are uncertain. [POS001, DIR002, LAG001]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that routing at A20 changed: in the later window, about four in five executions leaving A20 proceed to A21, A22 and A23, which now run in roughly four of five traces and typically together, whereas before the same share went to A24, A25 and A26, a branch that is now largely skipped (about 21 in 100 traces versus about 82 before). This rests on direct routing evidence at A20 computed on all 200 traces per window, participation shifts of roughly 60 percentage points for all six activities, and consistent co-occurrence flips with A30, A32 and A33. The 60 qualifying evidence items far exceed the roughly 0.9 expected by chance when nothing changed, so the pattern as a whole is not a typical random re-split, although many items describe the same underlying swap rather than independent confirmations. [DIR003, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, OCC007, REL009, REL012, REL020, REL038]

An important competing explanation remains: the two windows may simply contain different mixes of case variants — for example, a population shift in which the A21–A23 variant dominates the later sample — which would look identical to a rerouting without any change to the process definition. In addition, the secondary changes at A10 rest on only 14–33 traces (the lag comparison on 4) and could partly reflect sampling differences in variant frequencies. [POS001, DIR002, LAG001]
