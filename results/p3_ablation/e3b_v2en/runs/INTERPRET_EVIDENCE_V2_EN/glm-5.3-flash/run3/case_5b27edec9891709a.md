### Observed differences

- Four activities became intermittent. A22, present in every before-window case, appears in about 54 in 100 later cases; A31, A32 and A37, each present in every before case, appear in about 47 in 100 later cases. None of the four repeats within a case in either window, while A20, A21, A23 and A26 continue to appear in essentially every case. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, POS001, POS002, POS004, POS005]

- In the later window, cases split into two mutually exclusive groups: about 53 in 100 contain A22 but none of A31/A32/A37, about 47 in 100 contain all three of A31, A32 and A37 together but not A22, and both appear together in only about 1 in 200 cases. Before, every case contained A22 alongside the trio. [REL011, REL012, REL013, REL020, REL021, REL022]

- The trio changed its place in the case. Before, A31, A32 and A37 each ran before A21 and before A23 in every case; in the later window they follow A21 and A23 in nearly every case that contains them (about 47 in 100 cases overall), while the old order survives in only about 1 in 200 cases. Their mean relative position moved from about 0.39 to about 0.58 of the case, while A23 moved earlier (about 0.45 to about 0.39) and A21 slightly earlier (about 0.60 to about 0.54). [REL006, REL007, REL008, REL014, REL015, REL016, POS006, POS007, POS008, POS004, POS002]

- Routing changed around these activities. A20, which previously passed cases on to A37, A31 or A32 (roughly 35, 34 and 31 in 100 of its outgoing transitions), now goes to A23 in all but about 1 in 200 transitions. A21, which previously always led to A22, now leads to A22 in about 54 in 100 of its transitions and to A31, A32 or A37 in the remainder. The trio, which previously each led to A23 in roughly a third of its transitions, now leads to A26 instead, with A23 nearly gone as a successor. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005]

- Relations that stay stable: when the activities do co-occur, A20 still precedes A22, A23 and A21 still precede A22, A22 still precedes A26, and the trio still precedes A26. [REL001, REL005, REL009, REL010, REL017, REL018, REL019]

- Distances moved with the routing: the gap from A20 to A23 is now 1 step in all but about 1 in 200 cases (previously 4 steps in every case), and the distance from A21 to A26 is 2 steps in the A22 cases (about 54 in 100) against 4 steps in the trio cases (about 46 in 100). A few lag results about the trio's exact spacing rest on a single later-window observation and carry little weight alone. [LAG001, LAG005, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG006, LAG007, LAG008]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that, in the later window, the process branches after A21 into two mutually exclusive paths: about 53 in 100 cases run A20 → A23 → A21 → A22 → A26, while about 47 in 100 run A20 → A23 → A21 → the block A31/A32/A37 (in varying internal order) → A26. Before the cut, every case ran a single shape in which the A31/A32/A37 block sat between A20 and A23 and A22 ran between A21 and A26; in the later window the block runs after A21 and A23 instead, A22 is not executed in the block cases, and the block is not executed in the A22 cases. [REL006, REL007, REL008, REL011, REL012, REL013, REL014, REL015, REL016, DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, LAG001, LAG005]

Supporting signs: 47 items qualified, far more than the roughly 0.5 expected if nothing had changed, so chance cannot explain the pattern as a whole. The placement claim rests on direct relational evidence — the block's order relative to A21 and A23 reversed, A22 and the block became near-exclusive, and successor sets changed at A20, A21 and all three block activities — while the participation, position, routing and distance items all describe the same two-path behaviour without contradicting one another. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, REL006, REL011, REL014, POS006, POS007, POS008, LAG001, LAG005]

Limitations: the evidence cannot rule out that both behaviours already existed earlier with the A22-only path too rare to appear in any of the 200 before-window traces; even then, its rise to about 47 in 100 would itself be a major shift in how often each path runs. The exact split and the tiny residual co-occurrence (about 1 in 200) are sample estimates from 200 traces per window, several items describe the same underlying trace behaviour and so corroborate rather than independently confirm it, and a few lag details rest on single observations. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, REL011, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG006, LAG007, LAG008]
