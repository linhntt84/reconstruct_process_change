### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the segment between A44 and A29 was restructured: where the earlier window shows A44 followed by exactly one of A19, A20 or A21 and then A29, the later window shows A44 followed by A26, A27 and A28 — each executed once in every trace, in varying order — before A29. The participation flips (0 to 200 of 200 traces for the new activities; about 60–71 of 200 down to 1 of 200 for the old ones), the routing changes at A44 and at the block activities, the direct order relations (A44 before each block member and each block member before A29 in about 99 in 100 traces), and the longer A44→A29 distance (2–3 to 4–5 steps) all describe the same structure. [OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, OCC007, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006, DIR007, DIR008, DIR009, REL012, REL013, REL031, REL033, REL034, REL038, REL039, REL042, REL043, LAG005, POS008]

Limitations: the later-window routing profiles for the nearly vanished A19, A20 and A21 rest on a single observation each, so their apparent new successors are not established as meaningful edges; A10's decline and rerouting is clearly observed, but its connection to the restructured segment rests on few later observations (some lags around A10 on 7 or fewer). An important competing explanation that cannot be fully ruled out is a sampling shift in variant frequencies between the two finite windows — the old one-of-three variant becoming rare and a new all-three variant becoming dominant by chance — which the magnitude of the shifts (about 70 of 200 to 1 of 200; 0 to 200 of 200) makes unlikely but cannot exclude.
