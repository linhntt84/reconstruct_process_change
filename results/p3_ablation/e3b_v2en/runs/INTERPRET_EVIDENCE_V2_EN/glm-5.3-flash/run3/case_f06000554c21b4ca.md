### Observed differences

- The relative order of two groups of activities reversed. In the earlier window, A26 ran before A35, A36 and A41 in about 99–100 of 100 traces, and A27 ran before those three in about 99–100 of 100; in the later window, A35, A36 and A41 run before A26 in about 99 of 100 traces and before A27 in about 98 of 100. All six activities still co-occur in every trace in both windows, so participation did not change — only their order did. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006]

- The routing around this segment changed at its boundaries. In the later window, A25's outgoing transitions, previously about 99% to A27, are split roughly equally over A36, A41 and A35 (about 33% each); A26's outgoing, previously split over A41 (about 37%), A35 (about 34%) and A36 (about 28%), now goes to A30 in about 98–99 of 100 cases; and each of A35, A36 and A41, which previously led to A30 in roughly 28–37% of cases, now leads to A27 in about 31–34% instead. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005]

- Separation distances moved with the same boundaries. A25 and A27 were adjacent (one step apart) in about 99 of 100 traces before and are 4–5 steps apart in about 99 of 100 in the later window; A26 and A30 were 4–5 steps apart before (about 93 in 100 at four steps) and are adjacent in about 98–99 of 100 after. [LAG001, LAG003]

- Relative positions shifted consistently: in the later window A35, A36 and A41 each appear earlier in the trace (mean relative position from about 0.58–0.59 down to about 0.41), while A27 (about 0.38 to 0.47) and A26 (about 0.53 to 0.62) appear later; A27 remains ahead of A26 in both windows. [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005]

- A few distance profiles concerning the displaced pairs (A26 with A35/A36/A41, A35/A36/A41 with A30, A25 with A35, A35 with A27) rest on only 1–2 observed occurrences in one window and are weak on their own; the description above rests on items with about 200 supporting observations per window. [LAG002, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006, LAG007, LAG008, LAG009, LAG010]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the group of activities A35, A36 and A41, which in the earlier window ran after A26 and before A30, is executed earlier in the later window — after A25 and before A27 — so that A27 and A26 now follow the group and A26 leads directly to A30; in relational terms, the relative order of the group and of the two activities A27 and A26 is reversed, while all six activities still occur in essentially every trace. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006]

The support is direct and consistent: the order reversals are observed relationally in about 98–100 of 100 traces for all six activity pairs; the routing changes sit exactly at the boundaries of the reordered segment (A25 now feeds the group, the group now feeds A27, A26 now feeds A30); the distance changes match them (A25–A27 from adjacent to four steps apart, A26–A30 from four steps to adjacent); and the positional shifts point the same way. With 26 qualifying items against roughly 0.63 expected if nothing had changed, and each item's difference larger than in about 199 of 200 random re-splits, the pattern as a whole is far beyond what an unchanged process typically produces. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, LAG001, LAG003, POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006]

Limitations remain: the evidence cannot rule out that both orderings already existed as variants of the process and only their frequencies shifted between the two sampled windows of 200 traces each — a strong shift in variant mix would look the same, although the near-complete reversals make pure sampling noise unlikely. The internal ordering among A35, A36 and A41 is not established by this evidence, and a few distance profiles rest on only 1–2 observations. [LAG002, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006, LAG007, LAG008, LAG009, LAG010]
