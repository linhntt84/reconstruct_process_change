- In the later window, A39 shows only a marginal shift: A40 was the target of all 56 observed outgoing transitions earlier and about 91 in 100 of 34 later, with three targets at about 3 in 100 each — roughly one transition apiece, consistent with a rare variant appearing only in the later sample [DIR003].

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the typical one-step order of A35 and A36 reversed in the main flow: earlier, A36 was usually followed by A35 and A35 by A32, whereas in the later window A35 is usually followed by A36 and A36 by A32 [DIR001, DIR002]. The two routing profiles changed in complementary ways — each activity's dominant successor swapped by roughly 20–24 percentage points — and both differences are larger than in about 199 of 200 random re-splits, so sampling noise alone is an unlikely explanation; the three qualifying items are also about seven times the 0.44 expected by chance across the 89 tested units, more than an unchanged process typically produces [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003].

The main limitation is that the evidence covers one-step successors only, with no positional or longer-range relational items: it cannot distinguish a genuine reordering of A35 and A36 from a shift in the relative frequency of two pre-existing variants containing both orders, or from a change in how a loop between the two activities (mutual succession occurs in both windows) is entered — either would produce the same successor shares [DIR001, DIR002]. The two routing items describe complementary sides of what is likely one underlying change rather than independent confirmations; DIR shares are computed over outgoing transitions rather than cases; and the minor targets that appear or disappear (about one to two transitions each), like the marginal A39 item, are consistent with sampling differences in rare variant frequencies [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003].
