### Observed differences

- In the later window, A30, A31 and A36 each occur about once per trace (means of about 1.0–1.1) instead of about 3.4 times; all three still appear in every trace, but the share of traces in which they repeat falls from about 75 in 100 to about 1–2 in 100 [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003].
- The routing out of A36 changes sharply: before, about 70 in 100 of A36's observed outgoing transitions returned to A30 and about 29 in 100 went to A25; in the later window, about 93 in 100 go to A25 and only about 4 in 100 return to A30 [DIR003].
- The chain itself is stable: A30 still leads to A31 in about 97–99 in 100 of its outgoing transitions, and A31 still leads to A36 in about 98–99 in 100 [DIR001, DIR002].
- The A30/A36 relationship shifts from mostly interleaved or repeated (75 in 100 traces) to almost always A30 before A36 with a single occurrence each (98 in 100) [REL001].
- A36 following A30 within two steps becomes the dominant pattern (about 89 in 100 observed pairs, up from about 12 in 100), while A36→A30, A36→A36 and A30→A30 pairs within five steps nearly vanish, from 150 observed pairs each before to 1–2 after [LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG001].
- The event positions of A30 and A36 narrow (standard deviation from about 0.12 to about 0.06–0.07), consistent with each occurring once at a fixed point in the case rather than several times at spread positions [POS001, POS002].

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the repeating cycle A30 → A31 → A36 was largely removed, so that in the later window the block executes once per case and control leaves through A36 to A25 instead of looping back to A30. The signs are direct and mutually consistent: repetition of all three activities collapses [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003], the A36→A30 back edge drops from about 70 in 100 of A36's outgoing transitions to about 4 in 100 while the exit to A25 rises to about 93 in 100 [DIR003], the A30/A36 pair changes from mostly interleaved-or-repeated to strictly ordered [REL001], and A36→A30 pairs within five steps disappear from the sample [LAG003]. With 13 qualifying items against roughly 0.46 expected by chance across 93 tested units, the pattern as a whole is unlikely for an unchanged process [screening].

Limitations: the two windows are finite samples of 200 traces each, so a shift in the mix of process variants between windows cannot be fully ruled out as an alternative to a structural modification, although the magnitudes involved (75 in 100 to 1–2 in 100 recurrence; 70 in 100 to 4 in 100 routing) make pure sampling noise implausible. The lag items for repeated pairs rest on only 1–2 observations in the later window and are weak on their own, and the agreeing items likely reflect one underlying difference rather than independent confirmations; the conclusion rests mainly on the occurrence, routing and relation evidence [OCC001, DIR003, REL001, LAG003].
