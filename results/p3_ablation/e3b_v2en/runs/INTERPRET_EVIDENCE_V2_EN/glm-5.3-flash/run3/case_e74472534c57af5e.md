### Observed differences

- In the later window, A30, A31 and A36 each occur about 3.5 times per trace, up from about once per trace before; traces containing a repeated occurrence of each rise from about 1 in 100 (or fewer) to about 68 in 100, while every trace still contains each activity at least once in both windows. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003]
- A36's outgoing transitions shifted decisively: before, about 98 in 100 led to A25; in the later window about 71 in 100 lead back to A30 and about 28 in 100 still lead to A25. The internal links A30 → A31 and A31 → A36 remain dominant throughout (about 98–99 in 100 of outgoing transitions in both windows). [DIR005, DIR003, DIR004]
- The previously strict ordering dissolved: A14 before A30, A14 before A36 and A30 before A36 each held in about 99 in 100 traces before but in only about 32 in 100 later, with interleaved-or-repeated orderings rising to about 68 in 100; all three pairs still co-occur in every trace. [REL003, REL004, REL005]
- The adjacency A36 then A30, seen in only one before-window observation, now appears at varied gaps (immediately in about 11 in 100 traces, four positions later in about 5 in 100); conversely, A30 followed by A36 two positions later, near-universal before (about 96 in 100), now appears in about 12 in 100. [LAG002, LAG001]
- Secondary changes: the spread of A30's and A36's relative trace positions roughly doubles (standard deviation about 0.06 to about 0.12), A05 appears in slightly fewer traces (55 vs about 52 in 100) and routes about twice as often to A07 (about 19% to 39% of its outgoing transitions) and about half as often to A14 (about 35% to 18%); A14's links changed only slightly (A15 still about 91 in 100). [POS003, POS004, POS001, POS002, DIR001, DIR002]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the fixed block A30 → A31 → A36, previously executed once per trace after A14 and closed by a handover from A36 to A25, is in the later window executed as a repeating cycle in which A36 returns to A30, running about 3.5 times per trace on average before exiting to A25. Each element is observed directly: within-trace repetition of all three activities (about 68 in 100 traces versus about 1 before) [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003]; the return edge A36 → A30 in both routing shares and adjacency data [DIR005, LAG002]; unchanged, dominant internal edges A30 → A31 and A31 → A36 [DIR003, DIR004]; and pairwise orderings flipping from near-universal precedence to interleaved-or-repeated in the same roughly 68 in 100 of traces [REL003, REL004, REL005]. The exit share of about 28 in 100 per A36 execution is also arithmetically consistent with the observed average of about 3.5 repetitions [DIR005, OCC003]. With 19 qualifying items against roughly 0.5 expected for an unchanged process, the pattern as a whole is unlikely to be a sampling artefact. [OCC001, DIR005, REL003, LAG002]

Limitations remain: the samples cannot distinguish a modified flow from an existing repeating variant whose share rose sharply — a move from about 1 in 100 to about 68 in 100 traces far exceeds ordinary sampling variation, but the windows alone cannot rule out a shifted variant mix. [OCC001, OCC003] About 31 in 100 later traces still show A30 before A36, and the evidence cannot tell whether these are single-pass executions or another variant; the before side of the A36 → A30 adjacency rests on a single observation; and the changes around A05 (lower participation, routing towards A07) and the wider, slightly earlier positions of A05 and A14 are consistent with the same behaviour but their connection to the repeating block is not established. [REL005, LAG002, DIR001, POS001, POS002]
