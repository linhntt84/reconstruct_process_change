### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that, in the later window, the block A14 → A20 → A15, which previously always ran between A36 and A21, is skipped as a whole in roughly 47 in 100 cases, with A36 followed directly by A21, while the original sequence A36 → A14 → A20 → A15 → A21 is retained in the remaining cases. The routing change is observed directly (a direct A36→A21 transition now carries about 47% of A36's outgoing transitions), the placement change is observed directly (the A36–A21 distance drops from four positions to one in the same share of traces), and participation behaves exactly as this hypothesis predicts: the three block activities fall from all traces to about 53 in 100, always together, while A21 and A36 remain universal, and A21's slightly earlier average position is consistent with the block being absent in part of the traces. The skip share of about 0.47 agrees across the occurrence, routing, relation and lag indicators. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, DIR001, LAG001, REL001, REL003, REL004, POS001]

The main limitation is that the two windows are finite samples of 200 traces each, and most items describe the same underlying traces rather than independent confirmations, so the agreement in the 0.47 share is consistency, not replication. The evidence also cannot rule out that the direct A36→A21 path already existed as a rare variant before — the earlier window shows only A14 among A36's observed transitions, but an unobserved edge is not proof of absence — so the change could be a large frequency shift of an existing alternative path rather than a newly added one.
