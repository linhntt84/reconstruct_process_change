### Observed differences

- A22 ran in every earlier-window trace but in only about 51 in 100 later-window traces (mean occurrences per trace 1.0 → about 0.5, with no repetition in either window). Accordingly, A21 and A22 co-occurred in 100% of earlier traces but in about 51 in 100 later traces, with A21 appearing without A22 in the remaining 49 in 100. [OCC001, REL001]
- Three activities not observed in the earlier window — A27, A28 and A33 — each appear in about 49 in 100 later-window traces, once per trace, around the middle of the trace (mean relative position about 0.49, tightly concentrated with a spread of roughly ±0.05–0.06). [OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, POS001, POS002, POS003]
- Routing out of A21 was redistributed: earlier, all observed outgoing transitions went to A22; in the later window, about half still go to A22 while the rest split to A28 (about 18 in 100), A33 (about 17 in 100) and A27 (about 15 in 100). [DIR001]
- The three new activities behave as one block: every pair co-occurs in about 49 in 100 traces (so all three appear together in the same traces), they never co-occur with A22, and their internal order varies — for example A27 precedes A28 in about 23 in 100 traces while A28 precedes A27 in about 27 in 100. Each of the three sends outgoing transitions both to the other two block members and to A23. [REL006, REL007, REL008, REL012, REL013, REL014, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004]
- The block sits between A21 and A23: A21 precedes each block member, and each block member precedes A23, in about 49 in 100 later traces, and A23 now occurs without A22 in about 49 in 100 traces. [REL002, REL003, REL004, REL009, REL010, REL011, REL005]
- The event distance from A21 to A23 was always 2 positions in the earlier window; in the later window it is 2 in about 51 in 100 traces and 4 in about 49 in 100, consistent with two extra steps inserted between the two activities. [LAG001]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that, in the later window, roughly half of the executions replace the segment A21 → A22 → A23 with A21 followed by a block containing A27, A28 and A33 — each executed once, in varying internal orders — before reaching A23, while the other half keep the original path through A22. A22 is skipped exactly in the traces that run the block, with which it is mutually exclusive. [OCC001, OCC002, DIR001, REL002, REL005, REL009, REL012, LAG001]

Signs supporting this: 26 items qualified where roughly 0.4 would be expected for an unchanged process, a volume that chance cannot account for as a whole; the items agree across occurrence, routing, positional, relational and distance indicators rather than restating one measure; the order evidence is direct (A21 before each block member, each block member before A23, block members excluding A22, and the A21→A23 distance shifting from 2 to 4 positions); each difference is larger than in about 99 in 100 random re-splits of the pooled data; and the profiles rest on 98–200 observations per item. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, POS001, POS002, POS003, REL001, REL002, REL005, REL009, REL012, LAG001]

Limitations: the evidence cannot distinguish a newly introduced fragment from a variant that already existed at a very low rate in the earlier window and became frequent, since zero participation only means the activities were not observed there (0 of 200 earlier traces); and the exact 51/49 split between the two paths is a finite-sample estimate, so the true share of the new routing may differ somewhat. Routing shares are also measured over outgoing transitions of the source activity, not over cases. [OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004]
