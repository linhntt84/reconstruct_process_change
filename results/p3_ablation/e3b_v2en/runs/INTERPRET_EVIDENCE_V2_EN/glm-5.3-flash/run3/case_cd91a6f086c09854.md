### Observed differences

- The relative order of two groups of activities reversed. In the earlier window, A30, A31 and A36 each came before A21 and before A22 in every trace containing both (100 in 100); in the later window, A21 and A22 each come before A30, A31 and A36 in about 99 in 100 traces. Both activities of each pair still occur together in all traces, so participation itself did not change — only the order did. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006]
- A20's routing changed completely. Earlier, its observed outgoing transitions went to A30 (about 40 in 100), A31 (about 33) and A36 (about 28), with none observed to A22; in the later window, about 99 in 100 go to A22, which now immediately follows A20 (one step apart in 99 in 100 traces, previously four steps). [DIR001, LAG001]
- A21's routing changed. Earlier, A21 was always followed by A25 after one step (100 in 100); in the later window, A21 leads to A30, A31 and A36 (roughly a third each) and A25 instead follows about four steps later (99 in 100). [DIR002, LAG004]
- The exits of the A30/A31/A36 group changed. Earlier, each of the three led to A22 in roughly 28–38 of 100 outgoing transitions; in the later window they almost never lead to A22 (under 1 in 100) and instead lead to A25 in about 30–36 of 100 cases. [DIR003, DIR004, DIR005]
- Event positions shifted consistently (relative event positions, not clock time): A21 and A22 occur earlier in the later window (mean position from about 0.62 to 0.53 and from 0.47 to 0.38), while A30, A31 and A36 occur later (mean from about 0.41 to 0.59). All five activities still appear in every trace in both windows. [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005]
- Four further lag comparisons (A20 with A31 and A36; A31 and A36 with A22) rest on only 2 later-window observations each and are too weak to support any conclusion. [LAG002, LAG003, LAG005, LAG006]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the order of two segments was reversed: the segment containing A21 and A22 versus the segment containing A30, A31 and A36, with A25 now placed after the A30/A31/A36 group rather than directly after A21. In the earlier window the flow ran from A20 into the A30/A31/A36 group, from there to A22, with A21 afterwards and A25 immediately following A21; in the later window A20 leads to A22, A21 leads into the A30/A31/A36 group, and that group leads on to A25. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, LAG001, LAG004, REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006]

This goes well beyond what chance would produce in aggregate: 22 evidence items qualified against roughly 0.42 expected for an unchanged process, and the core findings are direct relational observations — order flips seen in about 99 of 100 traces across six activity pairs with unchanged co-occurrence — consistently corroborated by the routing, adjacency and position items, each of which exceeds about 995 of 1,000 random re-splits of the pooled data. [REL001, REL004, DIR001, DIR002, LAG001, LAG004, POS001, POS003]

Limitations: the evidence shows the reordering but not which segment "moved" or how the change came about; the relative order of A21 versus A22 themselves, and of A20 relative to the A30/A31/A36 group in the later window, is not directly observed. Sampling differences in variant frequencies between the two finite windows cannot be ruled out in principle, although near-complete flips (from 100 in 100 to about 1 in 100) on 200 traces per window are hard to attribute to sampling alone; the four low-support lag items were excluded from this judgement. [REL001, REL004, LAG002, LAG003, LAG005, LAG006]
