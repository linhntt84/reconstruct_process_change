### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the choice around A21 was restructured. In the earlier window, traces ran A20 directly into A23, then A21, and then either A22 or the A31/A32/A37 group — the two were mutually exclusive within a trace — before A26. In the later window, traces instead run A20 into the A31/A32/A37 group (all three, in varying order), which then flows into A23, after which A21, A22 and A26 always follow; in effect, A31/A32/A37 now run in essentially every case between A20 and A23, and A22 runs in every case after A21. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, DIR001, DIR002, REL011, REL006, LAG001]

The support is direct rather than merely correlational: order reversals are observed for the pairs (A21, A31/A32/A37) and (A23, A31/A32/A37), the previously unobserved co-occurrence of A22 with the group appears at about 995 in 1000, the successor profiles at A20 and A21 change as the hypothesis predicts, and the A20-to-A23 distance changes from 1 to 4 events. With 41 qualifying items against roughly 0.5 expected if nothing had changed, chance is not a plausible explanation for the set as a whole, though many items describe overlapping aspects of this one difference rather than independent confirmations. [REL006, REL011, DIR001, LAG001, OCC001]

A competing explanation the evidence cannot fully exclude is a shift in the mix of process variants between the two 200-trace samples; this is, however, hard to reconcile with A22 and the A31/A32/A37 group being strictly mutually exclusive earlier while co-occurring in nearly every later trace, which a pure frequency shift among unchanged variants would not produce. Remaining limitations: the exact order of A23 relative to A21 after the group is inferred from average positions rather than observed directly, and the positional indicators use event positions, not clock time, so no timing claim is made. [REL011, POS004, POS002, LAG001]
