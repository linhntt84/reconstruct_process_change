### Observed differences

- The main difference is a reversal of execution order between two groups of activities. In the earlier window, A20, A22 and A23 preceded A24, A25 and A26 in about 98–99 in 100 traces for every tested pair, and the opposite order was not observed in any trace. In the later window the opposite order is common: A24 precedes A23 in about 84 in 100 traces, A24 precedes A20 in about 73, A26 precedes A23 in about 68, A25 precedes A23 in about 52, and A26 precedes A20 in about 51. Both activities of every pair still occur together in all traces, so participation itself did not change. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, REL007, REL008]
- Positions shifted consistently with this reversal: in the later window A20, A22 and A23 occur later within traces (mean relative position about 0.59 to 0.63, 0.56 to 0.59 and 0.62 to 0.68), while A24, A25 and A26 occur earlier (about 0.65 to 0.59, 0.70 to 0.68 and 0.67 to 0.64). [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006]
- Sequences almost unobserved before are now frequent: A24 before A20 appears in 1 of 200 earlier traces versus about 148 of 200 later, A24 before A22 in 1 versus about 94 (immediately consecutive in about half of them), A25 before A20 in 1 versus about 64, and A26 before A20 in 1 versus about 101. The earlier profiles for these pairs rest on a single observation each, but the relation indicators independently confirm the reverse order was absent before. [LAG014, LAG015, LAG019, LAG021, REL001]
- Distances between consecutive activities changed accordingly: A23 to A32 was four steps apart in about 94 in 100 traces and is now one step apart in about 49 in 100; A20 to A25 was always four steps apart and is now spread over one to four steps. [LAG013, LAG003, LAG005]
- Routing changed at several points (shares over roughly 200 outgoing transitions per window): A23, which previously led only to A24, now leads most often to A32 (about 49 in 100) with A24, A25 and A26 sharing the rest; A31, which previously led only to A22, now splits between A22 (about 52 in 100) and A24 (about 47 in 100); A24 now leads to A22 in about 23 in 100 transitions and to A20 in about 11, after almost always leading to A26. [DIR003, DIR007, DIR004]
- A20, A22, A25 and A26 also gained outgoing transitions that were rare before, for example A20 to each of A24/A25/A26 (about 10–11 in 100 each), A22 to A24 (about 27 in 100) and A25 to A20 or A23 (about 20 in 100 each). [DIR001, DIR002, DIR005, DIR006]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the relative order of two blocks of activities was reversed in the later window: A24, A25 and A26, which previously ran after A20, A22 and A23 in a fixed chain running from A31 through A22, A20, A23, A24, A26 and A25 to A32, now frequently run before A20, A22 and A23, with A23 often closing directly to A32 and A31 feeding either A22 or A24. The order change is shown directly by the relation indicators (all eight tested pairs reversed, with about 24–84 in 100 traces switching order while co-occurrence stayed at 100 in 100), the two groups' positions moved in opposite directions, and the newly dominant sequences were observed in only 1 of 200 earlier traces; the routing changes at A23, A31 and A24 point the same way. [REL001, REL006, POS001, POS004, LAG014, LAG015, DIR003, DIR007, DIR004, DIR001, DIR002, DIR005, DIR006]

The reversal is partial rather than complete — A22 still precedes A26 in about 77 in 100 later traces and A24 still leads to A26 in about 60 in 100 — so the later window mixes several orderings rather than one new fixed sequence. The evidence cannot rule out that both orders already existed as variants whose frequencies shifted, since the reversed order was observed in 0 of 200 earlier traces and a rare pre-existing variant becoming common looks the same as a reordering on two finite samples. The direction, position, distance and routing items also describe the same traces and are not independent confirmations. [REL005, DIR004, REL001, LAG015]
