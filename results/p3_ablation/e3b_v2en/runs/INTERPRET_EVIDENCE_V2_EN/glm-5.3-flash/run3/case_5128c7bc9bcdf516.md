### Observed differences

- In the later window, each of seven activities sends its outgoing transitions almost entirely to one successor, where before several alternatives existed: from A15 to A18 (about 995 in 1000, up from about 62 in 100), from A17 to A15 (up from about 56 in 100), from A18 to A19 (up from about 12 in 100, displacing A27, which previously received about 53 in 100), from A19 to A21 (up from about 55 in 100), from A20 to A27 (up from about 47 in 100), from A21 to A20 (up from about 65 in 100) and from A26 to A17 (previously an even split with A19). [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006, DIR007]
- Typical pair order has consolidated and partly reversed: A15 now precedes A19 in all but about 1 in 200 traces (before, A19 came first in about 75 in 100); A18 now precedes A19 (before, A19 first in about 88 in 100) and A21 (before, A21 first in about 70 in 100); A17 precedes A19, and A15 precedes A21, in every trace (even or near-even splits before). All eight pairs still co-occur in every trace in both windows. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, REL007, REL008]
- Distances between pairs have become fixed and mutually consistent: A15 reaches A18 in 1 event step in all but about 1 in 200 cases (about 62 in 100 before); A18 reaches A27 in 4 steps rather than typically 1; A26 reaches A19 in 4 steps rather than 1 (about 50 in 100 before); A15 reaches A27 in 5 steps in all but about 1 in 200 cases (spread over 2–5 steps before). [LAG001, LAG005, LAG013, LAG027]
- Typical within-trace positions moved in the same direction: A17, A15 and A18 now occur earlier (mean relative position lower by about 0.02–0.06), while A19, A21 and A20 occur later (higher by about 0.03–0.06). [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006]
- Taken together, the later window behaves like the single route A26 → A17 → A15 → A18 → A19 → A21 → A20 → A27, whereas the earlier window mixed several alternative successors and orders at each step. [DIR001–DIR007, LAG001–LAG029]
- Five lag results rest on a single after-window observation each (the pairs A19→A15, A19→A18, A20→A18, A21→A18 and A26→A20) and carry little weight alone. [LAG014, LAG015, LAG019, LAG021, LAG028]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

- The evidence supports the possibility that the branched routing among A15, A17, A18, A19, A20, A21 and A26 was replaced by one fixed sequence, A26 → A17 → A15 → A18 → A19 → A21 → A20 → A27, with A27 now reached only after A20 rather than directly after A18. [DIR001–DIR007, REL001–REL008]
- The hypothesis is shown directly by relational evidence, not only by frequency shifts: several pairs reversed their typical order (for example, A18 before A19 rose from about 12 in 100 to all but about 1 in 200 traces), and the after-window distances reproduce exactly the position gaps of a single chain (A15→A20 at 4 steps, A17→A21 at 4 steps, A26→A21 at 5 steps). [REL001, REL006, LAG003, LAG010, LAG029]
- All four indicator families point to the same sequence and no item contradicts it; most items rest on about 200 traces per window, and every difference exceeds those seen in about 199 of 200 random re-splits of the pooled data. [DIR001–DIR007, POS001–POS006, REL001–REL008, LAG001–LAG029]
- The 50 qualifying items are roughly a hundred times the number expected by chance for the 95 tested units (about 0.47), so their combined occurrence cannot be explained as routine screening noise — while noting that they largely describe one underlying trace behaviour and are not independent confirmations. [DIR001–DIR007, POS001–POS006, REL001–REL008, LAG001–LAG029]
- A competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is a shift in the mix of pre-existing variants — for example, a variant that was rare before dominating the later window — rather than a change in routing rules; the before-window spread (A26 splitting evenly between two successors, A18's transitions going mainly to A27) makes pure sampling drift unlikely, but the two explanations cannot be separated with this evidence. [DIR003, DIR007]
- The five single-observation lag items add little, and the position indicator uses event positions rather than clock time, so it corroborates the reordering but does not by itself establish it. [LAG014, LAG015, LAG019, LAG021, LAG028, POS001–POS006]
