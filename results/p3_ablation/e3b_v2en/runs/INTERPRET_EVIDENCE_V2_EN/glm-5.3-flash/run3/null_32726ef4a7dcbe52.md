### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

Seven control-flow items were flagged across the 96 tested units, where about 0.48 would qualify by chance if nothing had changed, and each difference exceeds roughly 199 of 200 random re-splits of the pooled data, so chance is an unlikely explanation for the set as a whole. The items converge, through successor shares and pair distances, on one directly observed possibility: in the segment following A32, the order of A36 and A37 is reversed in the later window. Before, the dominant reading is A32 → A37 → A36 → A33 (A32 reached A37 immediately in about 63 in 100 cases, A37 was followed by A36 in about 62 in 100, and A36 exited to A33 in about 59 in 100); in the later window it reads A32 → A36 → A37 → A33 (A32 reaches A36 immediately in about 62 in 100, A36 is followed by A37 in about 62 in 100, and A37 exits to A33 in about 58 in 100). Every link of this chain moves in the expected direction, which is the kind of consistency a genuine rerouting would produce. [DIR001, DIR002, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004]

Limitations: the chain wording is an interpretation of pairwise distances and successor shares, not a traced sequence, and the windows show only aggregate shares — a shift in the mix of pre-existing variants, with the A36→A37 ordering simply occurring more often in the later sample, would look identical, so true rerouting cannot be separated from variant-frequency sampling. The rare successors (about 1–2 in 100 transitions) appearing or disappearing between windows is consistent with ordinary sampling noise on rare behaviour. The A07 change — higher participation and a shorter A07→A14 gap — is not relationally linked to the rerouting; it may be a separate participation change or a sampling difference, and the evidence does not establish it as part of the same structural change.
