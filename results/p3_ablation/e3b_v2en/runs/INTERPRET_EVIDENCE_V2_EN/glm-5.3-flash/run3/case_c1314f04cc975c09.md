### Observed differences

- In the later window, A25, A26 and A31 each occur about 3.5 times per trace on average, up from exactly once per trace before; all three still appear in every trace in both windows, but repeated occurrences within a trace now happen in about 68 in 100 later traces, against none before [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003].
- A31's outgoing transitions are rerouted: before, every observed transition from A31 went to A20; in the later window, about 71 in 100 go to A25 and about 29 in 100 to A20. The 697 outgoing transitions observed after (versus 200 before) match the roughly 3.5-fold increase in A31 occurrences [DIR001].
- The fixed relationship between A25 and A31 dissolved: before, they co-occurred in every trace with A25 always before A31; in the later window they still always co-occur, but in about 68 in 100 traces they interleave or repeat instead of keeping that single fixed order [REL001].
- The pair's fixed spacing is largely gone: before, A31 followed A25 exactly two positions later in all observed pairs; in the later window that two-position gap holds for only about 12 in 100 pairs, and about 5 in 100 pairs sit five positions apart [LAG001].
- Occurrences of A25 and A31 became more spread out within the trace: the spread of their relative positions roughly doubled (A25 from about 0.06 to 0.12, A31 from about 0.06 to 0.12) while medians barely moved, consistent with repeated occurrences landing at varying positions rather than one fixed spot [POS001, POS002].

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that, in the later window, A25, A26 and A31 came to be executed repeatedly within most traces, with A31 typically followed again by A25. The repetition increase for all three activities (from one to about 3.5 occurrences per trace, repeats in about 68 in 100 traces), the rerouting of A31 towards A25, the shift from a fixed single A25-before-A31 pass to interleaved or repeated execution, and the disappearance of the fixed two-position gap all point to the same repeating behaviour, and the positional spread is consistent with it. The number of qualifying items (8, against roughly 0.3 expected if nothing had changed across the 60 tested units) is far beyond what an unchanged process typically produces, and every item exceeds about 995 in 1000 random re-split comparisons [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, DIR001, REL001, LAG001, POS001, POS002].

Limitations remain. The evidence cannot rule out that the later window simply over-represents a repeating variant that already existed at low frequency before: with 200 traces per window, a pattern covering about 68 in 100 later traces but none before is unusual for random splits, but sampling differences in variant frequencies are still a possible explanation. In addition, A26's repetition rests only on occurrence counts, with no relational evidence tying it to the A25–A31 pattern, so it may repeat as a separate block; and the three identical occurrence profiles likely describe one shared behaviour rather than three independent changes [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, REL001, DIR001].
