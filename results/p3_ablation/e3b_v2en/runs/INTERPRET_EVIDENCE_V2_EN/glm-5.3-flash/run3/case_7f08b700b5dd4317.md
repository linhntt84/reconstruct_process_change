### Observed differences

- Before, six activities ran in every trace in a fixed chain: A30, then the group A33/A35/A36, then the group A37/A38/A39, then A31 near the end. In the later window, A33, A35 and A36 each appear in only about 47 in 100 traces (mean occurrences per trace about 0.47, down from 1.0), and A37, A38 and A39 in about 56–57 in 100 traces (down from 1.0). [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006]

- The two groups of three became nearly mutually exclusive. Previously, any pair such as A33–A37 or A36–A39 co-occurred in 200 of 200 traces, always with the A33/A35/A36 activity first; in the later window such pairs co-occur in only about 3 in 100 traces, with only the A37/A38/A39 activity present in about 53 in 100 traces and only the A33/A35/A36 activity in about 44 in 100. [REL015, REL016, REL017, REL019, REL020, REL021, REL022, REL023, REL024]

- Within the groups, togetherness largely persists: A33, A35 and A36 still appear together where they occur at all (co-occurrence about 47 in 100, neither present in about 53 in 100) [REL013, REL014, REL018], and A37, A38 and A39 co-occur in about 55–56 in 100 traces with neither present in about 43 in 100. [REL025, REL026, REL027]

- A30 and A31 still occur in every trace in both windows. In the later window, A30 appears without any of A33/A35/A36 in roughly 44–53 in 100 traces (before, they always followed A30) [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006], and A31 appears without them in about 53 in 100 traces (before, A31 was always preceded by them). [REL007, REL008, REL009, REL010, REL011, REL012]

- Routing shifted: A31, previously not reached from A33/A35/A36, now receives about 32, 34 and 26 in 100 of their outgoing transitions respectively, and A30 — which previously led almost only to A33/A35/A36 — now also leads to A37, A38 and A39 (about 15–19 in 100 of its outgoing transitions each). [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004]

- Positions and distances adjusted: A30 sits slightly later in the trace (mean relative position about 0.62 to 0.67) and A31 slightly earlier (about 0.82 to 0.80) [POS001, POS002]; where A33/A35/A36 occur, their positions are later (mean about 0.68 to 0.74) and A39 slightly earlier (about 0.77 to 0.73) [POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006]. Gaps that were 4–5 steps — A30 to A37/A38/A39, and A33/A35/A36 to A31 — are now mostly 1–3 steps. [LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG006, LAG009]

- A few lag results rest on only about 4–5 later-window co-occurrences and are too weak to interpret. [LAG005, LAG007, LAG008, LAG010, LAG011]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the segment between A30 and A31 became conditional: in the later window a trace typically runs either the A33/A35/A36 block (about 47 in 100 traces, now followed closely by A31) or the A37/A38/A39 block reached more directly from A30 (about 56 in 100 traces), whereas before, every trace ran both blocks in sequence. The 54 evidence items are many times the roughly 1 expected by chance alone, and the support is direct and consistent: co-occurrence between the two groups collapses from 200 of 200 traces to about 3 in 100 while each group stays internally cohesive [REL013, REL015, REL022, REL025], participation of all six activities drops by roughly half [OCC001, OCC004], new direct transitions A33/A35/A36 → A31 and A30 → A37/A38/A39 appear [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004], and the affected step distances shrink from 4–5 to 1–3 steps [LAG001, LAG004]. A30 and A31 remain in every trace, anchoring the start and end of the changed segment [POS001, POS002].

Limitations: the many items largely describe one underlying difference, so they are not independent confirmations, and the lag items based on about 4–5 observations were not relied on [LAG005, LAG007, LAG008, LAG010, LAG011]. The before window contains only the all-activities behaviour, so the evidence cannot rule out that this alternative routing existed before at a frequency too low to observe and simply rose in frequency — a sampling shift in variant frequencies remains a competing explanation. High empirical strength (about 0.995 throughout) shows these differences are unusual for random re-splits of unchanged data, but it is not a probability that the process changed.
