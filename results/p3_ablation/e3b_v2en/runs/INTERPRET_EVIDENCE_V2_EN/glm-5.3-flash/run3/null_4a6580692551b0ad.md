### Observed differences

- The dominant successor of A35 flipped: in the earlier window about 58 in 100 of A35's outgoing transitions went to A32 and about 38 in 100 to A36; in the later window about 61 in 100 go to A36 and only about 37 in 100 to A32 [DIR001].
- The dominant successor of A36 flipped in the opposite direction: transitions to A35 fell from about 60 in 100 to about 36 in 100, while transitions to A32 rose from about 39 in 100 to about 60 in 100; four rare successors (A21, A30, A25, A07) at about 1 in 100 each appear only in the later window [DIR002].
- A35 moved closer to A31: among observed A31→A35 pairs, the one-step gap share rose from about 39% to about 61%, while the two-step share fell from about 60% to about 38% [LAG001].
- A36 moved one step further from A31: the one-step gap share fell from about 60% to about 38%, while the two-step share rose from about 39% to about 61% [LAG002].
- A32 now tends to follow A35 with a delay rather than immediately: the one-step share fell from about 58% to about 38%, and the two-step share rose from about 41% to about 62% [LAG003].
- The A36→A35 pair remains overwhelmingly immediate (one-step gaps were 100% before and about 94% after, with small two- and three-step gaps appearing), but this item rests on fewer observations, about 62 before and 34 after [LAG004].
- All six differences are larger than those in about 199 of 200 random re-splits of the pooled traces, and the underlying profiles are based on roughly 87–104 observations per window (LAG004: 62 and 34) [DIR001, DIR002, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004].

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that A35 and A36 exchanged their positions in the sequence between A31 and A32: in the earlier window the dominant flow reads A31 → A36 → A35 → A32 with a side loop A35 → A36, while in the later window it reads A31 → A35 → A36 → A32 with A36 → A35 as the side loop [DIR001, DIR002, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003].

The supporting signs are direct and mutually consistent: the two successor flips are exactly those a position exchange predicts, and the lag shifts move in the matching directions — A35 now immediately after A31, A36 one step further, and A32 pushed from an immediate to a delayed successor of A35, consistent with A36 sitting between them in the later window; six qualifying items is many times the roughly 0.5 expected by chance when nothing changed [DIR001, DIR002, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004].

Limitations remain: with 200 traces per window, a shift in the mix of two pre-existing variants — one running A36 before A35 and one the reverse — could produce the same pattern without any structural change; the rare new successors of A36 (about 1 in 100 each) may be variants appearing in one window only; and the A36→A35 lag item rests on only about 34 later observations [DIR002, LAG004].
