### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, every observed transition out of A20 went to A22; in the later window, A20's outgoing transitions spread almost evenly over A30, A31 and A36 (about 33 in 100 each), with A22 receiving only about 0.5 in 100. [DIR001]
- In the later window, almost all observed transitions out of A21 (about 995 in 1000) go to A25, whereas earlier they split roughly 38, 34 and 28 in 100 across A36, A30 and A31. [DIR002]
- In the earlier window, each of A30, A31 and A36 sent roughly 28–36 in 100 of its transitions to A25; in the later window, transitions to A25 have nearly disappeared (about 0.5 in 100) and each of the three instead sends about 30–34 in 100 to A22. [DIR003, DIR004, DIR005]
- The order between A21/A22 and the group A30/A31/A36 reversed: earlier, A21 and A22 came before each group activity in all 200 traces; in the later window, the group activity comes first in about 199 of 200 traces for all six pairs. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006]
- Step distances changed in a matching way: A20 to A22 went from adjacent (lag 1 in all 200 traces) to 4 steps apart (about 199 of 200), and A21 to A25 went from 4 steps apart to adjacent. [LAG001, LAG002]
- Relative positions shifted accordingly in the later window: A30, A31 and A36 each occur earlier (mean relative position about 0.59 down to about 0.41), while A22 (about 0.38 to 0.47) and A21 (about 0.53 to 0.62) occur later. [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005]
- Six further lag items for the ordered pairs A21→A30/A31/A36 and A30/A31/A36→A25 show these pairs within five steps almost vanishing in the later window, but each rests on a single after-window observation and carries little weight. [LAG003, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006, LAG007, LAG008]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the execution order around these activities was rearranged: the group A30, A31 and A36, which in the earlier window ran after A21 and before A25, in the later window runs directly after A20 and before A22, while A25 now immediately follows A21. This placement change is shown directly by the six order flips and the two distance changes, both based on all 200 traces in each window — the three-step gap that previously separated A21 from A25, with the group sitting between them, now separates A20 from A22. The successor changes at A20, A21 and the group members and the position shifts point the same way, and participation itself is unchanged, with every involved activity appearing in all 200 traces in both windows. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, LAG001, LAG002, DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005]

The number of qualifying items (24, all control-flow) is many times the roughly 0.41 expected if nothing had changed, so the signals as a whole are not what an unchanged process typically produces, and each difference exceeds those seen in about 99.5 of 100 random re-splits of the pooled traces. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006, LAG007, LAG008]

Limitations: many items describe the same underlying rearrangement, since each group member produces a mirror-image flip, so they are not independent confirmations, and the six lag items on the A21→group and group→A25 pairs rest on one after-window observation each. The main competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that both orderings existed as process variants before and after, with the two windows sampling very different variant frequencies (about 199 of 200 versus about 1 of 200); the near-completeness of the flip in both directions makes a pure sampling difference unlikely, but it cannot be excluded entirely. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, LAG003, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006, LAG007, LAG008]
