### Observed differences

- In the later window, three activities that were essentially absent before — A19, A20 and A25 — each run in about 30–38 in 100 traces (A19: 0 → 76 of 200 traces; A20: 1 → 59; A25: 0 → 69), each at most once per trace. Any pair of them co-occurs in only about 1 in 100 traces, and at least one of the three is present in about 99 in 100 later traces, so typically exactly one of them runs per trace. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, REL001, REL002, REL005]
- Routing into this step changed: before, A41 led directly to A26 in about 82 in 100 of its observed outgoing transitions; in the later window it leads to A19 in about 33 in 100, A25 in about 30 and A20 in about 24, with a direct move to A26 in only about 1–2 in 100. [DIR004]
- Routing out of the new activities: A19 is followed by A26 in about 95 in 100 of its observed outgoing transitions, and A25 in about 90 in 100. [DIR002, DIR003]
- Ordering relations place the new activities between A41 and A26: A41 precedes A19, A20 or A25, and each of these precedes A26, in roughly 28–38 in 100 traces per pair; before, A26 ran in every trace while none of the three appeared. [REL003, REL004, REL006, REL007, REL008, REL009]
- The A41-to-A26 distance widened: A26 followed within five steps in all traces in both windows, but at 1 step in about 82 in 100 before versus about 1 in 100 after, at 2 steps in about 18 before versus about 83 after, and at 3 steps in about 14 in 100 after. [LAG001]
- The new activities sit about a third of the way through the trace (mean relative position roughly 0.36–0.37); A26, still present in every trace, shifted slightly later (mean about 0.37 to 0.40). [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004]
- A secondary redistribution appears at A02, whose outgoing transitions lose their A26 share (about 26 in 100) and gain small shares to A19, A20 and A25; this rests on only 35 and 44 observed transitions and is weak. [DIR001]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that, in the later window, a new step is inserted between A41 and A26 in essentially every trace, taking one of three alternative forms: A19, A20 or A25, each covering roughly 30–38 in 100 traces while any two of them co-occur in only about 1 in 100. Before, traces passed from A41 to A26 without any of the three being observed (A20 in 1 of 200 traces, the others not at all). [DIR004, REL001, REL002, REL005, LAG001]

The hypothesis rests on direct relational and directional evidence rather than frequency counts alone: A41's outgoing transitions moved from A26 to the three new activities [DIR004]; A19 and A25 lead back to A26 [DIR002, DIR003]; the ordering relations consistently place A41 before each new activity and each new activity before A26 [REL003, REL004, REL006, REL007, REL008, REL009]; and the A41-to-A26 distance moved from one step to mostly two, as expected when a single activity is inserted [LAG001]. With 21 flagged items against roughly 0.5 expected if nothing had changed, chance screening noise cannot explain the pattern as a whole. [DIR004, LAG001, REL003, REL008]

Limitations: the items overlap heavily and largely describe one underlying change, so they are not independent confirmations; the apparent three-way exclusivity rests on low co-occurrence in a single sample and could reflect correlated skipping rather than a deliberate choice. The main competing explanation the evidence cannot fully exclude is a sampling difference in variant frequencies — a pre-existing rare variant surfacing in the later window — although a behaviour present in about 99 in 100 later traces but essentially absent from 200 before-traces makes pure sampling noise implausible. The A02 redistribution rests on few transitions and should be treated as weak. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, DIR001]
