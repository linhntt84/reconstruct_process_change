### Observed differences

- In the later window, six activities — A33, A35, A36, A37, A38 and A39 — run in far fewer cases: each appeared in every one of the 200 earlier traces but in only about 47–57 in 100 later traces (A33/A35/A36 about 47, A37/A38/A39 about 56–57). [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006]
- The six now behave as two mutually exclusive blocks. Pairs inside {A33, A35, A36} still appear together whenever one of them appears (together in about 46–47 in 100 later traces, neither in about 53), and the same holds for {A37, A38, A39} (together in about 55–56 in 100). [REL013, REL014, REL018, REL025, REL026, REL027]
- Cross-block co-occurrence collapsed: an activity from {A33, A35, A36} appears together with one from {A37, A38, A39} in only about 2–3.5 in 100 later traces, whereas earlier these pairs co-occurred in essentially every trace (for example, A33 preceded A37 in 100% of earlier traces, but the two appear together in only about 3.5 in 100 later traces). [REL015, REL016, REL017, REL019, REL020, REL021, REL022, REL023, REL024]
- A30 and A31 still appear in all cases in both windows, and the overall order is preserved: A30 still precedes the six activities, which still precede A31 whenever they co-occur. [POS001, POS002, REL001, REL002, REL007, REL008]
- Routing changed accordingly: in the later window A30 leads directly to A37, A38 or A39 in roughly 15–20% of its outgoing transitions each (unobserved earlier), and A33, A35 and A36 each lead directly to A31 in about 24–34% of theirs. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004]
- Spacing shrank accordingly: the gap from A30 to A37/A38/A39 and from A33/A35/A36 to A31 moved from 4–5 event positions to 1–3. [LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG007, LAG010]
- Relative positions shifted consistently with shorter cases: A33/A35/A36 sit later (mean position about 0.68 to 0.73–0.74), A37/A38/A39 slightly earlier (about 0.76–0.77 to 0.74), A30 later (0.62 to 0.67) and A31 slightly earlier (0.82 to 0.80). [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006, POS007, POS008]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that, in the later window, each case skips one of two three-activity blocks between A30 and A31: a case runs either {A33, A35, A36} (about 47 in 100) or {A37, A38, A39} (about 56 in 100), whereas earlier essentially every case ran both blocks in the sequence A30 → {A33, A35, A36} → {A37, A38, A39} → A31. [REL015, REL019, REL022, REL013, REL025, OCC001, OCC004, REL001, REL007]

This goes beyond what chance would produce: 57 items qualified where only about 0.79 would be expected if nothing had changed, and each difference exceeds about 99.5 in 100 random re-splits of the pooled traces. The mutual exclusivity is shown directly by the relational indicator rather than inferred from participation alone, and the direction and spacing evidence match it exactly (new direct successions A30→A37/A38/A39 and A33/A35/A36→A31, gaps shrinking from 4–5 to 1–3 steps). Each activity's profile rests on 93–114 later traces, so the fall in cross-block co-occurrence from 100% to about 2–3.5% is far beyond a rare variant surfacing in one window. [REL015, DIR001, DIR002, LAG001, LAG004, OCC001]

Limitations: the windows are finite samples, so the evidence cannot distinguish a newly introduced skipping choice from two block-only behaviours that existed earlier at negligible frequency and then became dominant — a shift in variant mix remains a possible reading. The many items are also not independent confirmations, since occurrence, relation, direction, lag and position largely reflect the same trace behaviour, and the cross-block lag items rest on only 4–5 later observations. Finally, about 2–3.5 in 100 later cases still contain both blocks, so the exclusivity is not absolute. [REL015, OCC001, LAG005, LAG006, LAG009]
