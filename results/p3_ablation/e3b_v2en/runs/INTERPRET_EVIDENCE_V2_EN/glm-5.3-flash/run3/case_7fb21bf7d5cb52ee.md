### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that, in the later window, A10 runs as a standard early step between A14 and A15 — A14, then A10, then A15 — where previously A10 appeared in only about a fifth of traces and A14 passed directly to A15; it also supports a second, separate difference: the near-fixed sequence within the A20–A26 segment gave way to a different, more variable ordering in which A24, A25 and A26 frequently execute before A20, A22 and A23, while A04 and A08 are executed less often. [OCC003, POS001, DIR002, DIR003, LAG002, LAG003, REL015, REL020, OCC001, OCC002]

Supporting signs: the A10 pattern agrees across occurrence, position, routing, relation and lag indicators, with 133–155 later-window observations behind the adjacency and routing items; each changed order relation rests on about 200 traces per window; every listed item exceeds roughly 99.5 in 100 random re-splits of the pooled traces; and the 77 qualifying items are many times the about 1 expected by chance, so the overall volume of differences cannot be explained by sampling as a whole. [LAG002, DIR002, DIR003, REL015, REL020, OCC003]

Limitations: the aggregates cannot show whether A24–A26 precede A20–A23 on a single new route or only in a subset of cases, nor what drives A23's new dominant exit to A32 (about 47 in 100, from under 1 in 100); the fourteen relation items about A10 all describe one underlying behaviour and are not independent confirmations; and the smallest shifts (position changes of about 0.03–0.07 for A20–A26, small lag changes around A45–A48) are of the size an unchanged process produces, so sampling differences in variant frequencies remain a competing explanation for mid-sized changes such as the A04 decrease, even though they cannot plausibly account for the A10 participation jump or the reversed order relations.
