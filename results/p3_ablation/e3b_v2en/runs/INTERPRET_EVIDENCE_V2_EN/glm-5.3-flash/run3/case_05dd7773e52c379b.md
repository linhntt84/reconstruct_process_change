### Observed differences

- In the later window, six activities run in nearly every case: A28, A30 and A31 rose from about 49 to about 99 in 100 cases, and A32, A33 and A34 from about 52 to 199 of 200 cases; none of them repeats within a case in either window. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, OCC006]
- Before, these two groups were mutually exclusive: pairs spanning the groups (e.g. A28 with A32, A30 with A33, A31 with A34) co-occurred in 0 of 200 cases, each group appearing alone in roughly half the cases. In the later window, such cross-group pairs co-occur in about 98 in 100 cases, and the first-group activity comes before the second-group activity in essentially all of them. [REL015, REL016, REL017, REL019, REL020, REL021, REL022, REL023, REL024]
- A25 precedes and A26 follows all six activities in about 99 in 100 cases in the later window; before, this held only for the roughly half of cases containing the respective group. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, REL007, REL008, REL009, REL010, REL011, REL012]
- Within each group, behaviour is stable: the three activities of a group always appear together, with mixed relative order (no fixed sequence), in both windows. [REL013, REL014, REL018, REL025, REL026, REL027]
- Positional distances shifted accordingly: the gap from A25 to A32/A33/A34 and from A28/A30/A31 to A26 moved from 1–3 event positions (roughly one-third each) to 4–5 positions (about half each); A28/A30/A31 now sit slightly earlier (mean relative position about 0.74 to 0.68), A32/A33/A34 slightly later (about 0.73 to 0.77), A25 earlier and A26 later. [LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006, POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, POS005, POS006, POS007, POS008]
- Routing changed consistently: A25's outgoing transitions moved from an even spread over all six activities to almost exclusively A28/A30/A31 (about 30–38% each, versus about 0.5–1% to A32/A33/A34); transitions from A28, A30 and A31 into A26 (about 30–36% of their outgoing transitions) nearly disappeared, replaced by transitions into A32/A33/A34 (about 8–13% each). [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the routing between A25 and A26 changed from an either/or pattern — only one of the two activity groups ran per case — to both groups running in every case in a fixed order: A25, then A28 with A30 and A31, then A32 with A33 and A34, then A26. This rests on direct relational evidence: cross-group pairs that never co-occurred before (0 of 200 cases) now co-occur in about 98 in 100 cases with the first group always before the second, while A25 and A26 bracket all six activities in about 99 in 100 cases [REL015, REL019, REL022, REL001, REL007]. The lag and routing items corroborate the same picture: distances from A25 to the second group and from the first group to A26 grew from 1–3 to 4–5 positions, A25 now leads almost exclusively into the first group, and the first group's transitions into A26 were replaced by transitions into the second group [LAG001, LAG004, DIR001, DIR002]. In total 51 items qualified, roughly 85 times the about 0.59 expected by chance for the 118 tested units, so the findings as a set are not what an unchanged process typically produces [OCC001, DIR001, POS001, REL001, LAG001].

Limitations remain: the windows are finite samples of 200 cases each, and the 1–2 in 100 later cases still missing one group could be genuine occasional skips or sampling noise; the evidence also cannot establish the exact control structure, for example whether the order within each group is fixed or free, since group members always appear together with mixed relative order [REL013, REL025]. A competing explanation based purely on sampling-driven shifts in variant frequencies fits poorly — a re-mix of the two previously exclusive variants would still show zero cross-group co-occurrence — but the items largely describe one underlying trace behaviour and are not independent confirmations of it [REL015, LAG001].
