### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that resource allocation changed: execution of A01, A03, A13, A14, A17 and A22 was handed from one group of resources to another, with User_18 and the other formerly leading resources losing roughly half or more of their share while User_35, User_15 and other previously minor resources became the main performers [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006]. Supporting signs: all six allocation profiles moved in the same direction with the same gaining and losing resources, suggesting one handover rather than six unrelated shifts; the resource family produced 6 qualifying items against about 0.005 expected without change; and no control-flow differences qualified, making a routing change unlikely. Limitations: the resource codes are anonymous, so no roles can be inferred, and the evidence cannot distinguish a deliberate reassignment of the same work from a changed mix of work reaching these activities.

Other differences: the A04→A01 completion-to-completion time roughly halved and became much less variable; the time family's single item also exceeds its chance expectation (1 against about 0.017), so a change in timing is a plausible second hypothesis in its own right, but nothing in this evidence links it to the reallocation [TWAIT001].
