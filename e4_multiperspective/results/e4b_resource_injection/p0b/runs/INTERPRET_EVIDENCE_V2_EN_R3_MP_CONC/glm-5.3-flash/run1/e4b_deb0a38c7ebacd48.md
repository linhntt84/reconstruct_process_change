### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that resource allocation changed for a set of activities: in the later window, work on A01, A03, A13, A14, A17 and A22 was reassigned from one group of resources to a largely different group (User_18's group out, User_41's group in), and on A13, A14 and A17 the work additionally became concentrated on User_27, whose share roughly quadrupled to about 19 in 100 assignments. Supporting signs: the change is measured directly on the assignment shares of each activity; all six activities move in the same direction with largely overlapping departing and arriving groups; every difference exceeds all but about 1 in 2000 random re-splits; six items were flagged in the resource family against a chance expectation of about 0.003; and participation counts are stable, so this is a reallocation of work rather than the activities running more or less often. Limitations: only the largest shares are reported per activity, so resources that dropped out may retain small unreported shares; A13 and A14 show identical profiles and may reflect one shared behaviour rather than two independent changes; and the evidence cannot rule out that a shift in the mix of cases, rather than a change in allocation rules, routed the work to different resources.
