### Observed differences

- **Resource allocation.** In the earlier window, assignments on each of A01, A03, A13, A14, A17 and A22 were spread thinly, with about 52–55 in 100 going to a long tail of occasional resources. In the later window, a small set of resources leads on all six activities: User_19, not among the leading resources earlier, holds about 10–11 in 100 assignments on each of them, with User_28 and User_17 at roughly 6–9 in 100, while several previously active resources fell to low single-digit shares or left the leading group; the long tail shrank to about 36–46 in 100 [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, ASSIGN007].
- **A10 moved the other way.** Earlier, its work was concentrated on a group of about ten resources each holding 5–11 in 100 assignments (long tail only about 18 in 100); in the later window every member of that group lost share, three left the leading positions, a few new minor resources appeared, and the long tail grew to about 43 in 100 [ASSIGN003].
- **Timing.** The elapsed time between the completions of A04 and A01 roughly doubled: mean about 27 h to about 51 h, median about 24 h to about 40 h, upper quartile about 40 h to about 70 h; the spread widened (standard deviation about 34 h to 71 h), but the median rose too, so the shift is not only a heavier tail. The pair occurred in all 200 traces of each window [TWAIT001].
- **Negligible time items.** Two further flagged intervals were essentially immediate in both windows: A13→A14 about 2 seconds earlier and about 1.5 seconds later (258 vs 281 occurrences); A17→A22 about 0.06 seconds earlier and about 0.03 seconds later (121 vs 112) [TWAIT002, TWAIT003].
- **Control flow.** A06 took part in about 31 of 200 traces (about 15 in 100) earlier and about 12 of 200 (about 6 in 100) later, never twice within one trace in either window [OCC001].

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that resource allocation changed: work on A01, A03, A13, A14, A17 and A22 was shifted from a diffuse group of previously active resources to a small set of resources (User_19, User_28 and User_17 among them), while work on A10 spread away from its previously dominant resource group into a broader pool. This rests on direct allocation evidence: all seven tested resource units were flagged where about 0.003 items would be expected by chance if nothing had changed, the same gaining resources take the lead across six different activities, and each item's difference exceeded those of essentially all 2,000 random re-splits. Limitations: the A13 and A14 profiles are identical because they cover the same traces, so the seven items are not fully independent; and the evidence shows that shares moved but cannot show whether an allocation rule changed or only the mix of cases reaching these activities [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, ASSIGN007].

Other differences:
- The A04→A01 completion-to-completion time increased consistently (mean about 27 h to 51 h; median about 24 h to 40 h); the time family flagged three items where about 0.02 were expected by chance, so this is a timing change in its own right, though the evidence cannot establish whether it is connected to the reallocation [TWAIT001].
- Two further time items concern intervals that were already near-instant and changed by well under a second [TWAIT002, TWAIT003].
- Participation in A06 fell from about 15 in 100 traces to about 6 in 100; the control-flow family produced one flagged item where about 0.17 were expected by chance, so this alone does not indicate a structural change [OCC001].
