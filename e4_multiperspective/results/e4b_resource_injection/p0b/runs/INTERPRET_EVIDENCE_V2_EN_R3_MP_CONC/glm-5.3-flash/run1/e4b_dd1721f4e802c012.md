### Observed differences

- Resource assignment changed for seven activities — A01, A03, A07, A13, A14, A17 and A22. In the earlier window one set of resources dominated (for example, User_16 was the most frequent executor of A01, A03, A13, A14 and A17, at roughly 7–8 in 100 executions each); in the later window a largely different set took over (for example, User_15 became the most frequent executor of A01 and A03, at about 6 in 100), while the earlier leaders lost share or dropped out of the top ranks. The profile differences are large (0.38–0.60 on a 0–1 dissimilarity scale), and each exceeds all but about 1 in 2000 random re-splits of the pooled data; the profiles rest on roughly 129–256 observations per window. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, ASSIGN007]
- A07 shows the sharpest shift: earlier it was spread across 17 distinct resources, with the busiest handling about 18 in 100 executions (concentration index about 0.09); later, all 123 observed executions went to a single resource, User_36 (top share 100 in 100, concentration index 1.0). [ASSIGN003, CONC001]
- Beyond the swap of named resources, assignment also became somewhat more dispersed: the share of executions handled by resources outside the top ten rose in most of these activities (for A13 and A14 from about 48 in 100 to about 56 in 100; for A17 from about 46 to about 56). A13 and A14 show identical profiles, so they likely reflect one underlying behaviour rather than two independent changes. [ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006]
- No control-flow or timing indicator qualified: 0 of 36 control-flow units and 0 of 31 time units, in line with the roughly 0.18 and 0.015 items expected by chance; all 8 qualifying items came from the resource family, where only about 0.004 were expected for an unchanged process. [screening]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that resource allocation changed: the activities A01, A03, A07, A13, A14, A17 and A22, which in the earlier window were executed mainly by one group of resources, were in the later window executed mainly by a largely different group, and A07 additionally became concentrated on the single resource User_36 after previously being shared across about 17 resources. Supporting signs: the resource family produced 8 qualifying items where an unchanged process would produce about 0.004, a count chance cannot explain as a whole; the direction is consistent across activities, with the same former resources losing share everywhere and the same new ones gaining; the A07 change is measured directly (17 distinct resources down to 1, top share from about 18 in 100 to all executions); and no control-flow or timing items qualified, consistent with a change confined to allocation. Limitations: the resource codes are anonymous, so the evidence cannot distinguish a change in assignment rules from a change in which resources were available or active in each window; and because A13 and A14 show identical profiles, the seven activities are not fully independent confirmations. Other differences: none were observed — control flow and timing showed no qualifying differences at the configured thresholds. [screening]
