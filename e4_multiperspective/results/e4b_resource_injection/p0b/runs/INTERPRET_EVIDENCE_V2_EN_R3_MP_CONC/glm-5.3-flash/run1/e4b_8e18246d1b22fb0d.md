### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The resource family produced 8 qualifying items where fewer than 0.01 would be expected if nothing had changed — far beyond what an unchanged process typically yields — while the control-flow and time families produced none (0 against 0.17 and 0.014 expected) [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, ASSIGN007, CONC001].

The evidence supports the possibility that resource allocation changed in two ways. First, for A01, A03, A13, A14, A17 and A22, assignments moved away from a group of resources that previously held the largest shares (including User_11 and User_4) toward a largely different group, with work spread more evenly and the "other" share rising. Second, A10, previously spread across 34 resources, became concentrated entirely on User_126. Supporting signs: the change is shown directly by the assignment and concentration profiles of each activity; the direction is the same across all six redistribution activities (the same resources lose share, the same largely disjoint set gains); participation volumes are stable, so the shift is not an artefact of the activities running more or less often; and every difference exceeds about 999.5 of 1,000 random re-splits. Limitations: A13 and A14 show identical before and after profiles, so they most likely reflect one underlying allocation pattern rather than two independent confirmations; A22 rests on fewer observations (112 vs 109) than the other activities; the resource codes are anonymous, so the groups can only be described by their codes; and the evidence cannot show whether the reallocation was one coordinated change or independent per-activity shifts. An important competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that the resource mix shifted because the mix of cases reaching these activities changed (with different cases assigned differently), rather than because assignment behaviour itself changed.

Other differences: none — the eight qualifying items all concern resource allocation, and no control-flow or timing differences passed the screening thresholds [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, ASSIGN007, CONC001].
