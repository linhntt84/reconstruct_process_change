### Observed differences

- Work on A01, A03 and A22 moved between two different groups of resources. In the earlier window the most frequent assignee handled about 9–12 in 100 executions (User_10 on A01 and A03, User_49 on A22), with roughly 40–50 in 100 of assignments going to a long tail of other resources. In the later window, User_19 is the most frequent assignee on all three activities (about 7 in 100 on A01, about 6 in 100 on A03, about 9 in 100 on A22), User_10's share fell to about 5 in 100, and User_49 no longer ranks among the ten most frequent assignees on any of the three. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN004]
- The gains and losses are consistent across activities: resources that lost share on A01 also lost on A03 and A22 (typically by about 4–7 in 100, and about 12 in 100 for User_49 on A22), while a set of resources that previously handled little of this work gained similar amounts on all three. The A22 profile rests on fewer observations (107 before, 110 after) than A01 and A03 (about 200 each). [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN004]
- A13 shows the starkest change: in the earlier window its executions were spread across 44 distinct resources, with the most frequent (User_10) handling about 8 in 100 and a concentration index of about 0.03; in the later window all 259 observed A13 executions were assigned to User_10 alone (top-resource share 100 in 100, one distinct resource, concentration index 1.0). [ASSIGN003, CONC001]
- All five qualified differences are larger than in all but about 1 in 2000 random re-splits of the pooled windows, and they are confined to resource allocation — no control-flow or timing difference passed the screening thresholds. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, CONC001]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that resource allocation changed. In the later window, executions of A01, A03 and A22 that were previously led by one group of resources are led by a largely different group headed by User_19, with the same resources gaining and losing across all three activities; in addition, A13, previously spread across 44 resources, is assigned exclusively to User_10. This reading is backed by the screening balance: the resource family produced 5 qualified items where about 0.006 would be expected if nothing had changed, while control flow (0 of 35 units against about 0.17 expected) and timing (0 of 31 against about 0.015 expected) produced none, every item's difference exceeds all but about 1 in 2000 random re-splits, and the ASSIGN and CONC indicators measure this behaviour directly. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, CONC001]

Limitations: ASSIGN003 and CONC001 both describe the A13 change and are not independent confirmations of it; roughly 40–55 in 100 of assignments in both windows sit in an unlabelled remainder beyond the ten most frequent resources, so the full extent of the shift is not visible; and the evidence cannot rule out that the shifted profiles reflect a different mix of cases or of active resources in the later window rather than a reallocation of the same work.
