### Observed differences

- Resource assignments were reallocated on five activities (A01, A03, A13, A14, A17). In the earlier window, one resource (User_10) held roughly 7–9 in 100 of the assignments of each of these activities; in the later window it no longer receives any observed assignments for them, while several resources that were absent or marginal earlier now hold roughly 3–8 in 100 each (the largest, User_41, about 7–8 in 100). Some incumbent resources keep or slightly grow their shares. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN006]
- Activity A16 moved from shared to single-resource handling: earlier it was spread across about 12 resources with the largest share about 18 in 100; later all observed assignments went to one resource (User_3), and the concentration index rose from about 0.12 to 1.0. Its participation was similar in both windows (about 150 vs 141 traces out of 200). [ASSIGN005, CONC001]
- The elapsed time between the consecutive completions of A04 and A01 fell markedly: median about 25 hours earlier vs about 13 hours later, mean about 43 vs 15 hours; the pair occurs in essentially all traces in both windows (200 of 200). [TWAIT001]
- For A09 followed by A23, the elapsed time between consecutive completions fell from a median of about 9 hours to about half an hour (mean about 13 vs 3 hours); the pair was also observed in fewer traces, about 20 in 100 earlier vs about 13 in 100 later. [TWAIT002]
- No control-flow difference qualified (0 items among 34 units tested, against about 0.17 expected by chance), so the detected differences are confined to timing and resource allocation; every qualifying item exceeded all but about 5 in 10,000 random re-splits of the pooled data. ### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that resource allocation was changed: the work on A01, A03, A13, A14, A16 and A17 shifted from the resources that performed it earlier to a largely different set of resources, and A16 in particular became handled exclusively by a single resource (User_3) after being shared across about a dozen. The resource family produced 7 qualifying items where about 0.005 would be expected without a change, the direction of the shift is the same on every affected activity, and two indicators (assignment shares and concentration) agree on A16. Participation in the activities themselves barely moved (for example A01 in 200 of 200 traces in both windows), so the change concerns who performs the work rather than whether the activities run. Limitations: A13 and A14 show identical profiles, so their two items likely reflect one underlying shift rather than two independent ones, and the evidence cannot rule out that part of the pattern stems from a change in how resources were recorded or attributed rather than from a genuine reallocation of work. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, CONC001]

Other differences: a consistent timing decrease that stands as a hypothesis in its own right — the elapsed time between consecutive completions fell for A04→A01 (median about 25 to 13 hours) and for A09→A23 (about 9 hours to about half an hour), with 2 time items against about 0.02 expected by chance. The evidence cannot show whether this decrease is connected to the reallocation or independent of it. The A09→A23 pair also appeared in fewer traces (40 vs 25 of 200), but with so few observations this is weak, and no control-flow item qualified to corroborate it. [TWAIT001, TWAIT002]
