### Observed differences

- Resource allocation changed for seven activities (A01, A03, A07, A13, A14, A17, A22). In the earlier window, each was handled mainly by one group of resources, led by a single resource (User_32) with about 11–16 in 100 assignments per activity and a second member of the same group at about 6–8 in 100. In the later window, a largely different and more even group took over: no member exceeds about 11 in 100, and User_39, one of its most consistent members, holds about 5–9 in 100, while the previously dominant resources no longer appear among the frequent assignees for these activities. Profile dissimilarity is 0.40–0.66 per activity, each more extreme than about 1999 of 2000 random re-splits, with roughly 85–260 observations per window behind each profile [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, ASSIGN007].
- The replacement is partial: a couple of resources keep or raise their shares across both windows, with User_39 rising from about 4 in 100 to about 5–9 in 100 on the activities where it appears in both [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN007].
- A07 shows the sharpest shift: in the earlier window its work was spread over about 14 distinct resources, the most frequent taking about 23 in 100 (concentration index 0.11); in the later window a single resource (User_52) handled all of it (top share 1.0, concentration index 1.0). The assignment and concentration items describe this one underlying change, not two independent ones [ASSIGN003, CONC001].
- The control-flow and timing families produced no qualifying items (0 of 35 and 0 of 32 units, against about 0.17 and 0.016 expected by chance), so no structural or timing difference appears at these thresholds [screening].

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that resource allocation changed while process structure and timing stayed as they were: in the later window, work on seven activities (A01, A03, A07, A13, A14, A17, A22) was reallocated from a group of resources formerly led by User_32 (about 11–16 in 100 assignments per activity) to a largely different, more even group in which no resource exceeds about 11 in 100 (User_39 among the most frequent at about 5–9 in 100), and A07 moved from about 14 sharing resources to being handled exclusively by User_52 [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, ASSIGN007, CONC001].

Supporting signs:
- Directness: the assignment and concentration indicators measure allocation itself, and all seven activities shifted in the same direction with sizes 0.40–1.0, each beyond about 1999 of 2000 random re-splits [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, ASSIGN007, CONC001].
- Consistency: the same before-group and after-group recur across all seven activities, and the A13 and A14 profiles are identical, which fits one coordinated reallocation rather than independent per-activity noise [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, ASSIGN007].
- Screening: the resource family produced 8 items where about 0.004 would be expected if nothing had changed — roughly two thousand times the chance expectation — so the pattern as a whole cannot be explained by sampling variation [screening].

Limitations and competing explanation: the resource codes are anonymous, so no roles or organisational meaning can be attached to the two groups. The profiles cover only these seven activities, so the evidence cannot distinguish a deliberate reallocation of this work from the earlier resources simply becoming unavailable for it; the persistence of a couple of resources across both windows shows the turnover was partial, not total [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN007].

Other differences: none — control-flow and timing indicators showed no qualifying differences at the configured thresholds [screening].
