### Observed differences

- The main difference is in resource allocation. At six activities — A01, A03, A13, A14, A17 and A22 — the resources performing the work changed markedly. In the earlier window, a stable group of resources dominated each of these activities, with User_94 the single largest performer (about 10 in 100 assignments at A01, A03, A13, A14 and A17, and about 16 in 100 at A22). In the later window, User_94 no longer appears among the main performers at any of them, and a largely different group took over: at A01, User_79 and User_88 rose from under about 3 in 100 each to about 7 and 6 in 100, and at A22 from unlisted to about 8 in 100 each. ### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

A change in resource allocation is supported: at activities A01, A03, A13, A14, A17 and A22, assignments moved from a group of resources that dominated the earlier window (including User_94, with about 10–16 in 100 assignments) to a largely different group in the later window (including User_79 and User_88, previously minor or unlisted), with the same losing and gaining groups recurring across all six activities. The resource family produced 6 qualifying items where about 0.006 would be expected if nothing had changed, so the pattern as a whole is unlikely under sampling variation; the control-flow family produced no items (0 against about 0.19 expected), so no structural change accompanies the redistribution in these windows. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006]

Limitations: A13 and A14 show identical profiles, so they likely reflect one underlying behaviour rather than two independent confirmations. The evidence also cannot rule out that the shift in shares stems from a changed mix of cases reaching these activities rather than from assignment behaviour itself, since case mix is only partially visible through the control-flow indicators; and the resource codes are anonymous, so no roles or groups beyond the observed shares can be inferred. [ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004]

Other differences: the elapsed time between consecutive completions of A09 and A23 lengthened (median about 1.3 hours to about 12.6 hours, on 27 vs 17 intervals). This rests on a single time item in a family where about 0.015 items are expected without change, which is the kind of difference an unchanged process can still produce, so it is not treated as support for a timing change.
