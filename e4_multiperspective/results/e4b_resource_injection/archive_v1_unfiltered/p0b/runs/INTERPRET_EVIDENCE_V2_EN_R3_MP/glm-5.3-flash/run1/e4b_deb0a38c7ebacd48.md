### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that, in the later window, the handling of activities A01, A03, A13, A14, A17 and A22 was reallocated from one group of resources to another: resources that previously each carried roughly 5–10 assignments in 100 — including User_18 — withdrew from the top contributors, a new group including User_41 took on comparable shares, and User_27 became the dominant handler of A13, A14 and A17 (about 18–19 in 100, up from about 5 in 100). The support is direct, since the indicator measures exactly these allocation shares, and consistent, since the same resources decline and the same resources rise across all six activities; 6 qualifying resource items against a chance expectation of about 0.003 is far beyond what an unchanged process typically produces, and each shift is larger than about 999.5 of 1,000 random re-splits. Limitations: the profiles show shares, not the mechanism — the evidence cannot distinguish a change in allocation rules from a change in which resources were available or in the mix of cases handled; A13 and A14 have identical profiles, so the six items are not six independent differences; and the anonymous codes allow no statement about roles or teams. No control-flow difference qualified at the configured thresholds, so the hypothesis is confined to resource allocation and implies no structural change.
