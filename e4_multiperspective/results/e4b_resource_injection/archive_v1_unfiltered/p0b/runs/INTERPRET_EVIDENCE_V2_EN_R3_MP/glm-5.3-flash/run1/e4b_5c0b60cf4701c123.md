### Observed differences

- In the later window, work on six activities (A01, A03, A13, A14, A17, A22) was assigned to a largely different set of resources. Earlier, one resource (User_94) held the largest share of every one of these activities — about 10–16 in 100 assignments — and a handful of other resources held roughly 4–9 in 100 each. Later, User_94 and most of that group no longer appear among the ten largest shares (each below roughly 3–4 in 100), while resources that were previously absent or marginal took over, each reaching about 4–11 in 100 (for example User_79 and User_88 at about 5–8 in 100). The assignment-profile difference per activity was 0.36–0.46 on a 0–1 dissimilarity scale, each larger than in more than 999 of 1,000 random re-splits. ### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that, in the later window, the assignment of work for activities A01, A03, A13, A14, A17 and A22 was reallocated from the group of resources that previously held the largest shares (including User_94) to a largely different group (including User_79 and User_88), while the volume of assignments per activity stayed roughly unchanged. The resource family produced 6 qualifying items where about 0.004 were expected without a change — far beyond what chance produces — and all six activities show the same direction of shift, with each profile resting on roughly 120–260 assignments per window. No control-flow item qualified (0 against about 0.19 expected), so no structural change accompanies the reallocation. Limitations: the profiles rank only the ten largest resources and pool the rest, so resources that lost share may keep small, unlisted shares; A13 and A14 show identical profiles, so the six items partly repeat one underlying difference; and only these six activities are profiled. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006]

A competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is that the resources that lost share reduced their participation across the whole process rather than being released from these six activities specifically, since the profiles cover only assignments to these activities. Other differences: the elapsed time between consecutive completions of A09 and A23 increased (median about 1.3 to about 12.6 hours; 27 vs 17 intervals). On its own this does not establish a timing change: the time family produced 1 qualifying item where about 0.015 were expected without a change, and a single item is within what an unchanged process typically produces; the intervals also rest on few observations.
