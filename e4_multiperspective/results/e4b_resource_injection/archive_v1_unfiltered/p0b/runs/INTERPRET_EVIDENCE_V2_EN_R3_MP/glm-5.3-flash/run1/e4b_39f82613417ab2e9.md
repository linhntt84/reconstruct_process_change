### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility of a change in resource allocation: in the later window, the work on six activities — A01, A03, A13, A14, A17 and A22 — is assigned to a largely different group of resources than before. Earlier, one stable group led every activity (top individual shares about 6–12%, led by User_18); later, User_35 and User_15 — absent from the earlier top lists — hold the largest shares (about 9–11% and 8–9%), while the previous leaders shrink to about 4–6% or leave the top lists. The hypothesis is direct (the indicator measures allocation itself), consistent (the same direction in all six activities, each beyond about 1999 of 2000 random re-splits), and the resource family produced six qualifying items where about 0.003 were expected without change, with no qualifying control-flow item — so the difference is in who executes the work, not in structure at these thresholds. Limitations: the identifiers are anonymous codes, so nothing can be said about what the groups represent; A13 and A14 share identical profiles and are not independent confirmations; and a change in the mix of cases reaching these activities (different case types routed to different resource pools) could produce the same pattern — the evidence cannot separate a reallocation from such a case-mix change.
