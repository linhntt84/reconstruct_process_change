### Observed differences

- Work on A01, A03, A13, A14, A17 and A22 was reallocated between resources. In the earlier window, assignments were diffuse: no single resource held more than about 8–9 in 100 assignments on any of these activities, and a long tail of other resources covered roughly 53–55 in 100. In the later window, a small group dominates: on A01 one resource (User_1) took about 57 in 100 assignments after being absent from the leading shares, and on the other five activities a consistent group led by User_19 (about 10–11 in 100) and User_28 (about 7–8 in 100) took the largest shares, while the long tail shrank to about 14–45 in 100. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006]
- Resources that were among the most frequent assignees in the earlier window (each about 4–9 in 100) barely appear in the later window's shares. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006]
- The elapsed time between consecutive completions of A04 and A01 roughly doubled: the median rose from about 24 h to about 40 h and the mean from about 27 h to about 51 h, with the upper quarter of cases now reaching about 70 h instead of about 40 h. [TWAIT001]
- Two other inter-completion times shifted only on a seconds scale: A13→A14 went from about 2 seconds to about 1.5 seconds on average, and A17→A22 changed by fractions of a second; both are unusual for random splits but negligible in magnitude. [TWAIT002, TWAIT003]
- A06 appeared in about 31 of 200 traces (about 16%) in the earlier window and in about 12 of 200 (about 6%) in the later window; its mean occurrences per trace fell from about 0.16 to 0.06. [OCC001]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that resource allocation changed: in the later window, assignments for A01, A03, A13, A14, A17 and A22 shifted from a diffuse spread over many resources to concentration on a small group (User_1 on A01; User_19, User_28 and a few others on the remaining activities), largely replacing the previously frequent contributors. This rests on six resource items all pointing the same way, where about 0.003 would be expected by chance if nothing had changed, and on each difference being larger than essentially all reference comparisons. The identifiers are anonymous codes, so the evidence shows a reallocation between groups of resources but cannot distinguish a change in assignment rules from a change in workforce availability or case mix; that competing explanation remains open. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006]

Other differences: the time between A04 and A01 completions increased consistently (median from about 24 h to about 40 h) — a timing change in its own right, though the evidence cannot link it to the reallocation; A06 participation fell from about 16% to about 6% of traces, but as the single control-flow item (about 0.17 expected by chance) it does not on its own establish a structural change; the sub-second shifts for A13→A14 and A17→A22 are statistically flagged but practically negligible.
