### Observed differences

- All four qualifying indicators measure how the events of a single activity are divided among resources (activities A01, A03, A10 and A22). In the resource family, 4 items qualified where about 0.005 — roughly 1 in 200 — were expected if nothing had changed; the control-flow family (35 units) and the time family (31 units) produced no items, against expectations of 0.17 and 0.015 [screening].
- On A01, A03 and A22 the handlers changed substantially. In the earlier window each activity's work was spread broadly: a group of six to eight resources together handled about a third of A01's events, about a quarter of A03's and about half of A22's, including one resource (User_49) with roughly 6–12 in 100 events on each. In the later window these resources handle none of the observed events of these activities, and a largely new group of six to eight resources takes their place, led on all three activities by one resource (User_19) with about 6–9 in 100 events. One resource carries over on all three activities but at about half its earlier share (about 9–10 in 100 before, about 5 in 100 after). Each of the three differences was larger than in about 1,999 of 2,000 random re-splits; the profiles rest on 200 of 200 traces before and after for A01, 200 and 197 for A03, and 107 and 110 traces (about half) for A22 [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN004].
- Activity A10 moved from shared to single-resource handling. Before, its events were spread over about ten resources — the largest (User_113) with about 15 in 100 and five others with 9–13 in 100 each; in the later window User_113 handled every observed A10 event and the previously involved resources none. The profile rests on 247 observations before and 254 after, and the difference was larger than in about 1,999 of 2,000 random re-splits [ASSIGN003].
- The reallocation is consistent across activities: the resource that loses work does so on all four activities, and the new leading resource gains on A01, A03 and A22 alike, which points to a coordinated shift rather than an isolated change on one activity [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004].

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the later window reallocated work among resources: on A01, A03 and A22, events moved from a group of previously frequent resources (including User_49) to a largely different group led by User_19 — roughly a third of A01's events, a quarter of A03's and half of A22's moved between the groups — and on A10, work previously shared by about ten resources became concentrated entirely on User_113. The support is direct (the indicator measures each activity's allocation profile), consistent in direction across all four activities, and far beyond what chance produces: 4 resource items qualified where about 0.005 were expected without change, and each difference exceeded about 1,999 of 2,000 random re-splits. No control-flow or timing indicator qualified, so the change is confined to resource allocation [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, screening].

The evidence cannot distinguish a reassignment specific to these four activities from a broader turnover in which one set of resource codes stopped appearing and a new set started across the whole process; it also leaves about 40–55 in 100 events per activity in an unitemised "other" bucket in both windows, so any turnover within that tail is not visible [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004].

Other differences: none — no control-flow or timing evidence qualified at the configured thresholds [screening].
