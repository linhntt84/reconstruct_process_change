### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that resource allocation changed. On A01, A03, A13, A14, A17, execution shifted from one group of resources that handled these activities earlier and largely stopped appearing afterwards (including User_10 at about 8–9 in 100 each) to a largely different group that took over similar shares (including User_41 at about 7–8 in 100); in addition, A11 changed from being shared across about ten resources to being executed only by User_31. This rests on six qualifying assignment indicators where the resource family expected about 0.004 by chance, on the same incoming and outgoing resource groups recurring across all five swap activities, and on every difference being larger than in all but about 1 in 2000 random re-splits. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006]

Limitations: A13 and A14 show identical profiles, so those two items likely describe the same underlying behaviour rather than independent confirmations; the evidence cannot show whether the five activity-level swaps and the A11 concentration stem from one coordinated reassignment or from separate per-activity changes, and it cannot say why allocation changed.
