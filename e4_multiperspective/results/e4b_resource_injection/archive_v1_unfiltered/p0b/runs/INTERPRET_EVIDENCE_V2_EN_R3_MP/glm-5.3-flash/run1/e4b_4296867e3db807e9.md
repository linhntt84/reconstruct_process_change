### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a change in resource allocation as a possibility: in the later window, assignment work on A01, A03, A13, A14, A17 and A22 was reallocated from a former group of resources, previously led by User_32 with per-activity shares of roughly 11–16%, to a largely different group that includes User_23, and A01 moved from a spread pattern to being handled entirely by User_47. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, screening]

Supporting signs: the resource family produced six qualifying items against a chance expectation of about 0.003, control flow and timing produced none, all six items point in the same direction (former group out, new group in), and each difference exceeds about 1999 of 2000 random re-splits. [screening, ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006]

Limitations: the resource codes are anonymous, so the evidence shows who executed the work but not why; an important competing explanation it cannot rule out is that the case mix itself changed — different kinds of cases reaching different resources — rather than a reallocation of the same work. A13 and A14 share one profile, so the six items are not fully independent, and A22, the largest shift, has the smallest support (about 125 and 113 observations). [ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN006]
