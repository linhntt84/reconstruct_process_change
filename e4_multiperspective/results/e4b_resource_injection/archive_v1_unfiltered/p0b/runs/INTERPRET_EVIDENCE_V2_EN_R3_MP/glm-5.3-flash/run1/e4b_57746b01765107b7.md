### Observed differences

- Resource allocation changed on six activities. On A01, assignments in the earlier window were spread thinly: the ten most frequent resources each executed roughly 4–8 in 100 assignments, and about 46 in 100 went to resources outside that top group. In the later window, a single resource, User_1, executed about 45 in 100 assignments without having appeared among the ten most frequent resources before, and the outside-group share fell to about 21 in 100. [ASSIGN001]
- On A03, A13, A14, A17 and A21 the same turnover appears in a milder form. Earlier, the most frequent resources formed one group, each taking roughly 3–9 in 100 executions per activity (for example User_49 at about 7–8 in 100). In the later window those resources fell to negligible shares or disappeared, while a new group led by User_67 (about 9–14 in 100) and a second code that was previously minor or absent (about 6–12 in 100) became the most frequent; two resources continued at roughly their earlier shares of about 5–7 in 100. [ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006]
- A13 and A14 show identical allocation profiles and identical observation counts (267 vs 270 events) in both windows, so these two items most likely describe one underlying behaviour rather than two independent changes. [ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004]
- All six allocation differences are larger than about 9995 in 10000 random re-splits of the pooled data, and they rest on substantial samples (about 135–270 assignment events per activity per window; 200 for A01 and about 198–200 for A03). [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006]
- Screening context: control flow produced no qualifying items (about 0.16 expected without change), the time family produced 1 (about 0.015 expected), and the resource family produced 6 (about 0.004 expected) — the resource count is far beyond what an unchanged process would typically produce. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, TWAIT001]
- Elapsed time between consecutive completion events in the A05/A09 unit: earlier, 120 intervals with a mean of about 1.1×10⁻⁵ hours; later, 113 intervals with a mean about eight times higher, almost entirely from a heavier upper tail (upper quartile up about nineteenfold, from about 1.1×10⁻⁵ to 2.2×10⁻⁴ hours) while the median stayed nearly unchanged (about 1.0×10⁻⁵ to 1.1×10⁻⁵ hours). [TWAIT001]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that execution of A01, A03, A13, A14, A17 and A21 was reallocated between groups of resources: in the earlier window each activity was carried out by a broad mix in which the most frequent resources (including User_49) each took roughly 3–9 in 100 executions, while in the later window a largely different group took over — on A01 one resource (User_1) alone executed about 45 in 100 assignments, and on the other activities User_67 and a second previously minor code became the most frequent resources as the earlier frequent ones fell to negligible shares. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006]

Supporting signs: the resource family produced 6 qualifying items against roughly 0.004 expected without a change, a gap chance cannot explain as a whole; all six point in the same direction, with the same new codes rising and the same old codes falling across several activities, which independent sampling noise would not produce; each difference exceeds about 9995 in 10000 random re-splits; and the observation bases are large (about 135–270 events per activity per window). [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006]

Limitations: the six items likely describe one underlying reallocation rather than six independent changes (A13 and A14 are indistinguishable), and the data show only which resources executed which activities — they cannot distinguish a change in assignment behaviour from a change in which resources were available, and any accompanying structural change would have stayed below the control-flow threshold (no items against about 0.16 expected). [ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004]

Other differences: Completion-to-completion times around A05/A09 developed a heavier upper tail in the later window (mean about eight times higher, upper quartile about nineteen times higher, median nearly unchanged). This is a single time-family item where about 0.015 were expected without change — within what an unchanged process typically produces — so it does not by itself support a timing change, and the evidence cannot show whether it is connected to the reallocation or is a chance fluctuation. [TWAIT001]
