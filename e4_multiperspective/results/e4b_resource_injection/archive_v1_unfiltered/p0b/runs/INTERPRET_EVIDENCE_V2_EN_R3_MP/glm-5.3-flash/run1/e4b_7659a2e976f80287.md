### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that, in the later window, A22 is executed more often on the way from A17 to A03: cases that previously passed from A17 directly on to A03 now more often go from A17 to A22 first, with A03 following one position later. Three control-flow findings agree: A22's participation rose from about 43 to about 62 in 100 traces; A17's outgoing transitions shifted from A03 towards A22 (about 45 down to 31 in 100, versus about 34 up to 51 in 100); and the A17–A03 distance moved from mostly 1 to mostly 2. The sizes also line up: the gain in A17→A22 transitions (about 38) is close to the loss of direct A17→A03 transitions (about 39) and to the number of A17–A03 pairs moving from distance 1 to distance 2 (about 33). All activities involved are well observed in both windows (A22 in 86 and 123 traces; about 195–197 A17–A03 pairs per window), so this is a shift between existing paths rather than an activity appearing or disappearing; each difference is larger than in about 995 of 1,000 random re-splits, and three control-flow items against roughly 0.19 expected by chance in that family makes sampling variation alone unlikely. [OCC001, DIR001, LAG001]

Two limitations remain: the three items describe one underlying trace behaviour rather than three independent confirmations, and the distance data show that an extra position now lies between A17 and A03 without identifying its occupant — the attribution to A22 rests on the routing shift at A17, whose gain is the only one large enough to account for the change. The evidence also cannot show whether the timing and allocation differences below are connected to this control-flow difference. [OCC001, DIR001, LAG001]

Other differences: the elapsed time between the completions of A09 and A23 dropped from a median of about 18 hours to about 0.4 hours — a timing change in its own right, but one resting on only 24 and 21 observations, so it stays tentative; separately, allocation of A11 concentrated on a single resource (User_109) where it had been shared, and the mix of resources handling A01 was partially replaced with the pooled remainder growing — the evidence shows these as allocation changes but cannot explain them or rule out that they are unrelated to the control-flow difference.
