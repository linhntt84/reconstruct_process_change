### Observed differences

- **Work moved between groups of resources on six activities (A01, A03, A13, A14, A17, A22).** In the earlier window, one resource (User_94) handled about 10–16 in 100 assignments of each of these activities, and a stable group of roughly seven further resources covered another 25–35 in 100. In the later window, User_94 and most of that group fall to negligible shares or disappear from the most frequent resources, while a largely new group takes over: User_49 roughly doubled its share (for example about 3 to 7 in 100 on A01, and about 5 to 11 in 100 on A22), User_79 rose from unlisted to about 5–8 in 100, and several further newcomers appear. The remaining assignments also spread slightly more thinly (the "other" bucket grew from about 44–45 to 47–50 in 100 on five of the six activities). The profile dissimilarity is 0.36–0.46, and each difference is larger than all but about one in 2,000 random re-splits; note that A13 and A14 show identical profiles, so the six items are not fully independent. - **Longer elapsed time between consecutive completions of A09 and A23.** In the earlier window the median gap was about 1.3 hours (mean about 3.4 hours) over 27 observed gaps; in the later window the median is about 12.6 hours (mean about 15.4 hours) over 17 gaps. The increase sits in the upper half of the distribution (75th percentile about 4.4 vs 35.9 hours) while the lower quartile is similar (about 0.5 vs 1.2 hours), so a subset of gaps became much longer rather than all gaps shifting. This measures elapsed time between consecutive completion events, not pure waiting or processing time. [TWAIT001]

- **Screening context:** 7 items qualified in total against about 0.22 expected if nothing had changed; by family, resources produced 6 items (about 0.01 expected) and time 1 item (about 0.02 expected), while control flow produced none (about 0.19 expected), so no structural or routing difference passed the thresholds. ### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a reallocation of work between groups of resources for activities A01, A03, A13, A14, A17 and A22: in the later window, assignments shifted from the previously dominant group (User_94 and its fellow frequent resources) to a largely different group (User_49, User_79 and several newcomers). This rests on direct allocation evidence: six activity-level profiles moved in the same direction, each difference larger than all but about one in 2,000 random re-splits, on substantial observation counts (roughly 123–258 per activity per window), and the resource family yielded 6 items where about 0.01 would be expected by chance. Limitations: the evidence shows which resources gained and lost share, not the mechanism; it cannot separate a change in how work is assigned from a change in which resources were active in each period, and with no control-flow items qualified it implies no structural change. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006]

Other differences: The elapsed time between consecutive completions of A09 and A23 grew from a median of about 1.3 hours to about 12.6 hours, with fewer consecutive pairs observed (27 vs 17). This is a timing hypothesis in its own right, but it rests on only 17 observations in the later window, and the evidence cannot show whether it is connected to the reallocation (it involves different activities) or driven by a subset of unusually long gaps.
