### Observed differences

- Across A01, A03, A13, A14, A17 and A22, event assignments moved from one broad set of resources to another. In the earlier window each of these activities was spread thinly (largest single resource about 6.5–8.6 in 100 events, over half of events in a long tail); in the later window a group of previously marginal resources took over: User_19 went from no share to about 10–11 in 100 events on every one of these activities and User_28 from none to about 7 in 100, while User_10 fell from about 5–8.6 in 100 to about 3 in 100 or less and several formerly used resources dropped out of the leading positions. The long-tail share fell from over 50 in 100 events to roughly 36–45 in 100. Each per-activity profile shift is large (cosine dissimilarity about 0.31–0.41) and exceeds about 999.5 of 1,000 random re-splits. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006]
- Assignments on A01, A03 and A22 became more concentrated on fewer resources: distinct resources dropped from 43 to 36 (A01, A03) and from 37 to 29 (A22), with the top resource's share rising to about 9.5–11 in 100. A10 moved the opposite way, from 34 to 39 distinct resources with the top share falling from about 10.7 to 7.6 in 100. [CONC001, CONC002, CONC003, CONC004]
- The elapsed time between consecutive A04 and A01 completions increased: median from about 24 to about 40 hours, mean from about 27 to about 51 hours, upper quartile from about 40 to about 70 hours, with 200 observations in each window. [TWAIT001]
- A06 took part in fewer traces: about 31 in 200 traces (roughly 16 in 100) earlier versus about 12 in 200 (6 in 100) later, with no repeated occurrences in either window. [OCC001]
- Two further completion-to-completion gaps shifted only at sub-second scale (between A13 and A14 by under half a second; between A17 and A22 by a few hundredths of a second) — statistically flagged but practically negligible. [TWAIT002, TWAIT003]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a reallocation of work across resources: in the later window, the events of A01, A03, A13, A14, A17 and A22, previously fragmented over a wide pool, are handled by a different and somewhat smaller group of resources (User_19 and User_28 prominent among the gainers, User_10 and the former pool reduced), and on A01, A03 and A22 the remaining work is more concentrated on fewer resources. Supporting signs: the resource family produced 10 flagged items where about 0.011 would be expected without a change; all six assignment profiles shifted in the same direction with the same gaining and losing groups; the concentration results agree for three activities; and every item's difference exceeds about 999.5 of 1,000 random re-splits. Limitations: A13 and A14 show identical profiles and likely reflect one behaviour rather than two independent ones; the assignment and concentration items describe the same reallocation from two angles rather than confirming it independently; and the evidence cannot rule out that the case mix itself shifted — different kinds of cases that happen to be handled by different resources — rather than the allocation behaviour. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, CONC001, CONC002, CONC004]

Other differences: the A04–A01 completion-to-completion time roughly doubled (median about 24 to about 40 hours), a consistent timing change that is a hypothesis in its own right, although this evidence cannot show whether it is connected to the reallocation; and A06 participation fell from about 16 to about 6 in 100 traces, but the control-flow family yielded a single item where about 0.17 are expected by chance, so that difference is also compatible with sampling variation between two 200-trace windows. [TWAIT001, OCC001]
