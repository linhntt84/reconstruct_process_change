### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that resource allocation changed: in the later window, work on A01, A03, A13, A14, A17 and A22 moved from the group of resources that previously carried the largest shares (led by User_32) to a largely different group of resources, and A07 — previously shared across 14 resources — was handled by a single resource (User_52) in every observed case. This rests on direct assignment profiles for each activity, a screening yield far above chance (eight resource items where about 0.01 were expected), differences larger than in essentially all of 2,000 random re-splits, the same before- and after-groups recurring across activities, and the concentration measure independently confirming the A07 collapse from 14 resources to one. Limitations: the profiles show only the top ten resources plus a residual bucket still holding roughly 35–52% of assignments, so continued small involvement of the earlier group cannot be excluded, and A13/A14 have identical profiles and likely reflect one underlying behaviour rather than two. An important competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is a shift in the mix of cases (case types that the earlier group used to handle becoming more common) rather than an explicit reassignment; no control-flow or timing items qualified that could tie the allocation shift to a change in case paths.
