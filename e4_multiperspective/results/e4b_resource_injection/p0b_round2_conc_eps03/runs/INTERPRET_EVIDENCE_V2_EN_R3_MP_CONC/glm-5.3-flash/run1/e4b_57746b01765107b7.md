### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a change in resource allocation: in the later window, work on A01, A03, A13, A14, A17 and A21 was reassigned from the previously frequent group of resources to a new group led by User_67 and User_132, while several previously frequent resources such as User_49 fell to negligible shares. This rests on direct allocation evidence: the resource family produced six qualifying items where about 0.01 were expected without change — many times the chance level — all six move in the same direction (the same resources gain and the same lose in every activity), each difference exceeds about 999.5 in 1000 random re-splits, and the shares rest on roughly 135–270 assignments per window. By comparison, the time family yielded one item against about 0.02 expected and the control-flow family none against about 0.16 expected. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, TWAIT001]

Limitations: the resource codes are anonymous, so no roles, people or teams can be inferred; A13 and A14 duplicate each other, leaving five distinct activity profiles; and a shift in the mix of cases between the windows (case types that happen to be handled by different resources) could produce the same reallocation pattern without any change in allocation rules, which the evidence cannot rule out. [ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004]

Other differences: the single timing item — A05–A09 completion intervals with a lengthened upper tail — is the only time item, against about 0.02 expected by chance for that family, so it is within what an unchanged process can produce and does not by itself support a timing change. [TWAIT001]
