### Observed differences

- Activity A07 moved from widely shared execution to single-resource execution. In the earlier window its 141 recorded executions were spread across about 17 distinct resources, with the most frequent handling about 18 in 100 cases (concentration index about 0.09); in the later window all 123 recorded executions were assigned to one resource, User_36 (top-resource share 100 in 100, concentration index 1.0). [ASSIGN003, CONC001]

- On six further activities (A01, A03, A13, A14, A17, A22) the executing resources changed as a group. In the earlier window a consistent set of resources led assignment on all of them (for example User_16 with roughly 7 in 100 executions of A01, with companions at about 4–8 in 100); in the later window that set has largely receded or left the top handlers, and a largely different set took over (for example User_13 with about 5–9 in 100 executions). The share falling outside the ten most frequent resources rose from roughly 42–48 in 100 to roughly 45–56 in 100. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, ASSIGN007]

- The shifts are large: profile dissimilarity of 0.38–0.60 for the six activities and 1.0 for A07, each larger than all but about 1 in 2000 random re-splits of the pooled data; the profiles rest on roughly 120–260 observations per window. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, ASSIGN007, CONC001]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that resource allocation changed. In the later window, execution of A01, A03, A13, A14, A17 and A22 was reassigned from a previously dominant group of resources to a largely different group — resources such as User_16, which led the earlier profiles, recede sharply or disappear from the top handlers, while resources such as User_13 take over — and A07, previously shared across about 17 resources, came to be executed exclusively by User_36. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, ASSIGN007, CONC001]

Supporting signs: the resource family produced 8 qualifying items where roughly 0.01 would be expected if nothing had changed — several hundred times the chance expectation — while the control-flow and timing families produced none; every item's difference exceeds all but about 1 in 2000 random re-splits; the direction is consistent, with the same resources receding and the same new group appearing across all six activities; and A07's concentration is corroborated by two indicators, the assignment profile and the concentration index. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, ASSIGN007, CONC001]

Limitations: the evidence shows only assignment shares, so it cannot show whether the incoming resources handle the same volume or mix of work; A13 and A14 have identical before and after profiles and likely reflect one underlying behaviour rather than two independent confirmations. [ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005]

An important competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is a shift in the mix of cases between the windows — different kinds of cases may naturally be assigned to different resources — or a re-labelling of the anonymous resource codes; the assignment-share evidence alone cannot separate these from a genuine reallocation. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, ASSIGN007, CONC001]
