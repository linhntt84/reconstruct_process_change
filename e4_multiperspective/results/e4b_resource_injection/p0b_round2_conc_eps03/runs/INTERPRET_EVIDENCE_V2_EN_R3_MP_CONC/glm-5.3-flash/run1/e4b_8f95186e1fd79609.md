### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that resource allocation changed for activities A01, A13 and A22: in the later window, executions of these three activities were assigned to a largely different set of resources, with A13 moving from a pool of about 44 resources to exclusive handling by User_10. This rests on direct allocation evidence: the resource family produced four qualifying items where about 0.01 would be expected by chance if nothing had changed, while no control-flow or timing item qualified; every item's difference is larger than in all but about 1 of 2,000 random re-splits of the pooled data; the two A13 items describe one and the same shift from complementary angles (assignment profile and concentration, on about 270 versus 260 executions), so they corroborate but do not independently confirm each other; and the A01 and A22 profiles rest on 200 and roughly 110 observations per window respectively. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, CONC001]

The evidence cannot show why the allocation changed, and because the resource codes are anonymous it cannot rule out that the pool of active resources itself changed composition (new codes replacing old ones) rather than assignment behaviour changing within a stable pool; for A01 and A22 the change is a broad redistribution across many resources, so no single reassignment can be pinpointed. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, CONC001]
