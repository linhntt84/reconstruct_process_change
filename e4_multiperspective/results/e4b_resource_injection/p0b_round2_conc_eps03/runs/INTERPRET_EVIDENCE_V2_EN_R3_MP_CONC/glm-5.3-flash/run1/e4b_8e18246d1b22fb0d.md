### Observed differences

- For six activities — A01, A03, A13, A14, A17 and A22 — the resources carrying out the work changed markedly. In the earlier window, each activity was handled by a mix in which a few recurring codes stood out: on A01, User_11 took about 11 in 100 assignments, and on A22 about 16 in 100. In the later window, User_11 fell to essentially none on both, while a largely different group took over: User_56, which earlier handled essentially none of this work, took about 6–7 in 100 of A01's assignments and about 12 in 100 of A22's. The same losing and gaining groups recur across all six activities. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, ASSIGN007]
- Alongside this swap, the work on these activities became less concentrated: on A01 the single most used resource's share fell from about 11 in 100 to about 7 in 100 while distinct resources rose from 34 to 42; A03 moved the same way (about 11 to 7 in 100; 35 to 42 distinct resources), as did A22 (about 16 to 12 in 100; 26 to 28). - A10 moved in the opposite direction. Earlier, its assignments were spread over roughly ten resources, with the largest, User_126, taking about 17 in 100; in the later window every observed A10 assignment went to User_126 alone, and distinct resources fell from 34 to 1. [ASSIGN003, CONC003]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the allocation of work to resources changed. In the later window, execution of A01, A03, A13, A14, A17 and A22 shifted from a former group of frequently used resources to a largely different group and became spread over more distinct resources (on A01, User_11 fell from about 11 in 100 assignments to essentially none while User_56 rose from essentially none to about 6–7 in 100, and distinct resources rose from 34 to 42); A10, by contrast, became handled by User_126 alone, which had already been its most used resource before. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, ASSIGN007, CONC001, CONC002, CONC003, CONC004]

The screening comparison makes chance an unlikely explanation: the resource family produced 11 qualifying items where about 0.01 were expected without change (23 units tested) — roughly a thousand times that expectation — while the control-flow family (0 items against about 0.17 expected) and the time family (0 against about 0.014) produced none, so the flagged differences are confined to allocation. Assignment and concentration items agree in direction for every activity, and the same codes lose and gain work across activities. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, ASSIGN007, CONC001, CONC002, CONC003, CONC004]

Limitations: the codes are anonymous, so the evidence shows which codes gained or lost work but not what they represent, and it cannot distinguish a change in how work is assigned from a change in which resources were available to these activities. A13 and A14 also show identical profiles, so their two items likely describe one underlying behaviour rather than two independent confirmations. [ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005]

Other differences: none — the qualifying items are exclusively resource-allocation items; no control-flow or timing evidence qualified. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, ASSIGN007, CONC001, CONC002, CONC003, CONC004]
