### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that resource allocation changed: across A01, A03, A13, A14, A17, A22 and A10, work moved from a group of previously dominant resources to a largely different group and became less concentrated. In the earlier window a stable set of resources (for example User_3 and User_8) held the largest assignment shares; in the later window their shares dropped to near zero, previously minor resources (for example User_49) took over, distinct resources per activity increased (A10: 24 to 51), and concentration fell (A10: 0.11 to 0.04). Supporting signs: the resource family produced 8 qualifying items where about 0.01 were expected for an unchanged process — a count chance cannot explain; all six assignment profiles and both concentration measures point the same way; each difference exceeds roughly 999 of 1000 random re-splits of the pooled data; and the profiles rest on substantial observation counts (about 200 versus 200 for A01, 119 versus 123 for A22, roughly 260–320 versus 260–285 for the others). Limitations: the resource codes are anonymous, so no roles, people or teams can be inferred; A13 and A14 show identical profiles and represent one underlying difference rather than two independent confirmations; and the evidence shows that allocation changed, not what drove it. A competing explanation the evidence cannot fully rule out is a shift in case mix between the windows (for example, somewhat fewer traces contain A13/A14/A17 afterwards), which could contribute to the reassignment, although the breadth and consistency of the turnover across activities makes a pure sampling artefact unlikely. Other differences: the routing out of A15 shifted toward A24 (about 50% to 87% of observed transitions, with A21 no longer observed); as the single control-flow item, based on only about 22–23 transitions and against roughly 0.18 items expected by chance in that family, it is within what an unchanged process typically produces and does not by itself support a structural change.
