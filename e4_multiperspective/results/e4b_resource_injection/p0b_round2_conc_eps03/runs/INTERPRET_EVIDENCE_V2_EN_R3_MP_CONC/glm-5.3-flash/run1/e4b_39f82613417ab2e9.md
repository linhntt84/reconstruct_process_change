### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that work was reallocated across resources. On A01, A03, A13, A14, A17 and A22, assignments moved from a previously dominant group of resources to a largely different one — User_35 and User_15 rose from little or no share to roughly 8–11 assignments in 100 each, while User_18 fell from about 10–11 to about 4–5 in 100 — and on A08 and A11 execution concentrated onto fewer resources, with the top resource's share roughly doubling. This is unlikely to be a sampling artefact: the resource family produced eight qualifying items where about 0.01 would be expected without change, the same gainers and losers recur across all six assignment profiles, and every difference is larger than in about 999.5 of 1,000 random re-splits. Participation counts per activity are similar in both windows (for example, 263 supporting observations before and after for A13 and A14) and no control-flow items qualified, so the change lies in who performs the activities, not in whether or how often they run. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, CONC001, CONC002]

Limitations: the resource codes are anonymous, so no roles, people or teams can be inferred; ASSIGN003/ASSIGN004 and CONC001/CONC002 carry identical profiles and are not independent confirmations. An important competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is a change in the resource pool itself — new resources absorbing work as others withdraw — rather than an active reassignment of tasks; both would produce the same assignment profiles. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, ASSIGN004, ASSIGN005, ASSIGN006, CONC001, CONC002]

Other differences: the elapsed time between consecutive completions of A04 and A01 roughly halved on average (about 48 to 24 hours; median about 32 to 25 hours). As the only time item, against roughly 0.02 expected by chance for the time family, it is within what an unchanged process occasionally produces on its own, and the evidence cannot establish whether it is connected to the reallocation or independent of it.
