### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the execution of A16, A01 and A03 was reallocated to a different set of resources: in the earlier window A16 was shared across roughly a dozen resources and A01 and A03 were led by User_10, while in the later window A16 is performed exclusively by User_3 and A01 and A03 are led by newly appearing codes such as User_41, with User_10 and most of the earlier set no longer observed. The resource family produced 4 evidence items where about 0.01 would be expected if nothing had changed; the same turnover pattern repeats on two activities; the concentration change on A16 is shown by two indicators (assignment shares and concentration index); and every item's difference exceeds about 9995 of 10,000 random re-splits of the pooled data. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002, ASSIGN003, CONC001]

Limitations: resource codes are anonymous, so the evidence cannot show whether the disappearing and appearing codes are genuinely different resources or re-labelled ones; several codes do persist across both windows, which argues against a wholesale re-labelling but does not rule it out, and no reason for the reallocation is visible in the evidence. [ASSIGN001, ASSIGN002]

Other differences: a consistent timing change. The elapsed time between consecutive completion events decreased for A04 then A01 (median about 25 hours to about 13, on the full sample in both windows) and for A09 then A23 (median about 9 hours to about half an hour, on 40 versus 25 observations); this indicator mixes waiting and processing time and is a timing hypothesis in its own right, and the evidence cannot show whether it is connected to the reallocation. The time family produced 2 items against about 0.02 expected without change, while no control-flow item qualified (0 against about 0.17 expected), so no structural difference is supported. [TWAIT001, TWAIT002]
