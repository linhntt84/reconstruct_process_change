### Inference

Support level: Weak hypothesis

The evidence suggests one specific hypothesis: the interval between the completion of activities A03–A08 and the completion of A09 was roughly halved, so that in the later window A09 completes about half an hour sooner after any of A03, A04, A05, A06, A07 or A08 than in the earlier window (about 0.5 vs about 1.0 hours between consecutive completion events). Supporting signs: all six measured pairs shift by the same amount in the same direction, each difference exceeds about 199 of 200 random re-splits of the pooled traces, and six items qualified where roughly 0.4 would be expected by chance, so sampling variation is unlikely to explain the pattern as a whole. [TWAIT001, TWAIT002, TWAIT003, TWAIT004, TWAIT005, TWAIT006]

What is missing: the evidence contains only timing items — no participation, ordering, positional or routing evidence — so it cannot show whether anything about the control flow around A09 changed (for example in which activities execute between these steps and A09) or whether only the elapsed times shrank while the structure stayed the same. An important competing explanation cannot be ruled out: every measured interval ends at A09, so the shortening could be process-wide rather than specific to the steps preceding A09. [TWAIT001, TWAIT002, TWAIT003, TWAIT004, TWAIT005, TWAIT006]

Other differences: the drop in observed A07→A09 intervals (42 to 27) against slight rises for the other five pairs (27–34 to 31–37) hints that A07 was less often the activity completed immediately before A09 in the later window, but this comes from the observation counts behind a timing indicator and is not direct ordering evidence.
