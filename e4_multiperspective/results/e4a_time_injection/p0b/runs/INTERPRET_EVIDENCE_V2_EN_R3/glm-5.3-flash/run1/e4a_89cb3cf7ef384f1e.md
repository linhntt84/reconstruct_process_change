### Observed differences

- The clearest change is in the elapsed time between the completion of A02 and the completion of A07. In the earlier window there were 30 such consecutive-completion intervals, averaging about 0.84 hours (median about 0.83, quartiles roughly 0.75–0.95); in the later window there were 22 intervals averaging about 1.74 hours (median about 1.65, quartiles roughly 1.58–1.89) — roughly double, with the whole distribution shifted upward. The difference was larger than in about 199 of 200 random re-splits of the pooled data. [TWAIT001]
- The interval from A06's completion to A07's completion also lengthened: about 0.11 hours on average before (median about 0.09) versus about 0.28 hours after (median about 0.18), with a much wider spread (standard deviation about 0.09 vs. 0.23 hours), while the number of intervals stayed almost the same (35 vs. 34). This difference also exceeded about 199 of 200 reference comparisons. [TWAIT002]
- The interval from A08's completion to A07's completion rose more modestly, from about 0.13 hours on average (median about 0.14) to about 0.20 hours (median about 0.19), with the interval count slightly up (33 vs. 37); again stronger than about 199 of 200 reference comparisons. [TWAIT003]
- The common pattern is that all three measured intervals end at A07's completion and all became longer in the later window, while the frequencies of the three predecessor→A07 sequences changed little. Note this indicator measures elapsed time between consecutive completion events, so it mixes any waiting between the events with the successor's own execution; it is not pure waiting or processing time. [TWAIT001, TWAIT002, TWAIT003]

### Inference

Support level: Weak hypothesis

The evidence suggests that in the later window A07 completes markedly later after its predecessor activities than before: the elapsed time from the completions of A02, A06 and A08 to A07's own completion increased on all three paths, most sharply after A02, where the typical interval roughly doubled from about 0.83 to about 1.65 hours, while the sequences themselves still occur at broadly similar rates (about 30→22, 35→34 and 33→37 intervals). Three consistent items, all centred on A07 and each stronger than about 199 of 200 random re-splits, are many times the roughly 0.34 items expected by chance across the 69 tested units, so the pattern as a whole is unlikely in an unchanged process. [TWAIT001, TWAIT002, TWAIT003]

What is missing: the measured interval spans both any waiting between the two completion events and A07's execution, so the evidence cannot distinguish a longer execution of A07, a delayed start of A07, or earlier completion of the predecessors relative to it. Because all three items share A07, they may also reflect one underlying difference rather than independent changes. The evidence contains no ordering, participation or positional items, so it does not show a control-flow restructuring (reordering, added or removed steps), and it cannot rule out that this is a pure timing shift without a structural counterpart. [TWAIT001, TWAIT002, TWAIT003]

Other differences: the count of A02→A07 intervals fell from 30 to 22 while the other two pair counts stayed flat or rose slightly (35→34, 33→37); with only about 30 observations behind that pair, a drop of this size can arise from sampling variation, so it is not clear evidence that the sequence itself became less frequent.
