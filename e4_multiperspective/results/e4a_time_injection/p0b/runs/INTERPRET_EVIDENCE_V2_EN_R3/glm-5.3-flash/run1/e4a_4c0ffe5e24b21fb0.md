### Observed differences

- On all four activity pairs that end in A25, the elapsed time between consecutive completion events roughly halved: in the earlier window the mean interval was about 1.0–1.04 hours for A16–A25, A17–A25, A18–A25 and A19–A25 alike, while in the later window it was about 0.49–0.53 hours on every pair, a drop of about half an hour [TWAIT001, TWAIT002, TWAIT003, TWAIT004].
- The shift affects the whole distribution, not just the average: the median interval fell from about 1.02–1.03 hours to about 0.48–0.53 hours, and the intervals also became more uniform, with the standard deviation dropping from about 0.14–0.17 hours to about 0.05–0.08 hours [TWAIT001, TWAIT002, TWAIT003, TWAIT004].
- Each of the four differences is larger than in 199 of 200 random re-splits of the pooled data, and four qualifying items is about eleven times the roughly 0.36 items expected by chance in an unchanged process [TWAIT001, TWAIT002, TWAIT003, TWAIT004].
- The number of observed intervals per pair moved unevenly between the windows: A19–A25 intervals fell from 61 to 35, A18–A25 rose from 46 to 64, A16–A25 rose from 41 to 53, and A17–A25 stayed roughly level at 52 to 48 [TWAIT001, TWAIT002, TWAIT003, TWAIT004].

### Inference

Support level: Weak hypothesis

In the later window, the interval between the completion of A16, A17, A18 or A19 and the subsequent completion of A25 is consistently about half an hour shorter than in the earlier window (from about 1.0 hour down to about 0.5 hours), with all four pairs into A25 changing in the same direction and by nearly the same amount, which points to a change centred on A25 or on the transitions leading into it; the supporting signs are that the item count is many times the chance expectation, the differences are uniform, and each exceeds 199 of 200 random re-splits [TWAIT001, TWAIT002, TWAIT003, TWAIT004].

What is missing: the evidence contains only timing items, with no participation, ordering or routing indicators, so it cannot show that A25 moved, that the paths into it changed, or that any activity became more or less frequent; an important competing explanation is that the control-flow structure is unchanged and only the gaps between consecutive completions shortened [TWAIT001, TWAIT002, TWAIT003, TWAIT004].

Other differences: the interval counts per pair shifted in different directions (A19–A25 down from 61 to 35, A18–A25 and A16–A25 up to 64 and 53), which could hint that A25 now follows A19 less often and A18 or A16 more often, but interval counts are not trace shares and no ordering evidence confirms this [TWAIT001, TWAIT002, TWAIT003, TWAIT004].
