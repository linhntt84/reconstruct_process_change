### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that two existing segments exchanged their places in the execution sequence. Earlier, the either-or choice between A14 and A15 sat between A22 and A23 (each alternative followed by A23), while the pair A16 and A17, executed in a mixed order, sat between A33 and A18; in the later window, the A14/A15 choice sits directly after A33 and leads to A18, while the A16/A17 pair sits between A22 and A23 and leads to A23 — A33 and A22 effectively exchanged their successors, and A18 and A23 received new predecessors. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006, LAG001, LAG002, REL009, REL010, REL011, REL012]

This is shown directly, not inferred from frequencies alone: deterministic order reversals in 100 of 100 traces for all four pairs spanning the boundary (A16/A17 against A18/A22), reversed precedence between A14/A15 and the A16/A17/A18/A22 group, exact lag changes (A33 to A18 from three steps to two; A22 to A23 from two to three), and matching position shifts (A14/A15 earlier, A16/A17 later, A18/A22 slightly earlier). All 22 qualified items fit this single reordering, far more than the roughly 0.36 items expected by chance among the 72 tested units, and each segment keeps its internal behaviour (mixed A16/A17 order, an about half-half A14/A15 choice, unchanged participation), which argues against a mere shift in how often existing paths are taken.
