### Observed differences

- A19 and A20 were almost never executed in the earlier window (A19 in about 1 in 200 traces, A20 in about 2 in 200); in the later window A19 runs in about 46 in 100 traces and A20 in about 56 in 100, in each case at most once per trace. [OCC001, OCC002]
- In the earlier window, A40 was followed directly by A25 in about 90 in 100 of its outgoing transitions; in the later window A40 leads instead to A20 (about 53 in 100) and A19 (about 42 in 100), and a direct A40→A25 transition has almost disappeared (under 1 in 100). [DIR003]
- In the later window, A19 leads to A25 in about 97 in 100 of its transitions and A20 leads to A25 in about 96 in 100; the earlier profiles of A19 and A20 rest on only 1 and 2 observations and are weak. [DIR001, DIR002]
- The ordering relations agree in the later window: A40 precedes A19 in about 45 in 100 traces and A19 precedes A25 in about 46 in 100; A40 precedes A20 in about 55 in 100 and A20 precedes A25 in about 55 in 100. Earlier, A25 and A40 ran without A19 or A20 in about 99 in 100 traces. [REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005]
- A19 and A20 act as alternatives in the later window: about 44 in 100 traces contain only A19, about 54 in 100 only A20, about 2 in 100 contain both, and almost none contain neither; earlier, about 98 in 100 traces contained neither activity. [REL001]
- The distance from A40 to A25 grew from mostly 1 step (about 90 in 100 cases) to mostly 2 steps (about 92 in 100), consistent with one activity now sitting between them. [LAG001]
- A25 still occurs in every trace in both windows and moved only slightly later (mean position from about 38% to about 40% of the trace); A19 and A20 now sit around 36% of the way through traces, just before A25, whereas the few earlier observations (1–2 traces) sat around 60%. [POS003, POS001, POS002]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that, in the later window, a step now sits between A40 and A25: A40, which previously led directly to A25 in about 90 in 100 of its transitions, now leads to A19 (about 42 in 100) or A20 (about 53 in 100), and A19 or A20 in turn leads almost always to A25 (about 96–97 in 100). [DIR003, DIR001, DIR002]

The change is direct and internally consistent: the successor change at A40, the ordering relations (A40 before A19/A20 before A25 in the same traces), and the lag shift from 1 to 2 steps are each backed by all 200 traces in both windows; the participation rise of A19 and A20 from about 1–2 in 200 traces to 46 and 56 in 100 matches; A25 itself remains in every trace and only drifts slightly later; and the 13 qualifying items are many times the roughly 0.5 items expected by chance across the 93 tested units, so the item count as a whole is unlikely without a change. [REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, LAG001, OCC001, OCC002, POS003]

Limitations: the earlier behaviour of A19 and A20 is based on 1 and 2 observations, so the evidence cannot show what, if anything, connected A40 to A25 through them before. An important competing explanation it cannot rule out is that A19 and A20 were a rare pre-existing path (seen in 1–2 earlier traces) that is now taken far more often, rather than steps newly introduced into the flow. [DIR001, DIR002, OCC001, OCC002]

Other differences: minor rerouting remains — in the later window A40 sends about 3 in 100 transitions to A06 and about 1.5 in 100 to A02 (earlier about 2.5 in 100 went to A10 and 2 in 100 to A02), and A19 and A20 each send about 1 in 100 transitions to other activities (A14, A11, A43 and A10, A04, A38 respectively). [DIR003, DIR001, DIR002]
