### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The 72 qualifying items far exceed the roughly 0.84 expected by chance when nothing has changed, so sampling noise cannot account for the pattern as a whole. The evidence supports the hypothesis that the sequencing between the two activity groups was inverted or dissolved: the segment A25, A26, A27 and A28, which in the earlier window always executed after A20, A21, A23 and A24, now typically executes before them — most clearly A25 before A24 (about 91 of 100 traces, previously 0) — while several pairs lost any fixed order, and A24 now hands over directly to A34 instead of to A25. The relational evidence shows this directly for all fifteen activity pairs with co-occurrence unchanged at 100%, and it agrees with the position shifts of all eight activities, the successor changes at A24, A26 and A33, and the step-distance changes at the group boundaries. [REL001-REL015, POS001-POS008, DIR004, DIR006, DIR009, LAG019, LAG022, LAG039]

Limitations: in the later window the order is mixed for several pairs (A20/A27, A21/A28, A23/A25 near 50/50), so the evidence establishes that the strict early-before-late constraint was lost and often inverted, but not the exact new structure — a clean relocation of the A25–A28 segment and a loosening into interleaved or repeated execution between the groups fit the data equally, and this competing reading cannot be ruled out. POS and LAG reflect event positions and step counts, not clock time, and many items describe the same trace behaviour, so they are not independent confirmations. [REL003, REL007, REL008, REL009, POS001-POS008, LAG001, LAG022]

Other differences: A48's routing between A45 and A49 shifted (A49 about 65 → 48 of 100 outgoing transitions, A45 about 32 → 51); this rests on a single indicator and is not part of the main hypothesis. [DIR010]
