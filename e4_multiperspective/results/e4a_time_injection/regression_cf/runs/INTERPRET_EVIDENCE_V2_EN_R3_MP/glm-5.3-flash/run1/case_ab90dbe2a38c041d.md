### Observed differences

- **Routing out of A25 changed completely.** Earlier, about 97 in 100 of A25's observed outgoing transitions went to A26, with only scattered single transitions elsewhere; in the later window they split about evenly between A33 (about 48 in 100) and A32 (about 47 in 100), with A26 down to about 1 in 100. [DIR001]
- **Routing out of A28 changed.** Earlier, A28 led to A32 (about 53 in 100) or A33 (about 45 in 100); in the later window it led to A29 in about 94 in 100 of its outgoing transitions. [DIR002]
- **Exits from A32 and A33 changed.** Earlier, A32 led mainly to A33 (about 54 in 100) or A29 (about 46 in 100), and A33 to A29 (about 54 in 100) or A32 (about 44 in 100); in the later window both led mainly to A26 (about 47–49 in 100) or to each other, with A29 chosen in about 1 in 100 or fewer. The either-order relationship between A32 and A33 itself persisted in both windows. [DIR003, DIR004]
- **Execution order reversed between the two activity groups.** Earlier, A26 ran before A32 in 99 of 100 traces and before A33 in 99.5 of 100, and A28 ran before A32/A33 in 99–99.5 of 100; in the later window A32 and A33 ran first in about 96–98 of 100 traces for all four pairs. All four activity pairs still co-occurred in every trace in both windows. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004]
- **Within-trace distances moved consistently with a block move.** A25→A26 went from 1 step (97 in 100) to 3 steps (about 93 in 100); A25→A28 from 3 steps (93 in 100) to 5 steps (about 98 in 100); A25→A32 and A25→A33 from 4–5 steps to 1–2 steps; A28→A29 from 3 steps (about 96 in 100) to 1 step (95 in 100); A32→A29 and A33→A29 from 1–2 steps to 4–5 steps; A26→A29 from 5 steps to 3 steps (about 90 in 100). [LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG005, LAG007, LAG010, LAG011]
- **Relative positions shifted in matching directions.** A26's mean position moved from about 0.43 to 0.51 of the trace and A28's from about 0.49 to 0.57, while A32's fell from about 0.54 to 0.46 and A33's from about 0.55 to 0.46; all four activities still appeared in every trace in both windows. [POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004]
- Three further distance items qualified but rest on only 3–4 after-window observations each; their before-profiles (A26→A33 at 3–4 steps, A28→A32 and A28→A33 at 1–2 steps) fit the same picture, and those ordered pairs became rare afterwards. [LAG006, LAG008, LAG009]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that two segments of the trace swapped places. Earlier, a typical trace ran A25 → A26 → (about two steps) → A28 → A32/A33 in either order → A29; in the later window it typically runs A25 → A32/A33 in either order → A26 → (about two steps) → A28 → A29. Concretely, A25 now hands over to A32 or A33 instead of A26, A32 and A33 now hand over to A26 instead of to A29, A28 now leads to A29 instead of to A32/A33, and A26 and A28 each sit later in the trace while A32 and A33 sit earlier. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG005, LAG007, LAG010, LAG011, POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004]

The support is direct and consistent: the four order reversals are relational evidence of the reordering itself, the successor changes at A25, A28, A32 and A33 show the new handovers, and the distance and position items all shift in the directions the swap predicts. Participation is unchanged — A26, A28, A32 and A33 occur in all 200 traces in both windows and still co-occur in every trace — which rules out the activities having been added, removed or made optional. In total 23 items qualified where fewer than 1 (about 0.57) would be expected by chance, and every item's difference exceeded 199 of 200 random re-splits of the pooled data. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, POS001, POS002, POS003, POS004, REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006, LAG007, LAG008, LAG009, LAG010, LAG011]

Limitations: the evidence covers pairwise relations and marginal event positions only, so the exact form of the reordering cannot be pinned down — a clean two-segment swap and a broader reshuffle of the middle section producing the same pairwise reversals and distances both fit, and the activities filling the roughly two-step gap between A26 and A28 are not observed. The position shifts alone would not establish movement, but here they are corroborated by the relational and successor evidence; the three weakly supported distance items add little on their own. [LAG006, LAG008, LAG009]
