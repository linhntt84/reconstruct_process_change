### Observed differences

- The choice between two existing paths after A20 reversed: in the earlier window about 78 in 100 of the transitions out of A20 went to A21 (which led on to A22), and about 22 in 100 went to A23 (which led on to A24); in the later window about 21 in 100 go to A21 and about 79 in 100 go to A23, with under 1 in 100 to A05. [DIR001]
- Participation flipped accordingly: A21 and A22 each ran in about 78 in 100 traces earlier but in only about 21–22 in 100 later; A23 and A24 each ran in about 22 in 100 traces earlier but in about 79–80 in 100 later. None of the four activities repeats within a trace in either window. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004]
- Branch co-occurrence mirrors this: A21 and A22 occurred together in about 78 in 100 traces earlier (A21 first in nearly all) versus about 21 in 100 later, with neither present in about 78 in 100 later traces; A23 and A24 co-occurred in about 22 in 100 traces earlier (neither present in about 78 in 100) versus about 79 in 100 later, almost always A23 before A24. [REL005, REL012, DIR002, DIR003]
- A20 now travels with the other branch: earlier it co-occurred with A21 in about 78 in 100 traces (A20 first) and with A23 in about 22 in 100; later it co-occurs with A23 in about 80 in 100 (again A20 first) and with A21 in about 22 in 100. The cross pairs (for example A21 with A23, A22 with A24) remain alternatives, co-occurring in at most about 1–2 in 100 traces in both windows. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL006, REL007, REL009, REL010]
- A28 moved with the dominant branch: earlier it co-occurred with A21 and with A22 in about 78 in 100 traces each; later it co-occurs with A23 in about 80 in 100 and with A24 in about 79 in 100, and appears without A21 or A22 in about 78 in 100 traces. [REL008, REL011, REL013, REL014]
- Everything else is at noise level: A21 still leads almost exclusively to A22 (about 95 in 100 of its later transitions, from only 44 traces), A23 and A24 keep the same dominant successors (A24 and A28 respectively), and only about 1–2 in 100 transitions shift to rarely seen targets (A12, A20, A14) or away from A19. [DIR002, DIR003, DIR004]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that the two paths after A20 reversed in frequency: earlier, roughly 78 in 100 traces ran A20 → A21 → A22 (with A28 present in most of them) and roughly 22 in 100 ran A20 → A23 → A24 → A28; in the later window the shares are roughly reversed, about 21 in 100 versus about 79 in 100. All four branch activities and A28 occur in substantial shares in both windows, and every ordering relation is preserved (A20 before A21 and before A23, A21 before A22, A23 before A24, A24 leading to A28), so this reads as existing paths being taken with different frequencies rather than activities being added, removed, repositioned or repeated. [DIR001, OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, REL001, REL003, REL005, REL012, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004]

The routing change is shown directly in A20's outgoing transitions and is mirrored consistently by the participation and co-occurrence changes of A21, A22, A23, A24 and A28; with 22 evidence items against about 0.45 expected by chance in an unchanged process, and every item's difference larger than about 199 of 200 random re-splits, chance sampling cannot account for the set as a whole. [DIR001, OCC001, REL001]

A competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out: a changed composition of cases reaching A20 would produce the same reversal of branch shares as a changed routing preference at A20, and an exchange of roles between the pairs {A21, A22} and {A23, A24} would leave the observed traces identical; the data cannot separate these readings. [DIR001, OCC001, REL005]

Other differences: only minor edge fluctuations — under about 2 in 100 of the transitions from A21, A23 and A24 go to rarely seen targets (A12, A20, A14) or no longer to A19, and under 1 in 100 of A20's transitions go to A05; some of these profiles rest on as few as 44 traces in one window. [DIR002, DIR003, DIR004]
