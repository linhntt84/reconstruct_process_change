### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, A14 and A15 were never observed (0 of 200 traces). In the later window, A14 appears in about 44 in 100 traces and A15 in about 56 in 100, in each case exactly once per trace containing it [OCC001, OCC002].
- In the earlier window, A35 was always followed directly by A20: all of its observed outgoing transitions went to A20, and the pair was consecutive (lag 1) in essentially every trace. In the later window, A35's outgoing transitions go to A15 in about 56 in 100 cases and to A14 in about 44 in 100, with a direct A35→A20 transition remaining in only about 1 in 200 [DIR003, LAG001].
- In the later window, A14 and A15 each lead to A20 in 100 in 100 of their observed outgoing transitions; neither activity was observed before, so no earlier outgoing profile exists [DIR001, DIR002].
- A14 and A15 act as mutually exclusive alternatives: in the later window they never occur in the same trace — about 44 in 100 traces contain only A14, about 56 in 100 only A15, and about 1 in 200 contain neither — whereas in the earlier window neither appeared at all [REL001].
- Ordering in the later window: whenever A14 occurs, A35 comes before it and A14 before A20 (about 44 in 100 traces); the same holds for A15 (about 56 in 100 traces) [REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005].
- Consequently, the distance between A35 and A20 shifted from immediate succession (lag 1 in all earlier traces) to one intervening event (lag 2 in about 995 in 1000 later traces) [LAG001].
- When they occur, A14 and A15 sit at about 36% of the trace's event positions on average (median about 0.35); this is event position, not clock time [POS001, POS002].
- All flagged items show differences larger than in about 995 of 1000 random re-splits of the pooled data, and the number of items (13) is far above the roughly 0.33 expected by chance in an unchanged process across the 67 tested units [OCC001, OCC002, DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, POS001, POS002, REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, LAG001].

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that, in the later window, a new step was inserted between A35 and A20: A35, which previously led directly to A20, now leads to either A14 or A15 — two mutually exclusive alternatives taken in roughly a 44-to-56 split — and the chosen activity then leads on to A20, so A20 is reached one step later than before. This rests on direct relational and successor evidence: the redirection of A35's outgoing transitions, the new A14→A20 and A15→A20 transitions, the consistent A35-before-A14/A15-before-A20 ordering, and the lag between A35 and A20 changing from 1 to 2 [DIR003, DIR001, DIR002, REL001, LAG001].

The picture is internally consistent: the roughly 1 in 200 later traces in which A35 still leads directly to A20 match the share of traces containing neither A14 nor A15, suggesting the old direct path persists rarely. The items largely describe one coherent structural difference rather than independent phenomena, so they should not be read as multiple confirmations. Limitations: the evidence cannot show what governs the choice between A14 and A15, and zero participation in the earlier window strictly means only that A14 and A15 were not exercised in those 200 traces, not that they were absent from the underlying process — although a behaviour occurring in roughly half of later traces is unlikely to be missed by chance in a stable process [DIR003, LAG001, REL001, OCC001, OCC002].
