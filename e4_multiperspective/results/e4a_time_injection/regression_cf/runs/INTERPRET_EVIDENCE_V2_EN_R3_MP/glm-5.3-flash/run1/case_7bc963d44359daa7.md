### Observed differences

- Before, A14 and A15 were strict one-or-the-other alternatives: every trace contained exactly one of them (A14 in about 49 in 100 traces, A15 in about 51 in 100), placed between A35 and A20. In the later window, A14 runs in about 26 in 100 traces and A15 in about 28, and in about 47 in 100 traces neither of them runs; they still never occur in the same trace. [OCC001, OCC002, REL001, REL002, REL006, LAG005]
- The missing block is bypassed: in the later window, A35 leads directly to A20 in about 47 in 100 of its outgoing transitions, whereas before it led only to A14 (about 49 in 100) or A15 (about 51 in 100). Accordingly, A20 now immediately follows A35 in about 47 in 100 traces (before, always one position later), and A21 follows A35 four positions later in about 47 in 100 traces (before, always five); the same one-step advance shows for A27 and A28. [DIR004, LAG005, LAG006, LAG007, LAG008]
- When A14 or A15 does run in the later window, its relationships are unchanged: it still co-occurs with A20, A21, A27 and A28, still runs after A35 and before A20, and never repeats. Only the share of traces containing it dropped. [REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, REL007, REL008, REL009, REL010, REL011]
- The two orderings of A27 and A28 between A20 and A21 changed frequency: before, the A27-first ordering accounted for about 54 in 100 traces and the A28-first ordering for about 46; in the later window this is reversed to about 43 versus 58. All four activities still occur in essentially every trace in both windows. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that, in the later window, the exclusive choice between A14 and A15 — executed between A35 and A20 — became optional: in about 47 in 100 traces neither activity is executed and A35 connects directly to A20, shortening the path from A35 to A21 by one step. The signs are direct and quantitatively consistent: participation in A14 and A15 fell from about 49 and 51 in 100 traces to about 26 and 28; the share of traces containing neither rose from zero to about 47; A35's outgoing transitions shifted to A20 in exactly that share; and the positions of A20 and A21 relative to A35 moved one step earlier in exactly that share. [OCC001, OCC002, REL001, DIR004, LAG005, LAG006]

A relocation of the block is not a plausible reading: whenever A14 or A15 still runs, its order relative to A35, A20, A21, A27 and A28 is the same in both windows, so the block kept its place and lost coverage rather than moving. [REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, REL007, REL008, REL009, REL010, REL011]

Chance is an unlikely explanation for the pattern as a whole: 24 items qualified where roughly 0.42 would be expected for an unchanged process, and every item's difference exceeds all but about 1 in 200 random re-splits of the pooled data. [OCC001, OCC002, DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL005, REL006, REL007, REL008, REL009, REL010, REL011, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006, LAG007, LAG008]

An important competing explanation remains: the evidence cannot distinguish a routing change within single cases from a mixture of case types in the later window (cases that never contained the A14/A15 block), nor can it show whether the bypassed traces differ in other respects; the after-window shares for A14 and A15 also rest on moderate counts (about 52 and 55 traces). [OCC001, OCC002, REL001, DIR004]

Other differences: the relative frequency of the two orderings of A27 and A28 between A20 and A21 shifted from about 54/46 to about 43/58 in favour of A28 first. Both orderings are observed substantially in both windows and all involved activities occur in nearly every trace throughout, so this is a change in how often existing paths are taken, not a structural replacement; the evidence cannot show whether it is independent of the bypass or linked to it. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004]
