### Observed differences

- **A24's immediate successors narrowed.** In the earlier window, A24 — with an observed outgoing transition in every trace in both windows (200 of 200) — was followed by A16, A17, A18 and A19 in roughly equal shares (about 23–28% of its outgoing transitions each). In the later window, almost all of these transitions go to A16 (about 44%) or A17 (about 55%), while A18 and A19 are nearly unobserved as successors (about 1% or less). [DIR005]
- **Who leads directly into A25 changed.** Before, each of A16, A17, A18 and A19 led directly to A25 in about half of its outgoing transitions (about 48–52%). In the later window, A18 and A19 lead to A25 in about 98–99% of their transitions, whereas A16 and A17 almost never do (about 1–2%, down from about 48–52%). [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004]
- **The order between the two activity pairs changed from mixed to one-directional.** Before, transitions between the pairs {A16, A17} and {A18, A19} ran in both directions in similar proportions (for example A16→A18 about 20% vs A18→A16 about 25%; A17→A19 about 21% vs A19→A17 about 20%), and transitions within a pair were not observed. In the later window, A16 and A17 lead to A18 (about 40–45%) and A19 (about 54–57%), while the reverse transitions are practically unobserved (about 0–1%, down from about 20–29%). [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004]
- **Position gaps match this new sequence.** Before, the gap from A24 to each of the four activities, and from each of them to A25, was one step in about half of the cases and two steps in the other half. In the later window, A16/A17 follow A24 after one step and reach A25 only after two steps, while A18/A19 follow A24 after two steps and reach A25 after one step (each about 98–99 in 100 cases). [LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006, LAG007, LAG008]
- **Activity volumes are stable.** The four activities together account for about 400 outgoing transitions in each window (about two per trace, roughly 88–112 per activity), so the amount of activity in this segment is unchanged; what changed is its ordering and wiring. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

In the later window, the segment after A24 is executed in a fixed order — A24, then one of A16/A17, then one of A18/A19, then A25 — whereas in the earlier window one activity from each pair ran between A24 and A25 in either order, with no fixed order between the pairs: both directions appeared in similar proportions, and whichever activity came second led directly to A25 (about 50% for each of the four). This is a change in ordering constraints, shown directly by the successor redistributions and corroborated by the position-gap shifts that fit the two-stage sequence exactly; 13 qualifying items far exceeds the roughly 0.35 expected by chance among 71 tested units, and every item's difference exceeds about 199 of 200 random re-splits, so sampling variation cannot explain the pattern as a whole. All involved activities remain well observed in both windows (about 88–200 supporting observations each). [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006, LAG007, LAG008]

Limitations: the DIR and LAG items summarise the same traces, so the two indicator families corroborate rather than independently confirm the change. An important competing reading the evidence cannot rule out is that this is an extreme shift in the frequencies of two orders that both already existed (from roughly 50/50 to about 98/2) rather than a newly hard ordering constraint; it also cannot show whether the choice within each pair (A16 vs A17, A18 vs A19) is free or governed by anything outside the observed segment. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, LAG001, LAG003, LAG005, LAG007]

Other differences: none — every qualifying item concerns the same reordering around A24, A16–A19 and A25. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006, LAG007, LAG008]
