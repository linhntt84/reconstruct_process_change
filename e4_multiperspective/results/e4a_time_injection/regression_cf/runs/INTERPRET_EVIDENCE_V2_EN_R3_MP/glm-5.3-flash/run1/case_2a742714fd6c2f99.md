### Observed differences

- Activities A16, A17, A18 and A19 each appeared in about 83 in 100 traces in the earlier window but in only about 27 in 100 in the later window (165 of 200 traces falling to 54); each occurs at most once per trace in both windows. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004]
- Activities A20, A21, A22 and A23 moved the opposite way, from about 18 in 100 traces earlier to about 73 in 100 later (35 rising to 146 of 200). [OCC005, OCC006, OCC007, OCC008]
- Among the outgoing transitions observed from A15, the share leading to A19 fell from about 82% in the earlier window to about 27% in the later window, while the share leading to A23 rose from about 18% to about 73%. [DIR001]
- In the earlier window, A15 was followed by all four of A16–A19 together in about 83 in 100 traces (always with A15 first, and A19 ahead of A16–A18); in the later window, A15 usually runs without them (about 73 in 100) and the four co-occur in only about 27 in 100. [REL001, REL002, REL003, REL004, REL011, REL018, REL024]
- Conversely, in the earlier window A15 ran without any of A20–A23 in about 83 in 100 traces; in the later window it is followed by all four in about 73 in 100, with A23 preceding A20, A21 and A22, which then follow in mixed order. [REL005, REL006, REL007, REL008, REL037, REL040, REL042]
- The two groups are strict alternatives in both windows — a member of A16–A19 and a member of A20–A23 never occur in the same trace — so what changed is the take-up of the two paths, not their availability. [REL012, REL019, REL025, REL030]
- A27 occurs in every trace in both windows; earlier it ran alongside the A16–A19 group (about 83 in 100 traces), later alongside the A20–A23 group (about 73 in 100), in mixed order in both windows. [REL016, REL029, REL038, REL044]
- Positional gaps shifted mildly: the A15–A17 and A19–A17 distances concentrated on shorter gaps in the later window, and A17–A27 gaps on longer ones, consistent with the different composition of the block now following A15. [LAG001, LAG002, LAG003]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that, between the two windows, the take-up of the two alternative paths after A15 reversed: in the earlier window roughly 82 in 100 traces continued from A15 into the A16–A19 block (the four executed together, A15 first, with A27 alongside) and roughly 18 in 100 took the A20–A23 block, while in the later window this became roughly 27 in 100 for A16–A19 and roughly 73 in 100 for A20–A23, with A23 preceding A20, A21 and A22 and A27 still accompanying whichever block executes. This is a change in how often two existing paths are taken, not a replacement of one path by another: both blocks and both A15 transitions are observed in substantial shares in both windows (A20–A23 in 35 of 200 traces earlier, A16–A19 in 54 of 200 later), and the internal ordering within each block is preserved. [DIR001, OCC001, OCC005, REL001, REL005, REL012, REL016, REL038]

The redirection of A15's outgoing transitions shows the routing change directly, and the participation, co-occurrence and ordering patterns all match it, with the two paths' shares together covering all traces in each window; 56 evidence items qualified against fewer than one expected by chance in a stable process, and each item's difference exceeds about 199 of 200 random re-splits, so sampling noise cannot account for the pattern as a whole. A competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out is a shift in the mix of coexisting process variants — two variant populations whose relative frequencies changed — which would produce the same aggregate shares; the data also cannot show whether the per-trace choice between the blocks is a single decision point or two separately triggered routes. [DIR001, OCC001, OCC005, REL001, REL012, REL016]
