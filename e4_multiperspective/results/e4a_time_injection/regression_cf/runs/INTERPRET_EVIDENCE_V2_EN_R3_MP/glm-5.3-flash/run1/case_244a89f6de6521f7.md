### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that, in the later window, roughly every second case replaces the step A22 with a group of four new activities — A27, A28, A33 and A34 — executed between A21 and A23: A21 (present in all traces in both windows) leads into the group in about 47% of cases, the four activities each run once in no fixed order, the group precedes A23, and A22 is skipped; in the remaining cases the original A21 → A22 → A23 sequence continues unchanged. [OCC001, OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, DIR002, REL011, REL016, REL017, REL021, LAG001]

Supporting signs: the participation, routing, co-occurrence and distance evidence all describe the same underlying difference, each item is backed by 94–200 traces, and the 47 qualifying items far exceed the roughly 0.5 expected by chance for the 104 tested units, so sampling variation cannot account for the evidence as a whole. [OCC001, DIR002, LAG001, REL025]

Limitations and competing explanation: the internal sequence of the new group is not fixed (both orders occur for every pair), so no exact ordering constraint inside it can be stated; zero participation in the earlier window strictly means the four activities were only not observed there, so a pre-existing but unexercised branch cannot be fully ruled out; and the evidence cannot indicate why a case takes one route or the other. [OCC002, OCC003, OCC004, OCC005, REL025, REL026, REL027, REL028, REL029, REL030]

Other differences: A06 now routes to A07 in about 47% of its outgoing transitions versus about 26% before (A08 and A09 decline), and A23 sits slightly later in the trace (mean relative position about 0.53 to 0.55); the evidence does not tie these to the A22/new-group change, although the position shift is consistent with traces becoming longer when the group runs. [DIR001, POS001]
