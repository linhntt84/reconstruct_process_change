### Observed differences

- The dominant difference is a reversal between two four-activity blocks. In the earlier window, A21–A24 were executed together in about 83 in 100 traces, while A25–A28 ran together in only about 18 in 100; in the later window the shares are roughly reversed (about 28 in 100 for A21–A24, about 73 in 100 for A25–A28). Both blocks keep their internal shape — the four activities appear together or not at all — and they remain almost mutually exclusive alternatives (A21 and A25 occur together in only about 1–2 in 100 traces in both windows). [OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, OCC007, OCC008, OCC009, OCC010, OCC011, REL029, REL032, REL059]

- The upstream links moved to the growing block. A20's dominant outgoing transition flipped from A24 (about 83 in 100 of its outgoing transitions earlier, about 27 in 100 later) to A28 (about 18 to about 72 in 100). A19 preceded A21–A24 in about 81 in 100 traces earlier but precedes A25–A28 in about 72 in 100 later; A31 and A32, present in essentially every trace, shifted from co-occurring with A21–A24 (about 82–83 in 100) to co-occurring with A25–A28 (about 73 in 100). [DIR003, REL013, REL017, REL021, REL025, REL036, REL037, REL063, REL071]

- Step distances in this region became more concentrated: A20 to A22 and A24 to A22 within one or two steps rose from about 33 to about 52 in 100, and A31 to A24 and A31 to A28 now sit three steps apart in about 75 and 82 in 100 cases, against roughly even three-/four-step splits earlier. [LAG004, LAG005, LAG006, LAG009, LAG010]

- A04 receded sharply and appears bypassed. It took part in about 55 in 100 traces earlier and about 15 in 100 later. Earlier, A31 led to A04 in about 52 in 100 of A31's outgoing transitions and A04 led on to A19 in about 87 in 100 of A04's outgoing transitions; later, A31 leads directly to A19 in about 83 in 100 of its outgoing transitions (within one step in about 84 in 100 traces, up from about 45 in 100), while A04's remaining outgoing transitions are spread thinly over many targets (A19 only about 32 in 100). A04's position in the trace also became more variable (median about 0.48 to about 0.41 of trace length, spread roughly doubled), on fewer traces (about 109 vs 31). [OCC002, DIR001, DIR004, POS001, REL001, REL011, LAG001, LAG007]

- Two small participation increases rest on few observations: A02 (about 5 to about 13 in 100 traces; 10 vs 27 traces) and A08 (about 4 to about 12 in 100; 7 vs 24 traces). A06's outgoing mix also changed (A07 and A12, about 20 in 100 each earlier, are no longer observed; A19, A35, A37 and A16 appear), but this rests on 10 vs 28 transitions. [OCC001, OCC003, DIR002]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that two alternative four-activity blocks exchanged their execution frequencies: the A21–A24 block, previously executed together in about 83 in 100 traces, dropped to about 28 in 100, while the A25–A28 block rose from about 18 in 100 to about 73 in 100 and took over the upstream links, with A19, A20, A31 and A32 now preceding or co-occurring with A25–A28 where they previously did so with A21–A24. [OCC004, OCC005, OCC006, OCC007, OCC008, OCC009, OCC010, OCC011, DIR003, REL013, REL017, REL036, REL063]

Supporting signs: the participation shifts are about 55 percentage points in each direction and are measured on substantial samples in both windows (about 165 vs 56 traces per A21–A24 activity; about 37 vs 146 for A25–A28); the relational items show the predecessor handover directly, while the blocks' internal structure (four activities together, mutually exclusive alternatives) is preserved, so this is a change in how often two existing paths are taken rather than activities appearing or disappearing. In total 99 evidence items qualified, against roughly 1 expected by chance among the 229 tested units, so the ensemble cannot be attributed to sampling noise. [OCC004, OCC008, REL029, REL032, REL059, DIR003]

Limitations: the evidence provides frequencies and pairwise relations, not a complete model. It cannot show whether A21–A24 became an optional branch of one model or belong to a separate trace family whose share grew, nor where the rare A21–A24 block sits within later traces; a shifted mix of two coexisting trace families is a competing explanation this design cannot rule out. [REL029, REL032]

Other differences:
- A04 receded from about 55 to about 15 in 100 traces and appears bypassed, with A31 now leading directly to A19; the evidence does not show whether A04 became optional on that route or was displaced for another reason. [OCC002, DIR001, DIR004, POS001, REL001, LAG001, LAG007]
- Small participation increases for A02 and A08 rest on 10–27 and 7–24 traces respectively and are weak. [OCC001, OCC003]
- A06's outgoing transition mix changed, but on few observations. [DIR002]
