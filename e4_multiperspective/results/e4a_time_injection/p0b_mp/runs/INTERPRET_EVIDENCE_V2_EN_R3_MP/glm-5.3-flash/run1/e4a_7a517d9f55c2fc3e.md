### Observed differences

- **Entry into the block:** In the earlier window, A24 — present in all 200 traces of both windows — spread its outgoing transitions almost evenly over A16, A17, A18 and A19 (about 25 in 100 each). In the later window, A24 leads almost exclusively to A17 (about 55 in 100) and A16 (about 44 in 100), while direct moves to A18 or A19 have nearly disappeared (about 1 in 100 or fewer). [DIR005]

- **First middle hop:** Earlier, about half of the outgoing transitions of A16 (52 in 100) and of A17 (48 in 100) went straight to A25, with most of the rest going to A18 or A19. In the later window, transitions from A16 and A17 to A25 are observed in only about 1–2 in 100 cases, and they instead go to A19 (about 54–57 in 100) or A18 (about 40–45 in 100). [DIR001, DIR002]

- **Second middle hop:** Earlier, A18 and A19 sent about half of their outgoing transitions to A25 (48 and 51 in 100) and the other half to A16 or A17. In the later window, about 98–99 in 100 of their transitions go to A25. [DIR003, DIR004]

- **Spacing:** Earlier, every measured activity pair occurred at one and at two events apart in roughly equal shares (about 50/50). In the later window the spacing is fixed: A16 and A17 follow A24 immediately (about 98–99 in 100 at one step), A18 and A19 follow one step after A16/A17, and A25 follows A18/A19 immediately, while A25 now follows A16/A17 two steps later (about 98–99 in 100) rather than immediately. [LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006, LAG007, LAG008]

- **Participation and position:** A18 appears in 53% of traces before versus 44% after; A19 in 47% versus 56%; both occur slightly later within the trace (mean event position up by about 0.02–0.03 of trace length). [POS001, POS002]

- **Timing:** The elapsed time between consecutive completion events grew on every measured edge: A16/A17 → A18/A19 from about 0.14 hours (8–12 minutes) to about 1.0 hour, and A18/A19 → A25 from about 1.05 to about 2.0 hours; the number of observed consecutive pairs also roughly doubled (for example A19→A25 from 48 to 111). [TWAIT001, TWAIT002, TWAIT003, TWAIT004, TWAIT005, TWAIT006]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that in the later window A16, A17, A18 and A19 execute as a fixed two-stage sequence between A24 and A25 — A24 is followed by A16 or A17, then by A18 or A19, then by A25 — whereas in the earlier window both stages could be bypassed: A24 went directly to A18/A19 in about half of its outgoing transitions, and A16/A17 went directly to A25 in about half. In the later window these direct moves are observed in only about 1–2 in 100 transitions. The change is shown directly by the successor redistributions of all five activities and by the positional gaps, which moved from mixed one-/two-step spacings to the fixed spacings of the chain; the number of qualifying items is far beyond what an unchanged process produces (15 control-flow items against about 0.35 expected, 6 time items against about 0.008). Limitations: the direction and spacing items describe largely the same rerouting and are not independent confirmations, and the evidence shows the bypass routes were almost never taken, not that they were removed — a strong shift in the choice among still-existing routes cannot be ruled out. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, LAG001, LAG002, LAG003, LAG004, LAG005, LAG006, LAG007, LAG008]

Other differences:

- The elapsed time between consecutive completion events increased on all six measured pairs of the chain — from about 0.14 hours to about 1.0 hour for A16/A17 → A18/A19 and from about 1.05 to about 2.0 hours for A18/A19 → A25, with observed pair counts roughly doubling (for example A19→A25 from 48 to 111). This is a timing change in its own right; since the measure spans everything between two consecutive completions, the added hour per hop cannot be attributed to waiting or to processing specifically. [TWAIT001, TWAIT002, TWAIT003, TWAIT004, TWAIT005, TWAIT006]
- Participation shifted only mildly: A18 appears in somewhat fewer traces (53% versus 44%) and A19 in somewhat more (47% versus 56%), both marginally later within the trace. [POS001, POS002]
