### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports a timing change centred on A09 as a possibility: in the later window, the elapsed time between the completion of each of A03, A04, A05, A06, A07 and A08 and the subsequent completion of A09 is consistently about half of what it was in the earlier window (from about 1.0 hour to about 0.5 hour), with a noticeably narrower spread. Supporting signs: all six predecessor pairs move in the same direction with the same magnitude; each difference exceeds all but about 1 in 2,000 reference re-splits; the six qualifying time items far exceed the roughly 0.013 expected by chance among the 26 time units tested; the underlying samples are substantial (27–42 observed gaps per window); and no control-flow item qualified, so the shift is not accompanied by a detected structural change — a consistent change in these elapsed times is a hypothesis in its own right and needs no structural counterpart. [TWAIT001, TWAIT002, TWAIT003, TWAIT004, TWAIT005, TWAIT006]

Limitations and competing explanation: T_WAIT measures elapsed time between consecutive completion events, so the evidence cannot distinguish whether the predecessors A03–A08 completed sooner, A09 completed earlier relative to them, or the intervening elapsed time simply shrank; nor can it separate reduced waiting from faster handling. Because all six items share A09, they may describe one underlying difference rather than six independent ones. [TWAIT001, TWAIT002, TWAIT003, TWAIT004, TWAIT005, TWAIT006]

Other differences: the number of observed A07→A09 consecutive-completion gaps fell from 42 to 27, while the other pairs changed little (e.g., 34→36, 33→37); this frequency difference did not qualify as a control-flow item and may be sampling variation. [TWAIT005, TWAIT001, TWAIT002]
