### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the hypothesis that the routing around the four-activity block A31, A32, A37, A38 changed: in the earlier window every case ran from A20 through the block (in mixed order) to A23, and later from A21 via A22 to A26, whereas in the later window A20 leads directly to A23 and the block instead runs after A21 as an alternative to A22, ending in A26 — so the block and A22 became mutually exclusive options, each taken in about half of the cases. [DIR001, DIR002, DIR003, DIR004, DIR005, DIR006, REL007, REL013, REL017, REL021, LAG001, LAG006, OCC001, OCC002]

This goes far beyond what an unchanged process typically produces: the control-flow family yielded 60 qualifying items against roughly 0.62 expected by chance, each difference exceeding about 995 of 1,000 random re-splits, and the successor replacement at A20 and A21, the order flips around the block, the collapse of the A20-to-A23 distance and the halving of participation all describe the same re-routing while A23 and A26 remain universal. [DIR001, DIR002, LAG001, OCC001, POS004, POS005]

Limitations: the exact internal sequence of the block stays unresolved, a few post-change lag profiles for the block's old and new neighbours rest on a single observed pair, and about one trace in 200 in the later window still shows the old routing; the aggregate windows also cannot show whether the same kinds of cases changed route or whether the two windows capture a shifted mix of case types that always followed different routes. [REL025, LAG002, LAG007, DIR001]

Other differences: the elapsed time between consecutive completions of A42 and A43 roughly doubled (mean about 1.0 h to about 2.0 h, 155 vs 162 intervals) — a timing change in its own right with no structural counterpart in the evidence, since A42 and A43 appear in no other item. [TWAIT001]
