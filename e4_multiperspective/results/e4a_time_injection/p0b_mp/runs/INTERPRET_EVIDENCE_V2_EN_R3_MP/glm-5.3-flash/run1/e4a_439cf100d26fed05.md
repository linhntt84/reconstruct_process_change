### Observed differences

- In the earlier window, A25 and A26 each occurred exactly once in every trace (participation 100 in 100, no repeats). In the later window they still appear in every trace, but each averages about 1.6 occurrences per trace, and each repeats in about 37 in 100 traces. [OCC001, OCC002]
- A26's successor changed: in the earlier window, all of A26's observed outgoing transitions went to A20; in the later window, about 64 in 100 go to A20 and about 36 in 100 go to A25 — a transition not observed in the earlier sample. [DIR002]
- The ordering of the pair loosened: A25 and A26 still co-occur in every trace, but the strict A25-before-A26 order, previously universal, now holds in only about 64 in 100 traces, while about 37 in 100 show the two interleaved or repeated. [REL001]
- Their spacing stretched: before, A25 and A26 were always exactly one event apart; in the later window, one-step adjacency covers only about 37 in 100 of the observed ordered-pair instances, with gaps of three (about 5 in 100) and five events (about 1 in 100) appearing, and the listed gaps together accounting for only about 43 in 100 instances — most pairs now lie more than five events apart. [LAG001]
- Routing at A01 shifted between two existing alternatives: of A01's observed outgoing transitions (97 earlier, 88 later), the share to A04 fell from about 42 in 100 to about 20 in 100, while the share to A05 rose from about 24 in 100 to about 45 in 100; the share to A03 stayed at about 34 in 100. - Elapsed times between consecutive completions leading into A33 roughly halved: the A12→A33 median went from about 2.0 to about 1.0 hours (59 observations in both windows), A13→A33 from about 2.0 to about 1.0 hours (42 vs 50), and A32→A33 from about 1.1 to about 0.5 hours (99 vs 91). [TWAIT001, TWAIT002, TWAIT003]

### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility that, in the later window, A25 and A26 changed from a single strictly ordered pass — A25 immediately followed by A26, after which A26 always led to A20 — into a repeating, interleaved pattern: in about 37 in 100 traces both activities now occur more than once and interleave, and about 36 in 100 of A26's outgoing transitions now return to A25 instead of going to A20. The signs are direct and mutually consistent across occurrence, routing, relation and gap indicators, each difference exceeding about 995 of 1000 random re-splits of the pooled data, and the control-flow family produced 6 qualifying items where roughly 0.29 would be expected if nothing had changed, so the ensemble is unlikely to be a sampling artefact. Limitations: the evidence shows that the two activities repeat and interleave but cannot resolve the exact cycle shape (how many passes and which activities separate them — the gap data indicate the repeated passes are usually not adjacent), and an edge unobserved in the earlier window is not proven newly added, so the A26→A25 transition may have existed as a rare path before; this is the main competing explanation the evidence cannot rule out. [OCC001, OCC002, DIR002, REL001, LAG001]

Other differences:
- After A01, the two existing alternative paths are taken with different frequencies than before: A04's share of A01's outgoing transitions dropped from about 42 in 100 to about 20 in 100 while A05's rose from about 24 in 100 to about 45 in 100, with both successors still well observed in both windows, so this reads as a shift in how often existing paths are taken rather than a replacement of paths. [DIR001]
- The elapsed time between consecutive completions into A33 halved consistently across three predecessor pairs (A12→A33, A13→A33, A32→A33; medians from about 2.0, 2.0 and 1.1 hours to about 1.0, 1.0 and 0.5 hours); the time family produced 3 qualifying items against roughly 0.007 expected by chance, but the evidence cannot show whether this timing change is connected to the A25/A26 difference or independent of it. [TWAIT001, TWAIT002, TWAIT003]
