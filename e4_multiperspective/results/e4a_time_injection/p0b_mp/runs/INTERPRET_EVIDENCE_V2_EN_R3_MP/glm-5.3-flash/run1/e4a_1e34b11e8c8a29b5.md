### Inference

Support level: Supported hypothesis

The evidence supports the possibility of a change in timing around A35: in the later window, the elapsed time from the completion of each of its predecessors A12, A13 and A34 to the completion of A35 is about half of what it was in the earlier window — from about 2 hours to about 1 hour after A12 and A13, and from about 1 hour to about half an hour after A34 [TWAIT001, TWAIT002, TWAIT003].

Supporting signs: the time family produced 3 qualifying items where about 0.01 would be expected if nothing had changed (control flow produced 0 items against 0.27 expected), so the pattern as a whole is unlikely under an unchanged process; the three intervals move in the same direction by a similar proportion, and each difference exceeds about 999.5 in 1000 random re-splits. A consistent change in these elapsed times is a timing hypothesis in its own right, so the absence of qualifying control-flow items does not weaken it [TWAIT001, TWAIT002, TWAIT003].

Limitations and competing explanation: T_WAIT measures elapsed time between consecutive completion events, so the evidence shows the interval shrank but cannot show where the time was saved — in the predecessors' completion, in A35's own execution, or in what lies between them. Because all three intervals share A35, the items may reflect one underlying change rather than three independent confirmations [TWAIT001, TWAIT002, TWAIT003].

Other differences: the pair frequencies shifted (A13 → A35 up from about 23 to 33 in 100 traces, A34 → A35 down from about 54 to 42), which could hint at a change in which predecessor typically precedes A35; however, no control-flow item qualified (0 against 0.27 expected), so this may be sampling variation rather than a routing change [TWAIT001, TWAIT002, TWAIT003].
